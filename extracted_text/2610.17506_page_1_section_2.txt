Understanding how groups can exceed individuals on cognitive tasks is a central question of collective intelligence. One way that groups may be collectively intelligent is by exhibiting greater cognitive capacities than their individual members. For example, murmurations of starlings attain an extended sensory range compared to individual birds, allowing flocks to better respond to threats in their environment (Cavagna et al., 2010). However, the cost of interaction between agents may exceed the relative performance gain of adding members. This interaction cost may be offset, however, if interacting groups can perform tasks more efficiently than the aggregation of individual efforts.
