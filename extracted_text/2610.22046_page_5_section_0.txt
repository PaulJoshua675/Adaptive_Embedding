Capability gaps, near misses and PITs are the evaluation targets of the remaining experiments. Evaluation protocol. The two scales share the problem set, but the traces, and therefore the incorrect traces and their failure types, differ between scales, so no comparison between scales is paired. Each point estimate has a 95% bootstrap interval obtained by resampling problems. We report no detected difference when the interval contains zero. Two decision thresholds were fixed before the probes were evaluated. First, a probe counts as using information in the hidden states only if its AUROC exceeds that of the strongest tested baseline for its task that uses no hidden states by 0.05, the margin, with an interval of the difference that excludes zero. We train the confidence probe, validate its detection AUROC and calibration, and then evaluate its predictions at the final step. Probe inputs. The probes read the hidden states of the frozen model at the read site, on traces that are generated once and reused for every probe (Table 3). For each step, a probe reads the hidden states at the read site at one token position, which lies at or before the last token of the step. For about half of the traces, the position read for the final step lies in an earlier step (Appendix D). We selected the read site, layers {23, 24} at both scales, from the detection AUROC of a probe at each layer, using the final label only and no failure type or PIT step (Table 10; Appendix D). Confidence probe. We call the probe trained on final labels the confidence probe. Every confidence score in this paper is an output of the confidence probe. Our confidence probe is a causal transformer encoder over the steps of a trace. At step t it reads the hidden states h1, . . . , ht at the read site and outputs the predicted correctness ct = σ(g(h1:t)) = c Pr(Y =1 | h1:t). We broadcast the trace’s final label to every valid step and minimize per-step Bernoulli loss. The same final label supervises every prefix, so the objective estimates E[Y | h1:t] [Yu et al., 2024], which equals V π t only when h1:t retains all the information in the prefix about the final label (Proposition 1, Section 4). We calibrate held-out scores by temperature scaling [Guo et al., 2017]. Training and validation traces are split by problem, so no problem contributes traces to both; we call this the problem split. Appendix B gives the architecture and training details and reports a split by trace for comparison. On held-out problems, the confidence probe predicts the final label and is calibrated before tempera- ture scaling at both scales (Table 13). Trace length, answer form and problem difficulty also predict the final label, so a probe can score well without using information beyond these variables; format variables have been shown to account for probe accuracy on a different prediction target [Sahoo et al., 2026]. Table 13 therefore also reports the detection AUROC of the probe’s score after five such variables are regressed out, and a variant of the probe trained with the Brier loss, which gives similar detection AUROC and calibration. Text baseline. Among the baselines that use no hidden states, the strongest we tested for detection is the text baseline, a gradient-boosted classifier that predicts the final label from eleven features of the trace text, such as the frequency of verification and backtracking sentences, the position of the final answer, and the number and length of sentences (Appendix B). A logistic regression on the same features has a lower detection AUROC, so a linear classifier understates the predictive power of these features (Table 13). The confidence probe reaches a detection AUROC of 0.81 at both scales, against 0.78 at 4B and 0.77 at 8B for the text baseline.
