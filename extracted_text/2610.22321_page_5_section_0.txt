Full event definitions and scoring criteria are given in App. A. 3.3 TRAINING DATA CONSTRUCTION AND ANNOTATION Our training data includes outputs from existing joint audio-video, audio-driven video, and video- to-audio generation models. We further add a small number of manually edited global-narrative negatives in GRPO to balance the distribution of failure cases. The overall pipeline is illustrated in Fig. 2. We provide two complementary forms of supervision: structured reasoning data teaches the model to follow the event-centric evaluation protocol for SFT, while human-calibrated data aligns its scores with human judgment for GRPO. The complete data sources and calibration details are provided in App. B. Structured reasoning data. Following the five-stage protocol in Section 3.2, the annotation orga- nizes modality-specific event analysis, bidirectional cross-modal verification, and the final coarse- to-fine judgment into a structured reasoning process. We construct the annotations using Gemini 2.5 Pro (Comanici et al., 2025) and Qwen3-VL-Max (Bai et al., 2025) as complementary annotators, with a silence detector providing auxiliary temporal cues. Each stage produces one structured record. The five structured records are concatenated and rewritten by Qwen3-235B (Yang et al., 2025a) into a single SFT target, ⟨think⟩C ⟨/think⟩{Smac, Stemp, Sattr, κ}, where C preserves the five-stage reasoning order. After filtering, approximately 8k samples are retained for SFT. These samples provide explicit supervision for learning event-specific evidence and event-dependent cross-modal relations under the three-level metric. Complete reward-model prompts and input–output examples are provided in App. E. Human annotation and score calibration. The structured annotation pipeline provides scalable reasoning supervision, but its scores are limited by the perceptual capability of the annotation models and can accumulate errors across stages. We therefore introduce human annotations to calibrate the scores toward human judgment. We design a 39-label taxonomy over the three consistency dimensions, covering normal states and common failure types.
