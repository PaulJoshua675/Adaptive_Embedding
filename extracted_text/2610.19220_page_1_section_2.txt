The Continuous Thought Machine (Darlow et al., 2025) evolves a neural state for a fixed number of internal ticks against frozen image features, choosing at each tick where to attend and reading a prediction from the synchronization between neurons — a decayed second moment of their co-activations. It has been applied to classification, mazes, parity, sorting and reinforcement learning, and displays emergent behaviour, including low-frequency trav- eling waves across its neurons, that has not been explained. We take the official ImageNet checkpoint, without further training, and ask what kind of object the internal trajectory is. The CTM’s neural synchronization latent acts like a memory: when it is held fixed, a tick is an ordinary map whose local behaviour is read from one Jacobian; that map is contractive in spectrum but strongly non-normal, so it has a locally unique attracting fixed point that the state never quite reaches. Because the memory changes each tick, the target moves, and inference is a curve of moving fixed points followed one step behind (§3). Most of the curve’s motion is shared across images, and attention follows a schedule that is shared as well (§4). Ordering neurons by the phase of that curve reproduces a traveling wave of neural activity reported by the CTM authors (§5), and the image’s class rides on the curve from the first tick (§6). We then turn to the memory’s role in sharpening the prediction (§7) and to the contrast with smaller MNIST and CIFAR models (§8). © 2026 T. Hannagan. arXiv:2610.19220v1 [cs.LG] 13 Oct 2026
