An environment-independent improvement. Stop-and-report has higher mean RHAE than Baseline in all four model–effort configurations, with gains of approximately 1.4–7.3 percentage points. Thus, changing only the return-of-control instruction improves the mean score with both underlying models, although the size of the gain varies across configurations. A remaining specialization advantage. Mean scores increase from Baseline to Stop-and- report, Controlled_RESET, and twma_v1.61 in every configuration. All four agents attain their highest mean scores with GPT-5.6-sol max, but twma_v1.61 still exceeds Stop-and-report by approximately seven RHAE points. The advantage of specialized scaffolding therefore persists in the strongest evaluated configuration. Adding scripted reset management recovers roughly one-third to three-fifths of the gap between Stop-and-report and twma_v1.61, without closing it. The reset convention is already stated in the environment description (Appendix A.1); the intervention supplies control around available information rather than a new game rule. The remaining differences from twma_v1.61 are not separately ablated. Performance and resource use. Within every model–effort configuration, the ordering of mean token-based cost matches the ordering of mean RHAE across the four agents. Stop-and- report costs approximately 2–52% more than Baseline, and both specialized agents cost more still.
