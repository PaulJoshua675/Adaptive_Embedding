Two literatures meet in this paper: a seventy-year debate on whether tacit knowledge can be made explicit, and a seven-paper programme that builds a memory in which that question can be posed algebraically. 2.1 Five positions on tacit knowledge Polanyi described tacit knowing as a from–to relation: we attend from subsidiary particulars to a focal whole, and the knower’s participation integrates the two (Polanyi 1958; 1966). He insisted that tacit and explicit knowledge are not sharply divided, because explicit knowledge must itself be tacitly understood and applied (Polanyi 1969). He also held that an art which cannot be specified in detail passes from master to apprentice by example rather than by prescription (Polanyi 1958). Kuhn built on Polanyi when he located much of a scientist’s competence in shared exemplars acquired through practice rather than in explicit rules (Kuhn 1962/1970). The management literature then split.
