To avoid be- coming trapped in feedback loops, biological sys- tems dynamically modulate the “precision” (re- liability) of sensory prediction errors (Pezzulo et al., 2015). In robotic control, engineering this adaptive precision-weighting acts as a neuromor- phic deadband, allowing the controller to sup- press transient sensorimotor errors (Lanillos et al., 2021; Oliver et al., 2022). This theoretical re- lationship is bidirectional: while active inference uses precision-weighting as a functional threshold, physical deadband models, such as the human- derived Disturbance Estimation and Compensa- tion (DEC) framework, produce sensory reweight- ing as an emergent macroscopic behavior with- out requiring explicit gain scheduling (Lippi & Mergner, 2017). This biologically plausible sym- metry empowers the agent to balance stabiliza- tion with epistemic exploration, ensuring it does not overreact to its action-induced sensory noise (Lanillos et al., 2021; Oliver et al., 2022; Friston et al., 2017). Embodiment and the Limits of Offline Learn- ing. The mathematical inevitability of the Ac- tion Error exposes a fundamental limit of of- fline learning: it treats the neural network as a disembodied artifact operating within an epis- temic vacuum. Stabilizing a complex dynamic system mandates structural and physical embod- iment, requiring continuous, situated interaction with the physical environment (Mergner et al., 2024). Statically deploying a frozen model com- mits a fallacy of functional anthropomorphiza- tion (Mergner et al., 2024); it expects robust adaptability while denying the system the con- tinuous physical experience, or historical embodi- ment, necessary to co-evolve with a shifting state space (Mergner et al., 2024; Mainzer, 2009). How- ever, continuous adaptation risks recursive feed- back degradation if the agent indiscriminately tracks its own self-produced errors. Safe histor- ical co-evolution requires the agent to distinguish external physical disturbances from the trajec- tory shifts induced by its own parametric sub- optimality, a control-theoretic requirement that mirrors the biological somatosensory sense of self- agency (SSA) (Mergner et al., 2024). By en- forcing time-scale separation, the architecture achieves this balance: it maintains the continu- ous structural coupling required for historical em- bodiment (Mergner et al., 2024; Mainzer, 2009), while mathematically bounding the network’s au- thority to prevent it from recursively chasing its own sensory noise. Classical Adaptive Control and Robust MPC. Architectures like Model Reference Adaptive Control (MRAC) (Ioannou & Sun, 2012; Naranjo & Annaswamy, 2025) and L1 Adaptive Control (Hovakimyan & Cao, 2010) mitigate parameter drift using robust bounds and low-pass filtering. They rely on linear-in-the-parameters (LIP) structures or continuous online tuning, which contrasts with static, non-convex offline models. While Robust Model Predictive Control (MPC) (Kouvaritakis & Cannon, 2016) circumvents of- fline bias through online optimization, supervised neural approximations of these MPC laws (de Barros Fontes et al., 2009) face spatial curvature artifacts that necessitate our proposed Hessian regularization and experience replay guardrails. Reinforcement Learning. These findings con- textualize Reinforcement Learning (RL) (Sutton & Barto, 2018). While online RL circumvents identification bias through active interaction, of- fline RL remains vulnerable to the Action Er- ror via out-of-distribution extrapolation (Fuji- moto et al., 2019; Levine et al., 2020). Con- tinuous RL tracks moving targets but relies on exploratory noise, which is dangerous for safety- critical hardware like exoskeletons (García & Fernández, 2015), reintroducing the tension be- tween Lyapunov stability and persistent excita- tion (Berkenkamp et al., 2017). Structural Parameterizations, Certificates, and Advanced Safety Wrappers. Advancements in closed-loop system identification, such as Dual IOP (Chen et al., 2023), Neural Identification (Boroujeni et al., 2025a), and the nonlinear gen- eralization of Independent Component Identifi- cation (ICI) (Boroujeni et al., 2025b), mitigate the initial Identification Bias by guaranteeing sta- bilized model extraction from closed-loop data. Mitigating the subsequent Action Error requires run-time safety. Certificate learning frameworks provide learned Lyapunov or Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) (Dawson et al., 2023), which, like inherent Lyapunov structures (Min et al., 2023), act as fast-loop spatial safety filters, while Safe Bayesian Optimization (BO) (Hirt et al., 2024) provides probabilistic constraints during closed-loop tuning. Because these structural methods prioritize guaranteed bounds over spa- tial representation recovery, our proposed time- scale separated adaptation (λmax(Γ) < 2/Lθ) does not replace them; rather, it acts as a slow-loop companion.
