A.2.2 TRAINING We train the camera predictor in three stages. First, we optimize the motion backbone using metric FRONT-camera trajectories from Waymo. Each training sample contains four observed poses at t−0.6, t−0.4, t−0.2, and t, together with ten future poses at 0.2 s intervals. The objective com- bines absolute pose error, adjacent relative-pose error, and ray-endpoint error with weights 1.0, 0.5, and 0.2, respectively. Rotation errors are converted to metric displacement using a 10 m lever arm. We first train for 12,000 steps with direct ten-horizon supervision using AdamW, batch size 1,024, weight decay 0.05, and a cosine learning-rate schedule from 10−4 to 10−6 after 300 warm-up steps. We then continue for another 12,000 steps with closed-loop training, recursively applying the pre- dictor over four consecutive rollout chunks. The closed-loop loss is weighted by 0.5. This phase uses batch size 384, a 100-step warm-up, and a cosine learning-rate schedule from 5 × 10−5 to 5 × 10−7. Gradients are clipped to norm 1.0 throughout motion training. Second, we train the visual adapter while keeping the motion backbone frozen. We use the same pose-based supervision, with the relative-pose and ray-endpoint terms both weighted by 0.5 and a 20 m rotation lever arm. Turning and speed-changing windows are assigned sample weights of 1.5 and 0.5, respectively. Training uses 123,789 windows for 20 epochs with AdamW, batch size 256, weight decay 0.02, and a cosine learning-rate schedule from 2 × 10−4 to 2 × 10−6. Gradients are clipped to norm 1.0. Finally, we adapt the visual adapter to the downstream depth-query task through the frozen geometry decoder. The last observed GeoState St is held fixed across future horizons and decoded using the predicted future camera poses. The decoded depth is supervised against future Waymo LiDAR. To account for the horizon-dependent difficulty of querying a fixed scene state, we normalize the Ab- sRel at each horizon by the corresponding GT-camera Copy-Last AbsRel estimated over the training set. This depth objective is combined with the trajectory loss and a parameter-anchor regularizer, weighted by 0.05 and 0.02, respectively. Only the visual adapter is updated in this stage. We train for 1,200 steps using AdamW with learning rate 5 × 10−6, weight decay 0.01, and gradient clipping at 1.0. A.2.3 EVALUATION RESULTS We evaluate on the same Waymo validation windows as depth forecasting. Predicted and GT camera-to-world poses share the metric frame of the last observed FRONT camera, with no post-hoc trajectory alignment. At each reported horizon, errors are pooled over all 100 sequences and all fu- ture steps up to that horizon before computing RMSE. ATE measures camera-center error.
