G.4 Naturally Occurring Pair Imbalance At each logging step, before the optimizer update, we track the maximum stabilized log-ratio imbalance across all layers: r(t) ℓ,δ := log A(t) ℓ F + δ B(t) ℓ F + δ , ∆(t) max := max ℓ |r(t) ℓ,δ|, δ := 10−12. (27) We record this statistic for each symmetry pair type Q–K, V–O, and MLP. This statistic is the absolute imbalance at step t. Table 16 instead reports its increase between the first and last logged states: G := ∆(29.5K) max −∆(0) max. (28) The reported G is a difference of maxima. The growth statistic G is distinct from both the endpoint ∆(29.5K) max and the maximum layerwise change maxℓ|r(29.5K) ℓ,δ −r(0) ℓ,δ |. For positive norms a := ∥A∥F and b := ∥B∥F, the ideal log-ratio r := log(a/b) used in the theory satisfies rδ −r = log(1 + δ/a) −log(1 + δ/b). Thus the stabilized measurement approximates r when both norms are large relative to δ, but the stabilized measurement does not obey the exact orbit-shift or contraction identities near the floor. Experimental references to measured log-ratios use this numerical convention. Since initialization is not exactly balanced, a nonzero endpoint alone does not establish growth from initialization; positive G does establish an increase in the maximum absolute imbalance. We train for 30,000 steps with two seeds, context length 256, 2K-step warmup, and logging every 500 steps. Peak learning rate is 6 × 10−4 for the small and deep models and 3 × 10−4 for the medium model. The last logged drift state is at step 29.5K, before the final 500 updates. The log-ratio increment depends on parameter and update inner products, as in Eq. 23; different gradient magnitudes alone do not determine the direction or persistence of drift.
