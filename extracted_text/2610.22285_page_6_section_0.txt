For each environment, we construct 50,000 trajectories from 5,000 random start-goal end-effector pairs, linearly interpolated in joint space and perturbed by 10 random smooth deformations each. Each trajectory has 21 waypoints encoding joint configurations and the xyz positions and rotations of all joints. We split the bank 60-20-20% into training, validation, and test sets. The simulated human draws its 10 initial demonstrations and all preference-query pairs from the training set. We model the demonstrator as Boltzmann-rational with ground-truth weights w⋆and a per-feature rationality vector β ∈Rk ≥0. A high βl produces demonstrations that reliably optimize ϕl, while βl = 0 simulates underspecification. Distractors are fixed to w⋆ l = 0. When answering queries without an explanation, the human uses βi = β01, so responses follow the full w⋆. When the robot provides an explanation directing attention to ϕexp ⊆ϕ, the human’s per-query rationality vector has nonzero entries only on the indices of ϕexp, so the response depends only on those features. Methods (1) Informed Prior constructs P(w | o) by block-wise modification of a Gaussian ap- proximation of P(w | D). The active query loop targets P(w | P, o) and selects queries by infor- mation gain. (2) Informed Prior + Informed Explanation further sets ϕexp to the predicted ϕu, so the human attends to the same features the prior leaves unconstrained. (3) Oracle Prior uses the true set of underspecified features. (4) Oracle Prior + Oracle Explanation further sets ϕexp to the true underspecified features. (5) Extra-Only Prior is a version of the informed prior that does not predict underspecified features. It combines the predicted irrelevant features ϕε, represented as a diagonal Gaussian, with the demonstration likelihood. (6) Standard models posterior P(w | P, D) by com- bining the demonstration likelihood with a uniform prior over the unit ball and selects queries by information gain. (7) Standard + Oracle Explanation sets ϕexp to the true underspecified features, isolating the contribution of the explanation alone. (8) Targeted uses the unmodified P(w | D) and no explanation (βi = β01), but selects queries by maximizing I(wu; qi | Qi, βi) over the predicted ϕu. (9) Gaussian Approximation is Standard but with the Gaussian approximation of P(w | D), indicating the penalty of summarizing the original posterior. (10) Random draws queries uniformly at random. We report normalized reward: the average ground-truth reward of the top-k test trajec- tories under the posterior mean, normalized by the range of ground-truth rewards across the test set. All experiments are repeated across 5 seeds, use k = 20, and we report mean and standard error. 5.2 Results The informed prior accelerates reward recovery across both environments and degrees of underspeci- fication. We evaluate settings with one and then two underspecified task-relevant features, averaging over every choice of underspecified feature or pair of underspecified features (Fig. 3). Results for each individual configuration are provided in Fig. 5 of Appendix B. In both cases, the informed prior recovers the true reward faster than the baselines. The relative ordering of the methods is also stable across the single-feature and two-feature underspecification conditions (Fig. 3, left panels), with the largest gains generally appearing in the low-query regime (K ≤10). This is practically significant as the early queries are the regime where reducing human effort matters most. Aver- aged over all 20 configurations, Informed Prior outperforms Standard by 13.0% at K = 5 and by 11.3% at K = 10. The largest gap of 30.8% occurs at K = 4 in Stove when human and point are both underspecified (Fig. 5b).
