Chen et al. 3 Method Our goal is to align dense representations produced by a pre-trained language model with SPLADE’s high-dimensional sparse vocabulary space, enabling ef- ficient sparse retrieval while leveraging the semantic richness of LLM represen- tations. To bridge the structural gap between dense hidden states and sparse term-based representations, inspired by the Scaling-Retriever paradigm [26], we propose a two-stage framework: (1) project encoder outputs into the vocabulary space with sparse-compatible activations, and (2) enforce adaptive, input-aware sparsity via a dynamic α-truncation mechanism. Following common practices in large-scale retriever models, we adopt a bidirectional attention configuration [26] to better capture contextual dependencies in the encoder representations. 3.1 High-Dimensional Projection Given an input sequence, a pre-trained encoder produces hidden states h ∈ RL×d. We project these states into the vocabulary dimension V and obtain the final sparse representation v via max-pooling [8]: v = log (1 + ReLU (MaxPool (hWp + bp))) ∈RV , (1) where Wp ∈Rd×V and bp ∈RV are learnable parameters, and the log(1 + ReLU(·)) transformation ensures non-negativity and sparsity. 3.2 Adaptive α-Truncation for Sparse Alignment Let ˆv ∈RV denote a reference sparse vector from SPLADE, treated as a struc- tured anchor space rather than a teacher. We define a dynamic truncation mask that adapts to the input’s activation distribution.
