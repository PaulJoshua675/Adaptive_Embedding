Following the DeepStack mechanism of Qwen3-VL (Meng et al., 2024; Bai et al., 2025), the three multi-level features of each stream are added directly to the hidden states of the first three language-model layers, one feature per layer, at the positions of that stream’s own visual tokens. The plot and delay streams thus inject into the same layers but at disjoint token positions. Rendering and tokenization details are provided in Appendix B.1. 3.4 TRAINING Following the staged training paradigm common in multimodal language models and recent TSLMs (Xie et al., 2025; Langer et al., 2026), we train our model, initialized from Qwen3-VL- 8B (Bai et al., 2025), in two stages that separate the alignment of the new delay stream from task- level instruction tuning. In Stage I (modality alignment), the Qwen3-VL vision encoder and the language model remain frozen, and we train LoRA adapters (Hu et al., 2022) on DINOv3 together with the projection M (0), which maps the final DINOv3 patch features to delay tokens, and the projections {M (i)}3 i=1, which map the intermediate DINOv3 features to the multi-level DeepStack features. This stage is trained on captioning data, encouraging the delay stream to produce represen- tations that the frozen language model can interpret. In Stage II (instruction tuning), the DINOv3 adapters are merged, the entire visual pathway is frozen, and LoRA is applied to the language model, which is trained on time-series question answering to reason jointly over both views. Both stages use the standard next-token prediction loss computed over the answer tokens only. The objective and hyperparameters are given in Appendix B.2. 4 TRAINING DATA We construct a dedicated training corpus for each of the two stages described in Section 3.4. Stage I uses caption supervision to align the new delay-embedding stream with the language model, whereas Stage II uses a heterogeneous instruction corpus to train perception, numerical readout, forecasting, and temporal reasoning. Most time series are generated procedurally, with labels obtained directly from generator parameters or exact functions of the realized signal rather than from a teacher model. The only real-world time-series sources are CaTS-Bench (Zhou et al., 2026) in Stage I and a small subset of sports win-probability series from GAMETime (Tan et al., 2025) used for abductive rea- soning in Stage II.
