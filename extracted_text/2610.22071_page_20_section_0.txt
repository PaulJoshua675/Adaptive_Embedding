For input [BOS, x0, . . . , xL−1], hidden state hj at j ≥1 ends at text byte xj−1. For 1 ≤j < L, the predictor compares projected hj and hj+1; an end flag at j therefore denotes a cut at text byte offset j, with xj as lookahead. The released zero-temperature rule cuts when the boundary log-probability is strictly greater than log(1/2), so identical visible prefixes receive identical decisions. It forces the BOS flag at j = 0 and suppresses the last byte’s flag at j = L, where lookahead is unavailable. The audit uses only 1 ≤j < L as interior cuts and counts the final text unit separately. The training implementation defaults to end boundaries, although the released configuration does not record which mode was used for training. The shifted-offset check validates the inference mapping without resolving that missing metadata. Boundary-Lookahead Probe Design. Each of the 18 runs trains a predictor with a 513-entry, 128-dimensional symbol embedding (including padding), a one-layer causal LSTM with 512 units, layer normalization, and H-Net’s cosine boundary head (Hwang et al., 2026) as adapted in Bolmo (Minixhofer et al., 2025). The head places a cut after position i when (1 −cos(f(hi), g(hi+k)))/2 > 1/2, where f and g are learned linear projections. Training runs for 4,000 steps with 64 rows per step. Each row begins at a uniformly sampled cluster start in a uniformly sampled language and contains up to 384 supervised scalars, followed by up to three scalars of unsupervised lookahead context. Row sampling depends only on the seed, ensuring identical text exposure across encodings and lookahead settings within a seed. The loss adds the mean binary cross-entropy (BCE) over scalar-end decisions to the mean BCE over within-scalar decisions. We use AdamW with learning rate 2 × 10−3, weight decay 0.01, 100 warmup steps followed by cosine decay to one tenth, and gradient norm clipping at 1; BCE probabilities are clipped to [10−3, 1 −10−3]. The encoder and projections use bfloat16, with the cosine and loss computed in float32. For each language, training uses whole documents in file order from the first FineWeb-2 shard until the retained NFC text reaches at least 8 million UTF-8 bytes. For each language, documents containing one of that language’s FLORES-200 devtest sentences are excluded. Targets are extended grapheme cluster boundaries from the same regex library version as the boundary audit. This design matches training text while allowing symbol counts and FLOPs to differ; UTF-8 processes more positions. The two loss terms have equal aggregate weight, but their gradients can differ across encodings. UBE-BE preserves UBE’s symbol count and loss weights while reordering the entire encoded history, so its contrast with UBE includes effects on history representation and optimization as well as the first lookahead symbol. 20
