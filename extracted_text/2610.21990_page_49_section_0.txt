The evaluations for VesperMUSE across all tasks is conducted on two main datasets: PST900 [8] and CART [25]. The low-light adapted images and later enhanced are conducted on the same splits as used for the semantic segmentation evaluation. PST900. The PST900 benchmark [8] consists of 894 synchronized RGB-Thermal image pairs ac- quired within demanding subterranean settings. The dataset features pixel-level semantic annota- tions for five distinct object categories: background, hand drill, backpack, fire extinguisher, and survivor. All modalities maintain a native resolution of 1280 × 720 pixels. For experimental con- sistency, the data is partitioned into training and evaluation subsets following a 2 : 1 distribution ratio. CART. The CART dataset [25] comprises 2,282 co-registered RGB-Thermal pairs captured across heterogeneous outdoor environments, including aquatic regions (rivers, lakes, coastlines), arid deserts, and dense forestation. Semantic labels are provided for a comprehensive 10-class taxon- omy: bare ground, rocky terrain, developed structures, road, shrubs, trees, sky, water, vehicles, and person. The imagery is provided at a resolution of 960 × 640 pixels. The corpus is stochastically divided into training, validation, and testing sets using a 6 : 1 : 1 ratio, respectively. Empirical contributions. The results achieved by VesperMUSE across all levels and tasks can be summarised as: • Robust Synthetic Low-Light Adaptation: The synthetic adaptation of the PST900 [8] and CART [25] datasets achieves a superior SNR of approximately 10.7 dB and a physical Gamma distribution of 1.00. These metrics surpass established synthetic low-light adaptation pipelines such as the one used for creating LOL-v2 synthetic [33] in terms of modeling low-light envi- ronments, ensuring that the low-light data maintains realistic luminance statistics and physics- informed consequences of light-deficiency. • Efficient and High-Performance LLIE: The VesperMUSE LLIE component demonstrates significant performance improvements over existing methods, achieving a 29.5% PSNR in- crease over LYT-Net [47] and a 2.3% increase over Retinexformer [50] on the synthetically- adapted CART dataset [25].
