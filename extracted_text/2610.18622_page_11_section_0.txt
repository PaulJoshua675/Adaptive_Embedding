Answer: For dense retrievers, representations based on core chunks with contextual enrichment use substantially fewer indexed units than Hierarchical Chunks while achieving comparable or better retrieval results in most cases. We found no clear evidence that full hierarchical context outperforms core chunks enriched with document metadata (title and summary). We therefore recommend the simpler Core Chunks + Metadata representation. 5.2 Practical Implications The results suggest that legal retrieval systems can use the structure of legal acts without index- ing all levels of their hierarchy. The Core Chunks + Metadata representation achieved results comparable to or better than Hierarchical Chunks while indexing substantially fewer units. They also show that contextual information is important, as removing document metadata consistently reduced retrieval effectiveness. Thus, practical implementations can focus on core structural units, such as articles, enriched with concise document-level metadata. 6 Limitations of the study This study has four main limitations. First, we evaluated only two Brazilian legal test collections, NormasTCU and Ulysses-RFCorpus. They are the only available datasets that match the charac- teristics required by this study and cover different types of legal documents and query formulations, but they do not represent the full diversity of Brazilian legal documents. Moreover, about one-third of the Ulysses-RFCorpus documents do not follow the expected structure and therefore cannot be represented using the proposed hierarchical segmentation. Second, we evaluated BM25 and three dense embedding models using max-score aggregation only. Our findings may not generalize to other retrieval models or chunk aggregation methods. Third, we tested four representation strategies based on Lima [23]. Although our experi- ment show the effects of core chunks and contextual enrichment, we dit not test every possible combination of structural components and contextual information. Finally, our evaluation focuses on retrieval effectiveness measured by nDCG@10 and MRR. These metrics capture different aspects of ranking quality, but do not measure other practical dimensions of a retrieval system, such as storage or computational costs. 7 Conclusions We investigated whether structural segmentation improves the retrieval of Brazilian legal doc- uments. We tested four document representations based on their multi-layered structure: one hierarchical representation and three simplified representations based on core chunks with different levels of context. The results show that context-enriched representations improve dense retrieval over whole- document indexing. On the evaluated test collections, Hierarchical Chunks increased nDCG@10 for all three dense retrievers relative to whole-document indexing, despite substantially increasing the number of indexed units. The same pattern was generally observed for MRR. For BM25, however, structural segmentation did not improve nDCG@10, although it improved MRR in some configurations. Ablation studies show that the complete hierarchical representation is not necessary to achieve these gains. The Core Chunks + Full Context and Core Chunks + Metadata representations achieved results close to – and in some cases better than – Hierarchical Chunks, while indexing substantially fewer indexed units. In contrast, removing context consistently reduced retrieval effectiveness. Overall, structural segmentation effectively boosts Brazilian legal information retrieval. How- ever, the complete structure of a legal act is not necessary to achieve these gains, and a simplified representation based on core chunks enriched with document metadata offers a favorable trade- off between representation complexity and retrieval effectiveness. Future work should evaluate additional structural representations, retrieval models, and aggregation methods, including seg- mentation strategies for legal documents that do not follow the structure considered in this article. 11
