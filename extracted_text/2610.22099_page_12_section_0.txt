A Expanded methodology A.1 Fitting the visibility schedule We select γ by fitting the visibility trajectory of a separately trained, uncalibrated pilot policy. Another feasible approach is to optimize γ via hyperparameter search on the validation set. We first train this pilot from scratch with RLVR and terminal correctness rewards, without supervised warmup or logit calibration. We then freeze the pilot and collect N trajectories on the held-out validation prompts, again without calibration. The policy observes the current time t/Tfit, but the fit uses the next-state time (t+1)/Tfit. We exclude deterministic finalization because its full visibility is imposed by the decoding algorithm. The fit uses neither reference answers nor correctness labels for the validation prompts. We then fix bγ during subsequent calibrated policy training and evaluate the same normalized schedule at other inference budgets. The pilot supplies the schedule shape; it is not the final calibrated policy. For blockwise decoding, we apply the same procedure to the active block, using its length and local decoder-call budget in place of L and Tfit. We average the expected visible fractions over validation trajectories and blocks at matching local times, exclude each block’s deterministic finalization, and fit one shared γ for that decoding geometry. A.2 Fast-dLLM budget matching For a target budget T, we tune the confidence-decoding threshold using test-set rollouts until the average NFE lies in [T, T + 0.5]. The one-sided tolerance gives the baseline up to 0.5 additional decoder calls on average. 12
