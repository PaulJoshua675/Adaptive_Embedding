In Fig. 5 and Fig. 6, we compare qualitative results at a compression ratio of 0.5. On PixArt-α, directly merging tokens based on feature similarity (ToMeSD) severely disrupts spatial structure, often leading to obvious structural distortion. Although the CFG-based method preserves foreground structures that are explicitly described in the prompt, it shows noticeably weaker preservation in background and other weakly conditioned regions, as highlighted by the red boxes. This qualitatively supports our analysis that CFG-based importance is strongly biased toward prompt-conditioned content and is therefore less effective at preserving the full image context. On FLUX, existing methods generally preserve the coarse prompt semantics, but still introduce noticeable deviations from the original generation. ToMeSD tends to alter the original composition, object geometry, or viewpoint, as seen in the relative arrangement of the cats, the fire hydrant structure, and the airplane viewpoint. ToMA shows similar structural drift while additionally introducing noisy visual degradation that weakens overall image quality. In contrast, DaTMe more faithfully preserves the original spatial configuration and object structure while maintaining the intended prompt semantics. These results demonstrate that DaTMe reduces both structural deviation and visual degradation across different architectures. Additional results under various compression ratios are in Appendix C.
