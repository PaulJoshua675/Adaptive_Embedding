Consistent with its per-series normalization, the delay-only representation is nearly invariant to the measured affine transformations: its clean accuracy of 44.7% changes only slightly under rescaling and offset shifts. Our model remains sensitive to affine transformations because its line-plot stream continues to en- code the observed measurement frame, while maintaining substantially higher absolute accuracy than Plot only across all reported interventions. Its sensitivity also depends on the type of shift: noise and smoothing degrade both Plot only and our model, whereas our model changes relatively little under changes in sampling horizon. Thus, combining the two views does not impose a single invariance profile or provide uniform robustness to every perturbation. Together with the TSRBench results, these evaluations indicate that the benefit of the dual representation is not confined to a single benchmark: the model remains strong across perceptual, numerical, and broader temporal reasoning tasks, despite using a Qwen3-VL-8B backbone. 6 DISCUSSION Time-series reasoning requires both numerical readout and understanding of temporal dynamics, yet these capabilities favor different representational properties. This tension motivated our central question: can complementary representations improve general-purpose time-series reasoning? We establish complementary representation as a design principle for TSLMs and instantiate it in a dual- view model that integrates measurement-preserving line plots and normalized delay embeddings through dedicated visual encoders and a shared language model. The resulting model raises TSR- Bench accuracy from 45.2% for the plot-only variant to 53.2%, surpassing all evaluated dedicated TSLMs. The gains are concentrated beyond basic perception and are not reproduced when both encoders receive line plots or both receive delay embeddings. The model also leads the evaluated baselines on TSExam and multivariate ChatTS numerical alignment, achieving 89.9% and 86.8% accuracy, respectively, demonstrating strong performance across perceptual, numerical, and broader temporal reasoning tasks. The STFT comparison in Appendix C.5 further shows that the preferred secondary representation varies across evaluations: delay embeddings perform better on TSRBench, while STFT provides small advantages on ChatTS numerical alignment and average reasoning accuracy. This suggests that representation choice should reflect the capabilities a model must support. The intervention analysis likewise distinguishes access to an invariant view from invariance of the combined model, which retains both normalized structure and absolute measurements. Future work could explore additional representations and encoder combinations, including more than two views and query- dependent mechanisms for combining them. 9
