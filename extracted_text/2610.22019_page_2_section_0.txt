Preprint can reduce how much the model later gains from RL (Liu et al., 2026b; Kang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026), and we have observed this in our own training as well. Midtraining fills this gap. Before the final long-context stage, it trains for hundreds of billions of tokens at 256K, long enough to hold whole repositories, commit histories, and long agent traces (§5.1). The data is mostly natural software-engineering data, with a bounded share of agent traces whose tool responses are masked from the loss. Much of this learning can therefore move out of SFT, so SFT can stay short and preserve the model’s later RL trainability (Liu et al., 2026b; Kang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026). We develop the recipe on Nemotron 3.5 Lightning-30B-A3B and then scale it to the larger Nemotron 3.5 Super-120B-A12B (NVIDIA, 2026a). On N-3.5 Lightning, 500B midtraining to- kens improve SWE-bench Verified, Multilingual, and Pro by 3.8, 7.0, and 3.5 points over the same model without midtraining after identical full SFT, with the most sustained gains on SWE-bench Multilingual (§4.3). On N-3.5 Super, 280B midtraining tokens improve DeepSWE from 11.7 to 15.7 and SWE-bench Verified from 72.9 to 74.3 after identical full SFT (§4.4). Midtraining also improves robustness to the agent harness, which matters for open models that are used with many different harnesses. Both models improve on every harness we tested, and on N-3.5 Super after 65B tokens and short SFT, the average gain across harnesses is 8.6 points on SWE-bench Verified and 6.8 on Terminal-Bench (§4.5). We make the following contributions. • A fully documented midtraining data recipe, showing that a small share of agentic traces adds clear gains on top of natural data (§3 and §4.2). • An evaluation protocol based on identical SFT runs, since base-model proxy metrics such as bits-per-byte and LoCoDiff did not reliably predict post-training results (§4.1). • We show that gains hold as we scale both model size and midtraining tokens up to 500B, across benchmarks and agent harnesses (§4). • Ablations of modeling choices, such as the learning rate, the share of replayed pretraining data, tool-response masking, and midtraining’s effect on long-context ability (§5). 2
