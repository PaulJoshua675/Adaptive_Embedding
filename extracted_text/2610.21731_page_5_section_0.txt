Limitations We study only one proprietary model, the lightest of the three GPT-5.6 tiers, chosen for cost. A natural extension is to run the experiments on additional frontier models, which may demonstrate different behaviour. Z.ai’s GLM-5.3-Flash discloses no training cut- off, so we check for contamination through its be- haviour instead. We find its control-fact perfor- mance and closed-book accuracy match those of the models with stated cutoffs (Appendix B). We include it because it is one of the most capable open-weight models available. Most prompts require a fixed answer line, so that answers can be graded mechanically rather than by an LLM. This ‘contract’ can result in dif- ferent model behaviour compared to exclusively free prose. In the study’s pre-registered answer- line check, it lowered Luna’s false endorsement by 17.5 points (Appendix C). Luna’s deployed failures, which anchor the carry-over analysis, are therefore likely to be lower bounds. We keep the answer line because it gives standardised, reliable grading across the more than 30,000 replies in the deployed and frozen runs. Unfaithful citations are rare for some models under frozen retrieval (1.0% of Luna’s graded an- swers and 1.8% of GLM-5.3-Flash’s), so a few grading errors could shift these rates substantially, in both absolute and relative terms. We therefore took particular care with grading. An LLM marker (DeepSeek-V4-Flash; DeepSeek-AI, 2026) helps grade that the deterministic grader cannot decide, but it can only pass a reply, never assign an adverse label. Every unfaithful citation, and every other adverse label that needs judgement, is assigned or confirmed by a human (Appendix D). Failures also vary substantially across domains: sports drives the severe rates, while SEC (13 facts) and chemical (8) are underpowered because few usable post-cutoff facts exist for them. We include them because they add variety to the study. That sports drives so many failures is a finding in its own right, since the answer usually sits in the first line of the player’s Wikipedia page, whereas FDA questions require reading long label sections and reasoning about often complex medical knowledge. The layer-wise analysis with the logit lens and J- lens uses sports transfers only, because the answer, a club name, is only a few tokens long. It charac- terises the failure but does not test a mechanism causally, for example by activation patching. Ethical Considerations Our finding that search-augmented LLMs often answer from snippets alone could help malicious actors spread misinformation, since misleading text that appears in a snippet may be repeated without the model opening the page. To limit this risk, we report only aggregate failure rates and release no tools for manipulating search snippets. All facts in the study are public. Most come from moderated sources (Wikipedia and Wikidata) or from govern- ment records (FDA drug labels and SEC filings); every finance value we use is the figure reported in an SEC filing, and we re-derive all 52 from SEC EDGAR. The people domain names individuals whose deaths are recorded on Wikipedia. These facts cannot be anonymised, because each concerns a named person, but they are already public, and the outdated claims about them are used only as test items. Outdated drug-label information carries clinical risk, which is one of the main reasons we include FDA label changes. Nothing in this study, including the drug-label facts, should be used for medical purposes. We queried all models in line with their providers’ terms of use, and no human participants were involved beyond the authors’ own grading.
