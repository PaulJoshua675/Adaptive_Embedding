Tool-using agents are increasingly wrapped in step-level guards: a model that inspects the user’s request, the trajectory so far and the candidate tool call, and blocks the call if it appears unsafe [2–4]. The guard reads two channels. The untrusted channel (tool outputs, web pages, files) is where prompt injections live, and its robustness is well studied [1, 3]. The trusted channel is the user’s own request. The released guard prompts are English, and so are the benchmarks on which purpose-built guards are trained and evaluated (TS-Bench, Agent-SafetyBench, ATBench), but the principal they protect writes in whatever language they speak.
