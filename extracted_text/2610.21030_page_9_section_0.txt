Figure 4 summarizes the performance-compute tradeoff on BRIGHT. Increasing search budget improves retrieval performance for both strate- gies, suggesting that additional reasoning can trans- late into stronger retrieval representations when guided by utility estimation. However, greedy ex- pansion consistently achieves better compute effi- ciency than trajectory-level Best-of-k search. Per- formance improves from 34.7 (k = 1) to 37.2 (k = 64), compared to 36.7 under Best-of-k on the same search scale. These results suggest that retrieval-oriented reasoning benefits more from early utility-guided decisions than from selecting among completed reasoning trajectories. By pri- oritizing promising intermediate states throughout the reasoning process rather than evaluating tra- jectories only after completion, AURA allocates inference-time computation more effectively. 4.10 Qualitative Analysis Trajectory inspection shows that discarded later steps can be semantically valid rather than incorrect. In the insects-and-LEDs example in Figure 2, early reasoning identifies the proximate-versus-ultimate causation distinction, improving separation from hard negatives. Later steps add evolutionary and sensory-ecology background. Though useful, these additions repeat encoded constraints or general- ize the query toward hard negatives about evolu- tion and insect ecology. The estimator therefore stops once the discriminative constraint is encoded. This illustrates AURA’s central distinction: infor- mative text need not provide retrieval utility; stop- ping should depend on document discrimination rather than textual detail. 5
