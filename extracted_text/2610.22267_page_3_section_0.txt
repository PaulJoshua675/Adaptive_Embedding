This motivates a single scalar measuring the collective volume a batch occu- pies, penalizing redundancy at every order up to B simultaneously. We realize this scalar as the log-determinant of an RBF Gram matrix over one-step Tweedie predictions, making all-order diversity differentiable. We further show that this objective is self-regulating: repulsion amplifies when the batch is most redundant and relaxes as diversity is achieved, an adaptation that an unweighted sum-of- pairwise objective lacks in the same analysis. We propose DetNO (Determinant Noise Optimization), illustrated in Fig. 2, which pairs this log-determinant diversity term with an anchor-based fidelity term via Lagrangian relaxation. A single hyperparameter λ controls the diversity- quality trade-off, with no training or sampler modification. Empirically, the col- lective objective delivers on both diversity and downstream utility. Across three backbones (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3), DetNO achieves leading intra-batch diversity in VendiScore and Mean Self-Similarity, with the advantage over CNO widen- ing as the batch size grows. A controlled toy experiment shows faithful recovery of all modes where pairwise methods distort mode structure, and downstream few-shot classification produces accuracy gains over both standard sampling and CNO, reaching statistical significance in the low-shot regime (p<0.01).
