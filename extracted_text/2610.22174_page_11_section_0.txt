Mosaic: Multi-Session Agentic Reinforcement Learning Cross-session state transfer as a central learning objective. Context compaction and subagent forking both involve information transfer, but serve different purposes: compaction must preserve sufficient working state for continued execution; forking must provide the information needed to complete a subtask; merging must integrate subtask results into a state usable by the main agent. These operations motivate a shared research question: under a limited context budget, what information should be carried forward to support subsequent decisions? CompactionRL already jointly optimizes task execution and context summarization (Li et al., 2026). A unified approach to learning state transfer across compaction, forking, and merging remains an important direction, alongside systematic investigation of information loss and error accumulation over repeated transfers. Asynchronous training for multi-session rollouts. For partial rollout or asynchronous training settings, multi-session execution can amplify variation in trajectory lengths and completion times, increase policy staleness and complicate within-group comparisons and sample reuse. For instance, prioritizing completed rollouts may favor trajectories with less sessions over longer ones associated with harder tasks, introducing selection bias and shaping learned execution strategies. Our experiments use a colocated setup with alternating rollout and training phases to minimize interference from asynchrony and isolate the effects of the optimization signals. Scaling toward actual production raises a further question: how can multi-session optimization remain reliable under asynchronous sampling and policy staleness? SAO explores asynchronous optimization with one rollout per prompt, using a learned value model (Hou et al., 2026). However, critic-free, group-relative optimization requires further investigation into when and how rollout groups are formed, how stale samples are handled, and how collected trajectories can be reused. Toward RL within (black-boxed) agent harnesses. General multi-session agentic RL can accom- modate workflows in which execution no longer follows a single, continuously accumulated context prefix. Context compaction and subagent forking, the two scenarios studied in this work, are represen- tative examples of such workflows in modern agent harnesses. Progress in multi-session optimization could thus provide an important foundation for training agents within harnesses whose orchestration is partially opaque (Xu et al., 2026a), such as Claude Code. Therefore, a critical next step is to systematically examine mechanisms across agent harnesses that disrupt context continuity, such as dropping reasoning tokens or tool responses, and improve general multi-session agentic RL under these conditions. Additionally, when discontinuity occurs, the choice between introducing a new session boundary and applying lossy concatenation with the preceding session may also influence the overall effectiveness of optimization.
