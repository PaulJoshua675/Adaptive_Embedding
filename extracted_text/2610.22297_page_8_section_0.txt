For DP-RP-G, achieving an (ϵ, δ) guarantee requires bounding sensitivity, typically by clipping each projected embedding to norm c, and then adding Gaussian noise N  0, c√ 2 ln(1.25/δ) ϵ  . If ϵ →∞, the noise vanishes; with a sufficiently large clipping bound, DP-RP-G approaches ours RP method. Thus, the utility loss arises from the additional constraints required for formal DP rather than from RP itself. DP aggregation methods face a similar trade-off, as their utility depends strongly on perturbation magnitude and therefore on privacy budget [6]. As shown in Section 4, even relatively loose privacy budgets can substantially degrade utility while still allowing non-negligible leakage. Clipping is typically required in DP mechanisms to bound sensitivity [36]; prior work similarly constrains vectors to bounded domains [37]. To isolate the effect of clipping and adding noise, we ablate the clipping operation without Gaussian noise. In Table 9, we compare RP with and without clipping, while Figure 5 varies the clipping bound. The results show that larger clipping consistently reduces perplexity, whereas sufficiently large clipping bounds recover performance close to RP. 5
