Preprint B.5 VISION–LANGUAGE DATA REKI is co-trained on vision–language data at 10% of the training samples (Table 2). The pool is every FineVision (Wiedmann et al., 2025) dataset except the text-only ones, plus the LLaVA-OneVision- 1.5 (An et al., 2025) datasets that FineVision does not already contain, ∼20.2M samples. We keep the datasets covering the tasks most relevant to manipulation: natural-scene objects and attributes, spatial relations, counting, grounding, captioning, scene text, and knowledge and commonsense VQA. Documents, charts, tables, mathematics, code, transcription, GUI grounding, memes and puzzles are dropped. 60 datasets remain, 7.64M samples or 37.8% of the pool. One question–answer pair is drawn per sample and sampling is uniform over samples, so exposure is proportional to the size of a dataset. The 51 kept from FineVision are a_okvqa (Schwenk et al., 2022), alfworldgpt (Shridhar et al., 2021), allava_laion (Chen et al., 2024a), allava_vflan (Chen et al., 2024a), aokvqa (Schwenk et al., 2022), art (Chng et al., 2019), cambrian(filtered)_processed (Tong et al., 2024), clevr (Lindström & Abraham, 2022), clevr_math (Lindström & Abraham, 2022), coco_colors (Karakus, 2024), cocoqa (Ren et al., 2015), cocotext (Veit et al., 2016), densefusion_1m (Li et al., 2024), drivelm (Sima et al., 2024), est_vqa (Wang et al., 2020), google_landmarks (Weyand et al., 2020), image_textualization(filtered) (Pi et al., 2024), indoor_qa (Quattoni & Torralba, 2009), laion_gpt4v (LAION, 2023), LLaVA_Instruct_150K (Liu et al., 2023c), lnqa (Pont-Tuset et al., 2020), localized_narratives (Pont-Tuset et al., 2020), lrv_normal(filtered) (Liu et al., 2024a), lvis_instruct4v (Wang et al., 2023), mimic_cgd (Laurençon et al., 2024), mmevol (Luo et al., 2024), nlvr2 (Suhr et al., 2017), objects365_qa (Shao et al., 2019), ocrvqa (Mishra et al., 2019), oodvqa (Tu et al., 2023), scienceqa (Lu et al., 2022), scienceqa(nona_context) (Lu et al., 2022), sharegpt4o (OpenGVLab, 2024), sharegpt4v(coco) (Chen et al., 2024b), sharegpt4v(knowledge) (Chen et al., 2024b), sharegpt4v(llava) (Chen et al., 2024b), sharegpt4v(sam) (Chen et al., 2024b), sketchyvqa (Tu et al., 2023), spatialsense (Yang et al., 2019), spot_the_diff (Jhamtani & Berg-Kirkpatrick, 2018), st_vqa (Biten et al., 2019), super_clevr(mathv360k) (Li et al., 2023), tallyqa (Acharya et al., 2019), textcaps (Sidorov et al., 2020), textocr(gpt4v) (Carter, 2024), textvqa (Singh et al., 2019), vision_flan(filtered) (Xu et al., 2024), visual7w (Zhu et al., 2016), vqav2 (Goyal et al., 2017), vsr (Liu et al., 2023b), and wildvision (Lu et al., 2024). The nine kept from LLaVA-OneVision-1.5 are gqa (Hudson & Manning, 2019), llava_cot_100k (Xu et al., 2025), llava_wild (Liu et al., 2023c), llrv_gpt4v (Liu et al., 2024a), sherlock (Hessel et al., 2022), svit (Zhao et al., 2023a), viquae (Lerner et al., 2022), vision_oritented, and visual_chat. C TRAINING CONFIGURATION Table 2 lists the training configuration. Table 3 gives the node count, the number of steps and the settings that differ between pretraining and the five finetuning runs. Settings absent from Table 3 are those of Table 2 in every run. C.1 CAMERA SELECTION In pretraining, two rules remove cameras from a sample after the random camera dropout. When two or more side cameras remain, one of them is kept at random and the others are removed. On a two-arm robot with both wrist cameras present, the wrist camera of a static arm is removed. An arm is static when, over the action chunk, every action dimension of the arm varies by less than 0.1 of its standard deviation in the dataset. Both rules keep at least one camera other than the overhead camera. Finetuning uses neither rule. Random camera dropout never removes the overhead camera, except in LIBERO finetuning (Table 3). 25
