Systematic literature reviews depend on a sequence of judgments, including eligibility check, full-text retrieval, and information extraction (Higgins et al., 2023; Page et al., 2021). Such a process if labor- intensive. Searches often result in high recall, so reviewers need to read thousands of titles and ab- stracts to find a few eligible studies (van de Schoot et al., 2021). A review can take over a year on av- erage from registration to publication (Borah et al., 2017). Automation can reduce this workload, but researchers still need to inspect decisions and docu- ment the process based on the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta- Analyses) 2020 process (Page et al., 2021). †Corresponding authors: Zhaoqian Xue and Lingyao Li. Large language models (LLMs) are now widely used to search and summarize literature. Deep research tools such as ChatGPT (OpenAI, 2025), Gemini (Citron, 2024), and Perplexity Research (Perplexity, 2025) can generate a synthesized re- port. These tools help researchers explore a topic but do not follow the PRISMA process. They do not show which papers are screened or excluded and why, or where each extracted value comes from. Other review tools only limit these decisions to a single stage, such as ASReview (van de Schoot et al., 2021) and LGAR (Jaumann et al., 2025) for screening, and RobotReviewer (Marshall et al., 2017) for extraction, or an agent-based system for a few stages like AgentSLR (Padarha et al., 2026). However, designing an AI-assisted literature re- view workflow involves two key considerations. First, screening criteria and field definitions need operational instructions. Domain terms and borderline cases need to be iteratively interpreted consistently for screening and coding. Second, outputs must reflect the current configuration. Researchers refine criteria and fields as a review progresses (Higgins et al., 2023); confirmed set- tings and corrections must therefore be retained, and affected downstream outputs identified. We implement these requirements within a workflow that records the counts and inclusion-exclusion cri- teria needed for PRISMA-guided workflow. We present ReviewPilot, a conversational multi- agent system that addresses these challenges from search through information extraction (Figure 1). Researchers define eligibility criteria and an ex- traction schema through natural dialogue with the system, and a Prompt Agent turns them into review- specific guidance. Agents screen records and ex- tract information, delivering screening decisions with their reasons and extracted values linked to source passages in the papers.
