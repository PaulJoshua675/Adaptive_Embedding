Experiments We distinguish a cumulative LangFlow ablation under shared training settings from evaluations of extended-training checkpoints and the Plaid-style adaptation. Checkpoint progress, planned training budget, sampling cost, and sample count are reported separately. 4.1. Experimental Setup Data and optimization. We train from scratch on OpenWebText using Qwen3 tokenization (151,936 output entries), document BOS/EOS markers, packing, a 10,000-document shuffle buffer, and random crops of 1,024 tokens. The LangFlow ablation uses a global batch of 512, AdamW with weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping at 1.0, and a 250k-update schedule: 5k warmup steps to 3 × 10−4 followed by cosine decay to 3 × 10−5. All seven configurations share the data order, objective, schedule, and sampler. Training uses bfloat16, dropout 0.1, seed 42, and EMA decay 0.999. The separate extended-training schedules are described in Appendix B.1. Generation and sampling cost. All local results reported here use 1,024 unconditional samples of 1,024 model tokens, temperature 1, and EMA weights. Generation seeds are 0–1,023. LangFlow uses the Euler–EDM sampler with unweighted self-conditioning and final argmax decoding. Its NFE counts all denoiser calls.
