Indeed, different response landscapes favor different selection strategies (Figure 1b). In conventional active learning, experimental feedback updates the surrogate model and changes subsequent selections, while the acquisition rule itself typically remains predefined. The same feedback can also inform how experiments should be selected: each experiment yields immediate discoveries and updates predictions for the remaining candidates, influencing future discovery opportunities. This motivates learning an acquisition function that accounts for both current and future discoveries from experimental feedback. We therefore formulate screening as a sequential decision problem and use experimental transitions to learn a dynamic acquisition function online (Figure 1a). However, unlike reinforcement learning with repeated episodes, a screening campaign follows a single trajectory that cannot be reset, with each candidate queried at most once and only one real transition generated per round. Learning an acquisition function from such limited feedback presents a particular challenge. Here we introduce Temporal Difference Active Learning (TDAL), which learns a dynamic acquisition function online from experimental feedback to maximize cumulative discoveries. Building on surrogate hit probabilities, TDAL uses temporal difference learning to incorporate observed discoveries and feedback-weighted successor value into experiment selection. It weights successor value according to changes in predicted hit probabilities for the remaining candidates, reflecting how experimental feedback updates their predicted outcomes. To alleviate the scarcity of real transitions, TDAL constructs additional training transitions from previously measured outcomes through leave-batch-out (LBO). Our main contributions are threefold. • We formulate perturbation discovery as a sequential decision problem and learn a dynamic acquisition function from experimental feedback to adapt to different response landscapes. • We introduce TDAL, which learns online from a single screening trajectory through temporal difference learning. It uses the Hellinger distance to weight successor value and LBO to construct additional training transitions, alleviating training data scarcity. • We evaluate TDAL across eight screens spanning single, double, and triple perturbations. TDAL ranks first on four tasks and achieves an average rank of 1.63 among eleven methods. 2
