Wan (Wan et al., 2025) and Cosmos 3 (NVIDIA, 2026) provide pretrained video priors. For action representation, Dexterous World Models (Kim et al., 2026) uses rendered scenes and hand-mesh trajectories, while DreamDojo (Gao et al., 2026b) learns latent actions from human videos for robot-world rollouts. Explicit scene and camera geometry can also help maintain spatial consistency. EgoSim (Hao et al., 2026) combines video generation with an updatable 3D scene and shared human–robot keypoints, and EgoGenesis (Yan et al., 2026b) uses camera-aware 3D action embeddings and online projective memory for autoregressive rollout. World action models generate actions alongside anticipated visual dynamics through causal video prediction and inverse dynamics, as in LingBot-VA (Li et al., 2026a), or joint video–action flow matching, as in DreamZero (Ye et al., 2026). Fast-WAM (Yuan et al., 2026), Foresight Without Seeing (Huang et al., 2026), and RIFT (Zhang et al., 2026) study predictive visual information for control without a complete iterative visual rollout at deployment. Monocular Hand Reconstruction. Recovering 3D hand motion from video requires estimating hand articulation while accounting for a moving camera. Single-image methods provide hand pose and shape estimates: HaMeR (Pavlakos et al., 2024) scales transformer capacity and heterogeneous training data, while WiLoR (Potamias et al., 2025) integrates localization, handedness estimation, and image-aligned mesh refinement. For egocentric sequences, HaWoR (Zhang et al., 2025) and Dyn- HaMR (Yu et al., 2025) combine hand-motion estimation with SLAM to recover global trajectories, using temporal infilling or interacting-hand priors and optimization. Concurrent work MINT (Zhu et al., 2026) jointly estimates camera and hand motion. Video generative models offer another source of information about hand motion. Concurrent works ViDiHand (Wang et al., 2026a) and ACE-Ego-Hand (Liu et al., 2026) use video diffusion features for hand motion recovery. We train reconstruction jointly with future prediction and planning, allowing the model to learn from how hand interactions unfold as well as from observed video. This joint training improves 3D hand reconstruction in our single-mode comparison (Sec. 4.1). Unified Multimodal Models. A shared model can connect these tasks by changing which modalities are observed and which are generated. Unified World Models (Zhu et al., 2025a) couples video and action diffusion for forward, inverse, policy, and video inference. Language provides a further interface for world modeling and control: Motus (Bi et al., 2026) combines language, video, and latent actions, while MotuBrain (MotuBrain Team et al., 2026) jointly models text, video, and embodiment- specific actions. Geometric modalities extend this shared formulation to spatial reasoning. Flex- π (Yan et al., 2026a) includes pointmap and feature streams alongside RGB and actions, and Aether (Zhu et al., 2025b) unifies 4D reconstruction, camera-conditioned prediction, and goal-image- conditioned visual planning. P3-WAM applies joint reconstruction, prediction, and planning to articulated hand interaction.
