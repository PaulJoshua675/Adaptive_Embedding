E Extended Readout Studies E.1 Readout Controls and Optimization Alignment We test whether a pretrained candidate scorer can replace an added classification head while retaining competitive spatial performance. The controlled comparison evaluates three ways to convert contextual representations into candidate scores. A position classifier applies LayerNorm (Ba et al., 2016) and a 32- output linear layer to the decision state; its outputs refer to candidate slots. A KEV pointer completes the language backbone and projects the decision and option-end states with two newly initialized affine 2560 →256 maps, scoring their dot product divided by √ 256. It adds 1,311,232 parameters and explicitly represents candidate content. The native mean model instead reuses the final attention projections at the earlier boundary in Section 3.2. The comparison therefore tests both representation source and the amount of downstream computation retained. Table 3 is the primary matched readout comparison. Native mean improves over the linear classifier by 1.58 points on CV-Bench, 7.72 on RoboSpatial VQA, and 2.00 on RefSpatial; MAE falls by 8.93%. The gains come with losses of 8.01 points on Where2Place and 3.28 on RoboSpatial pointing. Relative to KEV, native mean improves RefSpatial and length error but is not uniformly better on choice tasks. Its retained parameter count and measured training cost are lower. These results support pretrained candidate scoring as a compact shared design; they do not establish that its scoring formula alone causes every gain, since templates and initialization source remain architecture-dependent. 23
