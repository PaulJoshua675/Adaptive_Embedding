We present an approach for resolving underspecification in reward learning that shifts the burden from the human to the robot. Our insight is that the same information that indicates which features were underspecified can also be used to ask better questions about them. The robot uses this di- agnosis to shape an informed prior for active preference learning and prefaces its queries with an explanation that directs the human’s attention to those features. In simulation and in a user study, the informed prior recovers the true reward faster than standard active preference learning. Limitations The method takes the feature partition as input, so errors in the upstream diagnosis propagate into the informed prior. Updating the partition online as preferences arrive is a natural extension we leave to future work. We also summarize the demonstration-derived posterior as a Gaussian, discarding its higher-order structure. The attention scheme is similarly coarse: each query either directs the human’s attention exclusively to the underspecified features or gives no explanation at all. As features become resolved, the human’s attention should expand to allow later queries to also inform the well-optimized features. Softening the attention model is simple to simulate, but when and how much to broaden, and whether changing the instruction mid-task erodes ease and understanding, requires substantial human validation that we leave to future work. In addition, our experiments focused on tabletop manipulation with a small set of task-relevant features. Evaluating our approach on larger feature sets or dynamic objects would further test robustness and generality. 9
