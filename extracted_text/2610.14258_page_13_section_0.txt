On this benchmark, the converged MAS importance protects old tasks less well than a tempered version of itself, which is what we mean by bias. On CIFAR-100 superclass, MAS showed no detectable effect of shrinkage at any 𝑛, with all gains within ±0.6 points and a slope of 0.25 ± 0.16 points per decade, and no detectable effect of 𝑛 (−0.01 ± 0.12 points per decade). Every arm forgot heavily, with BWT near −31 points, and reached about 35% accuracy, which suggests that the selected penalty was weak on this benchmark. A 𝜆= 0 reference, part of the rerun described next, will show whether regularization helps there at all. The EWC cells of this benchmark are pending. In the first run, the importance accumulator fixed its numerical unit from the first sample. When that sample had a near-zero gradient, the unit became very large and later sums overflowed single-precision arithmetic, which produced non-finite dispersion values and EB weights in these cells. We replaced it with an accumulator that rescales as larger contributions arrive and are rerunning the cells [results to be added]. In every other cell of Studies 3 and 4, which used the earlier accumulator, all EB weights were finite and no run diverged, so the overflow did not affect those results, and the unshrunk and fixed-weight arms do not use the variance estimates at all. The P-MNIST results and the CIFAR-100 MAS results do not depend on the pending cells. We also checked that the Holm-adjusted significance of every result marked in Table 4 holds whatever values the four pending tests take, the closest case being the slope for MAS with 𝛽= 0.5 at 0.047.
