EMO couples question-specific context optimization with logits steering, using each re- sponse to refine the context that guides the next generation. Its logit-space analysis clarifies this connection and identifies conditions for one-round target-KL improvement. Across eight models and six benchmarks, the results show that model scaling and reasoning distil- lation leave scope for adaptation, with steering benefits that depend on the model and task. The broader implication is to treat generation and verification as coupled uses of test-time computation. Feedback can make further attempts more productive, while reliable selection determines how much of their expanded coverage reaches the final answer. This perspective motivates inference systems that adjust context revision, decoding strength, and verification effort jointly to the bottleneck encountered during reasoning. Limitations and future work. External judging can introduce misleading feedback or miss correct candidates. Calibrated ranking and independent checks of reasoning steps or tool outputs could improve verification; retrieval and multiple optimization trajectories could address low candidate coverage. Distribution access and two model streams limit deployment, and generation-equivalent budgets omit judge/rewrite costs. Smaller judges, selective M-step activation, and adaptive stopping could reduce overhead; evaluations should measure total tokens, FLOPs, and latency. Text-only interfaces could use response- level refinement and reranking, subject to separate evaluation. The one-round guarantee assumes successful context updates and faithful steering over full softmax distributions; it does not certify default settings or monotonic accuracy. Context-update acceptance checks, adaptive steering strength, and fallback to context-only decoding could improve robustness, using observable signals such as verifier disagreement and score margins. Evaluation is limited to reasoning benchmarks in two model families. Alternative judges, multilingual tasks, long-context workflows, and tool-using agents would test transfer and error accumulation over longer adaptation cycles. 11
