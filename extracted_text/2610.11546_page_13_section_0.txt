During this phase, evolutionary optimization and policy learning are performed simultane- ously, allowing the agent to progressively improve its regulation of the search process. The corresponding agent-inference performance after policy training is further investigated in Section 3.1.3. All three DRL-based algorithms incorporate a five-episode warm-up period (5000 generations) at the start of each optimization run, during which the population is independently reinitialized at each episode to accumulate sufficient replay buffer experience for stable policy learning (Section 2.5). This warm-up mechanism delays convergence onset and moderately penalizes early-stage performance, but in doing so ensures more reliable actor-critic training, making the resulting comparison more stringent and the reported convergence performance more conservative and credible. Several key observations are drawn from these results. 1) The developed DRL-DCO algorithm achieves consistent, well-rounded convergence across all five benchmark cases, with its generation cost, function evaluation count, and wall-clock time consistently ranking at or above the median among the six algorithms. This balanced performance, spanning landscapes as different as the dense multimodality of Rastrigin and Schwefel, and the ill-conditioned valley geometry of Rosenbrock, demonstrates that the learned orchestration policy generalizes across fundamentally different problem structures without sacrificing performance on any single class to gain on another. 2) Examining Rastrigin and Schwefel results in detail reveals a sharp divergence between CMA-ES- dominant and DE-based strategies. Among the non-DRL baselines, LSHADE-SPACMA substantially outperforms the purely DE-based JADE and LSHADE on Rastrigin, confirming that hybridizing DE and CMA-ES benefits dense multimodal landscapes. Among the DRL-based algorithms, however, DRL- CMA-ES (β(t) = 0) fails entirely on both functions, converging prematurely to suboptimal basins, which is consistent with CMA-ES’s known tendency toward single-basin distributional collapse on regularly multimodal landscapes [45, 46]. The results here again demonstrate that stochastic restarts alone, without an accompanying population size increase, are insufficient to reliably escape these deceptive local basins [46]. In contrast, the DE-based algorithms of DRL-DE, JADE, and LSHADE all eventually converge on these functions, since difference-vector mutation is recomputed fresh from the population’s spread at every generation and continues generating exploratory candidates as long as diversity is preserved [9, 39]. This robustness comes at a cost, however, since exploring multimodal landscapes largely by attrition rather than curvature-informed search is why JADE and LSHADE require substantially more generations than LSHADE-SPACMA, and why DRL-DE requires more generations than DRL-DCO, to reach the global optimum on Rastrigin. 13
