A dash indicates an unreported or inapplicable value. 5 Discussion The state-update results separate two requirements of historical question answering. A memory must first keep earlier values accessible. Once those values are available, the generator still needs to identify which one applies. In the ablation, historical labels improved hit rates without changing the facts or their source order. Retrieval therefore did not account for the difference between the two configurations. The result supports treating version status as part of the evidence presented to the generator, even when it can in principle be inferred from the ordered records. On LoCoMo, marked and unmarked hybrid memory obtained similar scores, and both exceeded session-aware RAG under the same evidence budget. The contrast with the state-update task helps define the role of the labels. They directly indicate whether a retained value has been superseded, but do not supply missing event details or cross-session associations. These are also needed for public dialogue questions. The hybrid-memory gain on LoCoMo is therefore better understood at the level of evidence organisation, without attributing it to historical labels alone. TEM uses source-session order to assign state, assuming explicit single-valued updates. This is sufficient for the controlled update setting considered here. A delayed report may place an earlier event in a later session, and contradictory values can occur within one session. Preferences may also remain valid simultaneously. Handling these cases requires event-validity times or explicit treatment of conflicting relations beyond the present ordering rule.
