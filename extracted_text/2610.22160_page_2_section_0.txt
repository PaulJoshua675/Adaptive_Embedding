This motivates our central question: how can feedback-driven context updates supply a controllable intervention on the generation they guide? We propose EMO, which alternates context optimization and distribution refinement for each question. The E-step revises a system context using textual gradients (Yuksekgonul et al., 2024) from an external judge without access to gold answers. The M-step compares the revised and preceding contexts at a shared generated prefix, constructs a steering vector from their distribution contrast, and applies a confidence-constrained correction to the revised context’s logits. The resulting response is evaluated to provide feedback for the next E-step. Each context update thus determines a decoding intervention whose output informs the next update, closing the loop while model weights remain fixed. A logit-space view explains the relationship between the two steps. Parameter and context updates both admit additive logit representations, while EMO uses the context-induced displacement to construct its additional steering correction. Context edits induce finite logit changes through a frozen model, whereas parameter updates also alter the model’s response to subsequent contexts and prefixes. This representation links adaptation to decoding, while our one-round analysis identifies sufficient conditions for improvement toward a target distribution. Across eight models and six benchmarks, EMO improves over Base in 39 of 40 aggregated settings, averaging +9.10 percentage points, with benefits spanning model scale, genera- tion, family, and thinking mode. With the judge and candidate count held fixed, adding the M-step yields +1.53 points on average over context-only optimization and improves MMLU-Pro for all eight models. At matched target-model generation budgets, EMO also achieves higher oracle coverage than independent sampling in seven of nine settings de- spite producing fewer candidates, indicating benefits in candidate generation alongside improvements in selected accuracy. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • EMO: coupled context optimization and steering. A training-free framework that turns successive context updates into prefix-dependent decoding interventions and uses the steered responses to drive further context adaptation. • Logit-space interpretation and conditional analysis. We relate the context-induced logit displacement to the implemented steering vector (Eq. 5) and establish one-round target-KL improvement under context-improvement and faithful-steering assumptions (Theorem 1). • Empirical evidence across four model axes.
