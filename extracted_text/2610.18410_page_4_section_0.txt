Graph-free Retrieval with Graph-Modified Embeddings Preprint Graph-based retrieval. Graph-based systems use explicit relations to find evidence that semantic similarity may miss, such as education records linked to a company through its employees. GRAFT-Net reasons over question-specific subgraphs of facts and text [6]; PullNet expands them through iterative retrieval [7]; and HippoRAG propagates relevance through entity connections using Personalized PageRank [29]. These systems use the graph during query processing, whereas we encode its relations into embeddings offline and retrieve evidence through stored vectors alone. GraphRAG uses community summaries to answer global questions about a corpus [30]. Although related to our use of graph communities, it produces text for corpus-level synthesis, whereas we produce node-level vectors for retrieving question-specific evidence under a fixed budget. Evidence-level evaluation. Multi-hop question-answering benchmarks support evaluation beyond final-answer accuracy by identifying the evidence needed for reasoning. HotpotQA provides sentence-level supporting facts alongside questions and answers, including both bridge and comparison questions [27]. MuSiQue constructs multi-hop questions by composing single-hop questions whose reasoning steps depend on one another, explicitly addressing shortcuts that allow apparently multi-hop questions to be answered without combining the required evidence [28]. Ground-truth-subgraph benchmarks such as SynthKGQA and GTSQA further support the evaluation of graph retrieval independently of answer generation [12]. We adopt this evidence- centred perspective: our synthetic corpus and questions are generated from the same structured facts, enabling us to specify question-specific gold-evidence sets and measure their recovery directly. The benchmark includes relational and collection questions and extremisation questions, allowing us to examine retrieval of both indirectly connected evidence and the competing records required to establish an extremum. 3 Experimental Setup We evaluate how graph-based embedding transformations and retrieval strategies affect evidence recovery on a synthetic professional-network benchmark. 3.1 Dataset and Knowledge Graph We use separate development and validation corpora, generated by the same pipeline and containing synthetic resumes and organisational personnel rosters. Each corpus is represented as a tripartite graph with three node types: documents, chunks (textual evidence from those documents), and entities (people and organisations). Three relation types connect them: part_of links chunks to documents, about links documents to their primary entities, and mentions links chunks to entities referenced in their text. The schema admits only these three edge patterns. Each corpus contains approximately 1,200 chunks, 370 entities, 230 documents, and 3,700 edges. The development and main validation corpora are node-disjoint: none of the 1,759 node identifiers in the validation corpus occurs in the development corpus. We use Cognee1 to ingest and store the generated graph and manage its associated node embeddings. We select configurations on the development corpus and report results on the validation set of 977 questions, including 90 generated on a supplementary corpus from the same pipeline.
