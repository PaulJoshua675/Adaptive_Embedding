Because step-level correctness is inherently vague, determining exactly when an action requires escalation remains a persistent challenge. Relying instead on observable cross-model disagreement provides practitioners with a reliable, objective signal to safely guide routing decisions. 6.2 Controlled Ablations on Single-Turn Benchmarks Single-turn reasoning benchmarks provide an ideal testbed for controlled ablations that are difficult to isolate in multi-step settings. Because these tasks feature canonical gold answers, they allow us to directly compare our label-free signals against a supervised proxy reward and study the impact of the weak-model sampling budget. Datasets and models. We use three diverse datasets spanning a spectrum of evaluation complexity: Math-500 [18] (Phi-3.5-mini-instruct →GPT-4o-mini; Disagreement indicator A is based on exact final-answer match), Spider [49] (Qwen3-1.7B →OmniSQL-7B; A is identical SQL execution), and AceCode [52] (Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct →Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B-Instruct; A is identical hidden unit test behavior). These tasks progress from closed-form verification to open-ended generation, where structurally distinct outputs can yield identical functional results. This spectrum allows us to observe how routing methods scale as as simple answer-matching becomes insufficient, reflecting the practical challenges of open-ended generations.
