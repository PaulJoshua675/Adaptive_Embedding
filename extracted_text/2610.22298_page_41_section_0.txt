Clinical Syndromes and Involved Mechanisms The primary syndrome is a localized retroareolar proliferative le- sion in a prepubertal male. It is confirmed by an 18-month history of a firm, mobile, 2 cm right retroareolar mass (DOI: 10.1056/NEJMcpc1503831) and a corresponding well-circumscribed, hypoechoic, hypovascular sonographic nodule measuring 1.4 x 1.3 x 0.8 cm with smooth margins and through-transmission (DOI: 10.1056/NEJMcpc1503831). The pathogenetic mechanism involves localized proliferation of ductal and stromal elements within the breast bud, either as a hormonally influenced benign process (prepubertal gynecomastia) or as a benign biphasic neoplasm (fibroadenoma). The presence of fine pubic and axillary hair with prepu- bertal testes (3 ml) and normal bone age indicates isolated premature adrenarche (DOI: 10.1056/NEJMcpc1503831). This peripheral androgenic activity may contribute to breast tissue stimulation via aromatization, but the absence of central puberty suggests the hormonal influence is limited. A malignant infiltrative syndrome is rejected on imaging grounds because the mass lacks spiculated margins, calcifications, and increased vascularity, and there is no skin fixation (DOI: 10.1056/NEJMcpc1503831). However, imaging alone cannot exclude rare low-grade malignancies such as secretory carcinoma, which can present as a well-circumscribed mass in children. Differential Diagnosis with Reasoning High probability: Prepubertal gynecomastia. Arguments for: most common cause of male pediatric breast masses; retroareolar location; small breast tissue also noted on the left; sonographic features consistent with benign glandular tissue (DOI: 10.1056/NEJMcpc1503831). Arguments against: the mass is distinctly nodular and firm rather than the typical diffuse discoid subareolar plaque, and there is no reported tenderness. Confirmation: histology showing benign ductal proliferation with periductal stromal proliferation without atypia, correlated with normal or mildly altered estrogen-to-androgen ratio. Refutation: histology showing a biphasic neoplasm or malignancy. High probability: Fibroadenoma. Arguments for: sonographic appearance—well-circumscribed, oval, smooth-margined, horizontally oriented, mobile—is classic for fibroadenoma (DOI: 10.1056/NEJMcpc1503831).
