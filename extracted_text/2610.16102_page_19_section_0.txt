The policy question that divides Tao, Sanderson and Weinreich is outside the scope of this paper. What matters here is that all three describe the same epistemic object: a result verified as explicit knowledge, whose tacit root has to be grown afterwards by human digestion. Sanderson’s phrase is the precise statement of condition 2: every AI-generated proof begins life as an unsolved exposition problem (Sanderson 2026). 7.3 Why LLM results are rootless by default Four lines of evidence explain why results produced by large language models tend to arrive rootless. 1. The derivation exists, but it is not a walkable trace. A transformer’s forward pass is itself a directed acyclic computation, and interpretability tools can recover parts of it as a computational graph. In Claude 3.5 Haiku, the question about the capital of the state containing Dallas activates an intermediate representation of Texas before the answer Austin; replacing that representation with California changes the answer to Sacramento (Anthropic 2025a). The method captures only a fraction of the computation, and understanding the circuits of a prompt of a few dozen words currently takes hours of human effort (Anthropic 2025a). The DAG is there; the trace is not. 2. The implicit DAG is directional and patchy. Models fine-tuned on facts that appear in separate documents fail at two-hop questions without chain-of-thought, at chance level, yet succeed when the facts appear together in training or in the prompt (Balesni, Korbak & Evans 2024). Models trained on “A is B” do not generalize to “B is A”, although they can deduce the reverse when “A is B” is in context (Berglund et al. 2024). Both results describe relations that run one way and compose unreliably inside the weights, in contrast to binding that is exactly invertible (§2.2). 3. Self-explanation imitates human externalization. Asked how it added 36 and 59, Claude describes the schoolbook carrying algorithm, while the traced mechanism combines a rough-magnitude path with an exact last-digit path; the researchers suggest this may be because the model learned to explain arithmetic by simulating explanations written by people, while learning to compute by other means (Anthropic 2025a). Introspection is not absent but is weak: under the best protocol, Claude Opus 4.1 detected an injected concept about 20 percent of the time, and when a concept was injected after the fact it accepted a word it had not intended as its own and confabulated a reason (Anthropic 2025b). In Polanyian terms, the model’s explanation is a borrowed externalization, detached from its own integration. 4. Generation outruns evaluation. In a blind comparison with more than 100 NLP researchers, LLM-generated research ideas were judged more novel than expert ideas (p < 0.05) and slightly less feasible, and the study identified failures of LLM self-evaluation and a lack of diversity in generation as open problems (Si, Yang & Hashimoto 2024). Taken together, these findings support a precise version of the claim that motivated this paper.
