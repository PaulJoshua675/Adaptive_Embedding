Contrastive vision–language pre-training supports zero- shot chest X-ray diagnosis, but standard objectives treat every off-diagonal image–report pair in a batch as a neg- ative. In radiology, recurring findings across patients mean that instance mismatch need not imply semantic in- compatibility, creating false-negative supervision that we term a semantic conflict. A training-trace audit shows that pair-observations selected by the online mask used during training are enriched for report-derived finding agreement, although this proxy analysis does not estab- lish clinical equivalence. Fixed thresholds can become miscalibrated as similarity distributions evolve, while soft reweighting leaves residual repulsive gradients. We there- fore propose Semantic-Conflict-Aware Contrastive Learn- ing (SCCL), which tracks modality-specific similarity dis- tributions with exponential-moving-average (EMA) statis- tics, derives adaptive thresholds, and hard-masks flagged candidates before softmax normalization. Inclusive (OR) and consensus (AND) rules define two SCCL teachers, whose bidirectional cross-modal rankings are consolidated through standard distillation into a single deployable stu- dent, SCCL-DT. Across five public chest X-ray benchmarks, SCCL-DT achieves a mean zero-shot AUC of 0.850, com- pared with 0.837 for the strongest evaluated baseline in our comparison.
