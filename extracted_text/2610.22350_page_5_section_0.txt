The Average FDE-Gym Score summarizes a model’s delivery performance across tasks and repeated runs. To prevent extreme business losses from dominating the reported average, we floor the incident-adjusted uplift at −1 before deducting the full stakeholder contact cost. Figure 4 presents the drilling scenario, its customer metric, and delivery results. 3 Building the Benchmark 3.1 Scenario Construction from Real-World Delivery Projects Figure 1 traces how a real delivery becomes a task in the benchmark. Industry experts adapted real-world customer delivery projects carried out in China, Singapore, and the United States since 2024 into 20 scenarios spanning 18 sectors (Table 2). Each scenario yields an original task and several variants of it, which share a common customer environment but differ in key facts. Each original task and each variant is a separate benchmark task, for a total of 95 tasks. Tasks require agents to deliver an agent, workflow, or service that operates in the customer’s environment. The scenarios are grounded in industry practice, with the relevant regulations, standards, and public sources documented. Companies and individuals are either de-identified or fictional, and some data are adapted from real-world industry sources. 3.2 Scenario Variants and Underlying Facts A single fixed task per scenario may allow models to rely on memorized solutions or recurring patterns. To reduce this risk, experts designed two to seven variants in addition to each scenario’s original task. Variants change key facts, including which rule is outdated, who holds critical information, and whether the sponsor’s request addresses the underlying problem. These changes may require different delivery solutions, so a solution that succeeds on one task of a scenario may fail on another. Across scenarios, no single problem pattern appears in more than one-third of the set. Together, these design choices encourage agents to evaluate the evidence in each task rather than apply a fixed solution. 5
