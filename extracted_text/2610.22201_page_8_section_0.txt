For CCV and 1st-price auctions, this failure to pass value backward triggers a cascading network collapse. Upstream agents (e.g., Fr→Sp and En→Ge) receive 𝑃in ≈0, fail to offset their inference costs, and go bankrupt. Their exit removes competition at earlier nodes (e.g., the En node loses the En→Ge buyer), turning those preceding nodes into new task-conditioned monopsonies that also drop their bids to zero. Because every edge incurs a positive inference cost, this backward unraveling inevitably starves every branch of the network, guaranteeing total market failure. When we transition to an imperfect competition regime by populating each edge with 𝑁> 1 agents possessing slight cost variances, the monopsonies are broken. Competitive pressure forces multiple buyers to raise their bids above the floor. Value successfully flows backward through the graph, enabling upstream agents to sustain positive profit margins and restoring the optimal $0.04 Fr→Sp→En→Ko route without requiring subsidies. We discuss the poor performance of TS and Bayes-Optimal baselines with 𝑁=2 in Appendix C. All methods succeed with 𝑁=4 (Appendix A). Crucially, this experiment validates our auction mechanics under imperfect competition. Because standard 2nd-price (Vickrey) auctions require theoretically unbounded platform subsidies, and CCV passes insufficient value to upstream agents when competition is imperfect (often starving them of the revenue needed to cover inference costs), we prioritize the simpler 1st-Price auction—where agents actively learn bid shading—as the default mechanism for subsequent experiments. 5.2. LLM Agent Economies Having validated the economic mechanics, we scale the framework to diverse LLM domains: News Market (information synthesis) and Omni-MATH (mathematical reasoning). We also examine Hot- potQA in Appendix A. These domains test the market’s ability to orchestrate multi-step cognitive workflows. To model imperfect competition, agents are differentiated along three axes: dynamic identity embeddings, asymmetric data access, and distinct system prompts (full prompts are detailed in Appendix D).
