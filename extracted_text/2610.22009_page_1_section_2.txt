Religious texts are often encountered through images: posters, screenshots, lecture slides, social media cards, and educational graphics. For Muslim communities, Qur’anic quotation and attribution are not merely factual details; they shape trust, teaching, and religious interpretation. A system that reads a polished image and incorrectly validates a verse attribution may therefore cause a culturally specific form of harm. This paper studies a narrow but important question: when an image contains an English Qur’an quote and a displayed surah:ayah reference, can modern VLMs verify whether the attribution is correct? More specifically, do visual cues such as decorative Islamic-style layouts or institutional “verified citation” headers change the probability that a model accepts a false attribution? This question sits at the intersection of rapid progress in VLMs [Radford et al., 2021, Alayrac et al., 2022, Liu et al., 2023, OpenAI, 2023] and growing evidence that multimodal systems can hallucinate or over-trust misleading inputs [Li et al., 2023, Bai et al., 2024]. Unlike object-hallucination benchmarks, Visual Sanad tests a fine-grained attribution decision in a culturally grounded religious setting, aligning with MusiML’s emphasis on AI systems relevant to Muslim communities [Muslims in ML, 2026]. Prior Islamic NLP benchmarks study Qur’anic question answering and broader Islamic knowledge evaluation [Malhas et al., 2023, Abdelaal et al., 2026]; Visual Sanad instead asks whether a VLM can verify a displayed quote–reference attribution in an image. Our contributions are: • a controlled 2,400-image benchmark for Qur’anic citation verification under six visual conditions; • a human-audited construction pipeline using valid references and three invalid foil types; 40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026).
