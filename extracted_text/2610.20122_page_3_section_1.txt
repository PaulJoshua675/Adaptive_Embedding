2.1 LLM Query Views for Sparse Retrieval Query expansion has long been used to reduce vocabulary mismatch in sparse retrieval, including classical pseudo-relevance feedback, Rocchio feedback, and relevance models [25, 12, 24]. A recent survey traces how this line has evolved from statistical expansion to pretrained-model and LLM-based generation, and identifies controllability, robustness, and downstream utilization of generated content as continuing challenges [15]. Representative LLM-based methods gener- ate richer query-side text: Query2doc produces pseudo-documents for expansion [27]; HyDE-style approaches use hypothetical content to bridge the mismatch between queries and documents [9]; and hypothesis and step-back prompting expose complementary formulations of the same information need [30, 29]. Dual- layer prompt ensembles further show that combining system- and user-level in- structions can improve the robustness of LLM-based expansion and rank fusion [16]. Generated retrieval evidence is also used in iterative and domain-specific set- tings. S2G-RAG explicitly judges evidence sufficiency and remaining informa- tion gaps to control iterative retrieval for question answering [18], while GLIER couples generative legal inference with evidence ranking for legal case retrieval [14]. Related surveys emphasize that long-document retrieval introduces addi- tional challenges in representing and locating evidence across extended contexts [17]. These studies motivate careful use of generated evidence, but address it- erative QA, specialized legal retrieval, or long-document retrieval. XViewRank instead studies a complementary question in general passage retrieval: given sev- eral query views generated in advance, how should their sparse retrieval evidence be combined into one ranking? 2.2 Rank Fusion and Cross-View Evidence Combining ranked lists is a classic information retrieval problem.
