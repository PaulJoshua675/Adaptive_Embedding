Preprint reasoning may improve action selection, but its temporal cost can reduce the opportunity to execute that action successfully. Relaxing temporal pressure helps but does not ensure success. To examine the role of this temporal cost, we hold reasoning effort at low and vary B ∈{32, 64, 128, unlimited} at a fixed observation rate (Figure 6). For a fixed token count, a larger B shortens the simulated delay before an action is applied. The setting of unlimited B corresponds to PL and assigns no simulated time to generation. Success generally improves as temporal pressure is relaxed: Claude Opus 5 rises from 30.0% at B = 32 to 62.0% with an unlimited budget, and Kimi-K3 from 22.0% to 60.0%. The improvement is not monotonic for every model, and even the unlimited setting leaves many tasks unsolved. Removing the time cost of inference therefore alleviates one constraint on task performance without resolving the full difficulty of effective interaction. 6.3 QUALITATIVE ANALYSIS By examining recorded videos and action trajectories, we identify three recurring failure modes. In an evolving environment, additional deliberation may improve a decision while leaving insufficient time to execute it effectively. Adaptive reasoning should therefore account for the time available to act, alongside task difficulty (Anthropic, 2026b; Wu et al., 2026). In Tetris, for example, a model may spend more time comparing placements while a falling piece is far above the stack. As the piece approaches the stack, however, it must shorten deliberation to leave enough time to execute the required moves. The appropriate reasoning effort thus changes with the environment, even within the same task. Future work should train models to recognize these changing temporal constraints and allocate deliberation by weighing the expected benefit of further computation against the risk of acting too late. 8
