3.3.2 Semantic Anchoring Effect To intuitively illustrate the proposed Semantic Anchoring Effect, we employ the UMAP algorithm in Figure 1 to visualize the evolution of representations within the latent space. Fol- lowing the message-passing mechanism of LightGCN, we extract both the initial ID embeddings and the final output representations after multi-layer aggregation and residual weighting. To construct a discriminative sample distribution, we randomly select anchor items from three distinct semantic categories and retrieve the 50 warm items with the highest semantic similarity to each anchor to form visible clusters. Furthermore, to simulate strict cold-start scenarios, we identify 5 isolated nodes with zero degree and the highest semantic proximity to the anchors for each category. Our observations indicate that under random ID initialization, representations exhibit signifi- cant representation drift after high-order graph smoothing. Cold-start nodes, lacking topological connections, remain completely dissociated from the collaborative manifold. Conversely, the introduction of semantic vectors provides a robust anchoring effect for the nodes. For active items, the feature distribution remains tightly clustered around the initial semantic anchors even after low-pass graph filtering. This empirical finding strongly supports the core motivation of this work: since semantic features can naturally anchor the distribution within the graph structure during the smoothing process, we can design a cross-modal mapping mechanism to align the semantic features s𝑖new of isolated items into the mature collaborative space. This alignment allows the model to effectively overcome the cold-start dilemma caused by the failure of graph topology. 4
