Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to obtain election information, and the web sources they use can shape the responses presented to voters. Existing work on LLMs in political settings mainly studies political bias in model outputs and AI-driven political persuasion, while much less is known about how external web content affects the political information presented by LLMs. Generative engine optimization (GEO) focuses on rewriting web content to improve its visibility in generated responses. Existing GEO research typically takes a fixed webpage pool as input and stud- ies how rewriting changes page visibility in generated responses, providing a natural framework for studying this question. To inves- tigate the potential risks of GEO in voting scenarios (VoteGEO), we introduce VoteGEO-Bench, a benchmark built on the 2024 U.S. presidential election and the 2026 Japanese House of Repre- sentatives election, combining election-related queries, historical webpages, and voter personas. We first analyze the sources cited by generative engines, find- ing that many citations come from lower-cost owned and open- platform sources and that some webpages often remain visible across days. We then benchmark 11 rewriting strategies across 4 categories and study whether specifying a target candidate or party in the rewrite matters, which strategies work across gener- ative engines and elections, and how voter personas affect expo- sure. Target-unspecified rewriting rarely increases target exposure, whereas target-specified rewriting more often does so. Notably, strategies that improve page organization or strengthen a posi- tion through argumentation show stronger gains than those that add supporting evidence or mainly change wording. For the two strongest strategies, rewriting gains remain broadly positive across the evaluated U.S. personas, while persona conditioning shows no consistent tendency to increase or decrease these gains. In contrast, explicit partisan identities can create baseline exposure gaps larger than the gain from rewriting a single webpage. When partisan in- formation is only implicit in the voter profile, baseline exposure shifts vary with how strongly the engine responds to the implicit partisan signal.
