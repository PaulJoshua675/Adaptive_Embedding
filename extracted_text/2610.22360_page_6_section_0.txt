Starting from a positive anchor (a, σ, True) where candidate a satisfies a reviewed specification σ, we construct negative pairs via two counterfactual mechanisms: Specification-side counterfactuals (Spec-CF) modify a key requirement while fixing a, producing (a, σ′, False). Candidate-side counterfactuals (Cand-CF) modify the asset, marked region, part pair, or contact while fixing the specification, producing (a′, σ, False). Native annotations take precedence over synthetic edits when available, and candidate evaluation links are retained only when both records share identical model-visible claims. Two domain experts manually spot-check 10% of the outputs at each model-assisted review stage shown in Figure 4, including the two reviewer checkpoints in Spec-CF, the final pair review in Spec-CF, and the consistency review in Cand-CF. • Parametric Data Synthesis. For {Identity}, we leverage FaceScape’s bilinear model (Yang et al., 2020) to construct 200 balanced face pairs. Specifically, identity variations are sampled using standardized identity coordinates, with labels assigned based on whether these identity coefficients are modified. To ensure that these parameter shifts yield human-perceptible identity differences, 6 domain experts were invited to establish explicit thresholds and validate the generated pairs. To systematically evaluate robustness against non-identity attributes, i.e., expression and texture, we vary them as nuisance factors across 4 conditions: (1) nearly gray texture with minimal changes, (2) texture changes only, (3) expression changes only, (4) changes to both. • Expert-Guided Data Construction. For {FineGeo., Motion}, each has 6 domain experts guiding data construction and annotation. For FineGeo., we apply Laplacian diffusion (Desbrun et al., 1999) to ground-truth T-LESS models (Hodan et al., 2017), with experts selecting surface regions and perturbation parameters. Original and perturbed models yield positive and negative photograph-CAD comparisons against fixed reference photographs. For Motion, we combine reference motions from AnimalML3D (Yang et al., 2024) with sequences generated by AnimateAnyMesh (Wu et al., 2025b) and Animate3D (Jiang et al., 2024). Experts classify motions as Correct, Unnatural Motion, Incorrect Motion, or Deformation Artifacts, retaining only samples with unanimous agreement. Stage2: Expert Review and Quality Control. We further invited 6 additional, non-overlapping domain experts to verify benchmark data. Checks cover annotation consistency, perturbation bounds, mesh validity, view coverage, rendering completeness, and temporal order where applicable. Source-specific preprocessing and validation details are in Appendix B. 4.1 Native Supervision and Quality Control When native labels already define the relevant distinction, we use them directly instead of forcing every task through the same editing backend. Part and relation annotations, action semantics, grasp outcomes, identity parameters, reference photographs, and reviewed motion sequences provide task-specific evidence for the corresponding claims. This keeps the verification interface unified while allowing the underlying evidence to match the semantics of each task. Benchmark examples undergo task-specific human review for label correctness, evidence sufficiency, and explanation quality.
