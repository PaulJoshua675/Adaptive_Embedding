Dense retrieval (DR) has long been a standard candidate-generation approach for recommendation. It encodes a user’s interaction his- tory and catalog items in a shared embedding space, then retrieves nearby items [3, 36]. Advances in language models and semantic IDs have made generative retrieval (GR) an increasingly viable al- ternative. GR represents items with semantic IDs and generates the next item’s ID one token at a time [6, 21, 25, 31, 32]. As shown in Fig. 1, normally DR retrieves the held out next item more often, while GR’s best relevant item has higher value on average for users both methods cover. The apparent winner depends on how retrieval is evaluated. This metric choice is particularly crucial in e-commerce candidate generation, where retrieved products differ not only in relevance to the user but also its commercial value. To understand why, we need to know whether GR’s value lead comes from the user’s history or from generally valuable items. Recall and target NDCG show whether the held out item is retrieved and where it ranks. They do not account for other relevant items, their ranks, or their value. To understand the disagreement, in this paper, we measure both the best relevant item and relevant value across the full list. We ap- ply these metrics in controlled GR and DR comparisons on Amazon Toys 2014 and 2023 [11, 14, 23, 25, 35]. The metric disagreement persists on both public e-commerce datasets. From analysis, popu- larity is the largest component of GR’s measured value lead. Among users where GR wins, popularity contributes 49.8% of its value lead on 2014 and 65.4% on 2023. Reranking DR with the static item value score reverses the best item value gap while preserving a recall lead over GR. Further checks with cold targets, substitute credit, a random control, and graded industrial relevance do not reveal a consistent GR advantage. Our main contributions are: • We define complementary metrics for sequential recommen- dation in e-commerce scenario and show that they favor different methods in the GR and DR comparison. • We decompose GR’s measured value lead and find that pop- ularity is its largest component on both public datasets. • We propose a training-free value prior, applied to DR as a single rerank of its retrieval results.
