Appendix D Inference preparation for case study This appendix describes preparation steps from our case study for using a utility approach to form sampling plans. We examine eliciting priors on SFP rates. An appropriate prior has two properties: right skewness to capture the possibility of high SFP rates, and increased variance as assessed risk increases. The SFP risk assessment process of Nkansah et al. (2017) establishes seven risk categories through appraisals of factors like population density and poverty levels. (18) Function h is the logit function; the prior is normal in the logit space. Parameter µq(v) is a regulator-designated SFP-rate median for node v with assessed SFP risk q(v). Our field-user co-authors established µ values of 1%, 2%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20% and 25% for the seven SFP risk levels of Nkansah et al. Parameter λ is a variance parameter, used for all assessed SFP risk levels. We used a λ of 2 for the case study, which produced densities matching field-user expectations. These densities are shown in Figure 7. For example, a “Very Low” assessment corresponds to a prior 90% interval of [1%, 38%], while a “High” assessment corresponds to an interval of [7%, 72%]. Use of the logit space provides the desired right skew in the (0, 1) space, as well as increased variance as the assessed SFP risk increases. This variance pattern is achieved through only a single choice of λ; using only one λ value is desirable as it concisely captures overall confidence in risk assessments. Estimating sourcing probability The sourcing-probability matrix for the supply chain, Q, is a crucial element of understanding what data may result from a proposed sampling plan. National sourcing records, aggregated records from sub-national or outlet echelons, and track-and-trace records can all be used to form Q. Our case study uses supply-chain observations from a previous PMS iteration to estimate Q, and we use bootstrap samples of these observations to form sourcing probabilities for untested nodes in the all-provinces case. We used 44 independent draws from the first iteration’s data set to form the sourcing probabilities for each test node; 44 was the average number of samples per test node in the first iteration. The uncertainty associated with an estimated Q may be incorporated into analysis through similar bootstrap samples that are integrated with MCMC draws of the SFP-rate vector, γ. In the efficient estimation algorithm (see Appendix B), each member of the set of SFP-rate draws would be coupled with a bootstrap sample of Q during the data-generation step. The utility estimate would thus be reflective of the uncertainty in both γ and Q. Further discussion on sourcing probability is contained in Wickett et al. (2023) and Wickett (2024). 47
