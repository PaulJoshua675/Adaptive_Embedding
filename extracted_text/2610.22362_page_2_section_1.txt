Agentic asset and scene synthesis. Recent methods im- prove visual asset quality [40, 50] and model part struc- ture, articulation, or physical properties [2, 3, 13, 17, 36, 46, 47, 51]. For robot learning, RoboGen [38] generates tasks, scenes, and training supervision, while RoboTwin 2.0 [4] scales bimanual manipulation data through ex- pert data synthesis and domain randomization. Sim- Foundry [29] generates variations across objects, scenes and tasks, and agentic systems automate scene construc- tion and refinement for simulation [25, 37, 39, 43, 44]. Our work focuses on adapting environment generation to the policy’s learning progress, using feedback from training to guide what is generated next. Curriculum learning and policy improvement. Cur- riculum methods prioritize environments using regret, learning potential, or parameter changes [6, 9, 23, 27, 49]. POET [35] coevolves agents and parameterized environments, while language models synthesize re- wards [19, 41, 42] and generate adaptive environment programs [10, 16]. For policy improvement, VLA poli- cies learn from their rollouts using sparse success re- wards [14, 32]. Q-Planning [7] improves action selection while keeping the base policy frozen. We adopt a critic over action chunks [15] with advantage-weighted regres- sion to update the policy [24]. Lifelong methods retain reusable prompts or skill libraries [18, 45]. CoDA links scene generation and policy learning through failure di- agnoses localized to scene factors and trajectory phases. 3. Method CoDA couples scene generation and policy learning through a shared memory that guides both. A data engine G, implemented as a tool-augmented coding agent, writes executable scene programs against a simu- lator SDK. Each program defines an environment and a machine-checkable success predicate ν. An action policy πθ collects rollouts in these environments, and a vision– language critic C diagnoses failures by identifying the responsible factor, a trajectory phase, and supporting rollout evidence. Failures intrinsic to motor execution are recorded separately from those attributed to scene factors. The memory M consolidates this evidence into reusable difficulty modes.
