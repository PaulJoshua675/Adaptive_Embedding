A.9 CAPABILITY-WISE EXPERT RETENTION AND SKILL ALIGNMENT To complement the macro-averaged results presented in the main text, we further examine expert capability retention and alignment across individual capabilities. As shown in Figure 5a and Fig- ure 5b, SCOPED consistently achieves higher amplitude retention and expert alignment across all four capabilities, demonstrating more effective preservation of specialized expert behavior. To examine whether these improvements arise from capability-specific guidance rather than addi- tional contextual information, we compare matched capability skills with randomly assigned skills. As shown in Figure 5c, most samples lie above the diagonal, indicating that matched skills induce functional changes more closely aligned with the corresponding expert directions than random skills. These results suggest that the extracted skills provide targeted guidance consistent with capability- specific expertise. A.10 QUALITATIVE ANALYSIS OF SKILL-GUIDED VISUAL ATTENTION To complement the quantitative attention analysis in Sec. 5.4, we further visualize how skill condi- tioning affects the visual attention of capability experts on a representative example. As illustrated in Figure 6, we compare the same capability expert with and without its matched skill under an identical input. Without skill conditioning, the expert distributes substantial attention over both task-relevant and irrelevant visual regions. In contrast, conditioning on the matched skill shifts more attention toward the visual evidence relevant to the current task, as further highlighted by the attention-difference map. This qualitative example is consistent with the layer-wise attention analysis in Figure 3c, providing an intuitive illustration of how skill conditioning can regularize expert visual attention toward task-relevant evidence. A.11 CASE STUDIES To better understand how skill-augmented distillation changes the behavior of the unified model, we present a representative example in Figure 7. The task requires identifying the age group with the highest percentage of Associate degrees. While SCOPED w/o Skill Augmentation follows an erroneous reasoning trajectory and predicts “25 to 34”, the full SCOPED model correctly identifies “35 to 44”. The visualization further illustrates how skill-augmented supervision affects the model’s use of visual evidence and token-level predictions. Compared with SCOPED w/o Skill Augmentation, SCOPED allocates more attention to the region supporting the correct answer while reducing atten- tion to the misleading region associated with the incorrect prediction. At the token level, SCOPED also suppresses responses along the erroneous trajectory and strengthens those supporting the correct prediction. This case complements the quantitative analyses in Section 5.4, showing that skill-augmented expert supervision can lead the unified model to rely more on task-relevant visual evidence and avoid erroneous reasoning patterns during generation.
