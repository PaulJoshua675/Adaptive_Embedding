The study uses one pretrained SR framework, one spatial region, and one 168-hour benchmark. The fine-tuning set improves seasonal coverage but does not establish geographic or architec- tural generalization. All configurations are compared at a single fine-tuning scale of 2688 samples. Earlier development runs at smaller scales used a different topology-inspired objective and the PD-distance convention of the workshop submission, so they are excluded from this analysis; those trained models remain valid and could be re-evaluated separately. Selection and variance. The final objective was chosen on the evaluation benchmark itself (Sec. 6.4); a held-out validation split would be preferable. Each configuration was trained once, so run- to-run variance is not quantified, and apart from the reduction of the fixed-pair weights described in Sec. 4.4, no weight search was performed. Supervision. LCV and Lpers were always used together, so the study does not isolate their individual contributions. Fixed-pair su- pervision operates only at ground-truth-selected endpoint locations and does not penalize topological features that arise elsewhere in the reconstruction; a re-matched diagram loss could complement it by identifying and suppressing such unsupported features. The fi- nal model regresses relative to the pretrained CNN on PSD slope and retains only part of the control’s fidelity gain. Topology crop. PD and MT are computed on a fixed 160×160 subregion of each 500 × 500 reconstruction. This keeps the topol- ogy pipeline tractable and all methods directly comparable, but the topological conclusions apply to that region and do not establish preservation over the full domain; the full field or multiple crops should be evaluated in future work. Descriptors. PD and MT are computed on scalar speed rather than on the two-channel training target. Our independent validation covers the PD distances and merge-tree construction, but not the merge-tree distance computation itself. Most importantly for appli- cations, persistence diagrams deliberately discard spatial embed- ding. Two features with similar birth and death values can match closely in a PD even when they occur at very different physical lo- cations: a pair born at 4 m/s and dying at 12 m/s near one corner of the domain matches an identical pair near the opposite corner at zero cost. This invariance is useful for topological comparison but is a limitation for wind-field applications, in which the geographic location of a reconstructed structure may be scientifically conse- quential. Neither PD nor MT distance localizes where structure is preserved or displaced, and downstream scientific tasks are needed to establish the practical meaning of the gains. 7.3 Future directions Generalization. The findings should be tested with larger evalua- tion benchmarks and fine-tuning sets spanning more time periods and geographically distinct regions, with repeated runs, and with other SR architectures. Generative models such as the pretrained PhIRE GAN are a natural target: they already match the accumu- lated Wasserstein distances well, but their bottleneck and MT agree- ment is weak. Beyond fine-tuning, the most effective losses could be used to train SR models from the start. Topology-aware learning. Hierarchy-aware supervision could target what the present losses leave out. Options range from fixed ground-truth constraints on branch and merge relationships, the hi- erarchy analogue of fixed-pair supervision, to merge-tree losses in which the prediction’s tree is recomputed and matched at each step, analogous to re-matched diagram losses. Learned MT simi- larity models such as MTNN [26] could also provide a surrogate hierarchy objective, provided that the extracted tree representation is connected back to differentiable scalar-field values during opti- mization and that the model’s accuracy is validated for wind-speed fields. Re-matched diagram losses [19] could complement fixed- pair supervision by suppressing unsupported features. Separating LCV from Lpers and studying the loss weights systematically would clarify which parts of fixed-pair supervision matter. Controlled syn- thetic fields with analytically known critical points and merge struc- ture (for example, two peaks merging, a saddle moving, a weak feature appearing, or identical diagrams with different merge hier- archies) would provide a complementary test of which losses re- cover which topological events, independently of the complexity of atmospheric data. Finally, extending supervision and evaluation from scalar wind speed to vector-field topology would connect this work to vector-field SR more directly. Spatial and scientific validation. Mapping matched persistence pairs back to their physical locations would allow persistent-feature preservation to be measured as displacement, splitting, merging, appearance, and disappearance of specific structures, rather than as a single scalar distance. It would also connect topological agree- ment to regions of practical interest, such as wind-farm sites and meteorological stations whose observations could provide physical validation. We are pursuing spatially aware persistence evaluation as a separate study.
