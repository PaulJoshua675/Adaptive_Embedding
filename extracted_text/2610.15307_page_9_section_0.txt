As shown in Table 1, FENAS achieves the best or highly competitive performance across all regimes, with particularly strong gains under extreme few-shot supervision. With only 78 samples, FE- NAS reaches a τ of 0.695 on CIFAR-10, surpassing DCL-ENAS [36] (0.610) by 13.9% and CAP [11] (0.600) by 15.8%. This advantage is mainly due to the inductive bias of FENAS: instead of learning topology embeddings from scarce labels, it directly exploits physically grounded initialization priors, including ex- pressivity, trainability, and connectivity, through a lightweight meta-ensembling framework. Such a design is well matched to NAS-Bench-201, where the topology is fixed and discriminative information lies primarily in operator-level behavior. Consequently, FENAS can use limited supervision more efficiently and produce more stable rankings than graph-based few-shot predictors [33,29]. 2) Comparison with Few-shot Methods on NAS-Bench-101. NAS-Bench-101 [34] is more challenging because of its diverse graph topolo- gies. As shown in Table 1, FENAS remains competitive in the extremely low- budget regime. With only 100 (0.02%) labeled architectures, it achieves a τ of 0.616, outperforming Arch2Vec [33] (0.435) and TNASP [17] (0.600). With 172 (0.04%) samples, FENAS further reaches 0.641, maintaining clear advantages over lightweight representation-based methods. Although some topology-aware predictors such as PINAT [16] (0.679) obtain higher correlations, these results can still clarify that FENAS can extract useful ranking signals from limited supervision even when structural diversity increases. This robustness is enabled by the model-agnostic design of FENAS. For NAS-Bench-101, the CAST module adapts feature transformation to better han- dle heterogeneous architectures while avoiding explicit graph construction and costly message passing. As a result, FENAS keeps low inference complexity and evaluates architectures at approximately 166.7 ms/arch, making it suitable for large-scale ranking in practical NAS scenarios. Overall, FENAS provides an efficient and scalable alternative to topology-heavy few-shot predictors. 3) Comparison with Pure Zero-Cost Proxies.
