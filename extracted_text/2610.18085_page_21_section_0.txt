Thus the obstruction does not cover all parameter-free first-order methods. Standard optimizer instances. Theorem 5 applies to gradient descent with or without heavy-ball momentum, both homogeneous of degree 1. The degree-zero class includes AdaGrad and RMSProp with zero additive stabilizers, Adam with ϵ = 0, signSGD (Bernstein et al., 2018), and Lion. This class also includes per-tensor preconditioned updates such as the zero-stabilizer limit of Shampoo (Gupta et al., 2018), with inverse roots taken on the ranges of the Gram factors. Adam and AdamW with ϵ > 0 are bounded near the zero history and also fall within the theorem’s scope. These rules are not orbit-equivariant under a common learning rate, with or without decoupled weight decay. C.8 Soft Rebalancing: Contraction and Orbit Dependence Contraction of the log-ratio. The residual log-imbalance after a single damped event contracts geometrically: log ρpost p := log Anew p F Bnew p F = (1 −α) log ρp, (19) so K events with no intervening optimizer updates multiply the log-imbalance by (1 −α)K. During training, optimizer updates can introduce new imbalance between events. Dependence on the starting representative. The soft map is not itself an orbit canonicalizer for α < 1. Writing Φα for the damped rebalancing map of Eq. 12 and Tc(A, B) := (cA, c−1B), direct substitution of ρ(Tc(A, B)) = c2ρ(A, B) gives Φα(Tc(A, B)) = Tc1−α(Φα(A, B)). (20) Thus one soft event retains dependence on the starting representative; away from balance the soft map is also not idempotent, since a second event changes (1 −α) log ρ to (1 −α)2 log ρ. Hard rebalancing with α = 1 has both canonicalizer properties. Corollary 4 uses that hard map. The seven-seed Lion comparison in Sec. 5.2 evaluates the damped variant on Lion training.
