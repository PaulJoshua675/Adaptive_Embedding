A perception system that must brake for a pedestrian does not need a low average latency. It must deliver an an- swer before the deadline on every frame, the worst case included. Commodity edge accelerators optimize the mean through dynamic scheduling, OS jitter, thermal throttling, and contention, so their per-frame latency can be measured but not bounded. We build FALCON, a camera-based 3D occupancy front-end on the AMD Kria KR260, an embed- ded FPGA drawing 4.3 W idle and up to 6.6 W under in- ference, and make the latency guarantee layered and ex- plicit: programmable-logic (PL) compute is cycle-bounded analytically; inference is a measured envelope over 100k- frame traces; and a watchdog enforces output delivery, publishing the previous output flagged stale when a frame is late. The deadline runs use a fixed-work proxy of six ResNet-50 DPU inferences per frame, not the full occu- pancy network: across three 100k-frame enforced runs, all 300k proxy frames delivered an output within the 100 ms deadline plus at most 10.2 ms, the worst of the three runs. The full six-camera FlashOcc pipeline composes to about 1.3 s per frame on the board and is not yet a 10 Hz system. The two operations the DPU cannot run, a view-transform gather and a bilinear resize, become fixed-trip-count PL IPs numerically matched to their software references. On the full Occ3D-nuScenes validation set the board reaches mIoU 29.88, 92.8% of an FP32 baseline the DPU cannot run. On a ResNet-50 probe over 100k frames the KR260 tail stays within 1.5× its median (2.0× for the six-inference proxy), where Jetson Orin reaches 4.4× and the worst datacenter GPU run (NVIDIA L4) 23.6×. The deployment recipe, HLS sources, and raw timing traces are released.
