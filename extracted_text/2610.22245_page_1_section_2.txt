Small language models offer a practical path to deploying AI agents on edge devices, robots, and other systems constrained by cost, latency, connectivity, or privacy. Yet their limited capabilities make it difficult to rely on them across the full range of tasks they may encounter [4, 30]. This has motivated growing interest in systems that primarily rely on a weak LLM, but occasionally route difficult instances to a stronger model when additional capability is needed [5, 15, 51]. The possibility of such routing changes how we should think about inference with weak LLMs. Rather than simply generating a response and returning it, inference now involves three decisions: whether to accept the current response, spend additional local computation on further reasoning or sampling, or seek assistance from a stronger model at substantially higher cost.
