Against the mixed-variant base, final directional gains are 34.29 (es →nah) and 28.36 (nah →es) points. 5.3 Backtranslation and Schedule Effects Training Schedule and Checkpoint Selection. Fine-tuning a 1.2-billion-parameter model on a small dataset requires balancing parameter adaptation against rapid memorization. In lora_v1, we evaluated a short 3-epoch schedule on real pairs. In lora_v2, we extended training to 8 epochs with evaluation every 200 steps. Extend- ing the schedule raised dev chrF++ from 40.26 to 46.18 (+5.92 points). Validation performance peaked at epoch 6 before plateauing by epoch 8, demonstrating that low-rank adapters need sufficient steps to align cross-lingual representations but quickly overfit if trained past convergence. Asymmetric Target-Preserving Backtranslation. Standard backtranslation risks degrading the generation decoder by training on hallucinated synthetic targets. To prevent this, we implemented an asymmetric protocol: using the nah →es capability of lora_v2, we generated synthetic Spanish source prompts for 192 continuous narrative utterances spoken by community elders, adding these pairs strictly to the es →nah training split (train_bt.jsonl). Integrating these 192 backtranslated pairs in lora_v3b increased dev chrF++ from 46.18 to 49.40 (+3.22 points) and raised both directional tests to approximately 49 chrF++. Although representing only an 8% increase over the real-pair inventory, they introduced multi-clause discourse patterns and authentic elder-authored syntax that isolated pedagogical pairs lack.
