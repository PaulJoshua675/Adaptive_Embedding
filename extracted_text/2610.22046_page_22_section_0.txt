The uniform-step baseline, a step drawn uniformly from the trace, is computed in closed form for each evaluation set. Pointer probe. The pointer probe predicts the PIT step of a PIT trace directly. It is a four-layer transformer encoder with bidirectional attention over the steps; at each step it reads that step’s hidden states at the read site together with those of the final step, and it assigns a softmax score to every step. Training minimizes the cross-entropy to the labelled PIT step smoothed by a Gaussian of width one step, and inference selects the most probable step. The encoder has three further outputs trained jointly, a three-way failure type, a per-step estimate of the envelope and a confidence output, with loss weights 1, 0.25 and 0.5 against 1 for the step distribution; only the step distribution is evaluated in this paper. All 22,877 incorrect training traces (8,971 PITs, 12,692 capability gaps and 1,214 near misses) supervise the failure-type and envelope outputs, and only the PIT traces supervise the step distribution. The two weightings of the failure-type loss are inverse-frequency weights (0.85 for PITs, 0.60 for capability gaps, 6.28 for near misses) and uniform weights; the step loss is the same under both. A held-out PIT trace counts as a hit when the most probable step lies within two steps of the labelled PIT step; held-out traces of the other failure types are not scored for localization. The run that excludes the gold problems trains on 17,143 traces. Value probe. The value probe uses the combined probe’s design, trains only on recoverable traces, and predicts the raw rollout rate ˆpt. A second input set appends to each step’s hidden states five statistics of the model’s next-token distribution at the step’s read position: the entropy, the varentropy, the probability of the most probable token, the difference between the probabilities of the two most probable tokens, and the probability mass of the five most probable tokens.
