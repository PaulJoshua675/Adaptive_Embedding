Quality and diversity in LLMs. A large body of work has quantified the quality-diversity trade- off in LLMs, across creative writing, open-ended generation, and idea generation (Zhang et al., 2025; Shypula et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026; Wadhwa et al., 2026; Padmakumar & He, 2024; Jiang et al., 2025). A common finding is that RL and preference tuning collapse the policy onto a few modes, trading diversity for quality (Kirk et al., 2024; Cui et al., 2025). Training-time fixes reward diversity across independent rollouts of a prompt (Li et al., 2025), or, in preference optimization, select diverse preference pairs (Lanchantin et al., 2026). More recent work rewards a set of outputs directly, either to cover a verifiable answer set (Puri et al., 2026) or to trade off a fixed reward vector (Bahlous-Boldi et al., 2026). All of these need an answer set or a fixed reward space to define coverage. In open-ended ideation neither exists, since whether two ideas are distinct is itself a judgment made in the context of the others in the set. Our objective addresses this by scoring each idea against the ones already written in the same generation. Research ideation. Literature-grounded ideation systems generally start from a small seed and build ideas outward from it. SciMON (Wang et al., 2024) revises an idea until it is sufficiently novel relative to prior papers, ResearchAgent (Baek et al., 2025) refines a proposal through rounds of reviewer feedback, and Nova generates hundreds of candidates from a seed paper and clusters them to select a diverse final set (Hu et al., 2025). Revision, critique, and clustering are all ways to obtain diversity, but they act after generation on candidates written without regard to one another, and the generator is never trained to write ideas that differ from each other. On the other hand, GIANTS (He-Yueya et al., 2026) trains a model with RL to anticipate a downstream paper’s core insight from its parent papers, and Tong et al. (2026) train a judge and an ideator on community feedback such as citations, both rewarding one idea at a time. A separate line grounds ideation in execution, using the outcomes of implementing and running ideas as the reward. Si et al. (2026) find that reinforcement learning from execution signal suffers from mode collapse: the model raises its average reward by converging on a few simple ideas. Because each idea is rewarded in isolation, nothing discourages repeating the same safe idea. We differ on both axes. Our input is a whole body of documents instead of a single seed, and the model writes a set of ideas in one generation, trained on a reward in which each idea is scored against the ones already written. We also use execution as an evaluation, checking whether sets of ideas succeed downstream (Section 6.2). 8
