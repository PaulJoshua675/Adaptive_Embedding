A Extended Related Work Spatial state prediction connects spatial evaluation with tasks that map a described world and a sequence of operations to an observable outcome. This appendix develops connections to spatial evaluation, instruc- tion execution and entity state, complete-state prediction, structured reasoning, and contextual robustness. A.1 Spatial relations, perception, and action Textual spatial benchmarks have progressively broadened the relations and compositions exposed in lan- guage. SPARTQA asks relation, object, and containment questions over human-written and generated scene descriptions (Mirzaee et al., 2021); SPARTUN and ReSQ broaden relation types, linguistic expressions, and human-authored questions (Mirzaee and Kordjamshidi, 2022). StepGame varies the length of relation chains and the presence of distractors (Shi et al., 2022), and later analysis separates relation parsing from logical composition while correcting template artifacts (Li et al., 2024). SpaRC formalizes properties of objects, relations, and contexts, while SpaRP supplies deductively verified reasoning paths (Rizvi et al., 2024). Visual benchmarks provide a complementary tradition of controlled spatial evaluation. CLEVR uses rendered scenes and generated questions to diagnose compositional visual reasoning (Johnson et al., 2017); NLVR pairs natural statements with synthetic images requiring visual and set-theoretic reasoning (Suhr et al., 2017). VSR evaluates natural text–image pairs across a broad inventory of spatial relations and reference frames (Liu et al., 2023b), while SpatialEval compares spatial questions across textual and visual inputs (Wang et al., 2024). These benchmarks make relation recognition and composition observable under different balances of perception and language. JigShape extends geometric evaluation to reconstructing jigsaw arrangements from interlocking pieces and visual content (Li et al., 2026c). OrigamiBench connects visual observations with a language of physically grounded fold actions (Agarwal et al., 2026). FigEdit studies another form of structured transformation: checking whether chart edits respect the intended data and graphical relationships (Li et al., 2026b). Maintaining a coherent visual world across time introduces further requirements. CAT-NeRF separates appearance shared across a video from frame-specific body variation (Zhu et al., 2023), while Memorize-and- Generate compresses prior frames into memory to retain historical scene information during video generation (Zhu et al., 2025). VISD uses feedback on answer correctness, logical consistency, and spatiotemporal ground- ing to train video reasoning models (Lin et al., 2026). Learning a symbolic description from observations is a related problem: abductive state grounding uses environmental knowledge to supervise the mapping from sensory inputs to symbolic states (Su et al., 2026). Changes in the external world also affect the knowledge needed to answer visual questions. LiveVQA evaluates seeking and updating recent visual knowledge (Fu et al., 2025); Worldwide LiveVQA extends this setting across languages, with entity-recognition and multi-hop questions (Huang et al., 2026). These evaluations concern keeping knowledge current as new observations become available. Grid-Bench supplies the initial world and the events that determine the requested final state. Other work places spatial reasoning inside path construction or interactive action selection. SPaRC pathfind- ing asks models to construct grid paths under interacting spatial, arithmetic, and geometric constraints (Kaesberg et al., 2025). TextWorld generates executable text games with controllable worlds and quests (Côté et al., 2018); ALFWorld aligns such textual interaction with embodied household environments (Shrid- har et al., 2021), and ScienceWorld provides an interactive text environment for scientific tasks (Wang et al., 2022). More recent spatial-agent evaluations include iVISPAR’s sliding-tile tasks across text, 2D, and 3D inputs (Mayer et al., 2025), CubeBench’s symbolic and visual cube manipulation (Gao et al., 2026b), and SpatialWorld’s egocentric multimodal environments (Gao et al., 2026a). Three-Step Nav combines landmark planning, local visual guidance, and trajectory review for language-guided navigation (Zheng et al., 2026). Ponder & Press separates instruction interpretation from locating interface elements for computer control (Wang et al., 2025d). These settings evaluate paths or actions selected toward a goal.
