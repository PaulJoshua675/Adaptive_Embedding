The full prompts are presented at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/Scale-Sensitivity-C418/ D LLM Scoring: Output Description In this section, we describe the distributions of LLM-generated scores. Figure 8 shows the cumu- lative distributions of normalized scores, with the human labels provided in each dataset shown as reference distributions. Yet, we do not treat these la- bels as ground truth for the following reasons. First, the coding instructions followed by human annota- tors are not fully documented and may differ from our scoring criteria. Second, the political leaning labels are categorical, which limits direct compari- son with our scalar scores. Third, this study focuses on the consistency of LLM-generated scores across rating scales rather than agreement between LLMs and human annotators. We include the human la- bels only to provide context for the observed score distributions. Figure 9 shows the proportion of texts catego- rized into low, middle, and high groups used to compute SSswitch. We observe distinct score distributions across tasks. Political leaning scores concentrate heavily in the middle category (44%–67% of texts, yet de- pending on the scale). Sentiment scores show a polarized distribution, with most texts falling into either the low (39%–48%) or high (32%–44%) cat- egory, and only 8%–27% in the middle. Toxicity scores vary highly depending on rating scales. On continuous signed scales, 47%–52% of texts fall in the high category, compared to only 12%–22% on continuous unsigned scales. This shift suggests that when a scale is centered on zero, models tend to treat 0—rather than the lowest endpoint—as the threshold for not toxic.
