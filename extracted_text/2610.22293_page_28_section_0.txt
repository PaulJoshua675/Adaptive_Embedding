The natural alternative to constraining where an update may move is to leave the update unconstrained and instead penalise how far the resulting policy drifts. We fine-tune Qwen3-8B on FinQA and on Science with L = CE+λ KL(πθ ∥πbase), a reverse KL over the response tokens against a frozen copy of the base model, at λ ∈{0.001, 0.01, 0.1}, plus a λ=0 control on FinQA. Table 6 and Figure 16 show that reverse-KL regularisation does not provide a reliable learning–retention trade-off. Weak regularisation leaves forgetting essentially unchanged, while stronger regularisation can reduce target-task learning without restoring prior capability. The effect also varies across tasks, unlike the consistent retention obtained by constraining the update subspace. The table reports the exact per-task and per-benchmark values.
