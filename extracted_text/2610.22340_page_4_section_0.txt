The goal thus enters through the reward, while the play policy and world model remain goal-agnostic. F. Policy Distillation MPC incurs inference latency from repeated candidate evaluation. We therefore propose optional policy distillation through self-play in the learned world model. Starting from recorded observation histories and sampled goals og, we run MPC within Mwm, advancing the history with model predictions after each selected action preﬁx. This produces a dataset Ddistill of tuples (h, og, ˆa⋆), where h is the current observation history and ˆa⋆= ˆa(i⋆) t:t+E−1 is the preﬁx selected by MPC. We train a goal-conditioned policy πθ by behavior cloning: Ldistill(θ) = −EDdistill [log πθ(ˆa⋆| h, og)] . (6) At deployment, πθ directly predicts an action preﬁx from the actual observation history and goal. It executes the preﬁx and updates its prediction from new observations, yielding a closed-loop policy that amortizes MPC search. IV. EXPERIMENTS Our experiments evaluate whether DSP can turn goal-free play into goal-directed manipulation across tasks and on real hardware, whether play-guided MPC is more interaction- efﬁcient than direct policy learning and task-speciﬁc RL, and how play-policy design, autonomous-play scale, and world- model design affect prediction and control. A. Experimental Setup a) Tasks.: We organize the evaluation around three tasks. Task A: Push-T tests state-based planar pushing to target poses under a ﬁxed tight 10k-frame teleoperation budget, measuring success and best mask overlap. Task B: simulated reorientation asks a multiﬁngered hand to move a cube between initial and goal orientations separated by more than 90◦. Task C: real-world reorientation tests whether the same play-guided framework can achieve arbitrary-goal reorientation on hardware while retaining performance com- parable to simulation. Task D: visual reorientation tests whether play-guided planning transfers from object-state observations to RGB input. The play policy observes RGB images, and the world model evaluates candidate actions in a learned 64-dimensional latent representation.
