A single agent owning the full SFT data research loop collapses to one kind of change and loses context repeatedly. AutoSFT adds structure that an executor separates execution from analysis, several analysis subagents propose complementary changes, and a synthesis subagent writes data for uncovered capabilities. Adding each component one at a time under from-base retraining shows that the executor frees the meta agent’s context, analysis broadens changes and improves scores earlier, and synthesis reaches capabilities with almost no data in the pool. The same loop transfers to coding without a ready-made taxonomy, where the system builds its own structure from the benchmark and pool alone. These results suggest that the bottleneck may not be any single action but the structure that lets an agent cover the full range of data-engineer changes, and that multi-agent decomposition is a practical way to provide it.
