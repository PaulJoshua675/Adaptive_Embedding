Stage I aligns the complementary visual representations with the language-model represen- tation space using descriptive time-series supervision. Stage II performs instruction tuning over a mixture of time-series understanding, numerical, and reasoning tasks. We distinguish throughout between the number of examples available in each source (raw examples) and the number sampled during one training epoch (effective draws), since several sources are explicitly rebalanced. D.1 TRAINING CORPUS OVERVIEW Table 17 summarizes the datasets used in both training stages. Stage I contains 55,696 raw examples drawn from three captioning sources. Because these sources differ substantially in size, we sample them with equal probability at the source level, such that each contributes one third of the effective Stage-I distribution. We draw 55,696 examples per epoch and train for three epochs. Stage II combines instruction-following data, multiple-choice and numerical supervision, and a 100,000-example reasoning corpus. The effective training epoch contains 182,815 draws. The rea- soning corpus and numerical corpus are retained at their natural sizes, contributing 100,000 and 11,000 examples, respectively. The remaining 71,815 draws are distributed approximately equally among ChatTS IFT, ChatTS SFT, and TSExam perception data. Consequently, the effective training distribution differs from the raw corpus distribution, preventing the largest individual source from dominating instruction tuning. D.2 STAGE I: MODALITY-ALIGNMENT DATA Stage I is designed to align the visual time-series representations with language while preserving the pretrained capabilities of the underlying vision-language model. Its supervision therefore consists of descriptive time-series examples rather than the downstream reasoning tasks used in Stage II. The corpus combines 15,995 CaTS examples, 32,000 ChatTS-style caption examples, and 7,701 structural caption examples, for 55,696 raw examples in total. The substantial difference in source sizes motivates source-level balancing. At each draw, one of the three sources is selected with equal probability, after which an example is sampled from that source. Thus, each source accounts for approximately one third of the effective Stage-I training distribution, irrespective of its raw size. We use 55,696 draws per epoch for three epochs.
