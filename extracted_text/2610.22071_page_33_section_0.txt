Scope of the Boundary-Target Proposal Stage-1 distillation aligns each patch with a parent token. Deleting boundaries merges parent units into longer patches, requiring teacher representations and decoder targets to be aligned to the merged spans. Target construction uses full-text Unicode segmentation; P5 and the probe characterize the information available to reproduce those labels at inference. The existing boundary loss is the natural supervision point, with the Stage-1 pipeline changes described in Sections 2 and 3. Boundary placement and patch count are separate experimental factors. Isolating cluster alignment requires an unaligned control with the same per-sentence target count, together with comparisons at matched realized density. Within an encoding, a target comparison must also hold initialization, architecture, and evaluation fixed. Across encodings, preserving the unrestricted parent target requires an explicit remapping policy; direct transfer to UBE need not be representable. Matching training text leaves computational cost dependent on the local encoder, global transformer, local decoder, and output head. Target and realized Ain, Bin, patch density, edit sensitivity, and language-model quality therefore remain distinct outcomes. Sequence lengths alone do not determine prefill or decoding latency, peak memory, or sensitivity to batch size and padding. Language-level results are also necessary to detect regressions concealed by aggregate quality. P1–P5 characterize the target segmentation and the information available to a causal boundary predictor. M.
