We keep observed video latents clean, add noise to frames that the model must generate, and represent missing actions with learnable query embeddings. 3.2 JOINT VIDEO–ACTION MODELING We build the model on top of the pretrained Wan2.2-Fun-A14B-Control model (Alibaba PAI, 2025). We retain its latent video representation, text and first-frame conditioning, skeleton-based control pathway, and high- and low-noise diffusion experts as the RGB generation backbone. Observed actions enter through two complementary paths: rendered hand skeletons and camera-ray maps provide spatially aligned controls for hand placement and viewpoint, while compact action tokens carry the corresponding states into joint attention. Specifically, the RGB sequence is encoded into a spatiotemporal latent using the Wan2.1 VAE (Wan et al., 2025). The temporally aligned hand trajectory is rendered as a 2D skeleton video and encoded by the same VAE (Fig. 3). The resulting control latent is concatenated with the noisy RGB latent before DiT patch embedding. For camera conditioning, we express all camera-to-world poses in the first camera coordinate frame, making the first pose the identity, and construct per-pixel Plücker ray maps (Plücker, 1865) from the relative poses and camera intrinsics. After temporal packing to the video-latent frame rate, a lightweight convolutional adapter maps the Plücker representation into the DiT patch-feature space. Its output is then added to the joint RGB–hand patch embeddings. The same conditioning interfaces are used by both diffusion experts. P3-WAM represents RGB and action as coupled latent streams (Fig. 3). A separately pretrained action autoencoder, frozen during joint training, encodes hand and camera observations into compact tokens that are appended to the video tokens. Within each transformer block, the RGB and action experts share the base projection weights. The action expert adds action-specific low-rank updates and normalization. Both streams attend to the combined RGB and action keys and values, allowing bidirectional information exchange. We optimize both the shared base weights and action-specific adaptations. This modality split is used within each of Wan’s high- and low-noise experts, which operate at different diffusion noise levels. Appendix A specifies the shared layers and token encoding. 3.3 ACTION REPRESENTATION We represent hands with MANO (Romero et al., 2017), a parametric model that maps hand pose and shape to an articulated 3D mesh. For each hand, the model predicts global and local rotations ˆRt, sequence-level shape ˆβ, camera-space root position ˆrt, and hand-presence probability ˆpt. The frozen action decoder also outputs a visibility probability ˆvt,j for each joint. A fixed, differentiable MANO layer reconstructs camera-space joints ˆJt from the rotations and shape, placing the wrist at ˆrt. Observed hand states and camera states are encoded into compact tokens by a pretrained, frozen action autoencoder. A shared hand encoder embeds each hand independently, while a separate encoder embeds the camera state. Each frame is represented by three tokens: one for the camera and one for each hand.
