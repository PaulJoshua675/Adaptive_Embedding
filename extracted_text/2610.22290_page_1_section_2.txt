RL post-training therefore im- proves reliability (pass@1), but often by reducing reach (pass@k) (Oh et al., 2026). Several works seek to recover this lost reach by improving pass@k relative to the base model (Tajwar et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026b,a; Walder and Karkhanis, 2025). Since recovering reach may re- quire retaining alternative ways of solving prob- lems, pass@k has also become a common proxy for diversity (Dang et al., 2025; Song et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Yu, 2025; Jiang et al., 2026). Yet pass@k only asks whether some sample is correct, not whether the correct samples differ. A model can therefore achieve high pass@k by reliably produc- ing a single strategy for each question while losing the alternatives the base model once exhibited. Retaining these alternative ways of solving a problem, its solution diversity, is important when- ever the approach matters alongside the final an- swer. Different correct solutions may follow dis- tinct reasoning paths, differ in computational ef- ficiency, or encode different interpretations of an ambiguous request.
