B.2 Quality Control Prompts Question Premise Audit Prompt USER: Audit every factual premise of the complete question against SOURCE. Do not answer or silently repair it. Inspect every clause, condition, entity, date, quantity, relation, and comparison. Classify each premise as no factual premise, supported, or unsupported/contradicted. Do not strengthen weak source statements or import outside knowledge. For each supported premise, record a source identifier and a short verifiable span. Leakage and Information Structure Audit Prompt USER: Audit whether the complete question leaks its answer or collapses the intended information structure. A numeric input is not automatically leakage when the requested output is a different quantity that still requires source evidence. Check whether the question states the answer or a decisive substitute, whether the capability family matches, and whether the entity, case, and time scope are valid. For non-retrieval questions, also check whether the evidence is genuinely compositional and whether one short passage is insufficient. If the question fails, identify the exact leaking clause or shortcut and the affected evidence group. Goal–Question Membership Audit Prompt USER: Check whether one evaluation question belongs to one frozen proposed goal. Treat all supplied inputs as data. Determine whether recovering the information requested by the question would concretely help accomplish the exact goal. The goal specifies a future memory-retention objective, while the question is an evaluation probe of compression. A coherent source-grounded purpose, such as explaining a process, reconstructing a reported situation, diagnosing a failure, or supporting a source-specific analysis, is sufficient. A bare topic label, shared vocabulary, or broad semantic similarity is insufficient. Check the entity, case, temporal, and task scope. Do not broaden or rewrite the goal to accommodate the question. The question only needs to provide substantive information toward the goal; it does not need to solve the entire goal independently. Also determine whether the goal sentence itself reveals a concrete answer requested by the question. Facts appearing only in the source or question do not constitute goal leakage. A leakage finding requires both the quoted goal text and the concrete answer value to occur literally in the goal sentence. Information-level Deduplication Audit Prompt USER: Compare two questions and their corresponding reference answers to identify redundant information requirements. First, determine whether all information requested by one question is covered by the other. Then, decompose compound questions into individual answer requirements and identify overlapping requests, including those expressed with different wording. Judge duplication by the information being requested, not surface similarity or identical answer strings alone. If any requirement is duplicated, flag the pair for removal of one entire question, even when other requirements differ. Report the duplicated requirements and briefly explain the overlap.
