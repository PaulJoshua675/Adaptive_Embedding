Agent-safety evaluations can appear reliable even when their results depend on how the benchmark is constructed. We introduced shortcut auditing, a three-level framework that separates label pre- dictability, observational guard association, and paired decision sensitivity. Across LINUXARENA, AGENTDOJO-TRAJ, and AGENTHARM-TRAJ, simple diagnostics exposed weaknesses under do- main transfer and changes in evaluation unit. Shortcut sensitivity is therefore consequential but configuration-specific. A predictive benchmark property is a hypothesis, not evidence that a guard relies on it. Shortcut auditing provides these tests, replacing apparent benchmark performance with evidence about whether evaluation conclusions survive changes intended to preserve the underlying safety decision. 8
