Bit-level BPE repacks and deduplicates UTF- 8 prefix bits in Korean, Chinese, and Japanese byte fall- backs (Moon et al., 2025); Latom learns corpus-dependent atomic character codes (Hiraoka, 2026). UBE routes scalars through UTF-8 or UTF-16LE according to their UTF-8 length, assigning the routes disjoint alphabets (Kim & Jung, 2026). We compare this representation with UTF-8 and a matched-length big-endian control to study boundary pre- diction across scripts at limited lookahead. Script-aware segmentation. Grapheme pair encoding ini- tializes BPE with graphemes (Velayuthan & Sarveswaran, 2025), MorphTok constrains dependent-vowel segmenta- tion (Brahma et al., 2025), type-driven tokenization extends tokens to orthographic boundaries (Kapila & Bagavathy, 2026), and WWHO preserves rule-defined syllables (Dar- shana, 2026). These constraints refer to different written units. Indic grapheme parsers model orthographic syllables (Ansary et al., 2024), while a Devanagari grapheme-cluster tokenizer has been evaluated on Hindi and Marathi tasks (Dwivedi & Gopalan, 2026). Word-pooled tokenization groups bytes or characters at word boundaries (Thawani et al., 2023); HAT uses whitespace or Unicode word splits (Neitemeier et al., 2025). Korean methods exploit Jamo in character representations or tokenization (Kim et al., 2024; Lee et al., 2025), or use morpheme analysis to constrain sub- word segmentation (Lee et al., 2026a). Our target construc- tion uses Unicode grapheme clusters to constrain inherited cuts, while the predictor analysis asks whether the available encoded prefix distinguishes the required decisions. Korean and Chinese fragmentation. H-Net evaluates learned chunking against a SpaceByte-style rule on Chinese text without regular word spaces (Hwang et al., 2026). Our whitespace partition instead characterizes errors in predict- ing retained parent cuts; its targets are inherited subwords, not linguistic word labels. Thunder-Tok filters vocabulary candidates that mix complete characters with incomplete byte fragments but allows isolated fragments of a single char- acter (Cho et al., 2026), so it does not guarantee grapheme in- tegrity. Constructed combinations of incomplete tokens can cause failures (Jang et al., 2025), yet single-token Chinese characters can also perform worse on character-structure tasks (Haslett, 2025). These task-dependent findings sup- port evaluating boundary prediction, symbol compression, and linguistic quality as distinct outcomes; morphological alignment, measured across 70 languages, likewise explains little of the variance in the downstream performance of five pretrained models (Arnett et al., 2025). Disparity and character probes. Controlled tokenizer comparisons, multilingual token lengths, and commercial token pricing document disparities in representation cost (Rust et al., 2021; Petrov et al., 2023; Ahia et al., 2023). The Southeast Asian comparison by Lee et al. (2026b) also finds unequal language-level patch parity for BLT, extending this concern to entropy-based patching. Analysis of UTF- 8’s efficiency and equity tradeoffs motivates considering alternative input encodings (van der Goot, 2026). Work on vowel signs identifies letter-category pretokenization as one source of fragmentation in abugidas (Regmi et al., 2026), supporting the distinction between scalar and cluster boundaries without explaining every observed cut. Character-level evaluation provides a separate perspective on these representations. CUTE tests orthographic knowl- edge and manipulation (Edman et al., 2024). Bolmo re- ports approximately 75 million tokens of English-only CUTE-style tasks (about 0.04% of its data), augmenting approximately 172 billion Dolma 3 tokens counted with the Dolma2 tokenizer (Minixhofer et al., 2025, Section 4 and Appendix C). Our mark/base substitutions measure rel- ative corruption penalties, which complement task-based character evaluation but do not provide a general character- understanding score. 7. Limitations The lookahead evidence comes from small causal predic- tors with 2 seeds on the 17-language panel and 3 on the Korean/Chinese extension; the sentence-bootstrap intervals do not capture variation over training runs. Text exposure is matched across encodings but compute is not, and UBE-BE changes the entire encoded history as well as the first looka- head symbol. The first-symbol argument covers continua- tions within a known Unicode page; cross-page collisions preclude a universal one-symbol guarantee. The released- model analyses cover two parent families and one family of conversion recipes on FLORES-200 devtest translations, and because the checkpoints differ in architecture, size, data, and training history, their comparison does not isolate the effect of byte-level modeling.
