Data are only collected from the lower echelon of test nodes. “Upstream sourcing” and “Distribution” signify echelons for which information is unavailable. The approach of Wickett et al. (2023) seeks to infer the SFP rates associated with test nodes and supply nodes using PMS data d. Test-node SFP rates are stored in vector η = (η1, . . . , η|A|) ∈(0, 1)|A| and supply-node SFP rates are stored in vector θ = (θ1, . . . , θ|B|) ∈ (0, 1)|B|. A test can be classified as an SFP due to causes associated with either the test node or supply node (or further upstream). The consolidated SFP rate of a test with trace (a, b) accounts for SFP causes associated with either test node a or supply node b (or further upstream), and is stated as zab(η, θ) = ηa + (1 −ηa)θb . (1) The log-likelihood of γ = (η, θ) under data d is ℓ(γ|d) = X a∈A X b∈B  log[zab(γ)]N ab + log[1 −zab(γ)](N ab −Y ab)  , (2) where N ab is the number of tests in d from trace (a, b) and Y ab is the number of observed SFPs.
