Task and Preference Metrics. We report two types of metrics: task success rate (SR), which measures task completion, and preference satisfaction (SA), which measures how well a preference is satisfied using the Discrete Satisfaction Label from Section 3.2. The Discrete Satisfaction Label for episode n is dn ∈{0, 1/4, 3/4, 1}, and I[G(τn)] is its task-success indicator. We compute SA = 1 N PN n=1 I[G(τn)]dn, so preference satisfaction contributes only when the task succeeds. Conflict Policy. For a task-preference conflict, completing the original task takes priority. For preference-preference conflicts, SA is computed using the higher of the two preference satisfaction scores for each episode, with task-failure episodes contributing zero satisfaction. These scoring conventions probe execution under inconsistent instructions; they do not evaluate conflict detection, preference weighting, or when a policy should ask the user for guidance. Generalization. We evaluate in-domain, task-or-layout, and preference-level generalization using SR and SA. In-domain evaluation keeps the training tasks, preferences, and layouts with small object-position perturbations. Task-or-layout generalization changes either the task or layout, while preference-level generalization changes tasks under unseen layouts while keeping the preference unchanged. 4 Experiments We conduct experiments on the five task suites introduced in Section 3.3 to evaluate zero-shot personalization, preference learning ability, preference learning efficiency, preference generalization, composition, and behavior under conflicts. Our experiments focus on the following research questions: Q1. Can off-the-shelf policies follow preferences without preference-specific training? Q2. Can supervised fine-tuning teach policies to satisfy preferences on seen tasks and layouts? Q3. How does performance change as the number of demonstrations increases? Q4. Do learned preferences generalize across different generalization levels? Q5. Can a policy compose multiple preferences, and how does it behave under incompatible preferences? 4.1 Experimental Setup We select six representative VLA policies for evaluation: OpenVLA-OFT [53], π0-fast [1], π0.5 [6], GR00T-N1.6 [4], SmolVLA [74], and UniVLA [8].
