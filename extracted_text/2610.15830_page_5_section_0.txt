Their early nonmonotonicity is why first zero crossing is distinguished from sustained switching or convergence. 4 What keeps a formed specialization in place? 4.1 A formation condition need not remain as a lasting bias Once a bias has formed, observing its persistence does not reveal what currently supports it. We therefore remove or change the supporting conditions. In the nonlinear two-target network, making both acquisition precision and update weights symmetric reduces mean |s| from about 0.41 to 0.04 over 400 updates under the common probe. Decay continues through the final observation window. Because both controls are symmetrized, this intervention identifies their joint removal, not the necessity of biased updating alone. A separate linear bottleneck experiment crosses conditions during formation and continued learning. Its constrained condition combines a one-dimensional representation with one-target updating; its unconstrained condition uses two dimensions and updates both targets. Releasing the formation constraint reduces mean |s| from 0.9706 to 0.1303, above the never-constrained baseline of 0.0592 but far below the formed bias. Further crossings distinguish capacity from update opportunity (Appendix B). Together, these interventions show that a formation history can leave a residual without maintaining its original magnitude under changed current conditions. 5
