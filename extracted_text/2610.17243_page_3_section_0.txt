Config- uration controls the database path, relevance and align- ment thresholds, source definitions, model selection, and Web-retrieval limits. 3.3 Layer 3: semantic processing 3.3.1 Energy relevance gate The categorizer tokenizes each document and scores on- tology categories with weighted keyword counts. For category c and document d, the implemented score is S(c, d) = X k∈Kc wc,k n(k, d), (1) where Kc is the category keyword set, wc,k is the on- tology weight, and n(k, d) is the number of exact token occurrences. The highest-scoring category is selected. A document is rejected if no category matches or if its max- imum score falls below the configured relevance threshold. Current categories cover renewable energy, grid operations, policy and markets, conventional energy, and research and technology. Placing this gate before the extractors has two bene- fits. First, it makes the stored corpus domain-specific. Second, it avoids running later components on evidently unrelated material. The threshold is externalized because the appropriate precision–recall trade-off depends on the deployment corpus. 3.3.2 Parallel semantic annotations Relevant documents are passed to four analyzers: 1) NER combines energy gazetteers with rule-based patterns. En- tities are assigned labels such as energy organization, location, technology, company, feeder, power plant, or miscellaneous proper name. Canonical lowercase forms support comparison across documents. 2) Temporal ex- traction detects supported date formats and weekdays, then normalizes parseable values to ISO timestamps. 3) Event extraction uses domain trigger lexicons for out- ages, plant commissioning, policy changes, investments, accidents, and research breakthroughs. Confidence is in- creased when a sentence includes reporting cues, a normal- ized time, or sufficient context. 4) Sentiment analysis is applied only to social-media documents.
