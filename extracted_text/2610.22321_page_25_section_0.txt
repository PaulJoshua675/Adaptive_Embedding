The overall human reference score is then computed as: Shuman i = GT human i,mac + GT human i,temp + GT human i,attr 3 . Normalization is applied only to human reference scores to calibrate subjective annotations across annotators. For automatic metrics, we do not introduce additional normalization beyond each met- ric’s original formulation. Applying dimension-wise normalization to model outputs would addi- tionally modify the score distributions and aggregation behaviors of individual metrics. Since dif- ferent automatic metrics may have different output spaces, numbers of dimensions, and aggregation strategies, applying such transformations only for a specific evaluator would make the comparison protocol inconsistent. For Ours, we further evaluate a z-score normalized variant as a sensitivity analysis. As shown in Table 15, the difference between raw-score and z-score aggregation is negligible, indicating that the reported results are not sensitive to this normalization choice. The aggregation is used only for benchmark-level overall evaluation, while the three dimensions remain independently annotated and scored. Evaluation metrics. We use ∆-Pairwise Accuracy as the primary metric for measuring metric-to- human agreement. For a margin ∆, the valid pair set is P∆=  (i, j) : Shuman i −Shuman j > ∆ . We use ∆= 0.2 as the primary setting to exclude near-tied human pairs and focus the evaluation on perceptually meaningful score differences. Standard pairwise accuracy, SRCC, and PLCC are also computed in the complete benchmark evaluation. PLCC with five-parameter logistic mapping. Following common practice in subjective qual- ity assessment (Sheikh, 2003), we apply a nonlinear logistic regression before computing PLCC. Human perceptual judgments are often not linearly related to objective prediction scores due to per- ceptual saturation and scale differences. Therefore, a mapping is first learned to align the prediction scale with the human score space. Specifically, for each predictor and evaluation dimension, we fit a five-parameter logistic function on the same evaluation subset: f(x) = b1  0.5 − 1 1 + exp(b2(x −b3))  + b4x + b5, (19) where x denotes the predicted score and f(x) is the calibrated prediction.
