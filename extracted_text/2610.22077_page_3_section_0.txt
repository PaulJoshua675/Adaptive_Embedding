Models. We evaluate three leading probabilistic weather models that differ both in their training objectives and in the way they represent uncertainty. GenCast uses conditional diffusion to sample fu- ture atmospheric states (Price et al., 2024). FourCastNet 3 (FCN3) combines spherical convolutions with stochastic perturbations. It is trained using the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) in the physical and spectral space (Bonev et al., 2025). FGN optimizes the CRPS at individual locations and forms an ensemble by combining four independently trained networks; within each network, additional members are generated via learned perturbations (Alet et al., 2025). Evaluation settings. Four datasets serve as reference: IBTrACS (Knapp et al., 2010) supplies observed tracks and intensities; IFS-ENS (ECMWF, 2024), ECMWF’s operational physics-based ensemble, is the numerical baseline, especially for uncertainty representation; ERA5 (Hersbach et al., 2020) and HRES-fc0 are the gridded references for two experimental configurations, with two versions of GenCast available. The reanalysis setting uses GenCast and FCN3 trained exclusively on ERA5, evaluated against ERA5 and IBTrACS. The operational setting uses GenCast (operational) and FGN, trained on ERA5 and fine-tuned on HRES-fc0, the initial conditions of ECMWF’s high- resolution forecast system, evaluated against HRES-fc0 and IBTrACS. This design allows for the investigation of how the choice of reference data influences intensity bias, in particular whether ERA5-related biases remain after operational fine-tuning. The evaluation years follow the model training periods (Figure 1), extending the tests over several cyclone seasons. Because the storms and model versions differ between settings, the comparison does not isolate the effect of fine-tuning. Forecast protocol. We conduct a retrospective evaluation of historical tropical cyclones across all basins. 178 IBTrACS cyclones whose maximum reported one-minute sustained wind reaches at least 64 knots are selected and reproduced using the AI models. We assess predicted tracks, intensity, ensemble calibration, and storm structure, introducing each diagnostic alongside its scientific question. For each storm, we initiate a 15-day forecast rollout with 50 ensemble members per model in the reanalysis setting and 49 in the operational setting (Appendix A). Forecasts are initiated 12 hours before the first reported wind observation in the IBTrACS dataset, adjusted to the preceding forecast cycle where necessary. In total, the evaluation covers 26,541 member forecasts across lead times, using both cyclone tracks and atmospheric fields. Operational forecasts were obtained from existing archives, and we generated the forecasts for the reanalysis setting using more than 6,000 H100 GPU-hours.
