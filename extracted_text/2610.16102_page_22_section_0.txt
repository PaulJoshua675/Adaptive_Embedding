Exactness matters because the trace is for people. A trace that is itself approximate in an unknown way would reproduce the problem of §7.3 one level down; a trace whose only approximation is a closed-form CR2 decay can be read with its error bars attached. 8.3 Why the third layer is human: a virtue-epistemic reading Virtue epistemology explains knowledge as a cognitive success creditable to the knower’s cogni- tive abilities, whether innate faculties such as perception, memory and reasoning, or acquired intellectual virtues such as conscientiousness, which involve a desire for truth (Pritchard 2023, 54–59). Pritchard’s discussion of technology draws a contrast that fits the first two layers. Using a calculator puts an intellectual distance between a person and the answer: the device acts as an insentient “testifier”, and how the answer was reached may stay hidden, whereas working it out oneself makes the route visible (Pritchard 2023, 187–188). An LLM’s result arrives in the first way. A VaCoAl/PyVaCoAl trace shortens the distance: a reader can follow the route step by step, with CR1 at each step and CR2 for the whole path, and so check the derivation rather than take the answer on trust. The third layer supplies what the trace cannot. Intellectual virtues such as intellectual humility, conscientiousness and intellectual tenacity require motivation, effort and reflection, so they cannot be offloaded onto technology, however much else can be; they also direct how a person’s other cognitive abilities are used (Pritchard 2023, 189–191). They are, moreover, what lead people to seek understanding rather than rest content with knowing (Pritchard 2023, 197–200). Seminars, refereeing, digestion and teaching are where these virtues are exercised, and where a verified result comes to be understood. The substrate can shorten the distance between a person and a result; only the person can close it. 8.4 Auditable abduction Pearl’s procedure for counterfactuals begins with abduction: infer the exogenous variables con- sistent with the evidence, then intervene by setting a variable and cutting the arrows into it, then predict the outcome in the modified model (Pearl 2009; Pearl, Glymour & Jewell 2016). The hippocampal paper argues that reversible GF(2) binding supplies the surgical algebra of the do-operator and that the dual-orthogonalizer architecture supplies the parallel substrate coun- terfactuals need (2026b). Combined with the three layers, this gives a design we call auditable abduction. An LLM proposes a hypothesis in Peirce’s sense. The hypothesis is written into the DAG memory as bound records. Its consequences, and the counterfactual “what if this edge were absent?”, are computed by unbind–rebind, and the change shows up as a change in CR along named paths. The human reading the result sees which bindings the hypothesis depends on before deciding what it is worth. This is a proposal, not a demonstration. The Minsky paper states that the reachability of its Reflective layer is argued rather than shown, and that its sufficient conditions—a meta- control loop and context tagging—remain future work (2026f).
