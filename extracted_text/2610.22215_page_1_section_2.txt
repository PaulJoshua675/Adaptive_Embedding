Accurate motion forecasting enables autonomous vehicles to anticipate traffic participants and plan safely. Given agent histories and an HD map, models must represent multiple plausible futures over an extended prediction horizon. Mod- ern methods use vectorized scene representations [4, 13], graph- or attention-based context encoders [5, 21, 24], and expressive trajectory decoders [10, 33]. Yet even strong predictors leave residual displacement and ranking errors in their multimodal proposals. Existing refiners en- code trajectory features, retrieve context around selected an- chors, or model trajectory–trajectory and trajectory–lane re- lations [27, 32]. However, compressed embeddings, sparse anchors, and pairwise summaries do not spatially instan- tiate the complete proposed horizon. This is especially limiting for temporal correction: within a causal segment- wise sweep, later evidence cannot revise an earlier segment [10, 32]. Iterative relational refiners can revisit predictions [27], but require repeated computation and still reason pri- marily at the trajectory level. We instead argue that the complete proposed future should be explicit before correc- tion, allowing every segment to interact jointly with the map, agent histories, alternative modes, and other predicted agents while exchanging evidence in both temporal direc- tions.
