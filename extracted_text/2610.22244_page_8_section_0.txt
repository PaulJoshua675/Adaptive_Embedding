The data split by regime (Figure 3). Leaky steps already flip more under placebo (they sit later in trajectories), but for the strong guards and for the guards that lose recall the placebo-adjusted flip rate under translation is also larger on leaky steps: Claude Sonnet 5 +1.2 vs. +0.3 pp, TS-Guard +4.1 vs. +2.4, Nemotron Nano 9B +2.2 vs. +1.2, Qwen 3.5 9B +2.6 vs. +1.0; the same ordering holds under paraphrase (Claude +0.9 vs. +0.1, TS-Guard +2.1 vs. +0.2). When the agent’s Thought restates the original English request and the request field now says it in another language, these guards see two channels that disagree, and their verdict moves. For the over-blockers the placebo-adjusted flip rate is larger on the non-leaky steps: Nemotron 3.5 +1.8 vs. +2.8, Llama 4 Maverick +1.7 vs. +2.5, Gemma 4 26B +0.6 vs. +1.4 (self-hosted −0.2 vs. +2.8), AgentDoG +2.4 vs. +7.2. Safiron (+4.0 vs. +1.3) sides with TS-Guard; ShieldAgent moves on both (+8.0 vs. +7.3). Where nothing in the trajectory restates the request, these guards treat the foreign request itself as suspicious. Two mechanisms are consistent with this split, and they match the two regimes: inconsistency detection, which needs a restatement to fire and moves verdicts in either direction, and distrust of a foreign request, which fires when the request is the only source and adds blocks. Both are bounded by the restatement rate (60% of steps), which our intervention does not change, and a guard that decides from the trajectory alone, as the frontier models appear to do, is immune to both. 4.8 The sensitivity is to language more than to wording The same instructions reworded in English are the control. Across typos, four LLM styles and a human paraphrase, no general guard’s verdict flip rate exceeds its own placebo by more than 1.4 pp (TS-Guard among the pre-registered roster, Nemotron 3 Nano 4B in the small tier); the two purpose- built guards that do move under paraphrase (ShieldAgent +3.5 pp, AgentDoG +1.8) move two to three times as much under translation. Every guard that moves at all moves more under translation than under paraphrase. No paraphrase condition reduces any guard’s recall except gpt-oss-120b on the terse style (−1.3 pp), and the guard ranking is preserved (Kendall’s τ 0.96–0.98 against 0.93–0.96 under translation). Dose–response by surface-distance tercile is flat for all but two guards.
