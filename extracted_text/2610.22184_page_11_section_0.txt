WBD Grasp Dataset For WBD-EX training and the rest of the pipeline, we specialize this general method to grasps using the arms and torso. On the Unitree G1, the Dex3 finger joints are pinned to constant closed-grip values, and the twelve leg joints and three waist joints remain fixed. Bimanual generation optimizes the fourteen arm joints, giving 23 scalar variables including the root pose; single-arm generation additionally freezes the opposite arm, leaving seven optimized joints and 16 scalar variables. The numerical settings and validation procedure below describe this WBD grasp dataset. The candidate contact set contains 626 points, sampled uniformly on the link meshes and then hand-curated, across 34 links; 347 points over 25 links are active for the bimanual configuration. We sample K = 5 contact links per iteration. The dataset uses three contact configurations: right-arm con- tact over 13 links and bimanual contact over 25 links, both requiring torso contact, as well as bimanual contact over 24 arm links with the torso excluded. For this dataset, only torso contact points carry region tags, spanning the six regions shown in Figure 7. Restricting to a region has four effects. It filters the candidate contact points, so a generation run restricted to one torso region optimizes against roughly a sixth of the available torso points; it biases initialization, since the direction the body is aimed from is set per region; it assigns each region an equal share of the per-object grasp generation quota; and it labels the resulting grasps so that region coverage can be audited afterward. It does not restrict which links may make contact. Every accepted grasp is finally mirrored across the sagittal plane, which doubles the set exactly. The mirror is an exact relabeling of an already-validated grasp, so it is not re- simulated. D. Pre-Grasp and Post-Grasp Targets We adapt the contact-normal pressing strategy of [16] for the post-grasp target and extend it to a retracted pre- grasp. From a solved configuration, we derive a retracted pre-grasp and a pressed post-grasp target by backpropagating P k ∇ϕ(pk) · pk through the kinematics, with the SDF gradients held fixed, to obtain a joint-space gradient. We normalize this gradient to unit Euclidean norm, multiply it by +0.5 for retraction or −0.4 for pressing, and add it to q, clamping the result to the joint limits. The pre-grasp is the approach pose used to start the simulation trial; the post-grasp is the press target held during validation and later reused as the servo setpoint at each WBD-EX episode reset. E. Solver For the WBD grasp dataset, candidates are optimized in parallel on one GPU: 4096 candidates for 4000 iterations with AdamW at learning rate 10−3, weight decay 10−4, and a cosine schedule annealing to 10−4. Steps after a short warmup are replayed from captured CUDA graphs. Optimization candidates are initialized by sampling a random direction outward from the object centroid and rotating the robot to face the object centroid. Orientation is then jittered by a uniform roll about the facing axis over (−π, π) and two orthogonal tilts over (−π/8, π/8), and the seed posture is jittered per joint by ±25% of that joint’s range. Simulation Validation Because the optimizer minimizes a static surrogate objec- tive, the WBD grasp candidates are validated dynamically in two stages using mjlab [52]. The torso is pinned to its rest pose every substep, and the lower body is held fixed, so the torso frame is exactly static; the object is a free body. Physics runs at 1 ms with four substeps per control frame, an elliptic friction cone, and surface friction of µ = 0.6 on the robot with priority over the object’s. Each simulation trial starts the arms at the pre-grasp, closes to the grasp candidate configuration, presses to the post-grasp target, and holds there. Approach and press last 40 control frames each, followed by a brief 60-frame settle and the 300-frame hold, with 4 ms per frame. The gravity load and any perturbations are applied during the hold. A link is in contact when its normal force is at least 0.1 N. Stage one loads the object at 4× weight and, over the first half of the hold, sweeps a sustained 10 N force and 0.5 N m torque through the six axis directions for 100 ms
