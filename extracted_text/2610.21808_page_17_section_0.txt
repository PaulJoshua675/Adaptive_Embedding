Stages. We distinguish five stages of increasing cost: calibration-free PTQ; calibrated PTQ (GPTQ); scale distillation, which re-fits the scales and norm gains to the fp model’s next-token distribution with the codes frozen (0.02–0.08% of the parameters, 400 steps); fine-tuning of codes or weights with the quantizer in the loop (Malinovskii et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2026c; Tseng et al., 2024b), from tens of A100-hours at 8B (CAT-Q) to about 160 (ScaleQ-1.58) and about 100 at 70B for QuIP#-style fine-tuning (Section 3.4); and training from scratch (Wang et al., 2023). Tables 1 and 4 record the stage of every run, and every comparison in the paper is either stage-aligned or names both stages. The hardware case. Ternary weights offer two savings rather than one. (i) Memory traffic scales with bpw; it is identical for every format at equal bits and is the saving today’s GPUs realise. (ii) An integer weight at any width still multiplies (dequantize-and-FMA or an integer MAC, plus a scale and zero point every group), whereas a ternary weight adds the activation, subtracts it or skips it: no multiplier, no codebook and no group metadata in the inner loop. The per-vector scale costs one multiply per output or input coordinate, O(d) per token against O(d2) additions, and a second plane costs two accumulations and an add. Binary weights share this property but have no zero; four-level and wider grids do not have it. The second saving is realised today on CPUs by lookup- table kernels (Wang et al., 2024; 2025; Wei et al., 2025) and is the premise of add-only hardware, for which BitNet b1.58 estimates about 70× lower matmul arithmetic energy than fp16 at 7 nm (Ma et al., 2024). We report no latency or energy measurements of our own (Section 5). Four controls separate the placement effect from what it could be confounded with. The code and the scale count. On the four-level g128 grid, which changes both the code and the number of scales, the ordering RoleQ < per-OC < per-IC reproduces at every Qwen size, including 14B (10.76, 10.90, 11.64). At 1.7B both halves of the margin, the writer half (RoleQ against per- OC, which differ only on writers) and the reader half (RoleQ against per-IC, which differ only on readers), lie well outside the seed spread (19% and 30%); at 14B only the reader half does (8.2% at 2.9× the seed range, against 1.3% at 0.5× for the writer half). On Llama-8B the role and per- OC placements of the same grid tie in the recipe basis (Table 11), so on the four-level grid the ordering is established for Qwen only. The fit. Two different ternary fits give the same ordering within noise. The rotation. AdaDim’s per-layer search agrees with the placement rule on 96.4% of the cells in the rotated basis and on 14.3% unrotated (Table 8): the axis the rule fixes is the one a reconstruction-error search prefers only after rotation, which is why we state it as a rotated-basis rule (Section 3.2). The reference placement. The best uniform placement changes with size and family (per-IC at Qwen-8B, per-OC at 14B and on Llama), so every margin in the paper is reported against it. Llama-8B is the only cell in which both halves separate at once (writer 1.97×, reader 11.52×), between two runs at 9.2× and 18.2× full precision. As a consistency check, the 8B placement runs, PTQ-only under r1 and carried out separately from the recipe ladder, reproduce the corresponding bodies seed by seed (RoleQ 30.7 / 31.5 against 31.0 / 31.0, the r1 PTQ row of Table 9; per-OC 36.0 / 35.0 against 35.4 / 34.9). D THE CONVERSION RECIPE: DETAILS Solver settings. The GPTQ sweep runs in activation order (the act-order option of the reference implementation; the paper itself uses the natural column order) through the Cholesky factor of H−1, with damping of 0.01× the mean diagonal and lazy batching over blocks of 128 columns, which changes the speed but not the result. Each H is captured from the rotated model layer by layer, with the preceding layers already quantized.
