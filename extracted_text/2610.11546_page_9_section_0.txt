Positive values of ∆σ(t) encourage broader exploration, whereas negative values promote local refinement, with the magnitude of ∆σ(t) determining the strength of this adjustment. A key feature of the proposed orchestration mechanism is that covariance learning draws on top-performing candidates from the entire mixed offspring population, rather than solely from CMA-ES-generated solutions. Consequently, directional information discovered through DE mutation also contributes to covariance adaptation, allowing the Gaussian sampling distribution to align with promising search directions identified across both operators. To further improve robustness against premature convergence, stochastic restart (introduced in Section 2.3.2) is applied to both DE- and CMA-ES-generated offspring through the restart probability p(t) res: candidate generation is randomly reinitialized with probability p(t) res, enabling occasional exploration of previously unvisited regions in highly multimodal search landscapes. The proposed dynamic DE–CMA-ES orchestration differs fundamentally from conventional hybrid EAs, which typically combine operators through predefined rules, staged execution, or hard switching, imposing rigid interactions that often require problem-specific manual tuning. By contrast, the DRL-DCO algorithm formulates operator coordination as a continuous control problem, where the embedded DRL agent adaptively regulates population size, offspring allocation, and internal evolutionary update rates according to the observed optimization state, allowing the search process to transition smoothly among exploration-dominant, exploitation-dominant, and mixed regimes. Accordingly, for the high-dimensional FEMU and related optimization problems, this adaptive orchestra- tion offers two practical benefits: it preserves the global exploration capability of the DE while utilizing the covariance-learning mechanism of the CMA-ES for efficient local refinement, and it avoids rigid hybridization rules, allowing the framework to automatically adjust search behavior across optimization stages while reducing the need for manual algorithm configuration. 2.5 Implementation details and algorithm workflow The DRL-DCO algorithm can operate in either of two distinct phases, each capable of independently solving the optimization task, that together govern its interaction between evolutionary optimization and DRL-based policy learning. In the agent-training phase, the embedded DDPG-based actor–critic agent operates simultaneously with population evolution, as the evolutionary operators search for improved solutions, the actor and critic networks are concurrently updated through deterministic policy gradients and temporal-difference learning, respectively (Section 2.3.4). Alternatively, in the agent-inference phase, a previously trained actor network instead generates generation-wise control actions independently, without further parameter updates, critic evaluation, or reward feedback, offering a more efficient deployment mode once training has been completed. To achieve stable adaptive regulation of the evolutionary process, appropriate constraints are imposed on the action space. Although the DRL-DCO framework enables autonomous adjustment of evolutionary hyperparameters, unconstrained action outputs may lead to excessive parameter variation, unstable population dynamics, or inefficient policy exploration.
