In- stead, they impose additional supervision on the shared hidden rep- resentation through lightweight task-specific heads. Consequently, the generative objective remains unchanged while the backbone is encouraged to encode attribute structure that may otherwise be weakly supervised by interaction data. The auxiliary heads are maintained separately from the backbone checkpoint and saved with their architecture metadata and parameters. 6 Experiments Implementation Details. Each semantic ID consists of four tokens (𝐿0, 𝐿1, 𝐿2, 𝐿3), with a 256-entry RQ-VAE codebook at each level. Our hierarchical loss uses level-specific base weights (4.0, 3.0, 2.0, 1.0), confidence-based gating, an annealed transition from static weighting to confidence gating, and scheduled sampling to reduce the train–inference exposure gap. For attribute supervision, we use a CIE-LAB distance-based soft-label objective at 𝐿2, restricted to candidates sharing the ground-truth (𝐿0, 𝐿1) prefix, and apply it to color-conditioned conversations with weight 0.1. We further employ a hidden-state regression head to provide complementary attribute supervision. Training is performed in two stages, with embedding warm-up followed by full fine-tuning using learning rate 2 × 10−5, effective batch size 32, and a cosine learning-rate schedule. We train the Qwen3-8B model in two stages. Stage 1 extends the model vocabulary with semantic-ID (SID) tokens and initializes their embeddings. Stage 2 performs full-parameter su- pervised fine-tuning (SFT), initialized from the Stage 1 checkpoint. All experiments were conducted on NVIDIA RTX6000 single GPU instance with 96 GB memory. Additional implementation details and hyperparameters are provided in the Supplementary Material. Datasets. We evaluate HASTE across diverse recommendation domains and data regimes. We use four public domains from the Amazon Reviews benchmark [13] – Sports & Outdoors (Sports), Beauty, Toys & Games (Toys), and Fashion-140k, a 140K-item subset of the larger Amazon Fashion dataset, and an internal production- scale proprietary fashion dataset (internal-fashion). Sports, Beauty, and Toys follow the standard P5 5-core protocol [10], where each user and item has at least five interactions, and are used to evaluate the effectiveness of hierarchical supervision for sequential genera- tive recommendation. Fashion-140k and internal-fashion dataset provide richer item-level attribute annotations, including color and material, enabling evaluation of the proposed attribute-specific su- pervision, particularly in the long-tail setting. Across datasets, item representations are encoded as hierarchical semantic IDs using a 3-level RQ-VAE with 256 codes per level, with a deduplication token appended where applicable. Further details on dataset statistics, preprocessing, semantic-ID construction, and attribute availability are provided in the supplementary material. Baselines. We compare HASTE against representative baselines spanning three categories: traditional sequential recommendation methods (BERT4Rec [29], SASRec [15]), generative recommenda- tion methods (TIGER [26], HSTU [40]), and LLM-based methods (ID- GenRec [30], OneRec-Think [20]). We additionally compare against the standard cross-entropy (CE) objective to isolate the impact of our proposed supervision. Evaluation Protocol. We use Recall@K and NDCG@K with 𝐾∈ {5, 10} to evaluate recommendation performance. We evaluate the hierarchical loss on the sequential recommendation task, while attribute-specific losses are evaluated on attribute-conditioned prod- uct recommendation, both overall and on torso and tail items with fewer than 500 instances. 6.1 Results We compare HASTE against representative sequential, generative, and LLM-based recommendation baselines, and evaluate the contri- bution of its hierarchical and attribute-specific supervision through complimentary experiments. Overall recommendation performance across Beauty, Sports, and Toys. Table 1 compares HASTE with baseline methods across Beauty, Sports, and Toys datasets. HASTE with hierarchical super- vision consistently achieves strong performance, particularly on Recall@10, improving over the strongest baseline by 4.0%. 12.4%, and 15.5% on Beauty, Sports, and Toys, respectively. Similar gains are observed for NDCG@10, with improvements of 6.8%, 16.7%, and 1.2%. The stronger gains on Sports and Toys suggest that hierar- chical supervision effectively captures the structured relationships encoded by semantic IDs. Compared with the CE-based variant, hierarchical supervision generally provides further improvements, demonstrating the benefit of explicitly supervising intermediate semantic-ID levels. Tail-item and attribute-aware performance. Table 2 evaluates hierarchical and attribute-specific supervision on Amazon Fashion- 140k and the production-scale proprietary internal-fashion dataset. We use these datasets for attribute losses because Toys, Sports, and Beauty lack the required item-level attribute annotations. Hi- erarchical supervision consistently improves over CE, with larger gains on tail items, while color and material supervision provide further improvements, particularly for tail items. Under HL, color supervision gives the strongest gains, reaching 24.2% in Recall@10 on Fashion-140k and 17.8% on internal-fashion, while achieving the best overall and tail performance on internal-fashion. These results show that hierarchical and attribute-specific supervision are complementary: the former improves semantic retrieval, while the latter preserves fine-grained attribute information valuable for tail items.
