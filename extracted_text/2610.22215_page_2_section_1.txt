2.1. Structured Representations for Motion Fore- casting Vectorized representations preserve agent and map geome- try without the quantization and computational cost of ras- terized scene encodings. VectorNet [4] represents scene elements as polylines, while LaneGCN [13] constructs a lane graph with explicit actor–map interactions. Later ap- proaches capture richer relational structure: HDGT [9] in- troduces heterogeneous node and edge types, GoRela [2] uses viewpoint-invariant relative representations, and Holi- Graph [5] retains fine-grained spatial and temporal entities in a heterogeneous scene graph. These representations pri- marily organize the observed scene from which future tra- jectories are decoded. Recent architectures increasingly place relational rea- soning in the decoder. Query-centric methods such as MTR [19], QCNet [33], and DeMo [28] progres- sively decode multimodal futures; FutureNet [25] incor- porates predicted future context into joint forecasting; and DONUT [10] formulates trajectory generation as decoder- only autoregressive modeling. Foresight in Motion [16] ap- plies bidirectional state-space modeling to trajectory-state queries within a hierarchical end-to-end decoder, while NDPNet [7] separates near- and distant-horizon decoding and promotes kinematically coherent transitions. In these methods, the future representation is part of a specific fore- casting architecture. FutureLift instead operates after pro- posal generation: it lifts the temporal segments of already generated proposals into a common graph for backbone- independent refinement. 2.2. Trajectory Refinement Refinement is often integrated into the forecasting de- coder. MTR [19], for example, repeatedly updates intention queries using local motion information, while QCNet [33] follows proposal generation with a dedicated refinement stage. Such refiners can exploit backbone features directly, but this coupling makes them difficult to transfer across pro- posal generators. Post-hoc methods provide a clearer interface between proposal generation and correction. SmartRefine [32] uses scenario-adaptive anchors and local context to recurrently refine trajectory segments, with optional outer iterations for difficult cases. Within a forward sweep, information prop- agates only from earlier to later segments, while outer iter- ations repeat the causal refinement process. SRefiner [27] targets multi-agent refinement by constructing a soft-braid topology over trajectory–trajectory and trajectory–lane re- lations and iteratively updating joint predictions. Although its paper formulates the initial trajectory representation as an encoding of proposal geometry and agent pose, the re- leased AV2 implementation additionally consumes a pre- computed 134-dimensional per-mode embedding exported with each proposal. SRefiner is therefore the closest prior work in combining post-hoc joint refinement with an ex- plicit relational structure, but its released interface is not strictly proposal-only.
