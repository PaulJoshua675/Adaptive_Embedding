OmniVerifier-C1: Decomposed Graded Verifier for Rule-Governed Generative World Modeling Implication for GRPO. By Lemma A.1, GRPO assigns larger update weights to samples with larger rewards. Therefore, a reward model that better preserves the true quality ordering also provides more reliable relative policy updates. The decomposed graded reward is strictly monotonic with respect to the number of satisfied sub-questions and thus preserves all pairwise quality comparisons. In contrast, the binary reward collapses all imperfect samples into the same reward, making GRPO unable to distinguish partially correct samples from severely incorrect ones. Consequently, Decomposed Graded Verification provides a denser and more order-preserving reward signal for GRPO. B Other Results B.1 Training Dynamics across Six Game-Based Tasks Fig. 5 presents the training dynamics of pairwise and pointwise verification across our six constructed rule- governed tasks. Following the evaluation protocol in the main text, we evaluate intermediate checkpoints every 10 training steps on the corresponding validation sets, using Gemini3.1-Pro (Google DeepMind, 2026) as an evaluator not involved in data processing and providing ground-truth images as explicit references. Across all six tasks, pairwise verification generally yields more sustained performance improvements and higher final scores, whereas pointwise verification exhibits more pronounced fluctuations and limited progress. This trend is consistent with the aggregate results in the main text. By directly comparing generated outputs with ground-truth references, pairwise verification reduces reliance on internal target- state inference and can provide more reliable reward signals for policy optimization. These results further support the effectiveness of reference-based verification across different types of rule-governed visual generation tasks.
