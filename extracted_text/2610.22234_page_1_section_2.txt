The rapid progress of vision-language models (VLMs) (Team, 2026; Wang et al., 2025b; Team et al., 2026a; Anthropic, 2025; Zeng et al., 2026; OpenAI, 2025; Bai et al., 2026; Zou et al., 2026) has accelerated the development of computer-using agents (CUAs), which aim to execute open-ended tasks in human-facing digital environments. Most existing CUAs formulate digital automation as graphical interaction, where agents perceive screenshots, reason over interface states, and act through mouse and keyboard operations. However, GUI-centric CUAs (Liu et al., 2026; Xue et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026b; Zhang et al., 2026a; Luo et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2024; Feizi et al., 2026) still face practical bottlenecks in realistic desktop workflows. Visual grounding can fail on small or ambiguous interface elements, and fine-grained interaction can make structured state manipulation cumbersome even with batched actions.
