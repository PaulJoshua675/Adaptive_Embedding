Whole decomposition reconstructs a source- defined whole from its components and, where supported, derives a share or residual. Rule appli- cation applies a stated rule, threshold, or exception to a specified case, including a hypothetical change to an explicit input. Bounded trend extrapolation uses a stated rate, recurrence, or sufficiently speci- fied series to derive a limited next value or range. Conflict resolution identifies competing or revised claims, retains their provenance and scope, and de- termines which claim governs only when the source establishes an authority or temporal ordering. Each question has a reference answer and supporting evidence. A strategy is used only when it is sup- ported by the source. The generation prompts are provided in the appendix B.1.1. Before question generation, candidate facts and their locations in c are identified. Each question is generated with a reference answer and a record link- ing its requested answer components to the relevant facts and source passages. During quality control, these recorded links are used to assess source sup- port and whether the selected evidence is sufficient to answer the question. Using the source context and generated questions, an LLM groups questions that serve a shared future task and synthesizes a goal g for each group. Questions in a group may require different information but should jointly sup- port that task. The goal describes what the retained information should enable, without listing ques- tions or revealing answers. The resulting instance is z = (c, g, Qg), where Qg = {q1, . . . , qng} con- tains the associated evaluation questions. 3.2 Quality Control We screen instances for source validity, context dependence, question coherence, and goal align- ment before compression evaluation. Checks re- move invalid questions or goal groups; unrelated compound questions are separated only when their answer requirements can be assigned to coherent new questions. Source-Grounded Audits. A question is retained only if it is unambiguous, its factual premises are supported by the source, and the essential claims in its generated reference answer are correct. The recorded facts and source passages are used to check whether each requested answer component has sufficient support. To assess completeness in- dependently of the generated answer, an LLM iden- tifies all requested items from the question and source, including alternative correct answers when the question permits them. These requirements are audited before the final reference answer is con- structed. We also reject questions whose requested relations or conditions lack source support or do not follow their assigned generation strategy. Ques- tions about incidental details that do not serve a substantive downstream task, such as command counts or line numbers in SWE-agent histories, are removed. Goal Validity and Alignment. Each synthesized goal must describe a coherent future task grounded in the source and supported by its assigned ques- tions. Topic labels, restatements of questions, and goals that reveal answers are rejected. Within each group, questions must fit the goal, and their an- swer requirements must concern information worth retaining for that task. Three models assess each requirement; any valid negative judgment removes the question. When a context has multiple goals, an LLM must select the assigned goal for each ques- tion and an assigned question for each goal from alternatives in the same context. A failed selection removes the tested question or goal, respectively. A goal is also removed if it covers information re- quested by a question from another group, judged from that question and its reference answer without the source. Groups with fewer than four remaining questions are discarded. Context Dependence. Context dependence is checked in both directions: the full source must enable a correct answer, while the question alone should not. Answers must be correct across re- peated attempts with the full context. For ques- tions combining source facts, the selected source passages must also suffice to answer the question; within this selection, at least two passages must contribute necessary information. To test answer- ability without the source, four models each receive only the question, without the goal or reference an- swer. Their responses are judged against the full source; any correct response removes the question. Questions that disclose their own answers are re- moved as well. Information-Level Deduplication. Question pairs within each goal group are compared at two levels. First, if all information requested by one question is covered by another, the more informative ques- tion is retained.
