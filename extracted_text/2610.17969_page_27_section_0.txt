F.2 Dot-product variance and the calibrated temperature in Eq. 3 Suppose each qa is independent of ka, both coordinates are zero-mean with Var(qa) = s2 Q and Var(ka) = s2 K, and the products qaka are uncorrelated across channels. Channelwise independence is one sufficient condition. Then Var(q⊤k) = P a Var(qaka) = dhs2 Qs2 K exactly. With merely weak cross-channel correlations, the same equality is an approximation with the omitted cross-covariances added explicitly. Dividing logits by τ = √dhsQsK yields dimensionless logits of unit variance under the exact assumptions and O(1) variance in the weak-correlation regime. In the zero-floor GCT formulation, per-head-vector canonicalization enforces ∥¯q∥= ∥¯k∥= √dh. For weakly dependent directions, ¯q⊤¯k/√dh again has O(1) variance, and the learnable per-head gain tunes the regime multiplicatively. Attention in canonical coordinates. Queries and keys are canonicalized per head vector, ¯q := C(q) and ¯k := C(k). When ϵx = 0, RMS normalization removes the magnitude of each nonzero query and key before the dot product. The remaining 1/√dh factor accounts for the head dimension. If the query and key directions are approximately isotropic and weakly dependent at initialization, we have Var(¯q⊤¯k/√dh) ≈1, giving softmax dimensionless logits on an O(1) scale. A per-head dimensionless gain gh, initialized at 1, tunes the attention entropy within these canonical coordinates. Empirical calibration. If the coordinates are centered with Var(qa) = s2 Q and Var(ka) = s2 K, each qa is independent of ka, and the products qaka are uncorrelated across channels, then Var(q⊤k) = dhs2 Qs2 K exactly. Empirical per-head centered-RMS estimates give a calibrated approximation. Centered RMS is the RMS after subtracting the mean, RMS(x −¯x1), with ¯x computed over the same entries. Under that averaging convention, centered RMS equals the standard deviation. Ordinary RMS serves as a proxy when coordinate means are negligible.
