As compared to the LLAVA-One Vision (LLAVA-OV) baseline ( [17,19]), which offers a 0.5 B model, we see a significant improvement with InternVL3 1B model. While some of this can be attributed to the more number of parameters (since LLAVA-OV does not offer 1B model), the multimodal encoder structure in InternVL3 ( [7]) style of models also plays a significant part. Unlike LLAVA, both InternVL3 and Qwen support frame-level injection, enabling bounding boxes to be passed separately for each frame. This design yields consistent improvements, which are further amplified by our CAMP framework through constrained at- tention in Phase 1 and unconstrained fine-tuning in Phase 2. Interestingly, we observe greater relative improvements for the smaller InternVL3-1B backbone compared to the larger Qwen2.5VL-3B. This is expected: larger models can al- ready capture complex functions, making constrained training less impactful, whereas smaller models benefit more from CAMP’s inductive bias. Due to com- pute limitations, we were unable to compare with larger-scale models. However, we emphasize that the proposed task is most relevant for home assistants and surveillance systems, where inference typically occurs on edge devices. For this reason, our study focuses on small- to medium-scale models, which are more practical for such applications.
