KDE bandwidth. The bandwidth is chosen for each storm, model and lead time by five-fold cross-validation over 100 values, from 13.9 km (half a grid cell) up to five times the ensemble spread, or 55.6 km if that is larger; the spread is the standard deviation of the member positions, averaged over the two axes. The lower bound matters because TempestExtremes places storm centres on grid points: a smaller bandwidth would put a separate peak on each grid point instead of describing the ensemble spread. Retained storms and averaging. A lead time enters T only if all three models have at least ten matched members at that time, and a storm is retained if it has at least five such times: 106 storms in 2021–2023 and 41 in 2025. All models are therefore scored on the same storms and times. Tables report the mean and standard deviation of the storm NLLs across retained storms, and pairwise win rates compare them storm by storm. Fair-CRPS and normalization. At a grid point with reference y and ensemble x1, . . . , xN, we use fCRPS(x, y) = 1 N X i |xi −y| − 1 2N(N −1) X i̸=j |xi −xj|. (3) The usual ensemble estimator divides the second term by 2N 2 and depends on ensemble size; the fair form estimates the score of the distribution the members are drawn from (Zamo & Naveau, 2017). Each variable is divided by its fixed ERA5 1979–2018 standard deviation, using the same normalization file in both cohorts. Scores are area-weighted within the evaluation domain and summed across the seven variables; this is the sum of marginal scores, not a joint multivariate score. At each lead, the plotted curves average across storms with all three models available. Model ratios divide these mean curves, rather than averaging storm-specific ratios. The field sample is not restricted to the storms retained for NLL. Intensity bias and calibration. For storm s and lead t, let vs,t,m be member m’s maximum wind within the local region and v∗ s,t the reference maximum. We average bs,t = N −1 P m vs,t,m −v∗ s,t across storms at each lead. The plotted percentage is 100 bt/v∗, where the denominator is the reference maximum averaged over the available storms and leads in that cohort (16.6 m/s for ERA5 and 19.5 m/s for HRES-fc0, rounded). It is not an average of storm-specific percentage errors. Shading gives one cross-storm standard deviation under the same scaling. Rank histograms count ensemble values strictly below the reference at each grid point, area-weight the rank frequencies, and average across storms and the displayed lead-time groups; ties are not randomized. Spatial masks. The reference is thresholded at its own percentile; all members of a model share one threshold, the same percentile of their pooled values, computed separately for each storm, variable, and footprint or lead time. This removes the model’s uniform intensity bias while keeping intensity differences between its members. A wind threshold at percentile α retains values above that percentile; pressure uses values below percentile 100 −α. Footprint percentiles range from 50 to 99; the fields at each lead time use the 90th percentile. Ensemble mask frequencies enter area-weighted Brier scores, with a separate score restricted to reference-positive pixels. Geometric scores compare each member’s mask with the reference (error) and every pair of members (spread), and are averaged over members or pairs, then storms. Hausdorff distance and G-score use kilometre distances from a local equirectangular projection of each storm’s region, so equal displacements count equally at all latitudes. Quantile masks remove uniform intensity shifts but do not separate spatial displacement from shape differences. Spatial metrics.
