Association selects intervention targets, while paired edits show whether a particular guard is actually sensitive. 6 Implications for evaluation design Our results suggest a minimum audit sequence for agent-safety evaluations. 1. Diagnose and stress transfer. Test capability-poor cues, then deduplicate the semantic unit and hold out environments, domains, templates, or sources. A strong result is a trigger, not a verdict. 2. Name the evaluation unit. Step-, interaction-, trajectory-, and task-weighted scores answer different questions. Report at least the unit named in the deployment claim. 3. Intervene after association. Use observational results only to select hypotheses. Change one cue while retaining authorization, capability, arguments, and effect; record possible semantic changes. 4. Use controls for stochastic guards. Interleave original, edited, and placebo calls; report block- probability differences and identical-input disagreement.
