The logit interaction is likewise inconclusive. 5.5 RQ3: COMPOUNDING DIFFICULTY Which combinations of scope, dispersion and reasoning depth are hardest, and how well does the ad- ditive GLM explain their scores? Figure 6 groups questions by within-task attribute terciles. Scores are centred on each task–LLM mean to account for baseline differences. We compare observed scores with out-of-fold predictions of Equation 4; negative residuals indicate lower scores than pre- dicted. This analysis is exploratory, with no formal hypothesis tests; its intervals are individual and not adjusted for the many cells compared. Appendix G.4 gives full cell estimates, task-sensitivity checks and three-attribute configurations. The results show that (1) several moderate demands can coincide with low scores, and (2) deep rea- soning over dispersed evidence is especially difficult in SciTrek. Questions low on both scope and dispersion score 3.6 pp above their task–LLM mean, while those high on both score 2.9 pp below it (Figure 6a). The gap remains 6.1–7.2 pp when any single task is removed. The additive GLM broadly tracks this pattern: eight of nine cell residuals lie within ±1.0 pp (Table 12). Likewise, the 164 questions above their task median on all three properties, with none exceeding its within- task 90th percentile, score 5.4 pp below average. Their residual is −1.5 pp (95% CI [−5.7, 2.6]; Table 10). Low scores can therefore occur when several demands coincide, even without an ex- treme attribute. Questions high on both dispersion and depth form the lowest-scoring cell (−8.0 pp; Figure 6c), with a residual of −3.2 pp (95% CI [−7.1, 0.6]; Table 12). SciTrek supplies 62% of these questions. Removing it reduces the high-minus-low depth gap at high dispersion from −6.7 to −0.8 pp (Figure 6d). The wide residual interval and sensitivity to one task make this a candidate pattern for further study. 9
