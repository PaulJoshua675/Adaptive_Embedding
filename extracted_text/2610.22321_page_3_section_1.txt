Joint Audio-Video Generation. Audio-video generation first developed through audio-driven video generation (Prajwal et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2025; Cui et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025) and video-to-audio generation (Zhang et al., 2026b; Cheng et al., 2025; Shan et al., 2025), where one modality is fixed. Joint audio-video generation (JAVG) removes this constraint: early works either learned a joint generative process (Ruan et al., 2023) or coordinated pretrained unimodal gener- ators (Xing et al., 2024). Native JAVG then advanced toward open-ended generation, finer syn- chronization, and richer audio content, as represented by JavisDiT (Liu et al., 2025), Ovi (Low et al., 2025), UniVerse-1 (Wang et al., 2025), and LTX-2 (HaCohen et al., 2026). JAVG has fur- ther reached industrial-scale deployment through systems such as Veo 3 (Google DeepMind, 2025), Seedance 2.0 (Seedance et al., 2026), and MiniMax H3 (MiniMax, 2026), enabling the generation of more complex audio-visual scenes with multi-event composition, multi-layer audio mixtures, and complex cross-modal relations, which makes audio-video consistency evaluation more demanding.
