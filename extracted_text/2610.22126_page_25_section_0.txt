SCENIC evaluation graph construction. Data. We started from the PBMC scRNA-seq dataset of Kang et al. [2018], comprising 8 lupus patients, with each patient’s cells split into IFN-β-stimulated and control samples. We used the raw UMI counts throughout the SCENIC steps. Quality control. We kept cells with more than 200 detected genes, fewer than 4,000 detected genes, and less than 15% mitochondrial reads. We kept genes with more than 250 total counts across all cells, which matches the SCENIC recommendation of one count in 1% of cells. The filtered matrix contained 24,562 cells × 6,941 genes. We restricted the data to six training patients (1244, 1016, 1039, 1256, 1488, and 107), comprising 17,448 cells, leaving patients 1015 and 101 out. Multi-seed SCENIC. GRNBoost2 is stochastic, so we ran pySCENIC ten times with seeds {0, 1, 3, 4, 5, 20, 42, 420, 1000, 2000}. Each run had three steps: 1. GRN inference: GRNBoost2 on the raw count matrix, with the hg38 list of human TFs as candidate regulators. Output: TF–target adjacencies weighted by importance. 2. Module creation and cisTarget pruning: co-expression modules were built with the default settings, so TF–target correlations use only cells where both genes are detected. Modules were then pruned by motif enrichment. We used two hg38 RefSeq-r80 mc9nr gene-based ranking databases (10 kb up/downstream of the TSS, and 500 bp up/100 bp down) and the motif-to-TF annotation motifs-v9-nr.hgnc-m0.001-o0.0. 3. Regulon filtering: we kept enriched motifs with NES greater than 3.0 and motif-similarity q-value less than 0.001. Surviving motifs were merged into one regulon per TF and context, which takes the union of their targets. Consensus network across seeds. Each seed’s regulons were flattened into directed TF-to-target edges. When a TF had several contexts, an edge kept its largest GRNBoost2 weight. Seeds agreed moderately: pairwise Jaccard similarity of the edge sets was 0.58 to 0.60. We chose the recurrence threshold from the edge-recurrence curve (Table H.3). An edge entered the consensus if it appeared in at least 8 of 10 seeds, and its weight is the median over the seeds that contained it. For each seed we scored regulon activity per cell with pyscenic aucell, using an AUC threshold of 0.10. That means the top 10% of each cell’s ranked genes; the median cell detects about 500 genes. A regulon was kept if (i) its TF is in the consensus network and (ii) AUCell scored it in at least 8 of 10 seeds. Its activity is the mean AUC over the seeds that scored it, and a seed that did not score it counts as missing rather than zero. The result is 155 regulons. Extraction of stimulation-responsive SCENIC regulons. For each cell type, SCENIC regulon activities, quantified using AUCell on the training replicates, were averaged within each replicate and contrasted between the stimulated and control groups. Regulons significant according to a one-sample t-test across replicates (BH-FDR < 0.05) were retained, and the 50 regulons with the largest |∆AUC| were selected.
