In LIBERO-PrefScale, we further provide no-preference data, unseen layouts, and different tasks to support in-domain evaluation, task/layout generalization, and preference-level generalization over different tasks in unseen layouts. We use LIBERO-PeRM to conduct an initial study of five key research topics in personalized manipulation (see bottom row of Figure 1): 1) how policies follow preferences in zero-shot settings; 2) how policies learn single preferences from demonstrations; 3) how efficiently policies acquire preferences as demonstrations increase; 4) how learned preferences generalize across layouts and tasks; and 5) how policies compose multiple preferences and behave under conflicts. These topics organize our experiments and highlight the core capabilities that robots must develop for reliable personalization. Our contributions and findings are as follows: • We introduce a controlled formulation and taxonomy of observable preferences in simulation, together with an automated pipeline that produces preference-conditioned demonstrations, rich metadata, and preference-satisfaction metrics. • We construct five task suites, LIBERO-SinglePref, LIBERO-PrefScale, LIBERO-PrefConflict, LIBERO-PrefCompose, LIBERO-ComprePref, comprising 312 preference-conditioned tasks with pipeline-generated, human-reviewed demonstrations and evaluation protocols for controlled per- sonalization studies. • Extensive experiments show that current VLA policies struggle with zero-shot personalization, while SFT makes preferences learnable. Mixed training improves no-preference task success relative to sequential adaptation and contextual-prompt task success and preference satisfaction relative to preference-only LoRA, but weakens motion-preference learning; broader layout diversity and matched no-preference data also help, while preference generalization, decomposition, and execution under conflicts remain difficult. 2
