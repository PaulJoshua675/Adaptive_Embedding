The order in brackets counts the rounds of attention between positions above layer ℓ, like hops of message passing: the context reaches the state only through the state itself (0), directly from the prefix (1), through one relay in the prefix (2), or without limit (∞). where u is the attention input at position t, ¯uk and ¯ak are the means of the attention input and output of layer k, and Gk is the ridge regression of the attention output on its input, fitted once for each layer on unlabelled calibration text. Because the transport keeps every remaining layer, LEXFRAME[0] is still a nonlinear reading that depends on the state, unlike a lens, and it lacks only what the remaining layers would gather from other positions. Order 1. LEXFRAME[1] lets the state attend to its prefix. The state at position t is transported as be- fore, but in every layer its attention is the layer’s own attention, computed over the prefix. The states of the prefix are themselves transported from layer ℓby LEXFRAME[0], so the frame withholds only the attention among the prefix positions after layer ℓ. The state attends with the layer’s own queries, keys and values, but each prefix position evolves in iso- lation. The reading is what the state draws from its prefix; the step from LEXFRAME[0] measures what this one round of attention contributes. Order 2. In LEXFRAME[2] the attention also reaches within the prefix: the prefix positions at- tend too, each with the layer’s own attention over its own prefix, which is transported by LEXFRAME[0]. Information can therefore travel two steps, from an earlier token through a prefix position to the state, a route that LEXFRAME[1] cannot represent. The step from LEXFRAME[1] to LEXFRAME[2] measures what this one exchange among the prefix positions con- tributes. In general LEXFRAME[n] attends to a prefix of states of order n −1; each order thus computes one more round of attention between positions, and reading at order n runs n + 1 streams through the remaining layers. Higher orders add exchanges within the prefix and converge to the model. Order ∞. In the limit of the order nothing is withheld, and LEXFRAME[∞] recovers the model as the native frame: every position attends to the true states of its prefix, which are the states that the model itself computes. Two identities follow: the reading of every layer in LEXFRAME[∞] equals the model’s output, and LEXFRAME[n] is exact wherever at most n layers remain. Near the output all orders therefore agree with the model; they differ where much of the computation lies ahead. The reading of LEXFRAME[∞] adds nothing to the model’s predic- tion; its value lies in its lexical readouts and basis, which every frame of finite order approximates. 4 Experiments We compare frames with lenses and point clouds, then test their coordinates and their use in steer- ing and attribution.
