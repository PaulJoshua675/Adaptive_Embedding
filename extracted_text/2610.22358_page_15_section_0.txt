Preprint B MODEL AND TRAINING This section specifies the backbone and the training recipe for 126M and 300M models, then examines distillation, calibration, and the architecture controls that support the design. It closes with model-size comparisons of forecast accuracy and inference cost, including results on FEV, FPS, TIME, and GIFT-Eval. The flow model has 125.1M parameters, and its distilled version has 126.3M including the PDD heads. B.1 BACKBONE AND INPUT Backbone configuration. The settings below describe the general-domain forecasting model; Section F gives the separate dynamical configuration. The backbone uses velocity prediction, ReLU activation, rotary temporal positions, and dynamic adaptive RMS normalization. The latter applies (1 + α) ⊙RMSNorm(h) + β, with scale and shift obtained from a shared time projection plus block-specific offsets. Modulation and velocity readouts start at zero; the learned state-entry gate starts at 0.1. Temporal and channel attention use query–key normalization. The Gaussian source scale is fixed at σ = 0.75. Both model sizes use width 768, 12 attention heads, and feed-forward width 3,072. The 126M and 300M backbones have 12 and 30 blocks, with two and ten flow-free entry blocks, respectively. The supervision mask excludes missing targets, known covariates, and final-patch padding. Flow-time sampling. Each training row uses one scalar flow time, shared across all channels and forecast steps. For each row and draw, we sample t = sigmoid(−0.8 + 0.8ϵ), ϵ ∼N(0, 1). The interpolated state and velocity target follow Equation (1), with this same t applied to the entire future. Four independent source/time draws share each history, with their losses averaged and normalized by the batch’s transported-coordinate count. At inference, all channels advance at a common flow time.
