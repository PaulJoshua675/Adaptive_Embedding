This objective integrates contrastive learning and knowledge distillation with separate sparsity constraints on query and document representations, encourag- ing efficient inverted-index retrieval while maintaining retrieval effectiveness. 4 Experiments Hardware Setup. Experiments were run on a workstation with Dual Intel Xeon Gold 6248R CPUs and a single NVIDIA A100 GPU (40GB). Query encoding (LLM inference) used the GPU, while sparse retrieval was performed on CPU with Pyserini and AVX-512. Training Details. Stage 2 training uses a learning rate of 1e−4, with task weights [1.0, 0.05, 0.04] corresponding to ranking, query regularization, and doc- ument regularization objectives. We use 16 negative samples per query and LoRA with rank r = 16. Training is performed for 1 epoch with a batch size of 28 and gradient accumulation over 4 steps, totaling approximately 4700 optimization steps per epoch. 4.1 Analysis of Truncation Strategies We adopt LLaMA-3.2-1B [6] with two-stage training: Stage 1 performs sparse alignment pre-training on in-domain data, and retrieval ablations are run on a 222 MB MS MARCO subset, monitoring query (Top-10) and document (Top-20) gradient statistics. To assess input-adaptive support selection, we compare our dynamic α-truncation with three fixed Top-K baselines (α-min, α-mean, α-max), whose K values are derived from the minimum, mean, and maximum observed support sizes under the cumulative-mass criterion across training samples. Top-Ratio measures the proportion of gradient magnitude concentrated on the top-K SPLADE-ranked terms, while Gradient Entropy reflects the dispersion of gradients across the vocabulary. For SPLADE++, these statistics are com- puted from ranked sparse output weights as a proxy for gradient-based analysis. Mechanism Analysis.
