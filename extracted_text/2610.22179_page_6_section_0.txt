Storing the sign–fraction byte unchanged adds eight bits per BF16 value. Excluding overflow, LCT uses finite prefix codes with mean length ¯ℓ= P t ptℓQ(t), including escape literals. The expected code length per BF16 value is 8 + ¯ℓ= 8 + H(P) + DKL(P∥eQ) −log2 ZQ (2) where eQ = (eqt)t is the probability distribution represented by the resulting code lengths, rather than the design distribution Q, and 0 < ZQ ≤1 is the codebook normalization constant. LCT’s cost consists of source entropy, mismatch to the code-induced distribution eQ, and unused prefix-code capacity −log2 ZQ. The heavy-tailed weights mean the Laplace and Power-Law distributions give the best compression ratio, close to the theoretical optimum. 5.1 Adaptation to magnitude scale Tensor magnitudes change during training which LCT handles by shifting the codebook to match the exponent center at runtime. The following propositions explain why this compensates for rescaling. Proposition 1 (Rescaling mostly shifts exponents) Ignoring rounding and finite-range effects, multiplying finite, nonzero values by a finite, nonzero constant shifts their exponents by a common integer, with at most a one-bin difference remaining after correction. For power-of-two scaling, the shift correction is exact. Proposition 2 (Rare boundary effects give a small correction) For the same rescaling, let A and B be the stored BF16 exponent bytes before and after scaling.
