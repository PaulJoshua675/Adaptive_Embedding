Step 5: Evaluation. Diarrhea was evaluated against a ground truth from independent manual chart review by domain experts, who labeled each patient-timepoint cell Yes (medication-associated diarrhea), No, or Deceased/Not applicable. This produced labels for 56 patients across the six timepoints (Table 1), giving 183 evaluable cells inside follow-up, of which 47 are labeled Yes. Metrics include accuracy, precision, recall, F1, Cohen’s κ, Matthews correlation, and per-timepoint confusion matrices. Annotation procedure. The ground truth was established by the Rett Center clinical team, who reviewed each patient’s notes within a timepoint window using the same labeling scheme. The same reviewers performed the multi-phenotype expert spot-check. Annotation followed a consensus-adjudication workflow with no formal written guideline and no retrospective inter-annotator agreement statistic, a limitation for gold-standard reliability detailed in Supplementary Note S6 (TRIPOD-LLM items 8a–8b) [25]. Phenotype definitions The 18 phenotypes were selected by the Rett Center clinical team as the adverse effects and disease-related functional domains most salient to monitor in routine trofinetide care, rather than an exhaustive enumeration. At each clinical visit, each phenotype is classified according to whether it is documented as present in that visit’s clinical note, not as a direction-of-change judgment. They span three categories. Adverse effects (4): Diarrhea, Vomiting, Weight Loss, and Other Side Effects, each a physiological reaction to trofinetide. The diarrhea phenotype, defined as medication-associated diarrhea, is the target of the ground-truth validation and error analysis. Medication refusal (1): Refusing Medication is tracked separately because it is a behavioral response, not a physiological reaction, and captures documented instances of the patient declining or resisting the medication. Rett clinical features (13): General Mood, Gross Motor, Fine Motor, Language/Communication, Stereotypy, Attention/Alertness, Sleep, Breathing/Hyperventilation, Bruxism, Dysautonomia, Aggressive Behavior, Anxiety, Seizures. These are manifestations of Rett syndrome itself, part of the natural history of the disorder that clinicians track over the course of treatment. They are not adverse effects, and no finding in this category should be read as an effect caused by trofinetide. Each phenotype is defined by a keyword list (Supplementary Table S2) and phenotype-specific LLM prompts encoding what constitutes a true positive. Generic terms matching routine documentation are excluded (e.g., “alert” is dropped from the attention/alertness list to avoid vital-sign notes).
