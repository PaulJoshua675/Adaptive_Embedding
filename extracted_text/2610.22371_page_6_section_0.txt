Data Usage. We distinguish two types of input frames: encoder frames, which are input to the encoder to extract scene tokens, and query frames, which provide local RGB patches for decoder queries. They share the same aspect ratio but may differ in resolution. Encoder frames are maximally center-cropped to one of five predefined aspect ratios and uniformly resized to the largest resolution within a 480 × 832 bound, while query frames generally retain their original resolution. To speed up training, we use EVAE to pre-encode all video clips into latents and discard the VAE during training. We also apply temporal augmentation to handle varying motion speeds. To this end, each raw video is subsampled into 49-frame clips with temporal intervals ranging from 1 to 4. During training, we randomly slice the first 21 ∼49 frames in steps of 4, and head-truncate the video latent accordingly. We also apply downsizing-only spatial augmentation to the query frames, so that the model sees a variety of query-patch fields of view. See Secs. B.1 and B.2 for more details about datasets and data preprocessing & sampling, respectively. Architecture. We evaluate two adaptation variants. The basic variant, denoted as ‘Ours’, leverages motion priors from a large-scale video generator, with a trainable rank-512 4D-LoRA, ∆W 512 4D . The double-LoRA variant, denoted as ‘Ours-II’, additionally incorporates geometry priors through the frozen rank-512 Depth-LoRA, ∆W 512 Depth (Sec. 3.3). Both variants have 0.70B trainable encoder parameters. We use an 8-block cross-attention decoder, which has 0.18B trainable parameters. Optimization. We train for 75K optimization steps using AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2017) on 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs. Gradient accumulation yields an effective batch size of 32 video clips. The learning rate is linearly warmed up to a peak of 10−4 over the first 2.5K steps and then follows a cosine decay schedule to 10−6. We apply a maximum gradient norm clipping of 10. 4.2 WORLD-COORDINATE TRACKING Evaluation Protocol. We evaluate the performance of 3D tracking on WorldTrack (Feng et al., 2025), which includes Aria Digital Twin (ADT), Dynamic Replica (DR), PointOdyssey (PO), and Panoptic Studio (PS) (Joo et al., 2015). We report results for All and Dynamic points on ADT, DR, and PO, and for all points on PS, whose evaluated tracks are entirely dynamic.
