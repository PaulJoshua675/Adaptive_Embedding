Under Review. 6 DOWNSTREAM VALIDATION OF IDEA SETS To validate that the ideas generated by our model are actually promising for researchers, we conduct two experiments: (a) human study with domain experts and, (b) execution-based evaluation using coding agents. 6.1 HUMAN STUDY: PORTFOLIO QUALITY AND DIVERSITY We conduct a pilot user study to evaluate if the ideas are usable, promising and unique to humans. To recruit users, we set a minimum criterion that they have a PhD in the field of NLP and at least four published academic papers in top-tier conferences. We onboard four participants for the task, that meet our established criteria. Each user gives us a preference for the topics within NLP/ML they are comfortable with (self-declared expertise), and we match them with a subset of queries from our test set that fall under those topics. Each user gets assigned 15 instances and they blind evaluate sets of ideas from three systems: Claude Opus 4.8, Qwen3-8B and our Proposed (no think) model. Overall, each user gives judgments on 45 sets of ideas. For each set of ideas, our experts make three judgments: (1) which ideas are worth pursuing as early- stage research directions (promising); (2) ignoring quality, the largest subset of ideas that pursue substantively different directions (unique); and (3) the same subset, restricted to ideas marked worth pursuing (Unique Promising). Two ideas count as the same direction when carrying out one would largely address the other. Generally, a higher score is better for all these metrics. Appendix E breaks these results down by topics of the target paper and users. 6.2 CAN IDEAS BE EXECUTED? Having established the quality and diversity of ideas in the previous sections, we check if the gen- erated ideas hold up when carried towards execution. This is like a held-out quality check for the model trained on the impact signal. To test this, we take 15 instances and execute sets of 3 ideas from Claude Opus 4.8 and our no-think and think models. Every idea frozen into a card independently with the source hidden and handed to a Claude Code agent (Claude Opus 5) in a fixed sandbox with a 90-minute, $30 budget and no GPU, identical for every idea. The agent designs and runs a proof-of-concept test for early signal on the claim, then self-reports one verdict: supports, refutes or, inconclusive.
