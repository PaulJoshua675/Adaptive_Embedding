On a scale-invariant block, Prop. 3 shows that the induced quotient update is an exact great-circle step, and Thm. 3 and Cor. 2 show that p(r) ∝r2 is the unique isotropic radial preconditioner that removes dependence on the arbitrary parameter gauge. In this setting, the squared-scale factor makes the update take the same angular step at every rescaling of the parameter vector. To make the optimizer rule precise, let R ∈GL(Θ) be an invertible rescaling of a finite-dimensional real inner- product parameter space. Suppose corresponding gradients satisfy qg = R−⊤q, as the gradients do when a scalar objective is invariant under θg = Rθ. Consider preconditioned displacements u := −ηPq and ug := −ηP gqg with the same dimensionless learning rate η. Proposition 2 (Covariance law for preconditioned updates). Under the preceding setup, ug = Ru for every q if and only if P g = RPR⊤. (E.6) The proof is given in App. G.4. Carrying squared parameter units is both necessary and sufficient for the preconditioner to produce a covariant displacement. The same distinction applies to nonlinear direction maps. A map D from gradients to displacements is unit- covariant precisely when the transformed direction map satisfies Dg(R−⊤q) = RD(q). A duality map satisfies this law only when the resulting full displacement, including any scalar step, co-transforms with the parameters. For example, under a scalar rescaling R = cI, a scale-zero-homogeneous orthogonalization obeys D(c−1q) = D(q), whereas displacement covariance requires cD(q). Such an update becomes typed only after supplying an explicit primal-scale factor or an equivalently transformed scalar step. Two immediate consequences clarify the operator law. Ordinary Euclidean SGD with a fixed common learning rate satisfies that law for every gradient only when RR⊤= I. A scalar parameter rescaling R = cI instead requires the transformed learning rate ηg = c2η. The preconditioner law in Prop. 2 is an operator statement. For the common case of a scale-invariant parameter block and a scalar radial preconditioner, the following proposition and theorem show that the quotient update is an exact geodesic step. These results also show that the squared parameter scale is the unique radial scalar correction that makes that step independent of the arbitrary parameter gauge. Let V be a finite-dimensional real inner-product space of dimension at least two and let J : V \ {0} →R be continuously differentiable and scale-invariant, J(cw) = J(w) for every c > 0. Writing g(w) := ∇J(w), differentiation with respect to w and to c gives g(cw) = c−1g(w), ⟨w, g(w)⟩= 0. (E.7) Fix a > 0, η > 0, and a scalar radial preconditioner p : (0, ∞) →(0, ∞). Writing r := ∥w∥, consider w+ := aw −ηp(r)g(w). (E.8) Proposition 3 (Exact geometry of a radial quotient update). Under the preceding setup, at every nonstationary point, with ¯w := w/∥w∥and ¯g := g(w)/∥g(w)∥, the canonical update is exactly w+ ∥w+∥= cos ϑ ¯w −sin ϑ¯g, tan ϑ = ηp(r)∥g(w)∥ ar , 0 < ϑ < π 2 . Under the assumptions and notation of Prop. 3, the following three conditions are equivalent: For every scale-invariant J, every nonstationary w, and every c > 0, the updates from w and cw have the same canonical direction; the raw update is equivariant, (cw)+ = cw+, for every such J, w, c; and the preconditioner has quadratic covariance, p(cr) = c2p(r) for all c, r > 0.
