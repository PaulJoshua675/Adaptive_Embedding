7 Relation to prior work RLBench supports learning and evaluation across manipulation tasks [1], while ALFRED [3] and CALVIN [4] connect natural-language instructions to grounded sequences or policies. CLIPort [5], PerAct [6], SayCan [7], and RT-1 [8] further combine language with perception, skill selection, or action prediction. Our experiment examines a complementary upstream question: how reliably task labels can be retrieved from alternative written instructions when the reference gallery itself varies. The study compares established sparse and low-rank retrieval methods [9–12] under a shared, randomized reference-gallery protocol. Sentence-BERT [13] offers a natural follow-up for assessing semantic matching beyond token overlap. A script for all-MiniLM-L6-v2 is included in the reproducibility package; neural sentence-embedding scores are not part of the reported results. 7.1 Audit checks on scoring Exact cross-task duplicates are rare in this selected corpus, but their treatment should be visible. Over 80 draws, mean strict top-1 accuracy is 60.9% for character TF–IDF and 55.4% for BM25. Scoring either source label as correct for a duplicated phrase gives 61.0% and 55.6%, respectively; excluding duplicated query texts gives 61.3% and 55.6%. Thus the principal gap is not an artefact of dropping the switch instruction. Both mistakes collapse related actions despite distinctive wording. 8 Discussion and conclusion The findings characterize written instruction matching on 296 fixed descriptions from 62 RLBench task files. The subset omits tasks with dynamic instruction generation, and queries consist of benchmark-authored paraphrases rather than independently collected operator requests. The repeated-gallery design captures sensitivity to indexed wording; extending it to all task definitions, pretrained embeddings, and embodied task execution would test how these patterns transfer to broader settings. Two findings stand out. First, reference wording can alter the ordering of otherwise unchanged retrieval methods, making repeated gallery selections valuable for comparisons of short robot instructions. Second, adding one more reference description improves retrieval even when the evaluated queries stay fixed. The duplicate light-switch instruction further shows why label ambiguity deserves an explicit scoring policy. Together, these observations provide an empirical starting point for studying the language interface to larger robot-task libraries.
