Surrogate-gradient learning has enabled deep SNNs to achieve competitive performance on vision benchmarks [16,28], while Batch Normalization Through Time (BNTT) improves training stability and reduces inference latency [8]. However, continual learning in SNNs remains un- derexplored [14]. Replay-based approaches have only recently been adapted to SNNs, first through standard experience replay to mitigate catastrophic forget- ting [18], and more recently through compact latent replay to reduce storage and computation costs in neuromorphic systems [4]. To the best of our knowl- edge, no prior work has used explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) to guide SNN learning and mitigate catastrophic forgetting, despite its effectiveness in artificial neural networks (ANNs) [19]. In this work, we investigate whether temporally grounded saliency signals in SNNs can guide memory-efficient replay. Specifically, we adapt EPR [23] to deep SNNs by replacing conventional ANN saliency mechanisms with Spike Activation Maps (SAM) [9], which produce forward-only explanations from inter-spike in- terval dynamics. Using BNTT-trained VGG9 SNNs [8] operating at low latency (25 timesteps) on Split CIFAR10 and Split CIFAR100 CL benchmarks, we show that saliency-guided replay can be effectively transferred to the spiking domain, improving performance under equal memory budgets and reducing forgetting in low-memory regimes. 2
