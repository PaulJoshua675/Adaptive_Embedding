The student is CLIP ViT-L/14, also frozen, with a trainable three-layer MLP head (GELU, LayerNorm, 1024- dimensional output) as the only component that learns. Its input is four frames taken at −0.1, 0, +0.1, and +0.2 s around the annotated impact, each encoded independently and concatenated with their successive differences. Training minimizes a symmetric InfoNCE loss [van den Oord et al., 2018] between the projected visual embedding and the teacher’s audio embedding (τ = 0.07), with batch size 256 and AdamW [Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019] at a learning rate of 10−4. Because the space of plausible configurations is large, we fixed the feature variant and the number of epochs on a validation split drawn from 20% of the training videos (which selected the four frames + deltas input, trained for two epochs) and used the test split for no selection, reporting three seeds. One detail of the data handling carries more weight than it may appear to. We use the dataset’s video- level split throughout and train the head on training-split impacts only (22,793 of 28,444 impacts, from 780 of 975 videos), because the teacher’s own embeddings predict Q at ρ = 0.73: any impact seen during distillation has effectively had its target leaked, even though no Q label is ever used. 6.2 How Much of the Gap Is Temporal? Before introducing audio at all, we ask whether a single frame is the right visual input. A struck object deforms and recoils, and how quickly that motion subsides is what damping describes.
