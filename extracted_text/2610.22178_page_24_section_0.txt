We discard static and near-zero-variance recordings, including episodes whose follower arm never moves, a failure mode Qwen-RobotManip reports for ∼81% of RoboMIND’s UR subset. • Sudden jumps. We fit a smooth trend to each channel with median and Savitzky–Golay filters, and flag a frame when its residual and its acceleration or jerk exceed a per-channel threshold, the larger of 0.05 in raw units and four times the pooled 95th percentile. Grippers are exempt. Whole episodes are removed only from InternData-A1, where simulator collisions cause the jumps (6.3% of its episodes). • Action representation. Rotations are harmonised to one Euler parameterisation; other conventions are kept as the source provides them. Each action dimension is mapped to [−1, 1] from its per-dataset 1st and 99th percentiles (Wang et al., 2026a), which removes scale differences across embodiments while preserving relative motion within each source. • Convention correction. We fix per-dataset sign conventions that would otherwise break cross-dataset consistency (e.g., a sign-flipped elbow joint in InternData); differing scales and encodings are then absorbed by the per-dataset normalisation. B.3 SAMPLING WEIGHTS During pretraining, each dataset d is sampled with probability proportional to F 0.5 d L0.5 d V 0.35 d (Ta- ble 2). Fd is its number of frames, and Ld and Vd are its language and vision diversity, each divided by its median over the datasets. Datasets from the same source are grouped before weighting. Both diversities are the effective rank of a set of L2-normalised embeddings, 2H(p) with pi = σ2 i / P j σ2 j , where σi are the singular values of the embedding matrix. The embeddings are not centred, so that a dataset with two distinct instructions does not collapse to rank one. For the language diversity, the rows are the SigLIP (Zhai et al., 2023) text embeddings of the distinct episode task strings, each scaled by the square root of the fraction of frames it covers. For the vision diversity, the rows are the DINOv3 (Siméoni et al., 2025) class tokens of every 15th frame of the main external camera, at most 50,000 frames per dataset. The RoboCOIN and InternData-A1 collections are further weighted by 0.5, and the weights are capped so that no dataset is seen more than four times. B.4 EGOCENTRIC DATA Sources. The three egocentric sources differ in capture conditions: EgoDex is seated tabletop recordings on Apple Vision Pro, EgoVerse head-mounted rigs, and Xperience in-the-wild moving- camera footage. Frame rates range from 20 to 30 Hz and are left unaltered; the differences are absorbed by the per-dataset quantile normalisation of §5.1. Action target. We follow the layout of Qwen-VLA’s egocentric target (Wang et al., 2026a): per hand, a 6-dimensional wrist motion as translation and axis-angle rotation, and 10 articulation coefficients, 32 dimensions in total. Unlike Qwen-VLA, which expresses the wrist relative to its own initial pose, we express it in the observation camera frame through the rigid transform T −1 camTwrist. The articulation coefficients are the first 10 principal components (“eigengrasps”) of the finger-joint configuration under a PCA basis shared across our three sources. The target is defined relative to the frame that opens each action chunk, so it is composed at load time from the stored world-frame wrist poses and per-frame camera poses rather than precomputed. State and pacing. The egocentric data have no proprioceptive state, and the policy receives a learned null state in its place. Human hands move faster than a teleoperated robot, so the human actions are slowed down in time (Yuan et al., 2026). Waypoint i of a chunk is the hand pose at time iρ/f after the observation, where f is the frame rate of the recording and ρ ≤1 a per-source pace (Table 2). The pose is interpolated between the two nearest frames. A one-second chunk at ρ = 0.25 thus holds a quarter of a second of human motion. 24
