No single new model clears the earlier reference by the margin we set in advance; the seven fused with reading position do, and still trail a 3-billion-parameter model trained on reader highlights alone by 0.042 average precision. A larger backbone scores higher on the pages it was selected on; on fresh pages its advantage could not be confirmed, and in an exploratory split the gains of the new models over the previous one sit on familiar domains. Related work. Distilling a large model’s rankings into a small one is established in retrieval, where a 440-million-parameter student of ChatGPT’s rankings outperformed a 3-billion-parameter supervised ranker [8], in the tradition of knowledge distillation [4], and a language model has outperformed crowd workers on several annotation tasks [3]. Readers’ highlights carry information about their comprehension and interests [13], and in our earlier work, asking a model which sentences a reader would highlight agreed with readers less than asking it which sentences to keep [10]. What we add is a measured comparison, on naturally accumulated reader behaviour, of the two levers a platform can pull: a stronger teacher, or more of its own readers’ data. 2 Setting Pages, readers and labels. We use public highlights on one social highlighting platform. A page’s text is split into sentences and each reader’s highlights are mapped onto them; a page enters an evaluation set with at least 6 readers and 30 sentences. Following our earlier protocol [12], the readers of a page are split in half 40 times; on each split the sentences half B marked most (up to round(0.15N) of a page’s N sentences, only sentences half B marked, ties broken at random) are the positives, every model’s ranking of the page’s sentences is scored by average precision (AP) against them, and half A’s counts give a split-half reference (how well half the readers predict the other half). A page’s AP is the mean over its splits, and a set’s AP the mean over its pages. Differences are paired by page with 95% intervals from a bootstrap that resamples domains (4,000 draws) [2]; they reflect the sampling of pages, not variation between training runs. Families of comparisons are Holm-adjusted [6]. Sets. The development set is our earlier 217-page evaluation set, re-extracted under the rules below; 215 pages over 84 domains pass the gates. The training pages are the 819 pages of the August model with their readers re-extracted under the same rules (801 keep at least one reader). Fresh pages are pages that never entered any earlier training or evaluation set, by page identity, URL and text near-duplication. The fresh pages with 3–5 readers form a second training set (3,160 pages); those with at least 6 readers and fetchable public text form the test set (131 pages over 42 domains). Near-duplicates of test pages are kept out of the second training set. The test set was sealed when it was written and read once, after the candidates were frozen. Only highlights public under the platform’s own rule, recorded before a fixed cutoff, were read. What was fixed in advance. The comparisons, the margins that decide each of them, the rules that choose the candidates for the test set and the three comparisons on it were registered before any of them was run. At the test set’s reader depth the smallest difference it could detect (0.0247) exceeds half the effect it was meant to confirm (0.0179), a condition we fixed in advance, so every number on the test set is reported as a description, not a confirmatory test. 2
