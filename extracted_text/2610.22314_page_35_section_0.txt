Sessions. Each patient meets the counsellor LM for three sessions, each with at most 30 turns (Figure 8). The pa- tient opens each session with its scenario’s opening mes- sage. The patient model is DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at temper- ature 1.0 with thinking on, and the same model compacts the memory (at most 30 entries) and writes each later sce- nario. All runs use seed 0. Counsellor LM. Every counsellor LM receives the benchmark’s generic system prompt (Figure 13). All run at temperature 1.0 with their provider’s defaults. Judge and score. Every counsellor reply is judged by GPT-6.1-Sol with the MHQR evaluation prompt (reason- ing high), which sees the counsellor’s system prompt as it was sent, including the memory section, the conversation so far and the reply, and returns a severity and issue flags. A session’s MHQR is the mean severity weight of its responses (0, 1, 5, 10); the headline table reports mean MHQR over all 150 patient × session scores and the distribution of session scores, with whiskers at the 5th and 95th percentiles. Intervals are 95% bootstraps that resample patients with all their sessions (2,000 draws), since a patient’s sessions are not independent. G.1 Additional results Severity over a session. Severity rises over the first ten turns and then levels off (Figure 9): the mean severity weight goes from 1.46 in turns 1–4 to 3.72 in turns 11–20, and rises for all 18 counsellors. The increase comes mostly from guidance calibration, as counsellors keep exploring once the patient expects direction: under-guiding is flagged in 12% of replies in the first two turns and in about 70% from turn 9 on, and over-exploring follows the same course. Formulaic responding grows steadily, from 2% of replies in the first two turns to 26% in turns 19–20, and missed shared context from 6% to 14%. 35
