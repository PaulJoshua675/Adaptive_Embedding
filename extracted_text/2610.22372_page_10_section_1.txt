In this work, we introduce LIBERO-PeRM, a comprehensive benchmark for personalized robotic manipulation. By treating preferences as a first-class building block alongside tasks and layouts, LIBERO-PeRM provides five task suites that isolate and evaluate preference learning under single- preference, scaling, conflict, composition, and comprehensive preference reasoning settings. Exper- iments show that current VLA policies struggle with zero-shot personalization, while SFT makes preferences learnable. Compared with sequential adaptation, mixed training better preserves no- preference behavior and improves contextual-prompt robustness, but it weakens motion-preference performance relative to preference-only LoRA. Preference learning benefits more from layout diver- sity and same-layout no-preference data than denser demonstrations, but generalization to unseen layouts and different tasks remains difficult. Composition is only partially observed, decomposition fails, and conflicts often degrade execution instead of producing resolution. Limitations and Future Work. LIBERO-PeRM instantiates predefined, language-expressible, auto- matically measurable preferences in simulation. It therefore excludes implicit preference inference, interaction history, non-linguistic signals, and open-ended or evolving preferences. Its generated demonstrations may contain pipeline- and skill-library-specific biases despite randomized initial states, multiple missions per preference, and human review. The current composition suite combines only two preferences, and the conflict suite does not test conflict detection or clarification. Finally, the human survey provides only initial validation; no real-robot or longitudinal user study is included. Future work could extend LIBERO-PeRM in directions ranging from three-or-more-preference settings to non-linguistic preference signals, human-in-the-loop preference acquisition, preference weighting and when-to-ask decisions, and real-robot evaluation. 10
