Figure 3 shows the cumulative ASR trajectory for each configuration on both targets. The curves separate primarily in their asymptotic ceilings rather than in convergence speed: all configurations show comparable initial slopes, but the ablated versions plateau at 88–94% while full REPRO reaches 98–100%. The log-rank test confirms that no configuration comparison reaches significance after Bonferroni correction on either target. The one near-significant result is −Failure Analysis on GPT-4o-mini (𝜒2 = 4.53, 𝑝= 0.033, Bonferroni 𝑝= 0.133), and it points in the opposite direction from what an efficiency advantage would suggest: full REPRO solves behaviors later in the iteration sequence than −Failure Analysis, reflecting the per-iteration overhead of failure classification on a target where refusal patterns are weak. The lower median iterations for some ablated configurations (1–2 vs. 3 for full REPRO) reflect a selection-bias artifact: ablated versions solve fewer hard behaviors, and the hard behaviors that only full REPRO solves are precisely those requiring many iterations. On the full 50-behavior distribution (with failures assigned iteration 101), Cliff’s 𝛿is negligible for all comparisons (|𝛿| ≤ 0.16), confirming no genuine convergence-speed difference.
