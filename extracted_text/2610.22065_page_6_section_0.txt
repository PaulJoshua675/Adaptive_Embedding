Figure 8 shows that at 𝜎=0.20, the block-averaged attention drift is 1.272 for NAT and 1.096 for NAT+KD, but only 0.181 for ABNAT—an 85.77% reduction relative to NAT. ABNAT suppresses drift most strongly in the late blocks, where CIM noise causes the largest deviations. Together with the observed correlation between mean attention drift and accuracy degradation in Fig. 3, these results sup- port that ABNAT improves robustness by stabilizing clean attention routes rather than relying only on output-level supervision. Accuracy–Training Cost Tradeoff Figure 10 evaluates structured block-selective variants against ABNAT with full alignment; here, S contains all heads in the selected blocks. Since attention alignment is training-only, all variants retain the same inference cost as NAT. Under the same four-block budget, Last4 outperforms First4 and Middle4, confirming that late blocks are more effective alignment targets. Accuracy improves and then saturates as the aligned late- block budget increases from Last1 to All. Last4 achieves 79.68% average accuracy, only 0.41 percentage points below full alignment at 80.09%, while reducing training-time overhead relative to NAT from 82.9% to 40.8% and peak-memory overhead from 54.4% to 23.3%. Thus, selective late-block alignment preserves nearly all robustness gains at substantially lower training cost. Head-Budget Sensitivity Head-selective alignment improves smoothly with budget: average accuracy rises from 78.24% to 78.90% and 79.44% as top-𝑘increases from 3 to 6 and 9 heads. At fixed 𝑘= 6, high-drift heads achieve 78.90% average accuracy, outperforming low-drift heads at 77.01% by 1.89 percentage points. These results show that ABNAT is not sensitive to one particular budget and validate the sensitivity-guided selection.
