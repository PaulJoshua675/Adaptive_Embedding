Preprint A NEGATIVE AND HIGHER-ORDER COALITION EFFECTS This section formalizes two mechanisms behind coalition-dependent action credit: competition un- der finite downstream capacity and misalignment between the criterion that determines selection and the value credited to selected candidates. The analysis is deliberately abstract and is not intended as an exact model of the production serving stack. Consider the simplified top-N set value Rtop(C) = X a∈TopNσ(Dedup(C)) v(a), (16) where σ(a) determines selection and v(a) ≥0 is the value credited to a selected candidate. For an action coalition S, let C(S) = Merge({Ci : qi ∈S}) and define ∆top i (S) = Rtop(C(S ∪{qi})) −Rtop(C(S)). (17) The superscript distinguishes this static abstraction from the production utility Rt in Section 3.1. Selection–value alignment excludes negative insertion marginals. Assume that σ, together with a fixed tie-breaking rule, induces a total order ≻σ that is co-monotone with credited value: a ≻σ b =⇒ v(a) ≥v(b). (18) Then Rtop is non-decreasing under candidate insertion, and hence ∆top i (S) ≥0 for every action i and coalition S ⊆A \ {qi}. Proof. It suffices to consider inserting one candidate a into a deduplicated pool. If a is a duplicate, the value is unchanged. If fewer than N positions are occupied, the change is v(a) ≥0. Otherwise, a enters the selected set only by displacing its lowest-ranked selected member b. This requires a ≻σ b, so co-monotonicity gives v(a) ≥v(b) and the value change is v(a) −v(b) ≥0. Inserting all candidates contributed by one action as a sequence of such operations proves the result. □ Finite capacity still induces higher-order interaction. This result rules out negative first-order insertion marginals under its alignment assumptions, but it does not imply additivity. Once the available positions are saturated, an action can have substantial value in a small coalition and little or no additional value in a larger coalition. For example, with N = 1 and two non-duplicate candidates of values 10 and 9, aligned selection gives Rtop({a}) = 10, Rtop({b}) = 9, and Rtop({a, b}) = 10, so the second-order interaction is 10 −10 −9 + 0 = −9. Relation to the auction-replay reference. Equation equation 16 is a sufficient-condition abstrac- tion rather than a literal reconstruction of the auction replay in Eq. equation 5. In the replay system, coalition insertion can change the winner/send set through ranking, auction, capacity, and other re- playable constraints. Negative first-order marginals are therefore possible whenever the induced selection logic is not perfectly aligned with the value credited by the replay reference. Even under perfect alignment, finite capacity is sufficient to produce redundancy and negative higher-order in- teractions. We consequently do not attribute an observed negative marginal or interaction to a unique serving mechanism without an additional intervention.
