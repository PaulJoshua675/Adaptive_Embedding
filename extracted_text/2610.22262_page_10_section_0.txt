Gradient-kurtosis error improves on average but on only 68 of 168 fields. 6.7 Qualitative interpretation of persistence improve- ments To reduce retrospective example-selection bias, we first ranked the 168 fields using conventional fidelity alone. For each field, we con- verted the absolute PSNR difference and the relative speed-MAE and speed-RMSE differences between the final model and the pre- trained CNN to percentile ranks across fields, and defined conven- tional closeness as the maximum of the three ranks, so that a field counts as close only if it is close under all three measures. The 34 closest fields (20% of the benchmark) form the primary near- tie cohort; no topology measure entered this selection. Only after freezing the cohort did we examine topology, requiring simultane- ous improvement in dB, W2,∞, and W2,2 and ranking eligible fields by the smallest of the three relative improvements. All 34 fields in the cohort improve on the pretrained CNN under all three PD dis- tances, as do all fields in a stricter 17-field and a broader 51-field co- hort. We show five predeclared fields from this cohort (Samples 78, 71, 80, 63, and 69), selected before image inspection to span strong topology consensus, very high conventional closeness, large bottle- neck gains, and different positions in the near-tie ranking. Relative to the control, the final model lowers dB by 28.7–50.2%, W2,∞by 31.5–37.2%, and W2,2 by 34.0–39.7% on all five fields, while MT gains range from 0.6% (Sample 69) to 15.7% (Sample 71). Figure 6 shows Sample 78, the principal example. Its RMSE is 1.58 for the final model and 1.62 for the pretrained CNN, while the control attains the lowest pointwise error (RMSE 1.34). Despite near-identical conventional error, the final model lowers W2,2, W2,∞, and dB relative to the CNN by 34%, 32%, and 38%, whereas the control changes them by +2%, +2%, and −13%. The persistence- diagram overlays show why: the final model’s diagram extends into the long-lived region occupied by the ground truth, while the CNN and control diagrams collapse toward the diagonal. The sur- vival curve, which counts pairs whose persistence exceeds a thresh- old, confirms that the final model better matches the ground truth’s long-lived tail. A reconstruction is not preferable merely because it contains more pairs, since additional pairs can also represent unsupported fragmentation; the distances above, computed on the complete diagrams, measure agreement in birth–death coordinates rather than feature count. 7 DISCUSSION 7.1 What the objective is measuring We read the loss study as separating two kinds of signal; what follows is an interpretation consistent with the results rather than an established mechanism. Gradient supervision changes the dis- tribution of sharp transitions in the predicted field, counteracting the smoothing introduced by standard fine-tuning. This is consis- tent with restoring moderately and highly persistent features whose birth–death coordinates strongly influence the accumulated Wasser- stein distances. Gradient supervision does not, however, constrain where critical values lie or what their values are, and its unfavor- able MT effect suggests that sharper fields can merge components in a different order. Fixed-pair supervision does constrain values, at both endpoints of every supervised ground-truth pair, includ- ing saddle-side endpoints, and it constrains the persistence of those pairs. This plausibly explains why it improves worst-case PD mis- match and MT together. Fixed-pair supervision still leaves important structure unsuper- vised. It fixes which ground-truth vertices are constrained but not which features the prediction actually forms there. Unlike re- matched diagram losses [19], it cannot penalize spurious pairs that the prediction creates elsewhere, and like all of our losses it does not constrain ancestry or branch assignment. These gaps may con- tribute to residual PD disagreement, and they do not address the hierarchy measured by MT; MT worsens on 59 of the 150 fields whose PD distances all improve relative to the CNN. The fixed- pair formulation includes supervision at saddle-side endpoints and improves MT on average, but because LCV and Lpers were always combined, the source of this improvement is not isolated; hierarchy itself remains outside the objective. The visually small field-space differences between the fine-tuned models do not imply that the PD result is meaningless. Persistence coordinates can change through localized shifts in critical values even when global pixelwise differences remain small. At the same time, a denser diagram or a lower PD score alone would not estab- lish scientific improvement. The matched control, agreement across three PD distances with different emphases, coordinate-level dia- gram overlays, the independent validation of the PD computation, and concurrent gains in several domain-structure metrics together provide evidence that the auxiliary objective is not merely increas- ing feature count or exploiting a single evaluation measure.
