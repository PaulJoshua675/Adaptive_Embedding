Specifically, we evaluate our linear adapter against three variants: a gated adapter, the Parcae adapter (Prairie et al., 2026), which multiplies the recurrent state with a learned per-channel factor within (0, 1) so that the hidden state remains bounded across recurrences, and no adapter at all. Our abla- tions show that, among the adapter variants, the linear adapter yields the best performance, whereas the gated adapter and the Parcae adapter score below the variant without an adapter. Furthermore, we show that at equal compute, a larger recurrent block results in better performance. We also show that distillation from the pretrained model improves recovery training and increases the performance of the retrofitted recurrent model. Finally, we find that a model trained at a fixed depth only works at the depth it was trained at, whereas a model trained with sampled recurrence works at other depths, but its performance does not improve beyond the trained depth. We also find that sharing the KV cache across recurrences during inference costs accuracy, which contrasts with the results reported for a model pretrained with recurrence (Geiping et al., 2025). We summarize our contributions as follows: (1) We introduce the first prune-then-loop retrofit of a pretrained VLM evaluated on VQA, supported by a three-stage curriculum that repairs the pruned language backbone and restores projector alignment using the base model’s vision-language training data. (2) We demonstrate that retrofitting recurrence directly into a pretrained VLM outperforms the alternative of adding a vision encoder to a pretrained recurrent language model. (3) We perform an extensive evaluation of the retrofitted recurrent VLM and quantify the impact of each design decision. 2
