These capabilities have motivated retrieval- augmented MT systems that provide bilingual ex- amples, idiom definitions, terminology, or support- ing documents to further support and improve trans- lation quality (Li et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025b). However, recent studies (Sun et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2023) have shown that retrieval is not uni- formly beneficial for translation. Existing retrieval- augmented MT pipelines typically apply a prede- fined retrieval strategy: retrieve for every input with a heuristic, similarity-based matching rule. This overlooks a fundamental gap in LLM-based translation: for some inputs, the model’s paramet- ric knowledge is already sufficient, while for oth- ers, external evidence such as idiom interpretations, canonical entities, technical terms, or background knowledge can be crucial. Moreover, heuristic, similarity-based retrieval can provide no help and even introduce noisy or misleading context that distracts the model and degrades an otherwise cor- rect translation (Sun et al., 2026). For example, a semantically close document may merely repeat query terms without providing actionable knowl- edge, while a more distant one may contain ex- actly the idiom interpretation, entity convention, or background knowledge the translation requires.
