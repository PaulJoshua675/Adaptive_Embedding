Robustness, calibration, and the application context On-board full-val mIoU is 29.88 with a 95% bootstrap CI of [29.61, 30.15] at the frame level and [28.58, 31.11] scene- clustered; board-versus-reference comparisons are paired on identical frames. Splitting by scene description gives day 30.26 (4449 frames), rain 29.80 (1088), night 16.76 (602; rain and night overlap on rainy nights). Rain costs almost nothing; night halves accuracy. The clip threshold is not the sensitive part: sweeping C ∈{16, 24, 32, 48, 64} on the night subset moves mIoU by at most 0.7 (16.4 at C=16 vs 15.7 at the deployed C=32), so the clipping recipe is robust to the lighting shift; night loss traces to the backbone. Injecting camera-artifact perturbations before the back- bone (matched INT8; baseline 28.9 on the same first-1000- frame subset, differing from the full-set value): Gaussian blur σ=1/2/4 gives 13.8/7.9/3.5 mIoU; exposure gain 0.5×/2× gives 25.2/23.4; sensor noise gives 18.2. Blur dominates, halving accuracy at one pixel of spread; moder- ate exposure change is nearly free; none of these can change the amount of work on the fixed-work path. Calibration drift is probed by perturbing the rig pitch on all cameras (stale static tables relative to the images): 0.1/0.2/0.5 degrees cost nothing measurable and 1.0 de- gree costs 0.1 mIoU against the 28.9 baseline; tables regen- erate offline on recalibration. As for where the remaining accuracy loss lives: of all bicycle ground-truth voxels, the board predicts 49.2% free and 29.5% background surfaces, keeps 5.7% as bicycle, and scatters the remainder across driveable surface (6.5%), veg- etation, and small movable-class confusions: thin structures are erased toward free space, a recall problem quantization amplifies (on-board VRU recall: pedestrian 14.8%, motor- cycle 12.3%, bicycle 5.7%; occupied-any 43.8% mean). The fallback is priced directly: ego-warping the previous on-board prediction by one and two 2 Hz keyframes (500 ms steps, five times the staleness a 100 ms miss produces, con- servative upper bounds forced by the dataset’s keyframe rate): pedestrian recall falls 14.8 →11.6 →8.1%, bicy- cle 5.7 →4.3 →3.8%, occupied-any recall on pedestrian voxels 42.5 →39.7 →37.0%. The expected delivered pedestrian recall under the enforced 100 ms budget is R ≈ (1 −pmiss)Rfresh + pmissRstale with pmiss = 8.8×10−4: a degradation of about 0.003 percentage points in expecta- tion, under an independence assumption between spike tim- ing and scene content. Discussion, Limitations, and Conclusion This work is a deployment result, not a new occupancy backbone: a camera occupancy network becomes easier to certify when algorithm, accelerator mapping, and runtime policy all expose fixed work. The latency guarantee is lay- ered, and only one layer is a bound: PL compute is cycle- bounded analytically; inference latency is a measured en- velope whose maxima are sample-size-dependent; delivery is structurally guaranteed, possibly with a stale-flagged fall- back. Raw miss rates span tenfold across enforced runs and raw maxima wander 125.2 to 139.8 ms, yet every frame de- livered within the deadline plus the envelope of Sec. 4.3. That envelope is itself measured: the enforcement timer is a host thread, and the PL-side timer that would remove its wake tail is not implemented. A back-to-back run after prior host activity misses more often than clean boot (0.671 vs 0.070%), drift that runtime monitoring must catch. The timing-closed K26 bitstream co-places the DPU and both resize IP instances; the gather IP runs from its own overlay, so the full pipeline has not run as one bitstream. The evi- dence is dataset replay on hardware, not a moving-vehicle test: on-car synchronization, planner-interface timing, actu- ation, and real-traffic fallback remain future validation, with runtime fault monitoring [5, 37, 42]. On-board VRU recall (pedestrian 14.8%, bicycle 5.7%) and night mIoU (16.76) are far below what a braking func- tion needs, and the fallback lowers recall further on late frames (Sec. 4.4); we claim no recall level as sufficient, since it comes from the system safety case [42]. The timing layer is model-independent, so a more accurate fixed-work model inherits it unchanged. The platform comparison is not matched in precision or isolation: GPUs run FP16, dat- acenter runs share cluster nodes, and the KR260 is dedi- cated. A real-time-configured GPU, Orin’s DLA, or another FPGA would be fairer baselines. Determinism does not remove compute cost (Sec. 4.3), but the condition is not FlashOcc-specific: fixed rigs and tensor shapes turn LSS splatting and known-shape resampling into static-index gathers with certifiable trip counts, extending FPGA determinism evidence [22, 35] to camera occupancy.
