This study investigates the extent to which LLM- generated scores remain consistent when only the rating scale design changes. Using an experimental design with 3 tasks, 2 prompting strategies, and 5 models, we find that changing scale format, range, or sign shifts normalized scores by about 0.10 on average and changes the category of roughly 20% of texts. Toxicity, the only unipolar construct ex- amined, is most sensitive, particularly to scale sign. Rubric-based prompting does not reduce sensitivity relative to zero-shot prompting, and model choice has a modest effect. Our findings indicate that rating scale design is a measurement decision that affects LLM-generated scores. We provide practical suggestions for future researchers. First, we encourage researchers to test whether their main findings hold under at least one alter- native rating scale. These robustness checks are essential for supporting the replicability and relia- bility of LLM-based measurement. Next, the numbers on the scale should match what the construct measures.
