(c) Robot study mean normalized reward at K = 10 by feature initially underspecified. Error bars are ±1 SE. 6.1 Experiment Design We conducted an IRB-approved study using the Coffee environment. The 2×2 within-subjects de- sign crossed the prior (uninformed vs. informed) with the query delivery (no explanation vs. expla- nation), yielding four conditions: (1) Uninformed, (2) Informed, (3) Uninformed + Explanation and (4) Informed + Explanation. For each condition, participants were told the four task objectives: keep the mug upright, stay close to the table, stay away from the laptop, and stay away from the human. Participants were shown three trajectories chosen under the ground-truth reward, followed by 10 preference queries for each of two tasks, one with laptop initially underspecified and the other with human. The explanation conditions prefaced each task’s preference queries with a natural language explanation that named the underspecified feature. All conditions used the ground-truth partition o, leaving the prior and the explanation as the only factors that vary. Reward inference ran online: the posterior was re-sampled after every response, so queries adapted to each participant’s earlier an- swers. We rendered queries in simulation because executing arbitrary trajectories is unsafe without a filter [29], and with a filter the robot only shows acceptable behavior. Reward models calibrated on such data over-reward the behaviors humans reject [30]. Participants answered six 7-point Likert items after each task (see Appendix C), completed the NASA Task Load Index (NASA-TLX) [31] after each condition, and ranked the four conditions by preference at the end. We recruited N = 12 participants (9 female, 2 male, 1 preferred not to say; age M = 23.8, SD = 2.3). Hypotheses We expected that: (H1) using the informed prior will lead to improved reward recov- ery; (H2) providing explanations to guide participant responses to queries will lead to improved re- ward recovery; explanations would make (H3) queries easier to answer, (H4) the purpose of queries more understandable, and (H5) participants feel they can better express to the robot what it needs to learn; and (H6) participants subjectively prefer having an explanation. 6.2 Results Objective For each participant, condition, and task we computed normalized reward, and ran a 2×2 repeated-measures ANOVA on its value at K = 10 (averaged across the two tasks) with prior and explanation as within-subjects factors. We found a significant main effect of prior (F(1, 11) = 126.68, p < 0.001, η2 p = 0.920), supporting H1. We also found a significant main effect of explanation (F(1, 11) = 71.92, p < 0.001, η2 p = 0.867), supporting H2. The prior × ex- planation interaction was also significant and positive (F(1, 11) = 15.33, p = 0.002, η2 p = 0.582). Fig. 4a plots the normalized reward by condition. Subjective We ran a 2×2 repeated-measures ANOVA on each Likert scale with prior and expla- nation as within-subjects factors. The explanation main effect was significant on ease (F(1, 11) = 15.31, p = 0.002, η2 p = 0.582), supporting H3, and on understanding (F(1, 11) = 6.66, p = 0.026, η2 p = 0.377), supporting H4, but not on expression (F(1, 11) = 2.96, p = 0.113, η2 p = 0.212), yielding no support for H5.
