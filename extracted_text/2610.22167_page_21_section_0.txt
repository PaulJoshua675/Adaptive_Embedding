Edit the original explanation by adding missing reasoning and making necessary corrections. Preserve both its supported reasoning and, where useful, an actual mistaken inference followed by a clear, source-grounded correction. The reader should be able to see what was initially inferred, why that inference was reconsidered, and what the revised understanding supports. Do not silently replace a mistaken conclusion with a polished argument for a different answer. A correct final answer does not compensate for erasing the correction process. Use the original `completion_text` as your editing base. Do not write your preferred explanation from scratch and substitute it for the original. Work by insertion first. Read each existing paragraph, keep its supported sentences unchanged, and identify the exact missing link or error before adding anything. Fill a gap next to the passage that needs it. For a meaningful mistake, keep the original span that actually commits to the inference or label, then add the rereading and correction; do not replace that span with a better narrative. After writing, compare against the original and check that these anchor sentences still occur verbatim. A supported sentence may not be rewritten merely to integrate an addition more smoothly. An unsupported factual statement or fabricated quote may require a local repair; that exception is not a license to remove an intelligible mistaken argument. Do not expose this editing procedure in the completion. Three placement and coverage checks are mandatory when applicable: - Keep new interpretation of the target or its disputed referent out of the opening relationship/background paragraphs. Add it after the original target inference that it checks. You may enrich those earlier paragraphs with relevant roles and background that do not preempt that check. - When repairing a false claim of adjacency or topic continuity, inspect any recorded gap between the cited passage and the target. If such a gap bears on the repair, name its actual duration and combine it with the intervening turns; do not replace the time information with a vague “”earlier or treat the gap alone as conclusive. Separately retain an older activity if it remains a plausible cause of the present state. - If the original uses punctuation, an interjection or repeated wording to establish an emotion or act, revisit that same cue in the correction. Explain its plausible contribution in the recent response sequence, whose state it bears on, and why it does or does not establish the final label. Do not change the label while leaving the original cue interpretation unexamined. Distinguish a resigned, at-a-loss background response from the target's subsequent reaction when the source supports that transition. Keep the discovery in the right place. Preserve the actual route from an initially plausible reading to the check that changes it. In this preservation pass, also check for errors that the old explanation presents as background facts: a quote's speaker, the order of events, what an offer has or has not accomplished, or a blanket exclusion using a stricter definition. A retained mistake must be addressed; do not keep it operative solely because its sentence was in the original.
