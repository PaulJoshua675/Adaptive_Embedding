Results 1) Task A: Push-T: Table I tests whether a goal-agnostic, autonomously collected play dataset can be reused for pre- cise planar goals. Play-guided MPC reaches 98% SR@0.90 and 74% SR@0.95, compared with 74% and 36% for the strongest policy baseline, hindsight BC, under the same amount of data. The advantage grows from 24 to 38 per- centage points as the tolerance tightens. This widening gap, together with the increase in mean coverage from 0.764 to 0.953, indicates that model-based selection improves place- ment accuracy rather than merely bringing the object into the target vicinity. Timesteps-matched RL fails under the same data budget, showing that task-speciﬁc reward learning does not yield useful control at this interaction scale. Figure 5(a) isolates the value of planning on 128 distant- goal trials. Executing the play policy directly reaches 15.6/36.7/54.7% at 0.4/0.6/0.8 rad. Hindsight relabeling improves these rates to 25.8/53.9/71.9%, but play-guided MPC reaches 72.7/91.4/96.1%. Thus, the play data contain useful motions, but relabeling alone cannot reliably compose them toward a requested orientation. Our method is able to beneﬁt from the diverse samples from the play policy and effectively plan on it with the learned world model. The inference time still meets real-time requirement via batched inference, as detailed in Analysis. The RL comparison exposes a precision–interaction trade- off rather than a uniform winner. With the same 701K- frame budget, PPO-TM reaches only 0.0/0.0/1.6%, whereas our controller already acquires most goal neighborhoods.
