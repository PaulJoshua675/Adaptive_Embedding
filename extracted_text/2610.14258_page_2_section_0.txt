2 S. K. Aryal The simplest shrinkage operator is familiar under another name. Pulling every importance value toward its group mean by a fixed fraction adds to the method’s own penalty an L2 penalty toward the anchor that is uniform within each group, and L2 penalties of this kind are a standard baseline [20]. To our knowledge, however, nobody has studied systematically when and why shrinking importance estimates helps in regularization-based continual learning. We did not start from statistics. This work began with a biological observation. Chang et al. [7] identified dendritic nanotubes, thin tubes that connect the dendrites of neighboring neurons, in mammalian cortex. In dissociated neurons these actin-rich tubes propagated calcium over long distances, and they actively transported small molecules and amyloid-𝛽. Calcium signals gate synaptic plasticity [27], and synaptic tagging and capture describes how a local tag decides which synaptic changes become permanent [12, 34]. A channel that moves plasticity signals between neurons is therefore a natural candidate for a cross-cellular consolidation mechanism. We implemented the idea as transient lateral links that diffuse importance tags between the hidden units of a layer and tested it on Permuted MNIST. The mechanism failed in the form we expected and helped in a form we did not. Links formed between co-active units gave no significant gain, while links formed at random reliably improved retention. This paper reports how that observation led to shrinkage, and what shrinkage does for standard importance estimators. We make four contributions. • We report a negative result for two biologically motivated mechanisms, dense lateral connections pruned during training and transient links between co-active units, and a positive result for transient random links on 20-task Permuted MNIST (Studies 1 and 2). • We show that random links implement linear shrinkage of importance toward the layer mean in expectation, with a weight set by the link lifetime and formation rate (Proposition 3.1). A simulation of the link process alone predicts the weights, and graph-free shrinkage at those weights reproduces both the accuracy and the dispersion of the consolidated importance. • We screen fixed and empirical Bayes shrinkage across EWC, SI, and MAS on Permuted MNIST and Split CIFAR-100 to see where shrinkage may help and where it costs plasticity (Study 3). • We separate two reasons why shrinkage helps by varying the number of samples used to estimate importance. On Permuted MNIST, the benefit for EWC is a variance effect that vanishes with enough samples, while the benefit for MAS has a component that persists, which we read as bias (Study 4). We also show that a standard heteroscedastic empirical Bayes rule shrinks the largest Fisher-type estimates most (Proposition 3.2), which may explain its poor results. The experiments use small networks and standard benchmarks, and they ran on a single laptop. We therefore present the work as a careful empirical note built around a simple mechanism, not as a new state of the art.
