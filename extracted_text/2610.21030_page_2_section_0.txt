Effective re- trieval requires adapting the depth of the reasoning to the query-specific need. A key limitation of existing methods is that reasoning is typically supervised only through final retrieval performance. While effective for evaluating end-to-end success, these coarse signals provide little guidance over the reasoning process. More importantly, current retrieval systems cannot predict whether additional reasoning will improve retrieval before observing retrieval outcomes, and thus can only allocate computation reactively: they expand reasoning trajectories regardless of whether further refinement is likely to help. In this work, we formulate retrieval-oriented rea- soning as a utility-guided computation allocation problem. Rather than treating reasoning as an un- conditional token expansion, we evaluate whether each intermediate refinement improves the model’s ability to distinguish relevant documents from chal- lenging distractors (Figure 2). Under this perspec- tive, useful reasoning is determined not by trajec- tory length or textual sophistication, but by its mea- surable effect on retrieval discrimination. Building on this perspective, we propose AURA1 (Adaptive Utility-aware Reasoning Re- trievAl), a framework for learning retrieval- oriented reasoning through utility supervision over intermediate reasoning states. During training, AURA uses relevance labels and retrieval candi- dates to estimate whether each refinement step im- proves retrieval discrimination, encouraging rea- soning trajectories that sharpen retrieval specificity while avoiding semantically noisy refinements. Since retrieval specificity is unavailable at infer- ence time, AURA additionally trains a lightweight utility estimator to predict the retrieval utility of each current reasoning state. Monitoring this esti- mate online allows AURA to stop when state utility ceases to improve, without exhaustively expanding the reasoning trajectory. We evaluate AURA on the 12 reasoning- intensive retrieval domains in BRIGHT (Hongjin et al., 2025) and on MS MARCO (Bajaj et al., 2016) as a conventional in-domain retrieval set- ting. AURA outperforms the compared reasoning- conditioned retrieval methods on BRIGHT. On MS MARCO, it remains comparable to a strong reasoning-trained retriever, suggesting no substan- tial in-domain degradation when extensive rea- soning is unnecessary. Controlled analyses fur- ther show that the gains cannot be explained by denser intermediate contrastive supervision or tra- jectory filtering alone, and that retrieval specificity is strongly aligned with corpus-level nDCG@10. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We formulate reasoning-conditioned retrieval as an adaptive refinement process and show that reasoning utility is highly heterogeneous. • We propose AURA, a framework that super- vises intermediate reasoning states accord- ing to their retrieval specificity, and trains a lightweight utility estimator to dynamically allocate reasoning during inference. • We demonstrate improvements over the com- pared methods across 12 reasoning-intensive domains, together with a substantially better performance–cost trade-off under matched in- ference conditions. 1Project repository: https://github.com/yingtaoluo/ Adaptive-Reasoning-Retrieval.
