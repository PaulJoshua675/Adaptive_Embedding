Preprint A MODEL ARCHITECTURE AND OPTIMIZATION A.1 ARCHITECTURE AND LATENT CONDITIONING Inputs and inherited backbone. For an episode in environment e, the model receives one ini- tial RGB frame I0 and an aligned sequence a = (at)t of 14-D robot actions, and predicts the subsequent video frames. We initialize the video diffusion transformer (DiT) and action encoder from BWM (BWM Team, 2026), retaining its Wan2.2(Wan et al., 2025)-based action-conditioning pathway. The frozen video VAE encodes the initial frame into the video-latent stream and de- codes predicted latents back to RGB. Text conditioning is disabled; the initial frame is not an extra cross-attention token. In short, I0 enters the video-latent stream, a follows BWM’s two action- conditioning paths, and the environment latent augments both paths. Environment-indexed latent conditioning. We associate each environment e with a freely opti- mized vector ze ∈R32. Episodes assigned to an environment share its latent; their initial frames and actions vary, so predicting their different outcomes constrains the environment representation. There is no observation-to-latent encoder and no supervision from the numerical physical parameter. Let D = 3072 be the DiT width. Two separate projectors turn ze into model-width vectors: qb(ze) = Wb,2 GELU(Wb,1 LN(ze)) , b ∈{token, mod}, Wb,1 : 32 →128, Wb,2 : 128 →D. (9) The two linear layers in each projector have no bias; the projectors do not share weights. The 128-D width is internal to these MLPs: both outputs are D-dimensional. Cross-attention branch. BWM’s action encoder maps each action to a D-dimensional condition- ing token. Let Atok(a) denote this token sequence and ue = qtoken(ze) the single environment token. With text disabled, each DiT block receives the conditioning sequence Ce = [ue; Atok(a)]. For video patch tokens Xℓ∈RN×D entering block ℓ, the attention operation can be written schemat- ically as CA(Xℓ, Ce) = softmax (XℓWQ)(CeWK)⊤ √dh  (CeWV )WO. (10) Here N is the number of video patches, dh is one attention head’s width, and WQ, WK, WV , WO are the block’s learned attention matrices. The actual operation is multi-head; the equation shows where the new token enters. Every video patch can attend to the environment token as well as the action tokens through keys and values. Modulation branch. BWM also groups four aligned 14-D actions at a time and maps each group to a D-dimensional action-modulation vector amod g . Index g identifies the corresponding latent- video temporal group, and s identifies a spatial patch within it. Let τ be the current flow-matching timestep and bg,s(τ) ∈RD be Wan’s base timestep embedding at that video patch, before action or environment conditioning. The initial-frame latent uses the unnoised timestep, while predicted- frame latents use τ. The environment-modulation vector is ve = qmod(ze) ∈RD. The implementa- tion adds these three vectors and applies the existing time projection: ˜bg,s(τ) = bg,s(τ) + amod g + ve, Mg,s = TimeProj(˜bg,s(τ)) ∈R6×D. (11) The action and environment vectors are broadcast to every spatial patch in group g. The six D- dimensional outputs of Mg,s supply the DiT blocks’ adaptive-normalization shifts, scales, and resid- ual gates; the modified timestep embedding also conditions the output head. Thus ze influences both attention and per-patch modulation, while leaving BWM’s original action pathways in place. A.2 TRAINING AND TEST-TIME ADAPTATION Training schedule. Training uses the inherited flow-matching video-prediction loss, excluding the initial conditioning frame. A latent block freezes the shared model and updates the environment table; a model block freezes the table and updates the DiT, action encoder, and two projectors.
