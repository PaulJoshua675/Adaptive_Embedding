Limitations and scope. LCT currently targets BF16, relies on user-specified tensor distributions, and requires explicit integration with training operations. Its effectiveness may vary for architectures or tensor types with different exponent statistics. Future work includes decoding within GEMM kernels, improved adaptation to distribution shift, and combining lossless with lossy compression. AI use statement Agentic LLMs were used to optimize the Triton kernels. We first manually designed a series of test harnesses that contain correctness checks and benchmarks (given in the experiments folder of the Supplementary materials). An Autoresearch agent was targeted at these specifications. The prompt is reproduced in program.md. Manual supervision was used throughout the process, with suggestions and advice given to the agent. Ideas such as the bounded Huffman scheme, OverflowBuffer and fused optimizer operators are the Authors, and given to the LLM to implement. Once the kernels were optimized, the rest of the code was written with less LLM assistance. This includes the PyTorch interface and autograd functions and main experimental setups. Generative AI tools were used to assist with manuscript drafting and revision, mathematical exposition, and the preparation of figures and tables. AI assistance was also used to check for errors in the manuscript. Reproducibility statement Appendix A describes the benchmark protocols and training configurations. Algorithm 1 specifies codebook con- struction, with its optimality argument in Appendix B.1. Appendix C details the GPU implementation, and Listing 1 illustrates integration with automatic differentiation. The assumptions and derivations underlying the compression analysis are provided in Appendix D, while Appendix E describes the weight and activation measurements. The supplementary materials contain our codebase, including the LCT package, Autoresearch prompt and code for the experiments. Ethics statement This work studies lossless compression of model tensors and evaluates it using synthetic data, the existing FineWeb dataset, and pretrained models. No human participants were recruited and no new personal data were collected. Use of web-derived training data remains subject to privacy, copyright, and bias considerations. The main impact of this work is lowering memory requirements, making larger training workloads more accessible. The energy impact of compression depends on the full training workload, including its runtime overhead. 9
