A.3 REWARD DESIGN We design task-specific automatic rewards to accommodate the heterogeneous output spaces of the training tasks. For textual predictions, the answer is extracted from the <answer> field when avail- able and normalized by lowercasing, removing punctuation and articles, and collapsing consecutive spaces. Structured predictions are additionally checked against their required output formats. Fine-Grained Perception and Visual Grounding. For perception tasks with textual outputs, we use token-level F1 between the normalized prediction and reference answer. For visual grounding, the prediction is parsed as a list of labeled bounding boxes. Predicted and ground-truth boxes with the same normalized label are greedily matched according to their intersection-over-union (IoU). The grounding reward is defined as rground = λfmtrfmt + λ0.5F1,τ=0.5 + λ0.75F1,τ=0.75 + λIoUrmIoU, (10) where rfmt indicates whether the predicted boxes can be successfully parsed, F1,τ=0.5 and F1,τ=0.75 denote label-aware detection F1 at IoU thresholds of 0.5 and 0.75, respectively, and rmIoU is the mean IoU obtained from one-to-one box matching. We set (λfmt, λ0.5, λ0.75, λIoU) = (0.1, 0.4, 0.3, 0.2). OCR and Chart Understanding. For OCR and chart-understanding tasks, we adopt token-level F1 as the training reward: rtext = 2PR P + R, (11) where P and R are computed from the token overlap between the normalized prediction and refer- ence answer. Boolean outputs are mapped to a unified yes/no representation before matching. Empty predictions or predictions without overlapping tokens receive zero reward. Multimodal Symbolic Reasoning.
