Fewer than 1 in 150 of these added candidates is a gold descriptor, versus 1 in 68 across the pool, so the cutoffs sit where further candidates enlarge the pool faster than they recover gold. 5.3 TAGHN configuration and training TAGHN operates at 𝑑= 1024 with two message-passing layers and dropout 0.1. We include the top 𝐾NT = 5 narrower children and 𝐾RT = 3 related terms ranked by cosine similarity to d as context for each candidate. The model consists of four relation edges: BT, NT, RT (from the thesaurus structure), and SELF carry edges. The scoring head takes 12 features and a 128-dimensional projection of the interaction vector and passes the concatenation through an MLP. We train for 30 epochs with batch size 32, learning rate 10−4 and weight decay 3×10−5, in mixed precision on Quadro RTX 8000 GPUs. Validation runs every epoch and we early-stop on descriptor F1 with patience 5 and a minimum improvement of 0.001; the reported model stopped at epoch 13. The loss weights are 𝜆1 = 0.3, 𝜆2 = 0.1 The positive and negative weights in the relevance term𝛾+/𝛾−= 6.0/1.5 were selected based on the validation set. The ratio controls where the model sits on the precision–recall trade-off. During inference, the learned threshold is used directly (𝜃= −0.053), followed by BT-suppression with margin 𝛿= 0.05. TAGHN predicts 5.64 descriptors per document on average versus 5.80 gold. TAGHN operates on document-specific subgraphs whose size is bounded by the candidate budget (typically 230–420 nodes per document in STW), its per document cost does not grow with thesaurus size. Earlier graph-based methods propagate over the full label graph at every step and keep all label embeddings in GPU memory, so both compute and memory grow with the vocabulary. In practice, the full model (57M parameters, fp16, precomputed embeddings) trained on 13.3k STW documents for 30 epochs with early stopping on a single Quadro RTX 8000, including validation after every epoch. Scoring all 1,660 test documents took less than 4 minutes end to end, or less than 0.15s per document. 5.4 Baseline models TAGHN is evaluated against two types of model, retriever based models that uses the same retrieve-rerank pipeline and models that are designed for XMLC that assume a fixed vocabulary. The retrieve-rerank models share the same candidate pool as TAGHN from Stage-1. However, the fixed vocabulary model does not require a candidate pool and works directly on the entire descriptor list. The fixed vocabulary baseline models are Omikuji-Bonsai and the XR-Tansformer. Omikuji is an implementation of partitioned label tree methods with Parabel and Bonsai variants, which are integrated as one of the backends in the Annif tool. We train it through annif in the Bonsai configuration using snowball analyzer, bigram features, and model specific parameter such as maximum depth, number of cluster. The XRtransformer is available in the pecos[29] bundle. Its label space is clustered by spherical hierar- chical 𝑘-means, the matcher is trained for 10 epochs with batch size 32, learning rate 5 × 10−5 and input truncation at 256 tokens, using teacher-forced negatives and a squared-hinge loss, and the linear ranker is fitted over the resulting shortlists.
