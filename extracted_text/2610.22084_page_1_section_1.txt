Neural networks offer critical adaptability for controlling complex systems where nominal modeling proves intractable. However, when offline-trained neural networks are deployed within closed-loop control systems, their actions fundamentally alter the state distribution upon which they were trained. This paper introduces a unified taxonomy to categorize these sequential failure modes: the initial Closed-Loop Identification Error, the deployment-induced Action Error, and the continuous Tracking Error. Through discrete-time sensitivity analysis, this work formalizes how offline-learned parameters locally guarantee a trajectory divergence upon deployment, degrading performance due to local loss geometry. To mitigate this distribution shift, we propose actionable design policies cen- tered on time-scale separated online adaptation. These mechanisms are validated through ablation studies on a nonlinear Duﬀing oscillator, demonstrating that bounded continuous learning success- fully mitigates trajectory divergence whereas static deployments fail, offering concrete guidelines for practitioners deploying machine learning components in safety-critical feedback systems. 1
