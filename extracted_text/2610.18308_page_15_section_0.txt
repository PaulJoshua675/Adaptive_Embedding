This Phase-2 au- dit is distinct from the Stage 4 Audit Agent that audits interviews before enrichment. GPT-4o-mini (temperature 0.2) checked the simulated user’s mes- sages for fabrication, caricature, role confusion, and persona drift, with a 512-token output limit. It assigned ACCEPT when there was no major issue (at most a minor issue in one turn), PARTIAL when one issue type was present but the conversation remained mostly usable, and REJECT when mul- tiple issue types or one severe issue were present; role confusion across multiple turns was explicitly severe. Model, prompt, temperature, and criteria were fixed across recommenders and conditions; only the condition-specific profile and generated conversation differed. Audit decisions were pro- duced before T3 extraction and without access to extraction outputs. The audit affected inclusion only and did not regenerate conversations. Al- though the auditor and one T3 judge both used GPT-4o-mini, they were separate calls with differ- ent prompts; the DeepSeek-chat evaluation used a different underlying model. Audit filtering and paired-sample construction. All four CRS–condition arms contained 480 tech- nically completed conversations (120 users × four opening strategies), and no conversation terminated with a transport or generation error. In the primary analysis, individual conversations assigned a Phase- 2 REJECT decision were excluded, whereas AC- CEPT and PARTIAL conversations were retained. Rejecting one conversation did not remove the en- tire user: a user remained evaluable in an arm when- ever at least one of their four conversations was retained. The paired analysis then used the inter- section of users evaluable in both conditions. This produced 111 paired users for KBRD (112 evalu- able in FULL and 118 in STATISTICALONLY) and 80 for UniCRS (94 and 99, respectively). The conversation-level rejection rates were 39.6% for FULL and 36.5% for STATISTICALONLY with KBRD, and 68.5% and 61.7%, respectively, with UniCRS. A post-hoc paired Wilcoxon diag- nostic over per-user rejection rates found no signif- icant condition difference for KBRD (p = 0.262), but found a higher rejection rate for FULL with UniCRS (p = 0.019; two-sided). We therefore report the audit-filtered analysis together with an all-conversation sensitivity analysis. Statistical inference. For each recommender– judge combination, inference was performed on paired user-level differences. We report percentile 95% confidence intervals from 10,000 paired boot- strap resamples (seed 0), two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with zero differences removed and SciPy’s automatic exact/asymptotic method se- lection, and paired Cohen’s dz. The table reports unadjusted p-values for these four T3 comparisons. As a robustness check, two-sided paired sign-flip tests with 100,000 randomizations (seed 1) gave the same significance conclusions. Per-user T3 aggregation. For each user and con- dition, the evaluator extracted aspect–sentiment pairs from every retained conversation and merged them into a single user-level aspect-frequency dis- tribution. Filtered T3 aspect entropy was then com- puted once from this merged distribution. We did not compute a separate entropy for each opening strategy and then average the four values.
