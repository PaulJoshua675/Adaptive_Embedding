Generative Retrieval is a retrieval paradigm in which an input query text is directly matched to a document identifier (DocID) using a lan- guage model, typically a sequence-to-sequence model. This design can offer in- ference and memory efficiency advantages (Kim et al., 2024). Two different DocID designs exist for genera- tive retrieval systems: numeric DocIDs and textual DocIDs. Both have their own strengths: Numeric DocIDs offer computational advantages, as they reduce the complexity of beam-search decoding (Wang et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2025). In contrast, textual DocIDs can exploit the semantic priors encoded in pretrained language models and are able to give more interpretable doc- ument identifiers (Bevilacqua et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2022; Askari et al., 2026). One of the major challenges in current genera- tive retrieval research is adapting to the retrieval of previously unseen documents. Generative re- trieval models typically require an indexing stage during training, where the model learns to associate documents with their corresponding DocIDs.
