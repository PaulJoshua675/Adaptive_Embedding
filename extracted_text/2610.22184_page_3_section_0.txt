Evidence that the hand and the humanoid form a structural continuum comes from reconfigurable hardware [30] and from commercial humanoids that already tuck containers under one arm, drape cloth over a shoulder, and lift appliances during everyday tasks [31], [32]. Humanoid loco-manipulation couples loco- motion with manipulation, enabling dynamic behaviors such as climbing and wall flips alongside picking, placing, and pushing objects [5], [4], [33], [34], [35], including humanoid pushing in simulation [36]. We instead focus on general object manipulation across body regions. Teleoperation is a direct route to whole-body manipulation and is well developed as motion transfer, through real- time human-to-humanoid retargeting and shadowing [37], [38], [39], [40] and tracking controllers that follow whole- body targets [41], [42], [3], [43]. However, without force or tactile feedback, it is difficult for the operator to tell whether an object is pinned or slipping, which largely limits teleoperation to hand-only pick-and-place behavior. Dexterity as Contact Sequencing. In-hand manipulation can be framed as re-grasping: an object is carried through a se- quence of grasps by repeatedly breaking and remaking finger contacts, a process known as finger gaiting [14], [13], [44]. Using this idea, prior work trains dexterous-hand controllers that high-level policies or teleoperators steer with motion commands [11], [12]. Related goal-conditioned approaches learn grasping and reorientation across procedurally gener- ated tools [45] or pre-train object reposing before adapting to downstream manipulation tasks [46]. Similar in mechanism to our own approach, a constrained sampler over feasible contact-rich states can guide goal-conditioned manipulation RL directly [47]. We lift this re-grasping principle from dexterous hands and arms to the whole humanoid.
