A PREPRINT - OCTOBER 8, 2026 3.5 Experimental setup What we record. Besides held out error and accuracy of the pipeline, we record for the best individual of every generation of every run two further quantities. One is the order of nonlinearity of the evolved program g, computed exactly by Walsh-Hadamard transform on the Boolean targets and, on the continuous ones, as the least polynomial degree reaching relative squared error 10−4 on a held out probe sample. The other is the behaviour of g before the learner is applied, which we call its innate behaviour and which Section 4.4 needs. These two are the instruments of the paper: they look at the programs rather than at how well the pipeline predicts. Learners. Linear regression, ridge, decision tree, random forest, gradient boosting, and a multi-layer perceptron with one hidden layer of 50 units and early stopping, spanning a range of capacity V . Canonical GP, the first row of Figure 1, is the seventh condition. Controls. A limit of 30 nodes applies identically to every condition, so none has a larger budget than another. The Boolean primitive set is {+, −, ×} with the constant 1, neutral between the two routes described after Proposition 3; the continuous set is {+, −, ×, ÷, sin, cos} with an ephemeral constant. The learner in Equation 1 is fitted under 3-fold cross validation and fitness is the mean held out fold error, for every learner including the linear ones. This matters: fitted and scored on the same rows, a tree or a forest interpolates any one dimensional feature, training error falls to near zero for almost every individual, and selection is left with nothing to act on. Parameters and statistics. Population 100, 100 generations, tournament selection of size 3, one point crossover 0.8, uniform subtree mutation 0.05, elitism of one. Thirty independent runs per condition, 1890 runs in total, none failed. Outcomes on the Boolean targets are bimodal: a run either classifies every held out row correctly or sits near chance, with intermediate accuracies rare. We therefore treat success as a binary outcome and test it with Fisher’s exact test on the number of exact runs out of thirty, which suits this outcome and is not weakened by the ties that blunt a rank test here. Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on paired accuracies are reported where they add information. Orders of nonlinearity are medians. 4 Results 4.1 Contribution of each component in isolation We first separate the mechanism from the search. Every learner is handed the same order-1 representation s(x) = P i≤k xi guaranteed by Proposition 3, and only f is fitted (Table 1). Given the same representation, a nonlinear learner is exact up to order seven and a linear one recovers nothing above order one. The difference between learners is therefore not a difference in raw power applied to a fixed problem; it is whether the learner can absorb the order the representation does not carry.
