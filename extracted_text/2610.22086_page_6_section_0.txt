ML: Multi-SFT ; FULL: extended mix; BL: baseline SFT; SFT†: official provider model. Average performance is given per group (see Avg columns). Impact of multilingual data. To disentangle the contribution of multilingual content from bare re- generation, we compare ML with BL. This offers a clear comparison since BL was trained on the same source datasets as ML but kept in their origi- nal English. BL also serves as a direct test against permissively and non-permissively licensed data. As expected, ML outperforms BL on 7 out of 9 mod- els on multilingual benchmarks. On English, BL retains an advantage on 7 out of 9 models given its larger share of English examples, yet the gaps remain modest in most cases, indicating that the multilingual focus of ML does not severely harm English capability. Differences across families and sizes. Eu- roLLM benefits most from ML: it outperforms SFT† on the multilingual average at all three scales (+0.6, +1.9, +2.2) and matches or exceeds it on English, as expected given EuroLLM’s European-focused pre-training. OLMo 2 trails SFT† on the multilin- gual average, narrowly for the 1B (−0.1) and 7B (−1.4) models and markedly for the 32B (−10.2), while English consistently favors SFT†, reflecting its English-centric pre-training.
