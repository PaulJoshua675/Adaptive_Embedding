In Figure 3(b), replacing semantic partitions with random boundaries (0.86) or fixed 3-token windows (0.87) causes a severe 8–9% drop relative to SCOPE (0.95) on Qwen-Image. Arbitrary groupings sever coherent compositional phrases across window borders, proving that linguistic boundary alignment, rather than generic multi-token smoothing, which is essential for effective credit assignment. Effect of the advantage rule. Figure 3(c) evaluates SCOPE under three completion-level advantage estimators on FLUX.1-dev: pure baseline subtraction (∆bg, 0.827), standard group normalization (z-norm, 0.832), and our hybrid threshold rule (0.841). First, all three variants substantially outperform uniform GRPO (0.760), confirming that SCOPE’s gains stem fundamentally from semantic span modulation. Second, the hybrid rule yields an additional +0.009 boost by anchoring updates to the original prompt when initial quality is low (bg < τ) and switching to fine-grained group discrimination once generation competence is established. Training dynamics. Figure 3(d) reports the Qwen-Image training metric over the first 300 steps, with curves smoothed by a 20-step exponential moving average. The smoothed trajectory of uniform GRPO remains lower and varies only mildly, at approximately 0.83–0.85. The smoothed trajectory of SCOPE lies above that of GRPO throughout the observed window and falls mainly in the range of approximately 0.86–0.90, whereas its unsmoothed trace exhibits substantially larger fluctuations. 8
