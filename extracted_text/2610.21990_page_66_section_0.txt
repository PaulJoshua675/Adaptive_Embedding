Their impact is very similar when individually employed, with an average of ≈3% performance deficit when the surface normal is not used and an average of ≈3.5% performance deficit considering both datasets. The combined benefit is observed mainly on the PST900 [8] dataset with an average improvement of ≈5% for both modalities, confirming again the fact that the CART dataset [25] provides more informativeness in its RGB maps than PST900 [8] because of the different light conditions. The relevance of the RGB modality is again confirmed when comparing the two configurations where LLIE RGB and surface normal representations are switched so that each one is alternatively a main modality and an auxiliary. The results on both datasets show that there is an average of ≈7.0% performance deficit when the surface normal map is used as a main modality. Thermal as Main Modality. Given that this ablation study is conducted under low-light config- uration, it would be expected for the thermal modality provide more information about the over- all composition of the image, as it is not affected by noise, exposure and loss of global and local structures. However, a configuration with thermal used as a main modality, with surface normals, reflectance and illumination as auxiliaries shows a large performance decline compared to the other configurations where LLIE RGB is used as the main modality. The reflectance and illuminance maps represent a decomposition of the LLIE RGB image conducted under the assumption of the Retinex Theory [40] which suggests that visual information can be sepa- rated into two core components: reflectance responsible for the colour information and illuminance encoding the intensity of the pixels. The decomposition can be done using various ways, from classical to neural networks which are trained specifically for accurate representation of both components. However, for the purpose of this experiment, a simpler approach was used, where the illumination is considered as the maximum value across the channels and reflectance as the original image divided by the illumination, both filtered using, a Gaussian Kernel.
