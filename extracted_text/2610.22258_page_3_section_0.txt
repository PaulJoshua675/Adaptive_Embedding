EXPERIMENTAL SETUP Despite the strong performance of modern CNN and transformer-based denoisers on synthetic Gaussian noise and real-world datasets such as SIDD [19], their performance when the distribution of images shift to texture-rich ones remains unaddressed. It is important to understand the dissim- ilarities between natural images and textures. While textures are components of natural images, they differ structurally when considered independently. As defined by Portilla & Simoncelli [24], “An image of visual texture is spatially homo- geneous and typically contains repeated structures, often with some random variation (e.g., random positions, orientations or colors).” In contrast, natural images have been observed to predominantly consist of smooth (low-pass) regions [25]. This characteristic is fundamentally different from textures, which may or may not exhibit smoothness. The presence of high- frequency spectral information, which is also present in noise, is what makes texture denoising challenging. In this paper, the objective is to validate and benchmark the performance of SOTA deep denoisers, specifically on textures. To benchmark the performance, SOTA denoising mod- els, which have demonstrated exceptional performance in real-world image denoising, were selected — MIRNet [3], MPRNet [18], CAT [23], CycleISP [20], NAFNet [21], and Restormer [22]. These models were evaluated for denoising performance on a texture dataset using the same noise levels as the original SOTA models. Subsequently, the models are fine-tuned on the texture dataset, and their performance is evaluated again. Fine-tuning was adopted instead of training from scratch, as training these models from scratch requires a substantially larger dataset, which was not available for the present study. A. Datasets For qualitative and quantitative evaluation of the pre- trained and fine-tuned models, patches were extracted from the NUUR-Texture500 dataset [4] which contains 500 diverse and spatially consistent and uniform texture images spanning from regular patterns to stochastic ones. From each image in the NUUR-Texture500 dataset [4], ten 256 × 256 patches were randomly sampled, yielding 5000 patches in total, split into 75% for training and 25% for validation. To simulate real-world noise conditions, noisy patches were generated by adding white Gaussian noise, with the noise standard deviation (σ) determined by estimating the average variance of noise in the noisy images of the SIDD dataset [19]. Our goal was to match the noise characteristics of the synthetic noisy texture patches with those found in real-world datasets such as SIDD, enabling a fair evaluation of denoising performance. B. Implementation Details Six Nvidia A6000 GPUs were used to fine-tune the models using PyTorch. The fine-tuning of the denoisers is done using Adam Optimizer (β1 = 0.9, β2 = 0.999) for 100 epochs with a batch size of 16. The initial learning rate is set to 1 × 10−4 and a cosine annealing strategy is used to steadily reduce the learning rate to 5 × 10−5 during training. The loss function is chosen in accordance with the network architecture and the same objective used during training is retained for fine-tuning (e.g. L1 loss for CAT and Charbonnier loss for MPRNet). C. Evaluation Metrics Performance is evaluated using both fidelity and perceptual metrics such as PSNR, SSIM, MS-SSIM [7], CW-SSIM [8], STSIM1 [6], STSIM2 [6], DISTS [9] and LPIPS [26].
