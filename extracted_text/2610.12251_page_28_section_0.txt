A ternary row cannot hold the weight r, and reading r copies of an input with weight 1 is how it realizes one. Remark 8.11 (Thresholds and restoring organs). In von Neumann’s multiplexing [23] every compo- nent fails with a fixed probability p whatever its inputs, and a restoring organ of majority elements follows every level; reliable computation then needs redundancy logarithmic in the size of the cir- cuit [26, 27] and is possible only below a threshold on p [24, 25]. The amplification F (r) is Pierce’s interwoven redundant logic [28, 14] with threshold units. The restoring organ is absorbed into every unit, which sums the r copies of each input with one weight, and the identity rows of Lev become majority units. Amplification does not enlarge the tolerance to systematic errors: for F (r) the conditions of Proposition 8.3 are those for F with η/r in place of η. Construction 8.12 (The amplified host). Lev(Nhost) is a clocked netlist: its rows are units, each reading rows of the layer before it, and the rows of its last layer are the next-state values. Let Nr = Lev(Nhost)(r), a clocked netlist on r · 2057 state bits with 41,967 r units and 75,501 r2 edges. Its rows are again in layer order, each reading only the layer before, so Lev(Nr) = Nr, and Proposition 8.7 describes every row of Lev(Nr). Its device reads each bit of a register as the majority of the r copies and writes every copy. Corollary 8.13 (Exact self-reproduction of the amplified host). Nr realizes the transducer of Definitions 5.1 and 5.2 on replicated states. Run with N3 in place of Nhost under Gaussian read noise on every pre-activation, Ω∗emits the whole instance at s = 0.10, 0.15, 0.20 and 0.25, where the host itself fails at s = 0.10 after 143 bytes and at s = 0.15 and 0.20 before emitting one (Appendix E). A single wrong copy is outvoted in the next layer, and the computation fails only when two wrong copies meet, an event of second order in the probability of a flip. The following bound counts these events. Proposition 8.14 (Restoration). Let the perturbations of N3 be independent centred Gaussians of standard deviation s on every pre-activation. The probability that the state of N3 read by majority differs from the orbit of x0 after one of the first T steps is at most P t<T b(t), where b(t), given in Definition D.1, is computed layer by layer along the orbit from the distance of each row to the comparator and from the inputs whose errors move it toward the comparator.
