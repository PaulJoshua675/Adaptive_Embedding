In particular, the training and prefill optimizations in Section 5 enabled this daily update cadence, making continual model refresh practical at production scale. 8.4 System Efficiency and Robustness The system evaluation asks whether the amortization structure introduced in Sections 5.2–5.4 improves the end-to-end operating point rather than merely relocating computation. We evaluate training effi- ciency, online serving behavior, freshness, and the additional state-maintenance resources introduced by disaggregation. Certain deployment details and absolute cost measurements are omitted due to disclosure policies. The systems results should be interpreted within their stated measurement scopes and do not constitute a complete lifecycle-cost analysis. Training efficiency. We compare the optimized TransX-Base training pipeline with a straightforward FSDP (Zhao et al., 2023b) baseline on 32 H200 GPUs, holding the model, training data, history-length distribution, target-sampling policy, effective batch size, optimizer, and precision fixed. Multi-cutoff trajectory packing, batched memory extraction, shared prefix scans, and sequence–fanout execution increase supervised candidate-target throughput by 84% and full-pipeline training MFU from approximately 11% to 18%. End-to-end peak GPU memory remains approximately unchanged. Using stabilization of click and conversion AUC and gAUC within 0.01% as the convergence criterion, wall-clock time to convergence decreases from approximately 25 to 13 hours. Online serving efficiency. Production TransX-Base achieves approximately 250 requests/s/H200 with p99 e2e scoring latency of 49 ms. The GPU-resident behavior-memory KV hit rate is approximately 25%, while combined GPU/host residency exceeds 99%; behavior memory KV transfer contributes less than 5% of request feature-transfer volume.
