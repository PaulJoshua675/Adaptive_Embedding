Imaging and Diagnostic Interpretation There is uniform agreement that the sonographic features are reassuring and consistent with a benign process. However, there is a divergence in interpretation regarding the specific etiology: • One view suggests this represents unilateral prepubertal/pubertal gynecomastia, which typically presents as a firm, discoid subareolar plaque and can be managed with observation. • The alternative, and surgically more compelling view, is that the discrete, oval, well-circumscribed nature of the mass with through-transmission is atypical for classic gynecomastia and more characteristic of a benign solid breast neoplasm, such as a juvenile fibroadenoma. Given that the mass has persisted for 18 months and is actively enlarging, the clinical behavior is less consistent with physiologic gynecomastia, which typically regresses spontaneously over 6 to 24 months.
