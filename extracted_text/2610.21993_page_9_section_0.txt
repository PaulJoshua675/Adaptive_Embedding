Pretraining is comparatively inexpensive. The UNet-128 and ConvNeXt-512 configurations complete in 8.9 and 7.9 wall clock hours respectively on 4× NVIDIA V100 (32 GB) GPUs (35.6 and 31.6 GPU-hours; Table 3), well below the 8×A100 (80 GB) multi-node configurations reported for CROMA (Fuller et al., 2023) (300–600 epochs) and SoftCon (Wang et al., 2024) (100 epochs), though neither paper reports absolute GPU-hour or wall clock figures, precluding an exact comparison. Finetuning Protocol Every pretrained ConvNeXt-RS encoder is then finetuned end-to-end, separately, on each downstream task: AdamW at lr=10−3 (weight decay 10−4), OneCycleLR with final_div_factor = 100, 50 epochs (4 warmup), FP32, under a separate codebase running PyTorch 2.1.2 and CUDA 11.8. Pretraining and finetuning therefore run on different framework versions; we did not isolate the effect of this difference. Task-specific finetuning follows the Copernicus-Bench protocol: EuroSAT-S1 uses a linear classification head, while BigEarthNet-S1’s multi-label classification task is trained with a multi- label soft-margin loss. The two dense prediction tasks, DFC2020-S1 and Flood-S1, both employ a UPerNet (Xiao et al., 2018) decoder augmented with an auxiliary FCN head.
