Q1: Zero-shot Personalization. Q1 asks whether off-the-shelf VLA policies can follow user preferences without preference-specific training. Table 2 evaluates six policies on the LIBERO- SinglePref task suite. The no-preference column uses the original LIBERO task language with minimally edited LIBERO-SinglePref layouts. Finding. Table 2 shows that minimal layout edits from our pipeline preserve no preference zero-shot task performance, and existing SOTA VLA models fail to follow preferences in the preference- conditioned zero-shot setting. Most policies maintain high no-preference SR, with π0.5 reaching 97.6%, indicating that the edited layouts preserve the LIBERO task distribution. Thus, low SA mainly reflects preference-following failure rather than layout-editing artifacts. Notably, π0.5 reaches high SR on Motion and Skill Preference (97.50% and 100.00%), but low SA (24.38% and 25.00%), showing that it completes tasks while ignoring preference. Executing with a User Profile is harder since it changes the target object or semantic choice, reducing π0.5 SR to 19.12% and causing near-zero SR for weaker policies. Q2: Learning Ability. Table 3 reports in-domain and unseen-layout evaluation results under LoRA- based supervised fine-tuning (SFT). The π0.5-LoRA setting is used only on LIBERO-SinglePref to validate the effectiveness of our dataset. Finding. Sequential training degrades no-preference and preference-conditioned performance. Mixed training instead yields a category-dependent trade-off: it better retains no-preference behavior and contextual-prompt robustness, but weakens motion preference relative to preference-only LoRA. Table 3 compares the No-Pref and All-Pref settings. π0.5-Seq-LoRA obtains 52.33±0.37% No-Pref SR and 74.75±1.97% All-Pref SR, whereas π0.5-Mix-LoRA retains 91.00±0.70% No-Pref SR and reaches 84.55±1.94% All-Pref SR. The LIBERO-ComprePref columns further evaluate robustness to contextual prompt variation: Mix-LoRA reaches 72.83±0.95% SR and 70.07±0.91% SA, compared with 44.00±0.71% and 42.64±0.69% for preference-only LoRA.
