First, does knowledge-grouped credit as- signment yield consistent gains over outcome-only and process-supervision baselines (Section 4.2)? Second, are knowledge-state grouping and incre- mental knowledge gain each individually necessary (Section 4.3)? Third, does the knowledge state genuinely provide a better grouping anchor than surface observations (Section 4.4)? Fourth, how does KnowGPO change search behavior, and at what training cost (Section 4.5)? 4.1 Experimental Setup Benchmarks. We evaluate KnowGPO on three challenging deep search benchmarks that cover di- verse question distributions and difficulty levels: Xbench (Xbench-Team, 2025) (covering the 2505 and 2510 splits), BrowseComp-ZH (Zhou et al., 2025), and the text-only subset of GAIA (Mialon et al., 2023). Following standard LLM-as-judge protocols, GPT-4o (OpenAI et al., 2024) judges whether the agent’s final response matches the ground truth. All evaluations follow each bench- mark’s official protocol; we report the mean accu- racy over 3 independent runs. Baselines. We compare KnowGPO against three representative reinforcement learning approaches, all trained from the same SFT checkpoint with identical data and tool environments. GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) uses only the binary outcome reward and is the dominant baseline in current open-source systems. GiGPO (Feng et al., 2025) introduces step-level credit assignment by grouping steps that revisit the same intermediate state and computing within-group advantages from discounted future returns. IGPO (Wang et al., 2026) assigns each step a dense reward equal to the increase in the model’s predicted probability of the correct answer. In addition, we report numbers from several state- of-the-art open-source and proprietary deep search agents for context; differences in training data, con- text length, and tool stacks limit direct comparison, and we mark such systems separately. Implementation details. All trained models use Qwen3-8B (Yang et al., 2025) as the base model, with context extended to 128K tokens via YaRN (Peng et al., 2026). SFT is conducted with LlamaFactory (Zheng et al., 2024); RL training uses verl (Sheng et al., 2025). All baselines share the same SFT initialization, training data, learn- ing rate, batch size, and rollout configuration as KnowGPO; only the advantage estimator and re- ward design differ. Knowledge state verification uses DeepSeek-v3.2 as the LLM verifier; the choice and its quality analysis are reported in Appendix C. Full hyperparameters are in Appendix E. 4.2 Main Results Tables 1 and 2 report performance on all three benchmarks. We make two observations. KnowGPO consistently outperforms both outcome-only and step-level baselines. All RL approaches improve over SFT, confirming that ex- plicit policy optimization is necessary for deep search agents. However, existing step-level meth- ods show considerable cross-dataset variability: GiGPO degrades by 2.0% on Xbench-2505 relative to GRPO despite winning on BrowseComp-ZH, and IGPO matches GRPO on Xbench-2505 but barely improves on BrowseComp-ZH.
