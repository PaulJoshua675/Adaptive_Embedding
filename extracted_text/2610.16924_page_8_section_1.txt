We proposed FidLIF, a framework that enables parallel train- ing while preserving high-fidelity hard-reset LIF dynamics and step-by-step inference. FidLIF applies Newton’s method to recover the membrane-potential trajectory iteratively in parallel. In the backward pass, FidLIF’s reverse adjoint scan achieves BPTT-equivalent gradients without passing through the forward iterations, making memory cost almost inde- pendent of the number of iterations. FidLIF also supports input-dependent neuronal parameters, improving the repre- sentational ability. Experiments show that our Triton imple- mentation achieves significant speedup and memory savings, particularly on long sequences. In real sequential bench- marks, FidLIF achieves competitive accuracy within 3 itera- tions, and the trained model supports step-by-step inference without extra conversion. FidLIF thus combines high-fidelity hard-reset LIF dynamics with sequence parallelism, allowing SNNs to take advantage of modern accelerators during train- ing while executing step-by-step during deployment. These features help SNNs strike a better balance between biological plausibility, efficiency, and edge deployment.
