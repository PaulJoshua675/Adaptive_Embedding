Recent works have tried to overcome this hurdle by incorporating a separation-of-variables approach to help PINNs and PI-DeepONets scale to higher-dimensional problems [29, 25]. In the Separable PI-DeepONet, separate Trunk networks are employed to encode each coordinate axis (i.e. independent variable of the PDE). This PI-DeepONet structure reduces the number of Trunk network evaluations from N d to Nd, making it favourable for high-dimensional problems. However, this method achieves the best performance when the underlying physical system is compatible with a separation-of-variables approach, somewhat limiting the general applicability of the PI-DeepONet framework — one of its most compelling advantages. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, the work by [25] is the only so far that has aimed to reduce the computational expense of training PI-DeepONets. This work investigates an alternative Accelerated Tensor Handling (ATenH) PI-DeepONet architecture which aims to increase its computational eﬀiciency. This architecture takes inspiration from those suggested in [30], and applies it in the physics-informed setting. The main objectives of this paper are summarised as follows: 1. To introduce an alternative PI-DeepONet architecture, hereafter referred to as ATenH, that reduces the computational cost of training and inference. 2. To derive the theoretical computational speed-ups achieved by ATenH relative to the original PI- DeepONet architecture (Vanilla). 3. To evaluate the performance of ATenH through a set of increasingly challenging numerical experi- ments. 4. To demonstrate that extending PI-DeepONets to higher-dimensional problems presents significant computational challenges, and that architectural improvements such as ATenH are essential for making some problems computationally tractable. In doing so, we demonstrate that these architectural modifications enable PI-DeepONets to be applied to higher-dimensional PDE systems at greater spatial and temporal resolutions than was previously possible. All numerical results for the Vanilla and ATenH architectures are obtained using PyTorch, demonstrating that these eﬀiciency gains can be realised within standard, widely used, deep learning frameworks.
