B.3 Detailed Relation-Channel Statistics For each directional relation r, Figure 6 reports its density, the attention mass assigned to the node pairs covered by that relation, and density-normalized enrichment. Relation density is the fraction of off-diagonal node pairs in the encoded graphs covered by the binary relation indicator Mr, and attention mass is the fraction of off-diagonal attention assigned to those pairs. Enrichment is attention mass divided by relation density, averaged across evaluated runs, layers, and heads. An enrichment value of 1 corresponds to attention allocation proportional to relation coverage, placing local edges, directed reachability, and shared-neighbor relations on a common coverage-adjusted scale. Across the four second-budget settings, the six relation channels exhibit distinct enrichment patterns. On NAS-Bench-101, forward and backward edge relations have enrichment values of approximately 1.89–1.91 and 1.78–1.81, respectively, while the directed-reachability relations are close to 0.98. The shared-successor and shared-predecessor relations reach approximately 2.20 and 2.70. On NAS-Bench-201, the edge relations remain enriched, directed reachability is close to 1.04, and the shared-predecessor relation reaches 4.67. These statistics characterize how the learned attention weights distribute mass over the local, multi-hop, and branch-related structural supports supplied to the six directional channels. 18
