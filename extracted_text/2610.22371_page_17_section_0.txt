A.2 CONFIDENCE FILTERING We use the expp1 confidence activation, i.e., c = 1 + exp(ˆc) with ˆc denoting the raw confidence output, following VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a). This allows us to apply percentile-based confidence filtering to remove points that the model predicts with low confidence, typically distributed at the far-away background or foreground boundaries. In Fig. 8, we demonstrate an application of confidence filtering. As shown in the Raw column, the direct tracking outputs are already visually clean, but may contain a small number of flying points. By applying confidence filtering, with a percentile as low as 2%, these flying points can be effectively suppressed, resulting in cleaner foreground and background content. We hereby clarify that for fair comparisons, the visualization results for any method in Fig. 4 and Fig. 6 are NOT applied with confidence-based suppression. B TRAINING PREPARATION B.1 DATASETS We distinguish two types of input frames: encoder frames, which are fed to the encoder to extract scene tokens, and query frames, from which local RGB patches are extracted to construct decoder queries. The two share the same aspect ratio but may differ in spatial resolution. For encoder frames, each raw video is maximally center-cropped to one of five predefined aspect ratios. The cropped frames are then uniformly resized to the largest resolution within a 480 × 832 bound, resulting in five encoder frame sizes: 480 × 480, 480 × 640, 480 × 832, 352 × 832, and 256 × 832. The corresponding query frames use the same cropped content before resizing, and their resolutions therefore depend on the native resolution of each dataset. The resulting encoder frame and query frame resolutions are reported in Tab. A1. We preprocess each dataset under these spatial configurations and report the approximate number of resulting subclips for each configuration in the “Est. Subclip Count Per Size” column. During training, datasets are sampled according to the probabilities reported in the final column. In practice, we tolerate a slight distortion between them. To vary the effective field of view of query patches, we apply downsizing-only augmentation to the query frames. For each training clip, we first resize them within a 960 × 1664 bound, then sample a side length scale factor from [0.5, 1.0], and resize all query frames by the same factor. This augmentation is applied only to the query frames for the decoder, while the pre-encoded latents remain unchanged. We don’t apply random content cropping, color jittering or Gaussian blurring. B.2 PREPROCESSING AND SAMPLING Preprocessing. Each raw video is subsampled into 49-frame clips with frame intervals ranging from 1 to 4. We then pre-encode each clip with the Wan-VAE (Wan et al., 2025) and store the normalized latents. For each clip, camera poses are defined in the first-frame relative world coordinate. The 3D po- sition GT is expressed in the tcam camera coordinate. We adopt mean normalization as in Mo- tionCrafter (Zhu et al., 2026): valid visible 3D GT points are centered and normalized by their mean distance to the origin. Then they are transformed to tcam’s camera coordinate system. The displacement and normal GTs are represented in the same camera coordinate system. 17
