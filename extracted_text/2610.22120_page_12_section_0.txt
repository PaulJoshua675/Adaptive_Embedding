Path generation. The path set P(s, p) is produced by ray tracing the scene geometry for the transmitter–receiver pair. Each path is characterized by its interaction sequence with the structures of the scene: the direct path when it exists, first- and higher-order specular reflections off building facades, and diffractions at building edges. B ILL-POSEDNESS OF THE INVERSE PROBLEM This appendix formalizes the three sources of non-uniqueness described in the main text. B.1 EXACT INVARIANCE OUTSIDE THE PROPAGATION-RELEVANT SET By the definition equation 7, cells outside ak do not participate in any path of observation k, so the likelihood of that observation does not depend on them; aggregating over the observation set gives the invariance stated in the main text. Its consequence for the number of scenes consistent with a measurement is the following. Proposition 1 (Size of the consistent family). Fix an observation k and let s⋆be the true scene. Let Sk(Hk) = {s : F(s, pk) = Hk} be the set of scenes reproducing the noiseless measurement exactly. Then |Sk(Hk)| ≥2|ac k|. Proof. By the definition of ak, no cell in ac k participates in any path of P(s⋆, pk); the path param- eters (αℓ, τℓ, θℓ, ϕℓ) therefore do not depend on sac k, and neither does the path sum. Hence for any subset J ⊆ac k the scene s⋆⊕eJ , obtained by flipping the cells in J , satisfies F(s⋆⊕eJ , pk) = Hk and lies in Sk(Hk).
