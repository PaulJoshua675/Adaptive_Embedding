Passage-based retrieval addresses the limitations of whole-document representations by splitting documents into smaller textual units and scoring them independently. The passage scores can then be aggregated to produce a document-level ranking. A document can thus receive a high score when one or more of its passages are considered relevant to the query. Early studies showed that passage-level retrieval can outperform document-level retrieval [1]. Later work has extended this approach through language models, probabilistic passage scoring, and alternative strategies for aggregating passage scores into document-level rankings [11–13]. 2
