Context compression in long-running applica- tions of large language models (LLMs) must preserve goal-relevant information, often be- fore specific downstream questions are known. We introduce GACC-Bench, a benchmark for Goal-Adaptive Context Compression that evaluates how reliably LLMs adapt informa- tion retention to specified high-level goals. For each source context, we derive one or more high-level goals by grouping source- grounded questions according to shared down- stream objectives. The benchmark contains 500 goals and 2,571 questions over 281 docu- ment and agent-trace contexts from seven col- lections spanning diverse domains, with all instances reviewed by human experts. The compressor receives a source context, a goal, and a context-length budget, with the associ- ated evaluation questions withheld. A fixed reader answers these questions individually using the same compressed context. Our evaluation of ten LLM compressors reveals substantial gaps to full-context performance even for the best-performing model, partic- ularly in supporting correct answers to ev- ery question associated with a goal. Com- pared with goal-agnostic compression, target- goal conditioning consistently improves accu- racy, whereas mismatched-goal conditioning reduces it.
