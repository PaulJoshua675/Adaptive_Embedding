3. Materials and Methods Figure 1 illustrates how SparK-SAR adapts SparK’s sparse masked pretraining scheme (Tian et al., 2023) to S1GRD data under constrained compute. A 224×224 two-channel input tile, comprising VV (vertical transmit–vertical receive) and VH (vertical transmit–horizontal receive) backscatter in dB scale, is patch-masked at random, and the encoder processes only the visible patches through sparse convolutions rather than the full grid. The output feature maps are passed to a reconstruction decoder tasked with predicting the masked patches. Once pretraining finishes, the decoder is discarded and only the encoder is used for downstream finetuning. Figure 1: The proposed SparK-SAR pretraining framework. The ConvNeXt-RS encoder produces 14×14 feature maps (stride 16×) from 224×224 S1GRD inputs. Only visible (unmasked) patches are processed by the encoder via sparse convolutions; the decoder reconstructs the full image including masked regions. 3.1. ConvNeXt-RS Encoder ConvNeXt-Tiny’s (Liu et al., 2022) default stem downsamples by 4×4 before three further 2×2 stages, taking a 224-pixel input through 56→28→14→7 down to a final 7×7 feature map at overall stride 32×; every output token therefore corresponds to a 32×32-pixel patch of the input, coarse enough to erase spatial detail that dense SAR tasks such as segmentation and change detection depend on. ConvNeXt-RS keeps everything about this backbone fixed except the stem: halving its stride to 2×2 changes the spatial trajectory to 224 →112 →56 →28 →14 (stride 16×) (1) so the final feature map doubles in each spatial dimension to 14×14, four times as many to- kens as the stock 7×7 output, each now covering a finer 16×16-pixel patch instead of 32×32; the overall stride drops correspondingly from 32× to 16×. Depth, per-stage block counts, and channel widths are all untouched, so the parameter count stays at 27.8M, and the change is fully compatible with SparK’s sparse convolution mechanism operating on visible patches at every stage. Figures 2 and 3 contrast the resulting stage-by-stage feature maps for the two encoders: ConvNeXt-Tiny’s Stage 4 output has largely lost spatial structure by 7×7, whereas ConvNeXt- RS’s 14×14 Stage 4 output still shows recognizable scene structure. 3.2. Reconstruction Decoders SparK-SAR supports interchangeable reconstruction decoders. We evaluate three designs. UNet-768, SparK’s original decoder, is a hierarchical UNet (Ronneberger et al., 2015) that aggregates multi-scale encoder features through 768 channels.
