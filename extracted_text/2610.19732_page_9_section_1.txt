We formulated a TTFS-like model that learns in the time dimension with synaptic delays, and applied it to a dataset with strong temporal dependencies. The Delay Neural Network (DeNN) formulated in real time allows for a theoreti- cal control of the latency, through a simple bounding of its synaptic parameters. By varying a hyperparameter after the learning, we were able to experiment on the tradeoff between accuracy and latency. However, we found that the archi- tecture of the network (number of layers and number of neurons per layer) had a strong impact on the latency. While the theorem 3.2 is true from one layer to another, it fails to generalize to the whole network. Hence, with deeper DeNN models the latency vs accuracy tradeoff may be harder to study analytically. Nevertheless, we show high accuracy with relatively low latency on a temporal dataset. This result could be decreased to lower latency with further understanding of the model. Furthermore, we analysed the long-range dependencies learnt by the model and showed that the learning process allo- cated the long-term memory to some of the neurons only.
