The analysis included 3,040 outputs from 190 cases, eight models, and two response formats. Because rounds 4 and 5 were uncommon, outputs were grouped as one, two, or at least three completed rounds. For each group, we report the mean and population standard deviation (SD) of the output-level mean across numeric scores for criteria 1–8, the proportion of outputs with a mean below 2, and the criterion 10 case-complexity score. The round count described review of the retained report, whereas the Judge assessed the subsequent final answer and did not receive the round count or stopping reason. 3.11 Calibration of Judge Guidance We optimized one operational prompt, gk, for each criterion k ∈{1, . . . , 9} using GigaEvo and MAP-Elites [Khrulkov et al., 2025, Mouret and Clune, 2015, Lehman et al., 2022, Lim et al., 2024]. The general Judge instructions and all criterion definitions, scales, applicability rules, schemas, and reference policies were fixed. Candidate prompts were limited to 800 characters and could not migrate across criteria. The prespecified 110-case sampling frame was partitioned by DOI into 80 evolutionary-training cases and 30 internal- validation cases. All completed targets and ratings from one DOI remained in the same subset. Evolution used only eligible structured targets and physician ratings from the training subset. Ratings from the internal-validation subset were withheld from mutation and parent selection. MAE expresses Judge–physician disagreement directly in scale points and is less sensitive to large errors than squared loss [Gneiting, 2011, Willmott and Matsuura, 2005]. It is commonly used for ordinal prediction [Baccianella et al., 2009], but assumes meaningful distances between score categories and does not fully characterize agreement; we therefore treated it as an error summary, not a complete validity measure. The search used nine isolated criterion archives (capacity 540), initialized with two candidates per criterion. GPT-OSS- 120B generated mutations at temperature 0.3 with a 4,096-token limit. We retained 225 mutants (25 per criterion) from at most 320 attempts. For each criterion, the lowest-MAE prompt was selected, with ties resolved by repeat stability, length, and program identifier. Selected prompts were re-evaluated on training and internal-validation cases with fresh seeds and compared with no-guidance, initial-prompt, and training-median baselines before semantic review. Judge calibration was summarized by criterion-specific MAE against individual physician ratings, repeat-to-repeat SD, numeric coverage, and signed error where reported. The held-out clinical-quality analyses were descriptive. We retained the original 0–3 criterion scores and reported numeric means, population SDs, outcome distributions, and numeric coverage.
