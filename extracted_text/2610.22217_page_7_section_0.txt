We adopt Qwen3-VL as the backbone due to its competitive per- formance across the evaluated capability groups. Nevertheless, existing models often exhibit uneven performance across different capabilities, making it challenging to simultaneously maintain strong performance in perception, OCR and chart understanding, symbolic reasoning, and multi-image reasoning. In contrast, SCOPED consistently strengthens the backbone across all four capability groups. We further apply SCOPED to Qwen3-VL-4B, where it achieves an average improvement of 3.90 points and surpasses several larger open-source models. The consistent gains observed with both the 4B and 8B backbones demonstrate that the effectiveness of SCOPED generalizes across model scales and remains robust across diverse visual capabilities. 5.2 ABLATION STUDY As shown in Table 2, we conduct ablation studies to investigate the effectiveness of different multi- task training strategies and the individual components of SCOPED. We first compare SCOPED with SFT and Mix-RL to assess the benefits of our training paradigm. We then ablate key components of SCOPED to examine their respective contributions. For reference, we also report the performance of capability-specific experts, each of which is optimized exclusively with task-specific rewards. The evaluation results show that SFT underperforms the zero-shot backbone, suggesting that jointly fine-tuning on capability-specific data can introduce interference across capabilities. Although Mix- RL improves overall performance, it still underperforms the corresponding specialized experts on symbolic visual reasoning and multi-image reasoning, indicating that joint optimization may limit the acquisition of specialized capabilities. In contrast, SCOPED achieves an average improvement of 3.82 points over Mix-RL, demonstrating the effectiveness of separating capability acquisition from capability integration. The skill-augmented expert results further show that matching each expert with its corresponding skill consistently improves the target capability, while its effects on non- target capabilities are limited and occasionally negative. This result highlights the capability-specific nature of the extracted guidance and motivates explicit skill-expert matching during distillation. Furthermore, we compare two variants of SCOPED, SCOPED (Parameter Merging) and SCOPED w/o Skill Augmentation, which use parameter merging and distillation without skill augmentation, respectively, to unify multiple capability-specific experts into a single model.
