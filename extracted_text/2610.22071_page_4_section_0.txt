FLORES-200 provides parallel trans- lated sentences (NLLB Team et al., 2022). The boundary audit uses 22 languages with 1,012 sentences each, 22,264 sentences in total: 18 focal languages, namely those cov- ered by Belebele (Bandarkar et al., 2024) among the 30 FLORES-200 languages in which the Qwen3 tokenizer places a token boundary inside the largest share of non- ASCII scalars, and English, Russian, Thai, and Simplified Chinese as controls. The lookahead probe and the cost and corruption analyses use a 17-language panel in 13 Brahmic scripts: the eligible focal languages plus Thai. Amharic and Tigrinya cannot supply a substitutable mark pool, while English, Russian, and Chinese fail at least one of the two cor- ruption conditions (mark and base substitution; Section 5.5); those three languages remain reference points in the cost accounting. Converting a subword model. Bolmo combines a local encoder, a boundary predictor, the inherited global trans- former, and a local decoder. The released predictor uses one-byte lookahead during prefill, which its authors found largely sufficient to match subword tokenization (Minix- hofer et al., 2026); the decoder jointly predicts a byte and its patch-end flag. The output alphabet has (4+256)×2 = 520 identifiers, including special tokens. During Stage 1, the initial distillation stage, parent cuts supervise the predictor; at inference, the predictor supplies its own boundaries. The parent tokenizer enters this architecture through three mechanisms (Figure 3). First, the released input pipeline applies the parent tokenizer and concatenates the bytes of its pieces, preserving its NFC normalization but discarding the piece boundaries from the byte stream. Second, a reversed byte trie retrieves the longest parent-vocabulary suffix end- ing at each byte position and adds its embedding to the byte embedding. These suffix matches need not coincide with tokens in the canonical BPE segmentation. Third, Stage 1 supervises the boundary predictor with the parent’s segmen- tation while the global transformer remains frozen. The three mechanisms must be distinguished when interpreting inherited boundaries. Bolmo’s public Qwen conversion recipe1 weights the local- encoder, cross-entropy, local-decoder, and boundary losses by [1, 1, 1, 4]. In Stage 2, which trains all parameters end to end, Bolmo can optionally supervise merged subsets of the parent’s boundaries to raise compression (Minixhofer et al., 2025, Section 5.1). Although the same implementation provides several boundary-compression modes, its Stage-1 entry point rejects gradual boundary compression.
