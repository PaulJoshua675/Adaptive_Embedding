(i) Statistical grounding: we first compute behavioral statistics (acceptance rate, reference style, genre distribution, exploration ra- tio) from the user’s interaction history; the syn- thetic dialogue is then conditioned on these statis- tics and the user’s historical conversational evi- dence. (ii) Interview rather than peer dialogue: generation is cast as an asymmetric interview, with the interviewee anchored to the user’s profile and the interviewer as a neutral elicitor probing specific dimensions (Tigunova et al., 2019), preventing mu- tual reinforcement of speculative content. (iii) Turn- level auditing: an Audit Agent flags fabrication, his- tory contradictions, and caricature (generic persona templates and invented rationalizations). Depend- ing on severity, flagged turns are excluded from pro- file enrichment, while structurally compromised di- alogues trigger regeneration. (iv) Evidence-linked enrichment: an LLM agent enriches the user’s sta- tistical profile with attributes extracted from the audited interview, with each categorical or scored attribute paired with a supporting evidence quote; human annotators verify that 94% of these enriched claims are supported by the interview. Contributions. (1) We identify the profile bot- tleneck in LLM-based user simulation. (2) We propose SimInter, combining statistical grounding, asymmetric interview dialogue, turn-level audit- ing, and evidence-linked profile enrichment; the audit uses a flag taxonomy that distinguishes un- supported claims from invented rationalizations. (3) We evaluate SimInter on 300 ReDial users (1,200 simulated conversations), with matched 100-user comparisons against statistics-only and CSHI (Zhu et al., 2024b) baselines across four complementary tracks (item diversity, population- level preference alignment, aspect richness, and linguistic variety). We find a significant increase in aspect richness over statistics-only profiles (T3, d = +0.96, Holm-adjusted p < 0.001). 2
