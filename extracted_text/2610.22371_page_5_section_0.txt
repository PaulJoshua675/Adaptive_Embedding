Taking source time tsrc as an example, we define: α(tsrc) = tsrc T −1, tsrc ∈{0, . . . , T −1}, esrc(tsrc) := (1 −α(tsrc)) esrc,0 + α(tsrc) esrc,1, (6) where esrc,0 and esrc,1 are two learnable temporal embeddings representing the start and end of the video clip, respectively. This parameterization represents each timestamp by its normalized position within the clip, making the time query independent of the absolute sequence length T and thereby enhancing the model’s temporal flexibility. Cross-Attention Decoder. The decoder consists of multiple cross-attention blocks, where the query attends to the scene tokens F at each layer. We construct the query embedding q from the continuous time query embeddings esrc, etgt, and ecam, the 2D position embedding ϕuv(u, v) and the RGB patch embedding ϕpatch(pu,v). Then we feed q and F into a cross-attention decoder D to obtain the query feature zq, which is further mapped by an MLP head H to the complete set of 4D attributes, as defined in Eqs. (3) and (4). 3.3 PLUG-IN GEOMETRY PRIOR Recent GFM-finetuned 4D perceivers (Sucar et al., 2026; Luo et al., 2026; Lin et al., 2026) benefit from geometry priors, which are learned through large-scale dense 3D pretraining. In contrast, Gen4P adapts a pretrained video generator to exploit its motion prior, while its sparse query-based supervision alone provides a weaker signal for learning geometry than the GFM-finetuned methods. To narrow this gap, we propose a natural solution within our framework: by plugging in a pretrained video-depth adapter as a frozen geometry prior, we are able to reuse knowledge learned from dense depth estimation without a separate dense 3D pretraining stage. Double-LoRA Architecture. To incorporate pretrained dense geometric knowledge, we adopt a plug-in geometry prior from a pretrained video depth estimation model, DVD (Zhang et al., 2026b), which is also built upon Wan2.1. We combine the pretrained Depth-LoRA (∆WDepth) with our 4D-LoRA (∆W4D) (Sec. 3.2.1), forming a double-LoRA architecture. For each adapted layer, the effective weight is W = WDiT + ∆W 512 Depth + ∆W 512 4D , (7) where WDiT denotes the DiT weight of the pretrained video generator, and ∆W 512 Depth and ∆W 512 4D are the depth and 4D weight updates with a rank of 512, respectively. Lightweight Training. Following prior work on retaining pretrained adapters during task adapta- tion (Yu et al., 2025), we freeze the VAE encoder, WDiT and ∆WDepth, and jointly optimize ∆W4D, the query embeddings, the query decoder, and the prediction head. Thus, the pretrained depth update is retained while a separate update is learned for 4D perception under the task objective. We point out that the double-LoRA variant requires no additional dense prediction head and has the same number of trainable parameters as the variant using 4D-LoRA alone. In Sec. 4.5, we compare several adaptation strategies, providing empirical support for our design. 3.4 TRAINING OBJECTIVE Our method predicts a full set of 4D attributes and is trained with a composite loss: L = λ3DL3D + λconfLconf + λ2DL2D + λvisLvis + λdispLdisp + λnormalLnormal, (8) where L3D, Lconf, L2D, Lvis, Ldisp, and Lnormal denote the 3D position, confidence, 2D position, visibility, 3D displacement, and surface-normal losses, respectively.
