Deployed machine learning models alter their operational environments. In generative AI, recursive training on synthetic data causes “Model Collapse” (Shumailov et al., 2024) or “Model Autophagy Disorder” (MAD) (Alemohammad et al., 2024), demonstrating how iterative feedback acts as a lossy compres- sion mechanism that monotonically degrades data entropy (Straňák, 2026). Mitigating this, although delayed by accumulating human data (Gerstgrasser et al., 2024), requires structural guardrails to break the closed-loop feedback cycle. This cycle of data degradation mirrors philo- sophical critiques regarding the epistemology of artificial intelligence. Literature introduces the concept of generative algorithmic epistemic injustice, which investigates how generative AI can undermine the integrity of collective knowl- edge and the processes we rely on to acquire, assess, and trust information (Kay et al., 2025). Grounded in social and political philosophy, this phenomenon identifies how AI systems can produce or amplify misinformation, perpetuate representational harm, and create epistemic inequities (Kay et al., 2025). Whether in the physical state space of a robotic joint or the latent space of a large language model, the mech- anism remains identical: without interventions to preserve diversity or enforce exploratory per- sistent excitation, the agent’s control authority will degrade the environment it seeks to model. Breaking the Loop. Neurology offers inspira- tion for breaking these loops: sensory inputs are subject to natural detection thresholds of vari- able entity (Kobel et al., 2021). In human pos- tural control, non-linear thresholds and intermit- tent control paradigms for the inverted pendu- lum operate on integrated vestibular and pro- prioceptive signals (Hettich et al., 2013; Het- tich et al., 2015; Tigrini et al., 2022), modali- ties mimicked in robotics by IMUs and joint en- coders. Replicating these thresholds via dead- bands can block sensory noise from propagating through the continuous control loop (Mergner, 2010; Akçay et al., 2021), breaking the loop around defined safe zones to suppress control authority without endangering stability (Lippi & Molinari, 2020). Beyond rigid thresholds, cognitive neuroscience provides a framework for loop-breaking via Predictive Processing and Ac- tive Inference (Friston et al., 2017; Clark, 2013).
