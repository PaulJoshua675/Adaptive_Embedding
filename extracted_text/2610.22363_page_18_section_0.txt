Hand visibility. We evaluate whether each hand is in view at each frame, using the reference-hand eligibility defined above. For Ours, the frozen action decoder’s joint-visibility probabilities ˆvt,j give a positive hand prediction when maxj ˆvt,j ≥0.5. This is separate from the MANO head’s hand-level presence output ˆpt and its presence loss. Baselines use their same-side hand detections whose projected mesh boxes intersect the image. We pool these hand-frame decisions to compute precision, recall, and F1, without an IoU matching requirement. For Ours, these visibility decisions also select the action predictions before mesh-overlap matching. Visibility F1 measures hand-in-frame classification; joint errors additionally evaluate the spatially matched hand states. D.4 GOAL-IMAGE CONDITIONING Both PLAN settings receive the task instruction and initial image. The default additionally conditions on cgoal = IT ; the w/o goal image setting uses cgoal = ∅. The goal image fixes the terminal video latent during sampling, while hand and camera trajectories are generated jointly. Figure 9 shows the conditioning masks. Encoding the reference images. At inference, we repeat the goal image into an 81-frame constant clip and encode it with the video VAE, starting with a fresh temporal cache. Its final latent supplies the terminal observation at ℓ= 21. The initial image is encoded separately in the same way, using its first latent at ℓ= 1. These encodes use the two reference images alone. Both observed latents stay fixed during denoising, and the VAE decodes the entire output video. The terminal latent corresponds to frames t = 78–81; the intervening frames t = 2–77 are freely generated. The final column of Figure 7 shows the supplied goal image as a reference. During training, observation masks select clean latents from VAE-encoded training clips. The same mask construction keeps observed positions clean and excludes them from the video loss. Action supervision uses the annotations aligned to all video frames, including the terminal interval. At inference, hand and camera trajectories are predicted from queries for all frames. Evaluation interval. The VLM evaluates the complete video, including its transition into the goal segment, with the goal image supplied separately as a reference. Our qualitative analysis compares object placement before this segment begins. Appendix F.3 also reports video–action consistency over the freely generated interval.
