Knowledge distillation. Classical distillation fits a student to a teacher’s output distribution on a fixed corpus [15] or to sequences the teacher generates [16]. On-policy formulations supervise text generated by the model being trained, following the imitation-learning argument for learning on the learner’s own state distribution [9–11]; the idea has been applied to reasoning models, including with a model as its own teacher [17], and knowledge fusion extends distillation to several source models [18]. Recent work asks which rollouts on-policy distillation should use, selecting a few hard problems [19], weighting problems by the student’s pass rate [20], or balancing student-generated states across teachers [12]. All of these are optimization procedures. TF-OPD keeps two of their ingredients, a match-the-teacher objective and statistics gathered on model-generated text, and removes the optimization by solving the objective layer by layer. Our finding that the composition of the rollouts decides the outcome is a closed-form counterpart of theirs. Model merging. Weight averaging across fine-tuning runs improves accuracy at no inference cost [21]; task arithmetic composes updates relative to a common initialization [13], and TIES-Merging and DARE reduce interference among summed updates [22, 23].
