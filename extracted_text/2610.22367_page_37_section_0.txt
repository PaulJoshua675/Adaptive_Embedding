Phase estimation on U therefore yields the energies; because it applies U many times, the cost of one application sets the cost of the simulation. We use the Fe2S2 integral data published by Li and Chan [67], which models 30 active electrons in 20 spatial orbitals. Iron–sulfur clusters are important strongly correlated targets, in which many electronic configurations contribute significantly to the ground state [68, 69]. Each spatial orbital has a spin-up and a spin-down mode, so these 20 orbitals become 40 spin-orbitals and, after the Jordan–Wigner mapping, 40 qubits. Discarding terms with |wℓ| < 10−4 Ha, following the truncation practice of sparse qubitization [64], leaves L = 41,052 signed Pauli operators. State-of-the-art resource estimates for this system use double-factorized tensor hypercontraction (DFTHC), which compresses the Hamiltonian into factors and orbital rotations before circuit construction [69]. However, studying the DFTHC walk operator would require validating networks of continuously parameterized Givens rotations acting on superposed states, which goes beyond the kickmix framework. We therefore focus on the more direct sparse compilation, which stores the Hamiltonian as an explicit list of its Pauli terms. In this section we study the data-dependent part of one application of U: the alias selection inside Prepare, the Select application, and the erasure of the selection. Directly preparing the unequal amplitudes in Equation (69) is expensive, so a standard construction uses the alias method [4, 64, 70, 71]. Classical preprocessing spreads the probabilities |wℓ|/λ over L equal-sized buckets, one per term. Bucket i is shared between term i and one other term alt(i), and a 16-bit threshold keep(i) sets the split. The circuit draws a bucket i and a 16-bit value τ uniformly.
