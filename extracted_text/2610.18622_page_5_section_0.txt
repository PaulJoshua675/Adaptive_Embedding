Each chunk can be enriched with contextual information, including the document title, summary, and titles of all enclosing structural units (e.g., chapter, section, or subsection). To evaluate structural segmentation, we focus on segmentation levels from the document com- ponent level to the enumeration level. Document-level retrieval has already been evaluated in a public benchmark and is used as a baseline for comparison [8]. When applied to the two test collections, the segmentation procedure does not succeed for all documents. In NormasTCU, 534 documents (3.69% of the collection) do not follow the required hierarchical structure. The same occurs for 34,380 documents in Ulysses-RFCorpus, which also contains 126 additional documents that, despite following the standard structure, contain struc- tural deviations that prevent the parser from identifying their hierarchical components. Thus, 34,506 Ulysses-RFCorpus documents (32.65% of the collection) cannot be represented using the defined hierarchy. All documents that cannot be segmented are represented by a single retrieval unit containing their full text, so that every document has a representation in every index. 3.3 Document representation strategies Using the multi-layered segmentation described in Section 3.2, we evaluate four document repre- sentation strategies based on two dimensions: (i) which chunks are indexed and (ii) what context is added to each chunk. The first strategy, Hierarchical Chunks, corresponds to the multi-layered representation pro- posed by Lima [23]. It includes all chunks generated by the segmentation (Chunks 1–10 in Figure 3), with each chunk enriched with the document title, summary, and titles of enclosing structural units. In Figure 3, Chunks 1–3 lack enclosing structural units and are therefore enriched only with the document title and summary, which we refer to as the document metadata throughout this paper: the expression [Bill No. x/xxxx | Summary of the bill.] is prepended to these chunks. In contrast, Chunks 4–10 are nested under Chapter I and are therefore also enriched with its title: [Bill No. x/xxxx | Summary of the bill. | CHAPTER I Title of chapter I] is prepended to these chunks. The other three strategies retain only the Basic Unit chunks (articles) and the Document Component chunks that are not part of the normative text, such as the justification of legislative proposals. We refer to these chunks as core chunks. In Figure 3, the core chunks are Chunks 4 and 8 (Articles 1 and 2) and Chunk 2 (justification).
