Cohort 0: Healthy Normal Control. Represents unperturbed sleep homeostasis (the biological process regulating the accumulation of somnogenic sleep drive during wakefulness and its exponential discharge during sleep) and circadian entrainment (the synchronization of endogenous circadian oscillations to the 24-hour environmental photoperiod) [5, 16]. Circadian phase is synchronized with the external photoperiod (∆ϕ = 0.0 h), and circadian amplitude reflects standard homeostatic balance (A = 0.38) [10, 1]. Hypothalamic flip-flop parameters satisfy the bistability criterion (wav = 2.1, wva = 2.0, yielding wavwva = 4.2 > 0.8), orexin tone is intact (Ωtone = 1.0, corresponding to normal cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) orexin-A concentrations ≥200 pg/mL [11]), arousal bias is neutral (Iarousal = 0.0 pA), cortical inhibition is intact (γinh = 1.0), and REM entry latency adheres to the physiological NREM cycle duration (θrem = 70.0 min) [15, 7]. Cohort 1: Delayed Sleep-Wake Phase Disorder (CRSWD). Captures intrinsic circadian dysreg- ulation characterized by a delayed endogenous phase angle. In clinical cohorts, patients exhibit a 3 to 6-hour delay in the timing of dim light melatonin onset (DLMO) and core body temperature nadir [31, 38]. This is formalized by setting ∆ϕ = +4.0 h. Furthermore, clinical studies document an attenuated circadian pacemaking amplitude in delayed phase syndrome [31, 38], parameterized here as an approximate 50% reduction in circadian amplitude (A = 0.20), which impairs morning wakefulness and delays evening somnolence (the physiological drive toward sleep). Cohort 2: Chronic [Psychophysiological] Insomnia. Models conditioned cognitive and auto- nomic hyperarousal [30]. Chronic insomnia patients exhibit elevated sympathetic nervous activity, increased nocturnal cortisol secretion, and higher whole-body metabolic rates [30]. In DyssomniaNN, this allostatic load (the cumulative physiological wear and tear resulting from sustained neuroen- docrine and autonomic hyperactivation) is parameterized by a chronic depolarizing arousal bias (Iarousal = +0.60 pA). Concurrently, subcortical sleep-inducing strength is compromised by reducing VLPO inhibitory efficacy to wav = 0.95 [33, 30]. Because wav · wva = 0.95 × 2.0 = 1.9, the flip-flop switch remains above the critical bifurcation point (0.8) but operates close to the boundary, making the sleep attractor (the stable equilibrium state corresponding to consolidated sleep in phase space) shallow and vulnerable to spontaneous awakenings. Cohort 3: Paradoxical Insomnia (Sleep State Misperception). Characterized by a dissociation between subcortical sleep staging and cortical spectral activity [14, 20]. Subcortical hypothalamic parameters remain identical to the healthy control cohort (∆ϕ = 0.0 h, Ωtone = 1.0, wav = 2.1). However, proton magnetic resonance spectroscopy (1H-MRS) studies in insomnia patients docu- ment substantial (20%–30%) reductions in cortical GABA concentrations [28]. In our model, this is formalized by scaling cortical interneuron synaptic gain down to γinh = 0.25. This selective cortical disinhibition prevents the suppression of fast recurrent synaptic currents during slow-wave sleep, generating pathological high-frequency beta-band intrusion (15.0–30.0 Hz) while preserving subcortical slow-wave gating [25, 20]. 8
