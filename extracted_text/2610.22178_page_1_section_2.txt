Vision-language-action models (VLAs) have become an effective paradigm for language-conditioned robot control. A pretrained vision–language model (VLM) encodes camera views and instruction, based on which a smaller action expert predicts the next actions (Black et al., 2024; Physical Intelligence et al., 2025; Bjorck et al., 2025; Shukor et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2026; Ma et al., 2026). The strongest VLAs today are pretrained partly on proprietary data, and neither these data nor the pretraining pipelines are released (Physical Intelligence et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2026), making it hard to reproduce their results. Since each new model changes architecture, data and training recipe at once, gains cannot be traced back to a single choice. With REKI, we release the full pipeline, from data curation to deployment. REKI has a simple design, is pretrained on public data only and sets a new Pareto frontier between performance and model size. It pairs a VLM with a flow-matching action expert, and a memory mechanism gives the policy access to its recent past. Preserving past context can improve control (Octo Model Team et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025; Chi et al., 2025), yet most VLAs predict actions from the current observation alone (Kim et al., 2024; Black et al., 2024; Physical Intelligence et al., 2025; Bjorck et al., 2025; Shukor et al., 2025; Lee et al., 2025; Qu et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026). Existing ways of incorporating such context, however, often introduce substantial overhead (Fig. 2): feeding past frames to the VLM adds their image tokens to every VLM pass (Jang et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2026), recurrent methods require sequential training (Cherepanov et al., 2026; Jiang et al., 2026), and others introduce additional, dedicated memory modules to the policy (Shi et al., 2026; Koo et al., 2026). REKI summarises past context through a small set of registers (Darcet et al., 2024) in the action expert. The registers of each frame are cached across chunks (Fig. 3). Each frame is thus encoded only once and no separate module, sequential unroll during training or test-time update mechanisms are necessary. This yields clear gains at almost no overhead, and our 2.4B model REKI ranks first among VLAs pretrained on public data on LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin Clean2Rand and RoboCasa365 (Fig. 1), and generalises to real robots. Our ablations show that memory consistently improves performance, with the most pronounced gains for the first past frame (Fig. 5a).
