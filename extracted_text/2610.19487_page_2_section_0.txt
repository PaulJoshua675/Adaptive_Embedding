We instead ask how much of a neuron’s computa- tion is resolved at a given observational scale, which distinctions are erased by projection, and which reap- pear as the ruler is refined. We approach this prob- lem through an information-theoretic lens together with constructive counterexamples in which the efficiency of neural-network architectures depends on the representa- tion available at each scale. These examples show that the same coarse input–output relation can be compati- ble with markedly different internal computations, even when the reduced description is highly predictive. The coastline problem for a neuron is therefore not whether computation is visible or hidden, but how its apparent structure changes with the scale at which it is observed. II. METHODS & RESULTS A. What a Direct Model Measures Let Xε = (Xε,1, . . . , Xε,m) denote the effective inputs visible with ruler ε, and let Y ∈{0, 1} be the output. Larger ε denotes a coarser description. At each resolu- tion, define the direct conditional family Qθ,ε(y = 1 | x) = σ " b + X i ui(xi) # , (1) where each ui is an arbitrary one-coordinate function. For binary inputs this reduces to the familiar logistic model b+P i wixi. The generalized form becomes impor- tant when coarse variables possess several distinct states: assigning three categorical states the numerical labels 0, 1, 2, for example, should not impose an artificial linear ordering on their effects. Allowing an independent value of ui for each state makes direct dependence invariant to such arbitrary categorical coding. Let Q⋆ ε be the information projection of the true con- ditional distribution onto Eq. (1). Using base-two loga- rithms, define Stot(ε) = H(Y ), (2) Strue(ε) = H(Y | Xε), (3) Sdir(ε) = −EPε log2 Q⋆ ε(Y | Xε). (4) Here Stot is the uncertainty of the output before observ- ing the inputs, Strue is the irreducible conditional uncer- tainty at resolution ε (explicitly allowing for all syner- gistic and higher-order interactions present in the true distribution Pϵ), and Sdir is the uncertainty remaining under the best additive-logit description at that resolu- tion. The last quantity is a conditional cross-entropy. Moment matching connects it to the maximum-entropy expression (like what was used in Ref.11), while the cross- entropy form makes the decomposition explicit: Iavail = Stot −Strue = I(Y ; Xε), (5) Idir = Stot −Sdir, (6) Ihigher = Sdir −Strue (7) = EXεDKL [Pε(Y | Xε) ∥Q⋆ ε(Y | Xε)] . (8) This decomposition relies on the algebraic difference be- tween the direct model’s cross-entropy and the true con- ditional entropy in Eq. (7). Expanding these logarith- mic terms reveals that this difference is mathematically identical to the conditional Kullback–Leibler (KL) diver- gence. By combining the expectations, the subtraction of −EPε log2 Pε from −EPε log2 Q⋆ ε yields EPε log2(Pε/Q⋆ ε), which is precisely the conditional divergence in Eq. (8). Thus Ihigher is precisely the conditional information missed by the optimal direct family. It measures non- additive conditional structure relative to that family rather than a particular notion of information-theoretic synergy. By Gibbs’ inequality, KL divergence is non-negative, so Sdir ≥Strue. The direct family also contains the intercept-only model Q(Y ) = P(Y ), so optimality of Q⋆ ε gives Sdir ≤Stot. Hence Iavail = Idir + Ihigher, Stot ≥Sdir ≥Strue. (9) The available information therefore partitions exactly into the portion captured by the direct model and a non- negative interaction residual. Two resultant ratios answer different questions: R(ε) = Idir Stot , (10) F(ε) = Idir Iavail = 1 −Ihigher Iavail , (Iavail > 0). (11) R measures the predictive information captured by the direct model relative to the total uncertainty of the out- put. F, by contrast, asks what fraction of the information available at the chosen resolution is captured by the di- rect family. Since Iavail ≤Stot, we have R ≤F whenever Iavail > 0. This distinction between R(ε) and F(ε) is central un- der coarse-graining. Projection cannot increase Iavail, but it can alter how the surviving information is distributed between Idir and Ihigher. If non-additive structure is dis- carded more rapidly than direct structure, F can ap- proach unity even while the total predictive information decreases. Consequently, F can rise toward 100% even as the total-entropy ratio R falls. A high direct-information fraction F at a coarse scale therefore does not prove that the underlying biological mechanism is simple; it indi- cates only that little of the information surviving at that ruler lies outside the direct family. The coarse ruler may be blind to the higher-order interactions that are erased by projection. The reported quantity in Ref.11 is our R, the fraction of total output entropy explained by direct dependen- cies. The distinction between R and F will be important below.
