We study three open MoE families and analyze one layer per model, selected by routing divergence. Personalization features may also arise at other layers, and our con- clusions may not transfer to other MoE designs (e.g., shared experts or fine-grained expert segmen- tation). The dense counterparts are matched by active or total parameter count, but differ in pre- training data and recipes, so architectural effects cannot be fully separated from training differences. Data and notion of persona. Multifaceted- Collection describes preferences through synthetic system messages over four dimensions, with cate- gorical, long-tailed subdimension labels. This cap- tures group-level preferences rather than individual users, and our experiments are limited to English. Personalization signals in real user histories may be noisier and more entangled than in this controlled setting. Crosscoder fidelity. Crosscoders reconstruct activations imperfectly, and reconstruction quality varies across models (with fractions of variance explained of 0.80 for OLMoE, 0.74 for Qwen3- 30B-A3B, and 0.65 for Mixtral, whose activations have much larger magnitudes and heavier tails). In- formation in the reconstruction error is invisible to our latent-level analyses, and the set of personal- ized latents depends on the decoder-norm thresh- old. BatchTopK training and latent scaling mitigate known sparsity artifacts (Minder et al., 2025), but do not rule them out entirely. Probing. Persona concepts are properties of whole responses, whereas sparse probing labels individual tokens, thereby introducing label noise and lowering absolute scores across all models. Se- lecting the best of many components favors MoE models, which have more candidates (layers × ex- perts); we mitigate this by selecting on test data and reporting test scores, but selection effects may remain, particularly for k = 1. Probes measure whether a concept is decodable, not whether the model uses it. Expert attribution and causality. Expert sets are derived from routing frequencies at firing to- kens and are therefore correlational; they depend on the number of examples, the selection rule, and the background routing rate, and their stability is moderate. Evaluation of generation. ROUGE-L and ME- TEOR measure overlap with reference responses and are imperfect proxies for personalization: a response can satisfy a preference while diverg- ing from the reference wording, as our steer- ing results illustrate. Sampling-based generation adds variance. Automated explanations rely on an LLM explainer and scorer (Llama-3.3-70B- Instruct), whose own biases may shape the descrip- tions.
