Its action is a block route sampled from native attention mass inside a bidirectional diffusion transformer, its Gaussian velocity likelihood updates the dense generator, and the route disappears before inference. 3 PRELIMINARIES RPO fine-tunes a pretrained visual generator that already has a deterministic sampler. This section fixes the notation for the sampler and the reward, introduces attention over token blocks, and defines where RPO routes attention. Denoising trajectory and reward. Let c denote a text prompt, and let N denote both the number of denoising transitions and the largest noise level. Rollout i starts from Gaussian noise and follows the deterministic sampler: τi = (xN i , xN−1 i , . . . , x0 i ), xN i ∼N(0, I), yi = Dec(x0 i ). (1) Here τi is the reverse trajectory, and the noise level t ∈{N, . . . , 0} decreases during generation. Each transition is an Euler step of the learned velocity field, xt−1 i = xt i + ∆t vθ(xt i, t, c), where vθ is the velocity predicted by the generator with trainable parameters θ and ∆t < 0 is the step of the noise schedule. The frozen decoder Dec maps the terminal latent x0 i to the visual output yi, which is an image or a video. A black-box reward model Rd then scores the output on reward dimension d, giving ri,d = Rd(yi, c). Training uses these reward values but never differentiates through Rd. Attention over token blocks. The generator is a diffusion transformer that splits each latent into M visual tokens, one per spatial patch of an image or spatiotemporal patch of a video. In every self-attention layer and head, each token emits a query, a key, and a value vector. The attention weights of a query are a softmax over its scaled inner products with the keys. Dense attention lets every query attend to every key. Block-sparse attention coarsens this pattern. It arranges the tokens in a sequence, cuts the sequence into Q contiguous token blocks of B tokens, and applies a binary block mask µ ∈{0, 1}Q×Q. A query token in block q attends only to the key tokens of blocks j with µq,j = 1, and its softmax renormalizes over those keys. We order the tokens along a locality-preserving Gilbert curve, so each block is a compact image or video patch, and both generators use B = 128. We call the set of key blocks that query block q keeps its route, and the choice of routes routing. Sparse-attention methods choose routes to save computation. RPO instead samples routes as its exploration action during training and restores dense attention at inference. Routing sites and units. Routing acts at a small fixed set of noise levels Troute ⊆{1, . . . , N}, the routed levels, which every rollout shares. These are early levels, where a route can still change the final output, and every other level runs dense attention. At each routed level, every query block makes one routing decision, and all routed attention layers and heads apply it. A random subset of transformer blocks, the anchor layers, stays dense at each routed level. A routing unit is one such decision, namely the route of query block q at level t, written u = (t, q). Each query block keeps a deterministic safety set Sq = {q}, its own block. The remaining blocks form the candidate set: Cq = {1, . . . , Q} \ Sq. (2) The route of every unit contains Sq. The policy decides which blocks of Cq it adds, or whether query block q attends densely at that level. Every query therefore keeps its local context, and its softmax never runs over an empty set. Appendix A details the traversal, the blocks, and the executed mask. This setup confines all stochasticity to a few routed transitions, while the prompt, the sampler, and the safety block stay deterministic. 4 ROUTING POLICY OPTIMIZATION Sampler-space methods explore by injecting noise into the reverse trajectory.
