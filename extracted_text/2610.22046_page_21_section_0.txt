The failure-type classifier uses the same summation and binarization with a threshold set for each latent. The label of a problem is its failure type in the broad collection, capability gap or PIT. At 4B the population is a class-balanced sample of 1,722 problems within the difficulty range 7.0 to 9.5: 839 capability gaps and 883 PITs. At 8B it is all 6,372 such problems: 2,749 capability gaps and 3,623 PITs. The scored trace is not always the trace that set the problem’s label. At 4B it differs from that trace for 971 of the 1,722 problems. The scored trace is not a verified failure for 13.6% of problems at 4B and 20.3% at 8B, because of the grading defects described under Label corrections. Baselines and evaluation. The baselines use no hidden states. They are built from six feature sets: the question text with problem difficulty, trace counts and lengths, the question text with difficulty and the counts and lengths combined, the text of the scored trace, five statistics of the model’s own token log-probabilities on that trace, and the first five sets together, each fitted by logistic regression and by gradient-boosted trees. The strongest of these twelve baselines is taken in each repetition. Every model uses the same five folds grouped by problem, and the five-fold cross-validation is repeated with six seeds; the margin is applied to the mean over the six repetitions of the difference from the strongest baseline, and each repetition has a problem-clustered bootstrap interval. The same pipeline applied to the rectified hidden states at layers 19 to 24, summed over tokens, gives the comparison with hidden states; the transcoder input has 983,040 dimensions against 15,360 (4B) and 24,576 (8B) for the hidden states, and no random encoding of equal width was tested. Reordering the rows of identical inputs moves a baseline’s AUROC by about 0.01. The text baselines and the comparison with hidden states address two results on probes of sparse features. Between 45 and 90 percent of the sparse-autoencoder features that activate more on reasoning text than on other text also activate when a few associated tokens are inserted into non-reasoning text [Ma et al., 2026]. Probes on sparse-autoencoder features do not improve on logistic regression on the dense activations, on average over 113 classification datasets [Kantamneni et al., 2025]. A linear probe on the hidden state of the last prompt token separates competition problems that a reasoning model fails under greedy decoding from those it solves, before the trace begins [Zhang et al., 2025b]. A problem that one greedy run fails can be a capability gap, a near miss or a PIT under the failure types defined here. Results and label sensitivity. With the stored labels, the failure-type classifier meets the margin at 8B and not at 4B (Table 6). The difference from the strongest baseline is +0.055 at 8B, with every repetition at or above the margin. It is +0.049 at 4B, with three of six repetitions at or above the margin. Every interval excludes zero and contains the margin (Table 7). The same classifier on hidden states at the same layers scores lower at both scales. No probe in Table 3 is trained on this label. Removing problems whose scored trace may carry a wrong label raises the difference at both scales. The removal covers every problem whose scored trace carries a grading defect and, at 4B, every problem whose scored trace has a different failure type from its label. The removal leaves 1,294 problems at 4B and 5,078 at 8B and raises the difference to +0.065 and +0.083. With these problems removed, every repetition is at or above the margin at both scales. The removed problems are 62% (4B) and 65% (8B) capability gaps, and the baselines lose more AUROC on the remaining problems than the classifier does. The rise in the difference is consistent with two explanations that this analysis does not separate: grading defects that the question text identifies, and a subset of capability gaps that the question text identifies. The transcoders reconstruct the MLP outputs at layers 19 to 24 with a fraction of variance explained, pooled over tokens, of 0.338 to 0.525 at 4B and 0.366 to 0.564 at 8B.
