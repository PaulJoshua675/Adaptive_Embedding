Speech conveys multiple complementary types of information beyond its linguistic content. In addition to the lexical message, an utterance carries information about the speaker identity, the spoken language, emotional state, speaking style, and other paralinguistic characteristics. These complementary attributes are essential for a broad range of applications including multilingual speech retrieval, speaker verification, language identification, conversational agents, speech analytics, and affective computing. Nevertheless, they are generally modeled independently, leading to multiple task-specific encoders that duplicate computation and fail to exploit the common structure underlying speech. Recent advances in self-supervised learning (SSL) have profoundly changed speech representation learning. Foundation models such as wav2vec 2.0 [1], HuBERT [2], WavLM [3], and w2v-BERT [4] are trained on thousands of hours of unlabeled speech and produce generic contextual representations that achieve remarkable perfor- mance across many downstream tasks. While these models natu- rally produce frame-level representations, many applications require utterance-level embeddings that summarize global properties of an entire speech segment. Rather than redesigning SSL pre-training ob- jectives, several recent works have demonstrated that these foundation models can be effectively adapted through post-training, transferring knowledge from pretrained expert models into a shared speech encoder. A particularly successful direction consists in learning seman- tic speech embeddings through teacher-student knowledge distilla- tion. Frameworks such as SAMU-XLSR [5], SONAR [6], [7], and SENSE [8] align speech representations with multilingual sentence embedding spaces, enabling multilingual and multimodal retrieval. More recently, multi-teacher post-training has been proposed to jointly learn semantic and speaker representations within a single SSL speech encoder [9]. These results demonstrate that multiple utterance-level attributes can coexist in a unified representation while preserving competitive performance on both semantic retrieval and speaker verification. However, extending this paradigm beyond two attributes remains an open challenge. Semantic content, speaker identity, language identity, and emotion emerge at different abstraction levels within SSL speech encoders and rely on heterogeneous supervision signals originating from different datasets and teacher models. Simply adding new objectives may lead to conflicting optimization dynamics and representation interference. Whether a single post-trained SSL en- coder can simultaneously preserve these complementary dimensions of speech therefore remains largely unexplored. In this paper, we address this challenge by proposing a holistic multi-teacher post-training framework that learns four complementary utterance-level representations from a single shared SSL speech encoder. The proposed framework distills semantic, speaker, lan- guage, and emotion representations from four specialized frozen teachers while introducing two key mechanisms to facilitate joint optimization. First, each attribute learns its own layer aggregation strategy, allowing different branches to exploit the encoder layers that are most informative for their target representation. Second, we propose a gain-based loss weighting strategy derived from mono- task learning dynamics to balance heterogeneous teachers exhibiting different convergence behaviors. We evaluate the proposed framework on four downstream tasks covering all distilled attributes: multilingual semantic retrieval, speaker verification, language identification, and speech emotion recognition. The contributions of this work are fourfold: • We significantly extend recent multi-teacher post-training ap- proaches by jointly learning four complementary utterance-level speech attributes instead of two. • We propose a holistic post-training framework for SSL speech encoders combining attribute-specific layer aggregation with a gain-based loss weighting strategy. • We demonstrate that a single shared encoder preserves seman- tic, speaker, language, and emotion representations across four independent downstream evaluation tasks. • We provide an analysis of layer utilization showing how different speech attributes emerge at different depths of a shared SSL speech encoder.
