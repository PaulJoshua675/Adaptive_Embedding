The Jacobian lens is defined from layer 1 on, and every average over layers runs over layers 1 to L−1, without the embedding, where the Jacobian lens is undefined, and the output, where every reading is exact. We calibrate on 256 sequences of 128 tokens from the training split and evaluate on 64 sequences of 128 tokens from the test split. Estimation methods. The frames involve no training: the typical attention outputs are fitted in closed form from one forward pass over the calibra- tion text, with the ridge of every layer chosen from a fixed grid by two-fold cross-validation over the calibration sequences. Gradients are taken through the released weights in their stored half precision. The lexical basis sℓ(v) = J+ ℓsv solves minδ∥Jℓδ− sv∥2 + λ∥δ∥2 with λ = 10−3 ∥J⊤ ℓsv∥2/∥sv∥2, the same relative ridge in both experiments that use it. In the lexical-basis test we form Jℓexplicitly in 32- bit arithmetic, with the stored weights cast exactly, and solve the problem in closed form for all labels at once; every move is then evaluated in the model itself, whichever Jacobian produced the direction. In steering, which needs the basis at every position, we solve the problem without forming Jℓby 30 steps of LSMR (Fong and Saunders, 2011) from zero, where a product Jℓδ is one forward-mode pass and a product J⊤ ℓy one backward pass. Evaluation methods. Positions and tokens are sampled once with fixed seeds, every experiment uses the same test sequences, and all results are from a single run, which is deterministic up to the rounding of the arithmetic. The first identity of Sec- tion 3.3 holds up to the rounding of half precision, with a divergence of 0.0009 and 0.0002 nats. The calibration error is the expected calibration error of Guo et al. (2017) with 15 bins. The substitution test was repeated on a second, disjoint part of the test split, where it gives 0.94 and 0.95 for LEXFRAME[1] and 0.28 and 0.27 for the cosine distance, nearly the same as on the first. Intervals are 95% percentile bootstrap intervals from at least 300 resamples of the test sequences, so that positions of the same se- quence stay together. They appear as bands in the figures; in Tables 1 to 7, which report means, their half-width is at most 12% of a divergence, 0.004 nats for the change in the other tokens in steering, and 0.04 for every other value. A.2 Artifacts and Computations We use SmolLM3-3B (Hugging Face, 2025) and OLMo3-7B (Team OLMo, 2025) with their pub- lished intermediate checkpoints, both released un- der the Apache 2.0 licence. The text is the En- glish WikiText-103 corpus (Merity et al., 2016), released under the Creative Commons Attribution- ShareAlike licence, with about 103 million tokens in its training split and 0.25 million in its test split. Our use of these artifacts is consistent with their intended use, and the text contains no information that we collected or annotated. The experiments ran on NVIDIA RTX 4080 SUPER GPUs, and ev- ery run uses a single GPU. The whole project used about 500 GPU hours, of which about 100 hours produced the final experiments reported in this pa- per; the remainder was spent on preliminary exper- iments. The implementation is written in Python 3.12 with PyTorch 2.12 and Transformers 5.16. A.3 AI Use Disclosure AI assistants supported the writing of this paper and the preparation of its code. This departure is learned: at the first checkpoint the mid- depth cosine is 0.34 in SmolLM3, whose earliest released checkpoint is already well into training, and 0.71 in the randomly initialised OLMo3, and it falls over the course of training.
