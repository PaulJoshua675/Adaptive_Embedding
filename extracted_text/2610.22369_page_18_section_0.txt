Reference-free metrics instead judge a generated image or video by its own quality, independent of any other sample. We report two of them here, ImageReward (Xu et al., 2023) for images and VBench (Huang et al., 2024) for video. ImageReward (package 1.5, ImageReward-v1.0 weights) scores human preference and prompt alignment. We score FLUX on DrawBench-200 for the uncached sampler and for every method of Tab. 1 at the ≈3.7× and ≈5.2× bands. ImageReward separates the methods only where the fidelity metrics already do. TaylorSeer at the ≈5.2× band loses 0.89 against the uncached sampler, more than the across-prompt standard deviation of 0.79, on the run that Tab. 1 scores at 11.44 dB PSNR. Every other cache lowers the mean by 0.02 to 0.12 (Fig. B.3.1a). Paired by prompt, Soft- Cache scores above TaylorSeer at the ≈5.2× band on 81% of prompts (Wilcoxon signed-rank, p < 10−20), and the same test finds no difference in the other seven comparisons (p ≥0.14), where the mean difference is below 0.06 against a standard deviation of 0.49 in the per-prompt difference and SoftCache scores higher on 45% to 55% of prompts (Fig. B.3.1b). ImageReward therefore adds nothing to Tab. 1 that the fidelity metrics do not already show, and we do not use it to rank caches. 18
