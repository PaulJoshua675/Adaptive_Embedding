Geometry of Multilingual Embedding Spaces A long line of work characterizes multilingual em- bedding spaces through their geometry and corrects them accordingly. Lample et al. (2018) showed that a few hub vectors become nearest neigh- bors of almost everything in high-dimensional spaces and proposed CSLS as a correction, which later work extended with normalization steps that strip language-specific structure before alignment (Ruder et al., 2019; Aboagye et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2025). A second strand targets language identity di- rectly: Roy et al. (2020) and Reimers and Gurevych (2020) showed that embeddings clustering by lan- guage causes retrievers to prefer irrelevant same- language documents over relevant foreign ones, Libovický et al. (2020) and Yang et al. (2021) re- moved this bias by subtracting per-language means, and Xie et al. (2022) showed that language identity occupies a low-rank subspace that can be projected away. Orthogonal Procrustes alignment provides a third correction, long used to relate separately trained embedding spaces (Liu et al., 2019; Lample et al., 2018). We evaluate all three under identi- cal conditions and against a correction that, rather than assuming a fixed geometric form, derives its direction from retrieval behavior. Language Bias in Multilingual Retrieval Lan- guage bias in retrieval has been documented and mitigated at the level of the final ranking. Yang et al. (2025) showed that multilingual retrievers treat query languages unequally and proposed a training objective to reduce the gap. Wang et al. (2026) found that multilingual RAG pipelines sys- tematically promote English and query-language documents. Mitigations transform the document index (Jang et al., 2026) or edit language identity out of the output embeddings (Kim et al., 2026). All of these measure a language against gold labels: they establish that some languages retrieve worse than others, but not why, since a per-language score never compares one language’s retrievals against another’s. We measure languages against one an- other instead, which requires no relevance judg- ments and distinguishes retrieving different mate- rial from retrieving the same material in a worse position.
