What the synchronization memory does Across the ticks the model grows more certain: normalized predictive entropy (H/ ln 1000) falls from 0.52 to 0.05 (raw 3.56 to 0.32 nats) by the last tick, most of it within the first ten ticks (median tdec = 39 of 50 over 1000 images; Fig. 6a). This much is expected—certainty rising over the thought is reported by the CTM’s authors and is written into their loss (§2). Our question is what produces it, and the answer is the readout’s memory. The decision is read from the synchronization matrix St out, a decay-weighted running average of pairwise co- activations (Eq. 4); in this checkpoint the learned decays are effectively off over the budget, so it is close to a uniform running mean of the state’s co-activations. Like a memory it accumulates and settles: its per-tick increment ∥∆St out∥falls and then stays low, even as the state stays live—∥∆zt∥does not shrink but grows, the traveling activity of §5. Splitting each tick’s change in entropy into the change that would occur if the memory were frozen and only the state moved, and the change caused by the memory updating with the state held fixed, the memory term accounts for all of the drop over [1, tdec] (it exceeds it: −0.43 against a total of −0.32 in normalized entropy, 256 images; 95% interval for its share 111–159%), while the state’s own motion does not help and slightly raises entropy (+0.11); to first order its step is nearly orthogonal to the entropy-reducing direction (|cos(∆zt, −∇zH)| < 0.06 at every tick).
