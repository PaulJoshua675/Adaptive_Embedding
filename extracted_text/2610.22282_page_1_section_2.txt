Diffusion and flow models now generate high-quality images and videos from natural-language prompts (Shen et al., 2026b; Li & Wang, 2026). DDPM establishes stochastic reverse denoising, while DDIM exposes a deterministic sampling path under the same training objective (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021). Flow matching and rectified flow instead learn vector fields for continuous transport (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Esser et al., 2024). In parallel, latent diffusion, Diffusion Transformers, and video diffusion models make high-resolution visual generation tractable at scale (Rombach et al., 2021; Peebles & Xie, 2022; Yang et al., 2024; Wan Team et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2026). Learned preference models then expose alignment objectives beyond likelihood and reconstruction (Xu et al., 2023; Kirstain et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2025). Online reinforcement learning can optimize these objectives, but its central bottleneck is the policy. DDPO and DPOK treat stochastic denoising transitions as actions (Black et al., 2024; Fan et al., 2023). Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO extend this construction to deterministic flow models by converting their ODE samplers into stochastic SDEs (Liu et al., 2025a; Xue et al., 2025b). Temporal windows, shared branches, and one-step updates reduce the resulting rollout cost (Li et al., 2025a; He et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025b; He et al., 2026), while dense credit and regulated clipping improve how terminal rewards shape sampler transitions (Deng et al., 2026; Shen et al., 2026a; Yang et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2025a). Forward-matching objectives avoid reverse-trajectory likelihoods, but they also remove the explicit sampled action (Zheng et al., 2026; Luo et al., 2026; Xue et al., 2025a; Xu et al., 2026c; Choi et al., 2026).
