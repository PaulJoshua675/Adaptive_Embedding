Bum Jun Kim Lemma 7 (Forward invariance implies the gradient law). Let F : Rn →R and let J := diag(s1, . . . , sn) with all si > 0. Suppose that F(Jθ) = F(θ) for every θ ∈Rn. Then F is differentiable at θ if and only if F is differentiable at Jθ, and at every such point ∇F(Jθ) = J−1∇F(θ), i.e., ∂iF(Jθ) = s−1 i ∂iF(θ) for i = 1, . . . , n. (15) Proof. Let G := F ◦J, i.e., G(θ) = F(Jθ). By hypothesis, G = F as functions on Rn. Since J is an invertible linear map, G is differentiable at θ if and only if F is differentiable at Jθ; for the converse write F = G ◦J−1. In that case, the chain rule gives ∇G(θ) = J⊤∇F(Jθ) = J∇F(Jθ). Because G = F, G is differentiable at θ exactly when F is, with ∇G(θ) = ∇F(θ). Combining the two statements, F is differentiable at θ if and only if F is differentiable at Jθ, and then J∇F(Jθ) = ∇F(θ); multiplying by J−1 yields Eq. 15. For a symmetry pair, take J = diag(cIA, c−1IB) and leave all other coordinates unchanged. Forward invariance supplies the hypothesis, and the two blocks of Eq. 15 give Eq. 2. This hypothesis holds for the data loss L but not for a loss that also includes penalties on the weights, such as L2 regularization, because rescaling a pair changes such penalties.
