Explicit prompting increases DET but decreases GEN for every model, showing that stronger flaw cues improve detection at the cost of more false rejection of valid queries. 4.2.2 Validity Judgments and Behavior We pool 115,200 outputs from Implicit and Explicit prompting and exclude 145 malformed flawed- query responses, retaining 57,455 flawed-query and 57,600 original-query outputs. Among the 47,801 flawed-query outputs with is_valid=false, 96.06% receive DET = 1 and 94.12% completely abstain; among the 9,654 with is_valid=true, 98.66% re- ceive DET = 0 and 99.92% generate SQL. Thus, on flawed queries, the reported validity judgment closely tracks both flaw recognition and generation behavior, even when that judgment is incorrect. The originals show why such consistency does not imply correctness. Of the 24,568 original- query outputs with is_valid=false, 16,502 (67.17%) contain no SQL. By contrast, SQL is generated in 33,010 of the 33,032 original-query outputs (99.93%) with is_valid=true. Models therefore tend to act on their own validity judgment: when the judgment is wrong, the generation decision of- ten follows the same error. Explicit prompting amplifies this problem. Among outputs declaring the query invalid, the pro- portion corresponding to flawed queries falls from 20,100 of 25,119 (80.02%) under Implicit prompt- ing to 27,701 of 47,250 (58.63%) under Explicit prompting. Equivalently, original-query outputs account for 41.37% of all invalid judgments under Explicit prompting. The resulting increase in false invalid judgments suggests that models rely too heavily on the prompt’s premise that the query is flawed, even when their judgments and generation behavior are internally consistent. 4.2.3 Automatic Evaluation and Reliability All model outputs are independently scored by GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.6 under identical in- structions and scoring definitions. Figure 7 presents a representative evaluation prompt, while Ap- pendix B.2 provides the complete rubrics and val- idation details. Inter-evaluator Cohen’s κ reaches 0.9737 for SQL Behavior, 0.9100 for Detection, and 0.8586 for Type Identification. We further verify the protocol on 2,160 ran- domly sampled outputs from one open-weight model (DeepSeek V3.2) and one proprietary model (GPT-5.4). Against two human annotators, the mean Cohen’s κ scores are 0.9701, 0.9545, and 0.9072 for Claude Opus 4.6, and 0.9567, 0.9355, and 0.8188 for GPT-5.4, on SQL Behavior, Detec- tion, and Type Identification, respectively. These results support the reliability of the automatic eval- uation used below. 4.3 Error-Type Analysis Type identification remains more demanding than general flaw detection.
