A training sample is a window of K frames from one episode, taken at a 1 s stride and indexed from 1, the oldest, to K, the most recent. At the start of an episode, a window holds fewer frames. At pretraining, the LM head of the VLM is also trained to predict the subtask when one is annotated and the answers of the vision–language data, with a cross-entropy loss of weight 0.1 (Appendix Fig. 9). Multi-draw variance reduction. The loss of Eq. (2) is an expectation over the flow time and the noise, and a single (τ, ϵ) draw per frame gives a noisy estimate of it. An extra draw costs one pass of the expert, whereas the context and the registers it reads cost one pass of the encoder, which dominates the cost of a training step. As in Yuan et al. (2026) and Allshire et al. (2026), we therefore estimate the expectation from eight independent draws per frame. The eight noisy chunks share the encoded context and the registers of their frame, so the encoder runs once for all of them. The registers of frame s thus receive gradient from the eight draws of every frame from s to K. Implementation. The frames of a window are packed into a single batch and kept independent in the backbone by cu_seqlens boundaries. In the expert, the self-attention of Eq. (5) is computed by one variable-length FLASHATTENTION call. For this, the keys and values that each group of queries reads are gathered into one sequence: the register tokens M1:t for the registers of frame t, and M1:t followed by the action tokens of one noisy chunk for the queries of that chunk (Appendix Fig. 9b). 5
