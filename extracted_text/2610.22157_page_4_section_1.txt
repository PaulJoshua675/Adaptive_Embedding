2.1 General PI-DeepONet framework The DeepONet is rendered physics-informed (PI-DeepONet) by encoding the PDEs, boundary and initial conditions directly into the loss term, which can take the following form: N[G(u)(x, t)] = 0, x ∈Ω, t ∈(0, T] (2) B[G(u)(x, t)] = 0, x ∈∂Ω, t ∈(0, T] (3) G(u)(x, 0) = v0(x), x ∈Ω (4) Here, N[·] represents a general differential operator, B[·] is a boundary operator, x is the spatial coordinate, t is the temporal coordinate, v0(x) defines the initial condition, Ω⊂Rd−1 denotes the spatial domain, and T denotes the upper limit of the temporal domain. A composite loss function incorporates each of the conditions: L = LPDE + LBC + LIC + Ldata. (5) Here, LPDE enforces the differential equation by minimising the residual of N[G(u)] at collocation points, (xi, ti) inside the spatiotemporal domain, LBC penalises violations of boundary conditions at boundary collocation points, and LIC enforces the initial condition at initial-time collocation points; Ldata is an optional term that penalises the difference between the neural network’s prediction and available observation data. In all of our tests, Ldata is not included, ensuring that training is labelled-data-free. It is worth examining how input functions and collocation points are provided to the PI-DeepONet.
