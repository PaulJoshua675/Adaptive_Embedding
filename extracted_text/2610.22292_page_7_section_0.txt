Error bars are 95% bootstrap confidence intervals over prompts. Among Us. Among Us (Golechha & Garriga-Alonso, 2025) is a seven-agent social-deduction game sandbox in which the Impostor role can only win by lying consistently across many turns. We fix the matchup in our setting such that both impostors are the model with honesty interventions and all five crewmates are the base model of the same architecture. We run 100 games per method and compare each method against the baseline where the base model plays the Impostor role. We report mainly (i) impostor win rate, over completed games; and (ii) confession rate, the share of games in which an impostor admits its role in a meeting, detected by a fixed regex matcher over parsed messages.2 AuditBench. AuditBench (Sheshadri et al., 2026) starts from models that have been given a hidden behaviour and trained to conceal it when asked directly, and scores how often an auditing technique uncovers that behaviour. We use the two synthetic-document organisms provided in AuditBench, which are built on Llama-3.3-70B using SFT and Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO). We per- form five audits (as opposed to 16 in the published AuditBench results due to compute constraints) per 14 hidden behaviours, applying our LoRA adaptor trained with CATCH to the organism to see whether the model reveals its hidden behaviours, and report the discovery rate averaged over them. We leave out the transcript-trained organisms where no method beats the default auditor at all, fol- lowing Herbster et al. (2026). Capability benchmarks. To check that honesty training does not deteriorate general ability, we evaluate on (i) MMLU (Hendrycks et al., 2021), zero-shot over all 57 subjects (n = 14,042); (ii) MT-Bench (Bai et al., 2024), a two-turn benchmark with 80 questions, scored with gemma-4-31B- it (1-10); and (iii) AlpacaEval (Dubois et al., 2024), where gemma-4-31B-it chooses between the trained model’s answer and the corresponding base model’s. Since win rate is sensitive to response length, we report the length-controlled (LC) rate, fitting a logistic regression on the log token counts of both answers and evaluating it at the median length. A win rate below 50% indicates a degradation in instruction-following quality relative to the base model. 5 RESULTS We present the results of honesty-intervention methods on the three manifestations of dishonesty: instructed (MASK), incentive-driven (Among Us) and trained-in (AuditBench), followed by capa- bility preservation and the effect of the utility corpus. To keep the figures legible, the main text shows the honest-persona prompting variants, Steering (StTP), SFT-t, SFT-tau, and reports CAPO with the DPO loss; the full results of all baselines and both CAPO losses are in the Appendix. MASK Figure 2 shows that CATCH raises the honest rate from 34% to 86% on Llama-3.3-70B and from 47% to 91% on Qwen3.6-27B. The strongest baselines are the honest-persona prompt and SFT-tau. SFT-t falls below every prompting variant.
