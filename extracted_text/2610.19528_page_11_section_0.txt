A Experiment Details A.1 Results on Document-MT-LLM We show the evaluation results here on Document- MT-LLM (Wang et al., 2023) , a document-level En→Ru subtitle translation task. Unlike IdiomKB and RAGtrans, this setting has no predefined re- trieval corpus. Instead, the policy needs to generate queries for a live search engine and incorporates the returned information into translation. This intro- duces additional challenges from both query gener- ation and noisy, open-ended search results. Table 5 shows that selective retrieval remains effective beyond fixed offline corpora. SRAT achieves the best COMET, GRF, GRB, and MQM among all non-oracle systems, improving over SRAT-A by 1.6% COMET, 2.7% GRF, and 5.4% MQM. The learned policy retrieves on 88.6% of examples, substantially more often than on the sentence-level benchmarks, consistent with the greater contextual demands of long-form subtitle translation. Nevertheless, its advantage over always retrieving shows that even in this retrieval-intensive setting, external evidence is not uniformly bene- ficial. These results extend the partial-evidence observation to open-ended retrieval, where the pol- icy must learn both whether additional context is needed and how to formulate an effective search query. A.2 Technical Details We conduct all experiments on a cluster where each node is equipped with 8 A100 80GB GPU with 256 GB of RAM . For all models, we use the Adam op- timizer and tune the learning rate within the range ([1e-7, 5e-7, 1e-6, 5e-6, 1e-5]). By default, we fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct using GRPO (Shao et al., 2024). GRPO is configured with a KL coeffi- cient of 0.001 and a clip ratio of 0.2. During rollout, we use a group size of 8 and a sampling temper- ature of 1.0. We enable tool-calling via SGLang (Zheng et al., 2024) to allow the LLM to gener- ate retrieval queries and set maximal turns to 5. For retrieval, we use the Flat E5 retriever (Wang et al., 2022) following prior work (Jin et al., 2025a). Flat indexing encodes the corpus into dense embed- dings and retrieves relevant documents via efficient embedding matching in the embedding space. If retrieval fails, the retriever returns a blank place- holder. On average, training the policy to conver- gence takes 68/47, 334/382, and 97 GPU hours on the IdiomKB (Zh2En/En2Zh), RAGtrans (En2Zh), and Document-MT-LLM (En2Ru) datasets, respec- tively. For all baselines, we follow the original papers for implementation details and hyperparameter tun- ing. We run each experiment with three random seeds and report the mean performance. Statisti- cal significance is evaluated using a paired t-test with p < 0.05. All datasets used in our experi- ments are publicly available. All datasets used in our experiments are publicly available. They are ei- ther released under the MIT license, which permits research use, or come without a specific license, but their original papers explicitly state that pub- lic research usage is permitted. Our use of these datasets complies with their original license terms and respects the rights of their creators. A.3 Comparison with Oracle Retrieval Policy To estimate the remaining headroom in retrieval decisions, we construct an Oracle that selects, for each test example, the better output from SRAT-N and SRAT-A according to the same blended qual- ity score used in RL reward. The Oracle therefore represents an idealized per-instance choice between translating without retrieval and translating with re- trieval, while keeping the underlying policies fixed. As shown in Table 7, the learned SRAT policy approaches the Oracle on several settings, while the remaining gap reveals further room for improv- ing retrieval decisions. On IdiomKB Zh→En, for example, SRAT reaches 0.822 COMET and −2.74 MQM compared with 0.831 and −2.67 for the Or- acle. The largest headroom appears on RAGtrans, particularly En→De, where the Oracle reaches 0.869 COMET and −5.25 MQM compared with 0.857 and −5.98 for SRAT. This is consistent with the greater difficulty of deciding when retrieval is useful in knowledge-intensive translation.
