The typed messages {mq v}q are aggregated and added to the target-node state through a residual update. A position-wise feed-forward network (FFN) then transforms the aggregated state, followed by a second residual update; both sublayers use pre-LayerNorm. Separate relation parameters preserve the different semantics of history, map, temporal, modal, and social evidence. The phrase one-shot refers to the refinement topology, not to using only one neural layer: all L layers operate on one graph constructed from the initial proposal, and all seg- ments are updated simultaneously. There is no causal seg- ment sweep, intermediate coordinate update, or graph re- construction. Because Etemp is bidirectional, a path from a later map or social observation to an earlier future node exists immediately. Reconstructing a causal graph after each sweep can refine the updated coordinates again, but its forward-only temporal edges still provide no direct path from later to earlier nodes. After message passing, a type-specific MLP decodes a local coordinate correction and raw uncertainty parameters for every interval. We rotate the coordinate corrections back to the trajectory frame and concatenate them in temporal order to form ∆Y in Eq. (2); positive Laplace scales are obtained as eb = ELU(braw) + 1 + ϵ, with ϵ = 0.1. For marginal prediction, the updated states of one agent–mode pair are concatenated and decoded into ∆ℓa,k. For joint prediction, future states are pooled over the agents belong- ing to future scene k before decoding the shared scene-logit residual ∆ℓk. Coordinate, uncertainty, and confidence re- finement therefore use the same proposal-conditioned fu- ture representation. 3.4. Training Objective We train FutureLift with winner-takes-all trajectory regres- sion and likelihood-based mode scoring while keeping pro- posal generation fixed. Let eYk denote a refined mode and Y the ground truth. In marginal forecasting, the winning mode is selected independently for each agent, k∗ a = arg min k 1 |Ta| X t∈Ta ∥eya,k,t −ya,t∥2 , (11) where Ta contains valid future steps. In joint forecasting, we select one coherent future scene by aggregating the dis- placement error over all valid agents, k∗= arg min k 1 P a |Ta| X a X t∈Ta ∥eya,k,t −ya,t∥2 . (12) For trajectory regression, we minimize the coordinate-wise Laplace negative log-likelihood of the selected mode, |y − ey|/eb + log(2eb), summed over the x and y coordinates and averaged over valid agents and time steps. For score refine- ment, we minimize the negative log-likelihood of the end- point Laplace mixture over all modes. This loss uses the refined softmax probabilities as mixture weights and stops gradients through the endpoint Laplace terms, so it updates only the refined logits. In marginal forecasting the score loss is computed per agent; in joint forecasting the end- point likelihood is first averaged over valid agents in each scene. Both losses have unit weight. We also stop gra- dients through winner selection, proposal coordinates, and proposal logits. Consequently, training cannot modify the proposal mechanism or rely on backbone-specific latent fea- tures; it learns only how to lift and correct the supplied hy- potheses. Temporal resolution.
