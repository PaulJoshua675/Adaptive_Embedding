Illumination and Intensity. To accurately assess the perceptual quality and light profile from the synthetically adapted images, it is required to interpret the absolute intensity, the non-linearity in the captured illumination as well as uniformity of the light distribution. The Mean Luminance assesses the physical brightness in the scene which is observed to be low in the real world datasets LOL- v1 [32] (15.49) and LOL-v2-real [33] (16.08), but almost 4 times higher in LOL-v2-synthetic [33] (61.90). These values are strongly linked with the exposure which acts as a linear scalar multiplier for the incoming irradiance. The datasets obtained with the proposed method manage to share more similarity with the real-captured datasets in both mean luminance and exposure. Moreover, a critical flaw in earlier synthetic approaches, is the introduction of artificial non-linearity values, with Gamma being on average approximately 26% higher than the values observed in real- world scenarios. In contrast, the proposed pipeline demonstrates a perfectly linear mapping, much closer to other low-light imaging datasets conditions. Noise. A well-known problem of light-deficient scenes is the presence of noise due to the reduced frequency of photons captured by the sensor. Therefore, an accurate noise modeling must always be linked with the level of illumination.
