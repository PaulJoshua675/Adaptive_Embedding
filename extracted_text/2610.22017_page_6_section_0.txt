CARA runs m = 3 agents per query (question) with Tmax = 150 steps each; GlobalSearch returns k = 30 candidates and Neighbors returns k = 20. 4.3 Retrieval Evaluation We first evaluate the performance of the entity- retrieval task. We report Hit@1, Recall@20, and Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR). These metrics cap- ture complementary aspects of retrieval perfor- mance: the proportion of a correct entity ranked first, the proportion of ground truth entities success- fully retrieved among the top 20 ranked results, and how highly the first correct entity is ranked overall. 4.4 Question Answering Evaluation We next evaluate whether the retrieved evidence improves downstream question answering. To iso- late the effect of retrieval, all retrieval systems use the same answer generator (LLM, Qwen3.8-27B), prompt, decoding configuration, and context bud- get. The only difference between systems is the retrieved evidence supplied to the answer generator. In this end-to-end setting, each retriever produces its own ranked evidence set, and the common gen- erator answers the question using that evidence. To test whether better retrieval leads to better answers, we evaluate answer correctness using BERTScore (Zhang et al., 2020) between the generated answer and the ground truth answer. We also evaluate an- swer faithfulness using the Ragas framework (Es et al., 2024), measuring whether factual claims in the generated answer are supported by the retrieved context. 5 Results 5.1 Retrieval Performance As the first step of experiment, we tried different λ values from 0 to 1 (in Equation 3, balancing conse- quentiality and essentiality) and α values from 0 to 1 (in Equation 4, balancing node-level causal rele- vance and path consistency). It is worthnoting that the setting of λ = 0 or 1 or α = 0 or 1 is equiva- lent to a component-level ablation study. Averaged across datasets and metrics, λ = 0.5 and α = 0.5 achieve the highest performance, supporting the selection of these values. Table 1 compares CARA (λ = 0.5, α = 0.5) with conventional and agentic retrievers on the three STaRK datasets. CARA achieves the highest Hit@1 and MRR across AMAZON, MAG, and PRIME, demonstrating its effectiveness in ranking relevant entities. Compared with ARK, its closest agentic baseline, CARA’s improvements are par- ticularly pronounced on MAG, whose questions tend to be relation-heavy. These findings suggest that incorporating causal relevance assessment and the rank-based aggregation into adaptive graph ex- ploration improves retrieval effectiveness across diverse knowledge domains, especially in relation- heavy domains. 5.2 Question Answering Performance Table 2 evaluates the downstream question answer- ing performance of CARA against KAR and ARK using BERTScore and the RAGAS faithfulness. CARA achieves the highest BERTScore across all three datasets, demonstrating that improved re- trieval effectiveness translates into better answer quality. CARA also consistently improves faith- fulness over ARK, with particularly notable gains on MAG and PRIME. Interestingly, KAR achieves the highest faithfulness across all datasets despite substantially lower BERTScore values. This sug- gests that generating answers closely grounded in retrieved evidence does not necessarily guar- antee their correctness or completeness relative to ground-truth answers. Overall, these findings high- light the importance of evaluating both answer qual- ity and faithfulness, and demonstrate that causal- guided retrieval improves downstream question an- swering while maintaining strong evidence ground- ing. 5.3 Impact of Exploration Budget on Performance Figure 2 investigates how CARA’s retrieval and question answering performance varies with the exploration budget, controlled by the number of agents (1–3) and maximum exploration steps (Tmax), ranging from 5 to 150. Overall, both Re- call@20 and BERTScore improve substantially as the exploration budget increases, but exhibit dimin- ishing returns beyond a certain point.
