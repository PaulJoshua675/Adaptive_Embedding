Qwen3-8B natively supports a thinking mode (explicit chain of thought), whereas Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct do not. Non-thinking models gain more over Base on MATH-500 (+9.2/+22.4 vs. +8.0), showing that the full framework also benefits models without a native thinking mode. Because the checkpoints differ, these gains do not establish that steering substitutes for thinking; on Llama-3.1-8B, TextGrad already accounts for most of the MATH-500 improvement. Reasoning distillation. DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B was obtained by post-training Qwen3-8B-Base on chain-of-thought traces distilled from DeepSeek-R1-0528 (DeepSeek- AI, 2025). EMO improves its own Base on all five task groups (+6.02 points on average), demonstrating that this distilled checkpoint retains scope for test-time adaptation. Its mean M-step increment over TextGrad is −0.64, although MMLU-Pro improves by 1.2 points; thus the benefits of the full adaptation loop and of steering must be distinguished. A possible explanation is that distillation changes the conditional distributions on which steering operates. In Eq. 5, both the softmax Jacobian JPopt and the context-induced displacement ∆zt depend on the checkpoint; changes to either can alter the steering vector even with fixed µ and α. Our Qwen3-8B comparison is not a controlled pre-/post-distillation pair, so this remains a mechanistic hypothesis. Testing it would require matched checkpoints, contexts, prefixes, and decoding budgets, measuring conditional entropy, context-induced logit displacements, and reference-token probability changes. The current accuracy results do not establish reduced entropy, overconfidence, or reinforcement-learning saturation. For this model we report the v2 rerun with an extended generation budget (16,384 tokens), as its verbose thinking chains saturate the default 8,192-token cap (Appendix B.3). B.3 Experimental Settings Metrics. Final accuracy is rule-based and gold-answer-based; the judge score is never used for reporting, only as the internal optimization signal. • math (GSM8K): extract the #### <number> final line; exact numeric match after removing commas and decimal points. • math boxed (MATH-500, AIME): extract the content of the final \boxed{} ; equality is decided by a four-stage cascade—(i) numeric comparison with 10−6 tolerance (resolving 1 2 vs. 0.5), (ii) normalized string match (LaTeX normalization: stripping \left/\right, \tfrac→\frac, spacing), (iii) SymPy symbolic equivalence via parse latex+sympify combinations, and (iv) a non-trivial-substring fallback (shorter side ≥2 characters and length ratio < 3). • mc boxed (GPQA, MMLU-Pro): the letter inside boxed{} , falling back to an Answer: X regular expression; exact letter match with gold (options A–J supported). judge-select@k: the reported EMO accuracy looks at the first k+1 rounds (iter0..iterk) and selects the round with the highest judge score (ties broken toward the earlier round), reporting its rule-based correctness. The main tables use judge-select@4 over five candidates: the initial response and four optimized responses. pass@N is the oracle upper bound: the question counts as solved if any of the first N attempts is correct; it is reported only for reference. Hyperparameters. All experiments use four optimization rounds after the initial gener- ation, with one E-step sample and one M-step sample per round, and the shared initial context “You are a helpful assistant.” Steering defaults: total strength µ=2.4, confidence thresh- old α=0.1, per-step steering recomputation, and penalty value −100 on masked logits. The generation budget (max new tokens) is 8,192 for GPQA/MATH-500/MMLU-Pro/GSM8K and 12,288 for AIME in the greedy main experiments, with the DeepSeek-R1 v2 rerun using 16,384 (Appendix B.2); the top-p sampling experiments use 24,576 for Qwen3-8B (thinking chains are long) and 12,288 for the Llama models.
