Thalamocortical loops produce low-frequency delta oscillations (0.5–4.0 Hz) through hyperpolarization-activated cation currents (inward ionic currents carried by positively charged ions such as Na+ and Ca2+, specifically hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated currents Ih and low-threshold T-type calcium channels IT ) during deep N3 sleep [12, 36]. These currents interact to generate sleep spindles (transient 12.0–16.0 Hz waxing-and-waning oscillatory bursts lasting 0.5–2.0 s generated by reciprocal interactions between thalamic reticular interneurons and thalamocortical relay cells) during stage N2 sleep [12, 36]. In primary insomnia, quantitative EEG analyses demonstrate an elevated presence of high-frequency beta (15.0–30.0 Hz) and gamma activity during NREM sleep [25, 20], supporting the neurobiological hyperarousal model formulated by Riemann et al. [30]. However, existing SNN models typically simulate isolated windows of several seconds or minutes, lacking coupling to continuous multi-day homeostatic and circadian drivers.
