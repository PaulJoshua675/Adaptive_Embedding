S1 Candidate populations during search S1.1 Population and winner endpoints I first compared candidate and selected-winner scores in the first and final generations to distinguish progress in newly generated programs from retention of strong incumbents. For each task, I pooled evaluated candidates across ten searches and calculated their median search score. Separately, I calculated the median score of the ten selected generation winners. Scores average returns on ten fixed search episodes; discarded candidates without scores are excluded. These results show progress in the programs being generated beyond retention of a strong early winner. Generation one contains only fresh proposals, whereas later generations mix fresh proposals, edits, and remixes. The population changes include this composition change. This analysis does not isolate an operator effect or estab- lish held-out improvement of the full candidate popula- tion. S1.2 CarRacing-v3 distribution across generations I also examined the full CarRacing-v3 candidate-score distribution to determine whether rising medians reflected a broad shift or the disappearance of weak candidates. For each generation, I pooled evaluated scores across ten searches and estimated a Gaussian kernel density with a common bandwidth of 25 return units. Multiplying each density by its evaluated-candidate count gives a band whose area equals that count. The ten generations are stacked from bottom to top. A colored band’s thickness gives that generation’s count density; its cumulative upper boundary is not the density. All 4,992 evaluated scores are included, with 498–500 candidates per generation. Eight discarded slots have no score.
