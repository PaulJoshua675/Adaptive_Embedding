Why step balance matters. Panel (a) of Figure 4 uses the fraction of actions invoking code at each online RL update as a behavioral diagnostic. Under the same training setup, standard GSPO progressively drifts toward code-intensive behavior, whereas Step-balanced GSPO remains lower and more stable over the shared observed horizon. Panel (b) reports repeated-sampling coverage. Compared with the SFT policy, OS-Helix-27B-RL improves Pass@1 from 62.75% to 66.44% (+3.69 points) and Pass@3 from 70.88% to 76.44% (+5.56 points), with the latter supported by a paired- bootstrap confidence interval of 95% [2.24,8.97]. The larger gain under Pass@3 indicates that RL does more than strengthen a single preferred trajectory and increases the probability of discovering distinct successful trajectories across independent attempts. Training scaling and data distributions. Panels (c)–(f) of Figure 4 further examine the perfor- mance scaling and instruction distributions. Panel (c) reports success for the Work. category and the overall average across checkpoints from the first SFT epoch. Panel (d) holds the desktop data fixed and varies the fraction of the 40K general-purpose multimodal pool from 0% to 100%. Both sets of curves improve overall as the supervision grows, indicating complementary contributions from hybrid trajectories and general-purpose data. Panels (e)–(f) show kernel-density estimates of these per-task statistics. We encode each instruction with text-embedding-3-large (Ope- nAI, 2024), L2-normalize the embedding, and compute cosine similarity by vector dot product. The cross-corpus similarity to OSWorld is comparable to OSWorld’s internal-instruction similar- ity, indicating limited instruction-level overlap. Together with domain-removal ablation in Table 3, this provides complementary evidence against direct instruction overlap or reliance on data from the evaluated domains. Meanwhile, the synthesized corpus exhibits a higher but still broadly spread internal-similarity distribution, suggesting that it captures shared, transferable desktop-task structure rather than collapsing to a narrow instruction template family. 6
