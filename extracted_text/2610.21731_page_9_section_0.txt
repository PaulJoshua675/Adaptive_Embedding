Table 6 gives each prompt type for one fact, word for word as sent in the deployed run. D Grading Every reply is graded in three tiers, recorded in one append-only grade book. First, a deterministic en- gine decides everything mechanical. It matches the value the reply asserts against terms derived from the fact, requires a quote to be an exact substring of the archived page, and checks each citation against the value the reply asserts rather than against the ground truth. Second, what the engine cannot de- cide goes to an LLM marker, DeepSeek-V4-Flash served through OpenRouter. The marker is not among the evaluated models and shares no model family with them. It matches paraphrases rather than judging: it reads the archived page without seeing the reply, must back every finding with a verbatim quote that code verifies against the page, and may assign only passing outcomes. Adverse findings, splits and unverified quotes go to a human. A route can use the marker only once it agrees with human labels on at least 90% of its human-decided cards, with no contradicted pass (citation support: 30 of 31). Answer-value matching, which only sports and finance answers reach, was calibrated on every card the study could supply and agreed on all 24. Third, humans decide everything else, and only humans assign adverse labels that need judgement. Every unfaithful citation is read by a human before it counts, and finding nothing on a page is never graded unfaithful automatically. As further checks, a fixed 5% of cards never reaches the marker and is graded by hand throughout, and a seeded 10% of the marker’s passes is re-graded blind by a human until 300 have passed cleanly. In total, the authors hand-graded 4,108 answers on 3,856 cards. Two blind checks measure their consistency: a seeded re-code agreed with the orig- inal label on 28 of 31 cards (Cohen’s κ = 0.848), and a blind re-ask of 103 cards first answered while the grading engine was still escalating them agreed on 96 (κ = 0.865). In the blind audit of the marker’s passes, the human agreed with the marker on 181 of 194 cards. A further 41 cards exposed a defect on living-person control facts, where the marker marks faithful citations as unsupported; this changes no rate reported here. F Snippet Content by Domain To see which values a model can find without open- ing a page, we measure what the snippets carry (Ta- ble 8). We use each evaluated fact’s frozen menu, built from one canonical query: the search results the models see, without the inserted ground-truth page and with HTML markup removed. A snippet counts as carrying a value when it would satisfy the grading criteria for the statement. Names and labels match with their aliases, financial figures within the grader’s tolerance (1% for quarterly revenue), listings by their ticker, and FDA clauses only when every key-term group is present. For people, the current value is the death, and the stale value is an entity-anchored present-tense description. Among the open models, sports episodes answered from snippets alone are stale 20.2% of the time (53/262), against 2.1% (1/48) when an opened page is cited.
