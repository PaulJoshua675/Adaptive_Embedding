End-to-end production resource accounting for colocated and disaggregated TransX serving. TransX reduces online GPU consumption by 81.2% while storage and network traffic on the online serving path remain approximately flat because compact behavior-memory KV replaces the full pre-tokenization behavioral history consumed by colocated scoring. Additional state-production cost is isolated to the bulk and nearline paths. Nearline state maintenance runs entirely on CPUs in the measured production deployment; accordingly, the nearline CPU-hours account for the full compute consumption of the nearline stage. Finally, note that online, nearline, and bulk rows use different natural denominators and are not additive. snapshot or the designated non-sequential fallback, while continuation-state reconstruction proceeds asynchronously. Controlled failure-injection experiments in Appendix B.5 show that injected failures primarily reduce freshness or increase fallback traffic rather than causing request failure or synchronous history reconstruction. Disaggregation also allows bulk construction, nearline maintenance, and online prediction to scale independently according to their own bottlenecks. Bulk capacity follows rollout backlog, nearline capacity follows update load and freshness lag, and online capacity follows request traffic, candidate fanout, and latency pressure.
