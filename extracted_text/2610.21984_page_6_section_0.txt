We evaluate GRID-Planner in closed-loop simulation on two established autonomous driving benchmarks. Following the standard protocol [5, 31], we report nuPlan [2] results on Val14, Test14-random, and Test14-hard, covering general performance and long-tail robustness. We further evaluate on interPlan [7], which emphasizes interaction-intensive scenarios such as nudging around parked vehicles, unprotected overtaking, and jaywalker encounters. Evaluation Metrics. For nuPlan, we report the official Closed- Loop Score (CLS) under Non-Reactive (NR) and Reactive (R) simu- lation [2]. CLS aggregates route progress, comfort, and rule compli- ance while penalizing safety failures such as at-fault collisions. For interPlan, we report the Overall Score and representative scores on Nudge Around, High Traffic Density, and Jaywalk. Diagnostic Metrics. Beyond the official scores, we report Colli- sion Rate, corresponding to 𝑅safety; Lane Deviation, a lane/route- consistency proxy corresponding to 𝑅rule; Jerk, a smoothness indi- cator corresponding to 𝑅comfort; and Latency, the average planner- side inference time. These metrics are diagnostic only. Red-light violations are not evaluated separately due to limited traffic-signal state extraction. 4.2 Implementation Details GRID-Planner uses 2 seconds of history to predict an 8-second trajectory at 10 Hz, with 21 historical timestamps including the current frame and 80 future steps. Each waypoint is represented as (𝑥,𝑦, cos𝜃, sin𝜃) in the ego-centric frame. The scene encoder retains up to 32 neighboring agents, 70 lane polylines, 25 route-lane polylines, and 5 static control elements. Training has three stages: (1) base diffusion training for 400 epochs with AdamW (batch size 2048, learning rate 10−4, weight decay 0.01, and EMA decay 0.999), CFG, and 0.3 neighbor dropout; (2) graph-aware reward fine-tuning with GAIW and RRS for 10 epochs at 5 × 10−5; and (3) online self- distillation for 2 epochs, sampling four candidates per scene and ranking their graph-weighted returns to select the teacher and supervise the student. At inference, the distilled planner uses four update steps with a CFG scale of 1.8. Latency is the mean planner- side compute_trajectory runtime at batch size 1 on the same hardware, excluding simulator overhead, and is reported only as a relative runtime diagnostic. 4.3 Main Results nuPlan benchmark. Table 2 further evaluates interactive deci- sion making on interPlan, which emphasizes long-tail interactive scenarios beyond standard lane following. GRID-Planner achieves the best overall score of 57.25, outperforming both Diffusion Planner and Flow Planner (52.90 and 54.83, respectively). The improvement is especially evident in spatially constrained and highly uncertain scenarios, including High Traffic Density (65.37 vs. 56.41 against Flow Planner) and Jaywalk (43.20 vs. 41.64 against Flow Planner, and 26.20 against Diffusion Planner). Since success in these cat- egories depends on resolving ambiguous right-of-way conflicts rather than nominal path tracking, these results support the role of the proposed relational learning mechanism in shaping safer and more interaction-aware behavior. The gains are not uniform across all categories: in Nudge Around, GRID-Planner slightly ex- ceeds Flow Planner (58.77 vs. 58.02) but remains below Diffusion Planner (60.48), suggesting that our method is most advantageous in scenarios where relational reasoning and safety–progress trade- offs under dynamic uncertainty dominate the planning difficulty.
