Spiking neural networks target energy-efficient language modeling through sparse, binary activations, but the efficiency–accuracy tradeoff of spiking language models is uncharacterized at scale. We present the first Chinchilla-style IsoFLOP scaling study of a spiking language model, sweeping SpikeGPT-family models [Zhu et al., 2023] (RWKV backbone [Peng et al., 2023]) from width 384 to 1536 across compute budgets from 3 × 1017 to 1 × 1019 FLOPs on FineWeb-EDU. We report two findings. First, the compute-optimal frontier is Nopt ∝C0.56, close to the 0.50 that Hoffmann et al. [2022] report for dense Transformers, so spiking does not change how parameters and data should be traded off under a compute budget. Second, against non-spiking RWKV and Transformer baselines trained the same way, the loss gap splits into a backbone cost of about 0.16 nats that does not change with compute and a spiking cost that falls from 0.34 to about 0.23 nats and then levels off. These results give the first compute-optimal recipe for spiking language models and quantify the accuracy cost of their sparsity. 1
