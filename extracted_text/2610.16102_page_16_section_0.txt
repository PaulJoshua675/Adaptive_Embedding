We state this as an interpretation, not a finding of 2026b, and return to how it might be tested in §9. 6 The mentor–student DAG as a tacit-knowledge transmission graph The graph our programme traversed is not a graph of explicit knowledge. Its edges are ap- prenticeships, the channel Polanyi named for passing on what cannot be specified, so its three macro-structures are events in the history of tacit-knowledge transmission. 6.1 What an edge carries Each edge asserts that one person trained another; the relation merges Wikidata’s doctoral- advisor, student and student-of properties (2026d, Appendix A). Polanyi held that an art which cannot be specified in detail is transmitted by example from master to apprentice (Polanyi 1958). Collins’s study of laser builders found the same at the bench: groups that had spent time with a working laser’s builders could build one, while groups working only from published accounts could not (Collins 1974). An apprenticeship edge is therefore a socialization channel in Nonaka’s sense, and the topology of the graph records the routes by which tacit knowledge travelled. The nodes carry something else. Field, language, employer, society membership and dates are explicit attributes, bound into each hub’s composite vector (2026d, Appendix C). The pro- gramme’s own results show that the two layers can be read separately. The mentor–student topology cannot see the twelfth-century translation movement, which moved knowledge through books and translators rather than through masters; unbinding the FIELD component of the node vectors recovers it as an astronomy signal (2026d). A DAG memory of this kind thus holds both channels at once: tacit transmission in its edges, codified content in its node vectors. That is the concrete form of a DAG memory that accumulates both kinds of knowledge in one structure. 6.2 The hourglass as institutionalized socialization Leibniz built an apparatus of transmission: he founded the Berlin Academy of Sciences in 1700, kept one of Europe’s largest scholarly correspondences, and started a lineage running through Johann Bernoulli, Euler, Lagrange, Laplace and Poisson (2026d). In the graph this appears as an hourglass, 5.3 paths per node upstream against 53.4 downstream, and every one of his direct students produced descendants in the top tier of network influence (2026d). Across the same window learned-society membership rose from 6.5 to 82.1 percent of the cohort, from 0.08 to 3.81 memberships per person (2026g; 2026d). We read this as Mokyr’s Republic of Letters, as 2026d does, and add one gloss. Academies, correspondence and lineages are institutions for multiplying socialization. They are, nearly item for item, the follow-up activities Tao lists—talks, meetings with experts, workshops, newcomers joining a field. 6.3 Newton, Thurston, and the follow-up gap Newton and Leibniz have nearly equal calculus-content scores in the graph, but only four of the 64 lineages pass through Newton, against 47 through Leibniz; Newton, for all his genius, worked largely in isolation and trained few successors (2026d).
