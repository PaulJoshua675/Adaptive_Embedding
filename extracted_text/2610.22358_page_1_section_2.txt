Time series foundation models (TSFMs) are a hot topic (Ansari et al., 2024; Das et al., 2023; Ansari et al., 2025; Jain & Sen, 2026; Cohen et al., 2025). The field is growing fast: Every year, new models are released that refresh the leaderboard. The models are commonly evaluated through point accuracy (e.g., MASE) and marginal probabilistic scores (e.g., CRPS, scaled quantile loss) (Aksu et al., 2024; Shchur et al., 2025). Consequently, most of the recent TSFMs are designed and trained to produce quantile levels, which are optimized for these benchmarks. Point accuracy and marginal probabilistic scores are widely adopted for good reason: they provide an interpretable, per-step summary of forecast quality that directly supports common operational decisions such as inventory planning, resource allocation, and risk management. However, they do not capture all aspects of forecast quality. Other properties, particularly trajectory-dependent ones, are important but overlooked by these metrics. Among many, we illustrate two important aspects: • Learning distributions with multiple modes: Quantiles are defined through the inverse of the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the target distribution. These CDFs, however, are not always nice and smooth, for instance, when the target distribution has multiple modes or sharp peaks. With a few or a few dozens of quantiles, the resulting approximation of the CDF can be quite rough and suffer from a regression-to-the-mean issue (Yu et al., 2026).
