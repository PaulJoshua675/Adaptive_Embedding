Relevant sources can thus be available with- out contributing to subsequent research decisions. Fast judgment models offer a way to make re- peated source assessments more efficient (Almeida, 2026). Prior systems separate document processing from research reasoning (Li et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025). The challenge here is to make fast local judgments guide evidence acquisition throughout an evolving investigation. This requires inspect- ing many sources within budget while adapting to changing evidence needs; a partial finding may redirect search or reading. Our key observation is that research planning and local evidence judgments require different scopes of information. Planning uses the accumu- lated investigation, whereas a source judgment can be bounded by an explicit evidence objective. Inde- pendent judgments under that objective can run in parallel, while their findings return to the research process to inform the next objective. We introduce JevGPT, a dual-system architec- ture for autonomous research illustrated in Figure 1. System Two sets evidence objectives and integrates findings. Jev (Almeida, 2026) serves as System One, guiding source selection, passage localiza- tion, and continued reading; proactive discovery inspects additional candidates. Selected original passages return to System Two, connecting these local decisions to the next research action and the final answer or ranking. Experiments on BrowseComp-Plus and Neu- CLIRBench (Chen et al., 2026; Lawrie et al., 2025) cover fixed-corpus question answering and document ranking. Figure 2 shows that JevGPT achieves leading task performance at lower cost than comparably effective research agents. JevGPT also completes research faster.
