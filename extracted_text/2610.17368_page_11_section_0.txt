Six deliberate-intent engagement types are re- tained: BUY, LIKE, SHARE, DOWNLOAD, TRY_ON, and SAVE. TAP events are excluded because they represent a UI prerequisite rather than ex- plicit engagement; including them would add approximately 16.5M events and cause 17% of users to exceed the 100-item sequence cap. Users must have a resolved city or country in the persona meta- data. User identity is represented by the pair (user_id, profile_id), and users mapping to multiple profiles are removed. Both fully spec- ified and partially specified persona contexts are retained during training. Deduplication and sequence construction. Three deduplication passes remove system-generated duplicate events while preserving genuine repeated actions: ambiguous sessions containing multi- ple user-profile combinations are removed, exact row duplicates are discarded, and interaction-level duplicates are removed only when they match simultaneously under both session/position and timestamp-based windowing criteria. Interactions are then grouped by user-profile pair and ordered chronologically. Sequences have a minimum length of three and are capped at 100 items. Consecutive events separated by 30 days or more are discarded. Training-pair generation. Six interaction-pattern families gen- erate conversational training examples: cross-product transitions, same-product persistence, forward engagement, persona-conditioned prediction, backward engagement, and cross-signal escalation. Tem- poral gaps are bucketed into same-session (<30 min), within-day (<24h), within-week (<7d), and within-month (7–30d) intervals. This process produces 6,799,008 conversations, split into 6,255,087 training, 339,950 validation, and 203,971 test examples. Attributes. The Internal-fashion catalog provides brand, price, currency, gender, color, size, materials, category name, taxonomy, and VLM/attribute-assembled product descriptions. This attribute richness makes Internal-fashion, together with Fashion-140k, the primary setting for evaluating attribute-specific supervision. 9.1.4 Semantic-ID Construction. We construct semantic IDs us- ing a TIGER-style [26] residual vector quantization autoencoder (RQ-VAE). For Sports, Beauty, and Toys, title, description, cate- gory path, and brand are concatenated into a single context string and encoded into a 1024-dimensional representation. Fashion-140k uses the RQ-VAE checkpoint trained on the full 799,953-item Ama- zon Fashion pool; semantic IDs for its 141,300 items are obtained by nearest-codebook assignment in the same embedding space. Internal-fashion additionally incorporates multimodal product rep- resentations using 1024-dimensional Qwen3-VL embeddings. The RQ-VAE encoder follows a 1024 →512 →256 →128 →32 architecture and uses three residual codebooks with 256 entries each. For Sports, Beauty, and Toys, the RQ-VAE is trained for 20,000 steps per category on a single A100 GPU. A fourth deduplication token is appended to the three quantized codes when collisions occur, producing the final depth-4 semantic ID used for language- model fine-tuning. For Sports, Beauty, and Toys, the unique three-level SID rates are 94.6%, 96.3%, and 97.4%, respectively, corresponding to colli- sion rates of 5.4%, 3.7%, and 2.6%. The maximum deduplication indices observed are 13, 35, and 11. All 256 codes are utilized at each quantization level.
