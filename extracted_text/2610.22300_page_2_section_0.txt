Pre-linearity predicts Generalization better than Recall does (Pre-linearity versus Recall). Section 3 defines the three measures. research question is: What property of how a pretrained model encodes its knowledge determines whether new knowledge built on it generalizes? We hypothesize that the answer is linear structure. Linear relational embeddings were originally proposed as a mechanism for generalizing to unseen relational facts (Paccanaro & Hinton, 2000), and many relations in modern language models are, to a good approximation, decoded linearly from the subject’s representation (Hernandez et al., 2024; Merullo et al., 2025). In a synthetic setting, knowledge that is linearly encoded on the entity is the knowledge that fine-tuning can extract (Allen- Zhu & Li, 2024). But whether the linearity of a pretrained model’s real-world knowledge affects how new knowledge built on it generalizes has not been tested. The hypothesis we investigate is that the more linearly readable a relation’s answers are in the pretrained model, which we call the relation’s Pre-linearity, the better new knowledge built on that relation generalizes after fine-tuning. To this end, we construct new relations from existing ones (Figure 1). Take the pretrained relation that maps a place to its continent (Paris →Europe) and relabel each of its answers at random with a different arbitrary word, such as an animal name (Europe →crab, Asia →zebra). The resulting new relation maps Paris →crab. We fine-tune the model on the new relation for a subset of subjects, without telling it the original relation or the relabeling rule, and test whether it assigns the right labels to held-out subjects. Having learned Paris →crab, does it answer Munich →crab? The design directly tests for statistical generalization. The new relation is unknown to the model, but its structure is inherited from the pretrained relation, so the model can generalize only by building on what it already knows. We measure Pre-linearity with a cross-validated linear probe on the pretrained model’s subject representations, Recall as the pretrained model’s accuracy on the pretrained relation, and Generalization as the fine-tuned model’s accuracy on held-out subjects (§3). Across eleven open-weight language models, base and post-trained, from 2B to 27B parameters, and 70 relations, Pre-linearity strongly predicts Generalization. Within each model, the two correlate across relations at Pearson r = 0.87 to 0.92 (median 0.90; §5). The association is not explained by how well the model recalls the relation, by the numbers of answers and fine-tuning subjects, or by how often the subjects occur in pretraining text.
