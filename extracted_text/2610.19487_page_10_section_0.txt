Multi-contact connections that cluster on a few branches of an arbor with many branches correspond to χ(ε) ≪¯s(ε), the regime in which the resolved graph is far sparser than its cell-level projection while carrying many more edges. Sparsity is not an invariant of the physical network; it is a property of the graph induced by the cho- sen ruler, and Eq. (35) states how it changes with that ruler. The same distinction applies to degree. The cell-level in-degree of neuron i is kcell i = X j Acell ij , (37) whereas the number of resolved presynaptic-to-subunit relations carried by that same cell is k(ε) i = X j X a∈Si(ε) A(ε) ija. (38) The two degrees count different objects. Equation (37) counts distinct presynaptic cells; Eq. (38) counts distinct presynaptic-to-subunit relations. The difference between them is precisely the within-cell topology removed by the projection in Eq. (29). A network can therefore be sparse as an axon-to-cell graph while possessing a substantially richer contact- to-subunit graph. Conversely, resolving additional con- tacts or subunits does not guarantee improved predic- tion: coarse descriptions can generalize better when data are finite and the fine degrees of freedom are poorly constrained23.
