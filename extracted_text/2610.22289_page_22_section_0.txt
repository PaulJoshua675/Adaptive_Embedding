10 Conclusions We evaluated one Codex-based agent in two nearly identical prompt variants, each deployed unchanged across ARC-AGI-3, AGI Maze, and Terminal-Bench. Stop-and-report improves mean ARC-AGI-3 RHAE and AGI Maze RHAE70 across the tested configurations, while Terminal-Bench shows small, mixed changes. The comparison demonstrates that a small, environment-independent modification can improve performance beyond the environment that motivated it, although the performance gains come with higher token-based cost. Stronger models may eventually construct useful procedures for themselves and reduce the advantage of developer-supplied scaffolding. Our results show that this advantage remains with GPT-5.6-sol. The twma_v1.61 reference prescribes organization and procedures that the general agent has the information and tools to construct, and Controlled_RESET recovers part of its advantage by implementing a convention already stated in the environment description. The tutorial experiment adds a complementary observation: access to additional relevant experience does not uniformly improve performance. The unresolved question is whether scaffolding helps mainly through more effective use of information and tools, additional inference, or both. The present experiments do not separate these effects, which may reinforce one another. EnvTraverse allows new agent variants to be evaluated across environments and compared with Baseline. This supports further research on improving environment-agnostic agents through better procedures or additional general capabilities, while assessing performance gains, possible regressions, and changes in inference cost.
