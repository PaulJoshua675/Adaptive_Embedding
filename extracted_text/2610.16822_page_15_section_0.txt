These baselines evaluate local message-passing predictors and latency-oriented graph predictors. Transformer-based predictors. Graphormer [26] is a general graph Transformer with structural encodings such as degree, shortest-path, and edge-related biases; we adapt it to architecture DAG prediction. TNASP [24] is a Transformer-based NAS predictor that combines operation embeddings, Laplacian positional encoding, and a self-evolution framework. NAR-Former [14] tokenizes operation and topology information into architecture sequences and uses a multi-stage fusion Transformer for holistic attribute prediction. PINAT [25] augments a Transformer-like NAS predictor with permutation-invariance modules and Laplacian positional information. These baselines test whether global self-attention and topology-aware tokenization are sufficient without encoder-wide directional consistency. Hybrid predictors. GraphTrans [27] combines a GNN module with global Transformer atten- tion to capture long-range graph context. NAR-Former V2 [15] rethinks Transformer-based neural network representation learning for broader architecture-attribute prediction. NN-Former [16] com- bines topology-aware token mixing and bidirectional graph-isomorphism feed-forward updates to strengthen neural architecture representations. These baselines are closest in spirit to DTA-GT because they combine graph structure and attention, but they do not explicitly preserve direction across node initialization, spectral encoding, pairwise interaction, and feature update. A.6 Direction-Aware Spectral Encoding Settings For the Magnetic Laplacian spectral encoding, we set the potential parameter to q = 0.25 following prior Magnetic Laplacian practice [31, 32]. The graph-scaled phase convention and the sensitivity experiment for q are described in Appendix B.1. Eigenvectors are sorted by ascending eigenvalues, and the first k = 4 eigenvectors are used as spectral components.
