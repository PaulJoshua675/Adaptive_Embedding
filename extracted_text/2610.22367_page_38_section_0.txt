We require this map exactly for every i < 41,052, every τ, and every 40-bit system state. The baseline combines the circuits used in sparse qubitization [4, 64]. QROM loads the 34-bit alias word, merging repeated alias words [66]. A comparator then calculates h(i, τ) to choose between i and alt(i) and apply the selected coefficient sign. A second, independent traversal over sel applies the selected Pauli operator to the 40 system qubits. The baseline then erases the selected index, the comparison bit h, and the alias word. The lookup and the row-by-row Select traversal cost 13,000 and 41,000 Toffolis, respectively; together they contribute more than 97% of the total. The remaining comparison, selection, and cleanup bring the baseline to 55,000 Toffolis and 139 qubits. The main opportunity is to shorten the two table traversals. For arbitrary, unstructured table data, QROM has no pattern to exploit: in the worst case, every address may store an unrelated word. However, this table is not arbitrary. Its Pauli operators and coefficients come from a physical system with exact spin symmetries and repeated orbital environments, so many coefficients are equal and many Pauli operators are related. We therefore choose the table to suit the circuits. Two degrees of freedom make this possible. First, permuting terms together with their coefficients and Pauli operators leaves Equation (68) unchanged. Second, many different alias tables realize the same rounded distribution. The preprocessing uses the first freedom to group Pauli operators related by fixed Clifford transformations into blocks, shortening Select. It uses the second to place equal alias words into repeated patterns, shortening QROM. Frame-block Select. Many Pauli operators in this Hamiltonian are related by fixed Clifford transformations, which we call frames. Suppose, for example, that two commuting frames generate four related Pauli operators from one representative. Instead of selecting four operators independently, the circuit selects the representative once and uses two bits of the address (the selected row index) to control the two frames. More generally, k such bits can identify up to 2k operators in a block. The circuit pays for one block representative plus its controlled frames, rather than every Pauli operator separately. The circuit applies a frame by conjugation. It applies σ† to the system register under a control, then the representative P, then σ under the same control. The result is σPσ† when the control is set and P otherwise. Each frame is applied once for the whole table rather than once per block. Blocks that use the same frames are contiguous, so a few comparisons of the address with fixed boundaries determine which frames are active. These relations have a physical origin: the model contains two equivalent spin channels, two similar iron sites, and corresponding orbitals on repeated parts of the cluster. These repetitions create Pauli operators that are related by a fixed transformation. Each frame is a permutation or change of basis of the spin-orbitals. The Jordan–Wigner mapping turns it into a fixed Clifford transformation of the 40 system qubits. Two of the frames used here come from exact spin symmetries of the Hamiltonian. Because the model treats the spin-up and spin-down channels identically, σαβ exchanges the spin-up and spin-down modes of every spatial orbital. Spin-rotation symmetry produces a second pairing after the Jordan–Wigner mapping: σXY exchanges the X and Y factors and adjusts the accompanying Z strings required by that mapping. Both transformations preserve the corresponding coefficient magnitudes. The other five frames come from parts of the cluster that resemble each other; unlike the two spin frames, they are not symmetries of the full Hamiltonian. The frame σFe is the approximate left–right reflection of the cluster, which exchanges the two iron centers and their surrounding orbitals. The frame σ′ Fe combines this reflection with several local orbital swaps and matches Pauli operators that the bare reflection misses. The frames σthio, σ3d, and σµS exchange corresponding orbitals on the terminal thiolates, the iron 3d shells, and the bridging sulfur atoms. These sites have similar but not identical electronic environments: the permutation often maps one term’s Pauli operator onto another’s, but the coefficients differ. Only the Pauli relation Pℓ′ = σPℓσ† must hold exactly; wℓ′ and wℓremain independent entries in the alias table. The preprocessing thus finds a commuting family of seven frames and orders related Pauli operators into blocks whose sizes are powers of two, so that address bits index the operators within a block. The 41,052 terms form 3,221 blocks. Selecting the block representatives costs 3,327 Toffolis. Applying the seven frames and determining which are active costs 534.
