Models that spend more tokens deliberating there- fore act later, preserving the task consequences of delayed decisions while controlling for physical serving speed. Under each clock, we test low and high reasoning effort to examine the trade-off between decision quality and timely execution. Our analysis reveals that substantial performance gaps relative to Paused Clock persist under Token Clock, highlighting the difficulty of translating task competence into timely action even when temporal costs are standardized independently of serving speed. Higher reasoning effort improves success for all six models under Paused Clock but has mixed effects under temporal pressure. Further experiments show that relaxing the temporal cost of generation generally improves performance but does not ensure task success. These findings motivate future work on adaptive reasoning with temporal awareness and continuous perception and action, enabling agents to coordinate deliberation and execution with the time available. 2
