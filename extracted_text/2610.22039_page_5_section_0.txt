Graph Credit relies solely on executable outcomes and evaluator- side graph metadata; it neither imposes the historical PR order nor uses patch similarity or learned judges. 4.2 Evaluation and Agent Optimization Evaluation Protocol We report Graph Credit alongside two complementary metrics. Resolved measures full-task correctness and remains the primary benchmark metric, while Flat-F2P measures the fraction of functional tests passed. Graph Credit instead captures dependency-consistent partial progress, providing a structural diagnostic for un- solved tasks. Alternative graph operators, linearized scoring, and randomized-graph controls are examined in RQ2. Reinforcement-Learning Reward Graph Credit also pro- vides a structural signal for reinforcement learning. We com- bine flat functional progress, dependency-conditioned credit, full-task resolution, and regression preservation: RWEAVE = λf2p FlatF2P +λgraph GraphCredit(GA) + λsolve Resolved −λreg RegP2P . (11) Here, RegP2P penalizes regressions on previously passing tests, and all coefficients are nonnegative. Graph Credit is therefore a structural component of the reward rather than a standalone objective. Since every term is computed from executable test outcomes on the generated patch, RWEAVE provides dense, verifiable feedback. Training details and re- ward comparisons are presented in RQ4. 5 Experiments We evaluate WEAVE along the complete pipeline from task construction to agent evaluation and improvement. Common settings. We use WEAVE-Train for supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, a held-out calibration subset for threshold selection and hyperparameter tuning, and WEAVE-Bench exclusively for final evaluation. Complete implementation details and hyperparameters are provided in the appendix. 5.1 RQ1: Construction Validity To validate WEAVE’s construction pipeline, we evaluate whether typed-locus coupling recovers true dependency edges more reliably than single-signal alternatives. The eval- uation covers 30 repositories containing 1,198 validated atomic PRs. To establish ground truth, three expert annota- tors independently assess candidate PR pairs and label each as dependency, same-task but independent, loosely related, or unrelated; a senior annotator adjudicates disagreements, yielding 219 gold dependency edges (Fleiss’ κ = 0.791). Each method produces a scored ranking of candidate edges; we report macro-averaged AP over repositories, Coverage—the fraction of gold edges recalled at full emission—and pooled Precision at rank 50 (P@50). All methods feed into the iden- tical downstream executable construction pipeline (§3.3). We compare WEAVE against four single-signal base- lines: Temporal Window (merge-time adjacency within ∆= 30 days), Traceability Links (explicit cross-PR refer- ences such as closes and depends on), File Over- lap (weighted Jaccard over changed-file paths), and Sym- bol Overlap (shared function and class declarations via lightweight static analysis). Score-based methods retain at most 3NR top-scoring edges per repository (where NR is the PR count); threshold sensitivity is reported in Appendix. Results.
