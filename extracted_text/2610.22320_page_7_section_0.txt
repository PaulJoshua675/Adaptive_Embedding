To recover such appearance cues without lengthening the scene prompt, we construct three refer- ence subprompts around a minimal shared context: (1) Style Prompt: Style + Subject, (2) Identity Prompt: Style + Subject + Appearance, and (3) Scene Prompt: Style + Subject + Scene. The shared Style + Subject prompt serves as the minimal anchor for measuring appearance-specific displacement before transferring it to each scene prompt. This anchor is scene-independent, while still preserving the subject slot and visual style, making it suitable for defining an appearance cue that can be shared across all scenes. By keeping the scene prompt free of explicit appearance phrases, we pre- serve the scene description as the primary semantic context. Cross-Contextual Bridging then reintroduces the missing appearance cue at the embedding level. This allows the subject appearance to be recovered without modifying the word- level scene description. Building on our empirical observations (Sec. 3.1 and Ap- pendix A), we utilize the ⟨EOS⟩embedding as a controllable semantic handle, enabling phrase-level appearance transfer without increasing the textual length of the scene description. We obtain the corresponding ⟨EOS⟩embeddings, eossty, eosid, and eossc, from the CLIP [24] text encoder. Although eosid contains the desired appearance information, directly adding it to eossc can redundantly re- inforce the shared style and subject semantics, leading to unintended semantic drift. We therefore define the appearance residual as the local displacement from the shared style-subject anchor to the identity prompt, following the approxi- mate linearity of CLIP embedding space [32]: ∆app = eosid−eossty. We transport this residual to the scene prompt by constructing the infused embedding eosinf: \lab e l {eq : eos} eos_{inf} = eos_{sc} + \Delta _{app}. (1) We replace each scene ⟨EOS⟩embedding eossc with its infused counterpart eosinf. The identity prompt embedding and the modified scene prompt em- bedding are then fed into the diffusion model for the subsequent feature-level identity transfer. This formulation restores subject appearance cues within the scene prompt representation while preserving the original scene semantics. This appearance-aware representation further reduces the semantic gap between the scene subject and its identity counterpart before feature injection, providing a stronger basis for the subsequent feature-level identity transfer (Sec. 3.3). 3.3 Adaptive Identity Preservation Unlike spatially blind identity injection, we focus on anchoring identity features according to each scene’s subject structure, allowing the subject to adapt in scale and viewpoint while preserving its core identity cues. To achieve natural subject alignment across scenes, we transfer visual features from the identity image xid ∈ RHW ×C to N scene images {xn sc}N n=1 ∈RN×HW ×C. We use intermediate features from the first decoder block of the U-Net, where coarse spatial structure is known to emerge [29], to establish correspondence between identity and scene subject regions.
