We apply rank-8 LoRA to the MLP in each attention layer and v_proj layers in each self-attention layer. For Qwen3.5-9B, we additionally apply LoRA to the in_proj_qkv_proj layers in its linear-attention blocks. For MOSS-VL, we additional apply v_proj layers in each cross-attention layer. We use a four-layer self-attention network as the aggregator, with a hidden dimension of 512 and eight attention heads. For each layer of the base model, we learn an independent query and maintain a corresponding hidden state. We instantiate the SSM using a four-layer DeltaNet (Yang et al., 2024) with a hidden dimension of 512. To generate the LoRA parameters, we learn separate linear projection layers for each target module and LoRA rank component. We train MaLoW on the training data introduced in Sec. 3.4 for 2,800 steps with a total batch size of 64. We use the Adam optimizer with a peak learning rate of 8 × 10−5 and a cosine learning-rate schedule. The videos are sampled at 1 fps during training and inference of MaLoW. Each Ct is a 2-second video clip. Additional implementation details are provided in the Appendix C.1. Baselines. We categorize the baselines into four groups according to the model types and how they manage historical visual context during streaming inference. 1. Offline VLMs. This category includes LLaVA-Video-7B (Zhang et al., 2025b), LongVU-7B (Shen et al., 2025), Qwen3-VL-4B (Bai et al., 2025), and Qwen3.5-9B (Qwen Team, 2026). We compare to these models for their strong video understanding capabilities, acquired through large-scale video-text pretraining. We evaluate their pretrained checkpoints by treating streaming videos as offline inputs. 2. Streaming VLMs with Long-term (Full) Context.
