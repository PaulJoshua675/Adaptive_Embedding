Transformer-based models have become the backbone of a wide range of tasks, but their intensive computation and data movement incur substantial energy overhead. Analog compute-in-memory (CIM) offers an energy-efficient alternative, yet its inherent hard- ware non-idealities perturb stored weights and can significantly degrade model performance. Although existing noise-aware train- ing (NAT) methods improve robustness by injecting noise during training, they largely overlook the critical role of attention mech- anisms in Transformer inference. To bridge this gap, we analyze how CIM-induced noise reshapes attention in Transformers and identify two key observations: (1) the divergence between clean and noisy attention maps increases substantially in deeper layers, and (2) conventional NAT can remain overconfident even when its attention routes deviate markedly from the clean reference. Moti- vated by these findings, we propose ABNAT, an attention-based noise-aware training framework that teaches the noisy student to preserve the clean teacher’s headwise attention routes rather than merely reproduce answers, without hardware changes or inference overhead; selective variants further reduce training cost. Extensive experiments across vision, language, and multimodal Transformers show that ABNAT consistently improves robustness over corre- sponding NAT baselines, improving noise-degraded accuracy by up to 11.28% and reducing attention deviation by up to 85.77%.
