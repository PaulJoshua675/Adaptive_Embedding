ASR and XFER capture two distinct dimensions of attack quality: ASR measures whether an attack works in any deployment environment (max over E), while XFER measures the fraction of all task–environment pairs where the attack succeeds. An attack with high ASR but low XFER works only under permissive system prompts; high XFER means the attack is robust across realistic deployment variations. We define three standardized environments to characterize attack robustness across realistic deployment variations: an empty system prompt (provider defaults), a minimal assistant instruction, and a strict safety guideline. Together these span the range from no safety scaffolding to explicit defensive prompting. Prior red-teaming work typically evaluates against a single deployment environment, usually the provider default; we propose the three-environment evaluation as a richer characterization of attack quality, since an attack that succeeds only under permissive system prompts is less informative than one that succeeds across varied contexts.
