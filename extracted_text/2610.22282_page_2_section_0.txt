Sampler-space GRPO injects continuous noise into the reverse process and estimates an advantage from many complete visual rollouts. RPO retains the original deterministic sampler and pairs two rollouts that share the prompt, initial visual latent, routed denoising levels, and anchor layers. They differ only in their sampled attention routes, so their reward difference measures the aggregate effect of the two recorded routed trajectories. Generated visual outputs are schematic. Our key insight is that stochastic exploration need not perturb the denoising trajectory. A diffusion transformer splits each latent into visual tokens, and its attention layers already decide how much each token attends to the others. If we group neighboring tokens into blocks, each query block can attend to a sampled subset of key blocks instead of all of them as dense attention does. We call this choice of visible key blocks a route. Sampling routes exposes a stochastic action without adding a policy head or modifying the sampler. We introduce Routing Policy Optimization (RPO), which samples attention routes from the generator’s own attention mass at a few denoising levels. Each sampled route perturbs the velocity of its transition, and a Gaussian likelihood around the dense velocity scores this perturbation. This likelihood supplies a score-function surrogate for the dense generator itself. The routing action also enables a controlled two-rollout estimator, as Figure 1 illustrates. Both members share every non-routing source of variation and differ only in their sampled routes. Their signed reward difference therefore provides a leave-one-out advantage for the two routed trajectories. During replay, each routed transition is scored from its recorded input, so later levels condition on earlier routes. Routing serves only as a training interface. The pass that proposes routes carries no gradient, and the likelihood differentiates the dense velocity. The update therefore acts on the same velocity field that dense inference evaluates. After training, we remove all masks and evaluate the updated generator with dense attention and its original deterministic sampler. The central empirical question is whether reward learned through temporary routing transfers to the dense image or video generator used at inference. We evaluate this question on Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium and Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (Wan Team et al., 2025), covering text-to-image and text-to-video generation. Across both settings, RPO improves reward-aligned generation quality while retaining dense inference. On Stable Diffusion 3.5, RPO exceeds the final dense validation reward of Flow-GRPO within 25 of 50 training steps and ends 1.1 points higher. On Wan2.2, RPO attains the strongest overall VBench score among the evaluated checkpoints while using 24.1 GPU-hours, compared with 75.4 for Flow-GRPO and 102.3 for Dance- GRPO. This corresponds to 3.1× and 4.2× lower training cost, respectively. Our contributions can be summarized as following: • We introduce a sampler-preserving formulation for online alignment of continuous visual genera- tors.
