The left panels show the nominal model, and the middle and right panels show the performance as a function of the evaluation radius ρ, (a) on the training kernels and (b) on the held-out kernels. Kernel-robust RL is shown for ρtr = 0.25. Indeed, at ρ = 0, continuous treatment improves from 15.81 to 16.11 months, and kernel-robust RL improves from between 48.13 and 48.77 months to between 53.07 and 53.45 months. Nominal RL, however, drops from 50.35 to 45.74 months. Hence a policy trained for a single pair (α, β) is tuned to the pair used in training, and we benefit from exposing the policy to varying dynamics during training. Second, the best training radius increases with the evaluation radius. The policy with ρtr = 0.1 is best at ρ ∈{0, 0.1}, while the policy with ρtr = 0.5 is best at ρ ∈{0.5, 1} on both sets of kernels. Hence the training radius can be chosen according to the magnitude of the shifts expected in deployment.
