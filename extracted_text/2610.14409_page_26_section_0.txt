I THE HARDWARE-PLAUSIBLE ARCHITECTURE This appendix specifies the norm- and attention-free architecture of Section 5.1 in enough detail to reimplement, together with the training recipe it needed. It shows only that competitive generation is possible on an architecture an analog substrate could run, not that this particular architecture is the optimal one. We give our reasons for the individual choices where we have them, but which of them are necessary, and at what cost, we leave to future work. What was removed and what replaced it. The architecture is built from the Energy Match- ing UNet but with three changes. Firstly, all 51 group-normalisation layers are replaced by the identity. Secondly, all 6 attention blocks are replaced by the identity, which must be done on the built module tree rather than by configuration: the UNet’s middle block hardcodes ResBlock– Attention–ResBlock, so setting the attention resolutions alone cannot produce an attention-free net- work. Thirdly, the ViT head is replaced by a flat one, which flattens the UNet’s 3 × 32 × 32 output and applies Linear(3072, 512), SiLU, Linear(512, 1). We re-initialise the UNet’s final convolution from N(0, 0.02) rather than leaving it at the flow-matching codebase’s zero initialisation, after an earlier variant of the head failed to train from zero. We did not test whether the flat head trains without this. Two further changes are made to the remaining convolutions and residual blocks. Firstly, weight standardisation (Qiao et al., 2020) replaces each convolution’s weights by ˆwi = gi wi σi √ fan-in + ϵ , σi = std(wi), (41) per output filter i, with gi a learned per-filter gain initialised to σi √ fan-in so that the reparameteri- sation is exactly the identity at initialisation. No mean subtraction is applied. It is applied to every convolution except the zero-initialised ones — therefore 42 convolutions are standardised and 23 are left plain on the built model. Those 23 are each ResBlock’s second convolution, zero-initialised by the flow-matching codebase so that every block starts as the identity; standardising an all-zero filter would leave both its weight and gain gradients at zero, so it could never leave that initialisa- tion. Secondly, residual scaling replaces each ResBlock’s output by skip + ρ (f(x) −skip) with ρ = 1/ √ 22 = 0.213, one over the square root of the number of ResBlocks (De & Smith, 2020). Nei- ther requires computation at inference: ˆw depends only on the weights, so a crossbar is programmed with it once and never recomputes a normalisation at runtime, and ρ is a wiring gain. The resolution-changing path needed no modification: downsampling is a stride-2 convolution rather than pooling, upsampling is nearest-neighbour interpolation followed by a stride-1 convolution, and both are weighted sums like any other convolution. Module census. The built model contains 42 standardised convolutions, 23 plain convolutions, 22 ResBlocks, 26 linear layers, 69 SiLU activations, and zero group-normalisation, attention or layer- normalisation modules, against 51, 14 and 16 of those respectively in the architecture it replaces. It carries 78.4M parameters against 50.0M, from the flat head’s 3072 × 512 first linear layer and a base channel width of 192 against 128, which widens every stage through the same multipliers. How we arrived at this configuration. We reached it by trial rather than by systematic search. Attention and the ViT head could each be removed without much cost, but removing normalisation made training diverge, and weight standardisation was what made the fully stripped network train- able. We did not establish which ingredients are necessary, at what width, or under what schedule; that investigation is left to future work. Training recipe. This arm is trained by backpropagation, with the settings of Table 6. It differs from the other arms by using a cosine learning rate schedule, annealing to zero at 145k steps, then a constant learning rate of 6 × 10−4 from 145k to 175k steps, after which the second phase is conducted as usual. We arrived at this change by trial after a constant learning rate of 1.2 × 10−3 destabilised partway through the first phase; we did not try a lower constant rate from scratch, and the successful run spent most of its life below 10−3, so the schedule rather than the annealing may be what mattered. 26
