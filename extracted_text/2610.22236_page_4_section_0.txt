Fisher merging and RegMean bring in data through parameter importance and through input second moments respectively [14, 29]. TF-OPD uses RegMean’s closed form; what differs is what it is fed and what it is used for. RegMean was introduced for models fine-tuned on supervised tasks, with statistics computed on each task’s training inputs. The specialists here are reinforcement-learned reasoners whose inputs are overwhelmingly their own long generations, so the statistics have to come from rollouts, and we show that on AIME25 unfiltered rollouts gain nothing over task arithmetic, whereas filtered ones recover the specialist. Other training and evaluation settings. Multi-domain fine-tuning models interactions among samples during training [30]; distributional Pareto-optimal reinforcement learning studies trade-offs across objectives [31]. Policy optimization has also been studied with interactive guidance [32], while anomaly-guided learning addresses imperfect demonstrations [33]. Related choices about supervision include adaptive sample scheduling for direct preference optimization [34] and reward models that go beyond semantics [35]. Arena-based LLM evaluation [36] and medical vision-language work on adaptive causal reasoning [37] and latent-memory evolution [38] concern other evaluation or application settings. 3 Method 3.1 Distillation of one layer is least squares Let θb be a base model and θ1, . . . , θK specialists post-trained from it, one per domain; we call them teachers, and the model we construct, θ⋆, the student. Equation (2) makes the contrast with weight arithmetic explicit. Task arithmetic sets the update to P d ∆d and so applies every teacher’s update to every input, including inputs that only another teacher ever sees; on teacher e’s inputs the layer output is off by P d̸=e ∆dx. TF-OPD minimizes exactly that error, weighted by how often each input direction occurs. Three properties of the solution locate where teachers can conflict.
