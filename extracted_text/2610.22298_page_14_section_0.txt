AI Consilium: a Benchmark of LLM Clinical Multidisciplinary Second Opinions A PREPRINT 4.2 Consilium Dynamics Across models and response formats, the interactive review showed a clear pattern of early convergence. Report consensus-defined as unanimous role acceptance of the reviewed shared report before finalization-was achieved at the first review in 1,204 of 3,040 case–model–format instances (39.6%). By the end of round 2, 2,576 instances (84.7%) had reached consensus, and 2,974 (97.8%) ultimately did so (Figures 3a and 4). Importantly, convergence was not confined to reports accepted at the outset: among the 1,836 initially disputed instances, 1,770 (96.4%) subsequently reached unanimous acceptance. Overall, the review loop converted most contested initial reports into accepted shared reports, with agreement typically emerging within the first two rounds. The process pattern varied substantially among models. GPT-5.6-Sol accepted most reports at the first review and required the fewest rounds, whereas Qwen3.5-27B began with dissent from every role but still reached eventual consensus in almost every case. These contrasting trajectories show that the benchmark captures model-specific review behavior in addition to final-answer quality. Structured reports generally prompted somewhat more review. Structured and free-form pipelines averaged 1.89 and 1.76 rounds, respectively, and reached consensus in 97.3% and 98.4% of instances. The structured and free-form pipelines reached the five-round limit in 1.6% and 1.1% of cases. This aggregate pattern was model dependent (Table 3): DeepSeek-V4-Pro, for example, required fewer rounds for structured than for free-form reports (1.27 vs. 1.77). Consensus did not mark the end of generation because a separate finalizer produced the delivered clinical answer after review. A complete path averaged 106.9 thousand provider-reported tokens per free-form case and 133.3 thousand per structured case; the structured pipeline therefore used 26.5 thousand additional tokens. The protocol consequently produced consistent procedural convergence, while structured reporting increased inference requirements. These process outcomes characterize role-conditioned synthetic review behavior and complement, rather than replace, direct assessment of clinical quality. 14
