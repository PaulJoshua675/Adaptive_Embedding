Furthermore, several studies incorporate collabo- rative signals into the tokenization process: MMGRec [19] performs quantization over a user–item interaction graph, EAGER [36] de- codes behavioural and semantic tokens in two parallel streams, and LETTER [34] augments a learnable tokenizer with collaborative and diversity regularisers. LLMs for Recommendation. Chat-Rec [9] prompts a general- purpose model with textual interaction histories, and GPT4Rec [18] fine-tunes one to generate item titles; such models rank compe- tently only over small candidate pools and remain sensitive to in- teraction order, item position, and popularity [14]. Recommenders built on language-model backbones tune on recommendation data instead: P5 [10] unifies several tasks as text-to-text but with arbi- trary numeric item IDs, LC-Rec [41] aligns semantic-ID tokens with language through auxiliary alignment tasks, and IDGenRec [30] generates textual identifiers instead of quantized codes. This work follows the semantic-ID line and investigates the training objec- tive itself, given a fixed architecture. Whereas LC-Rec introduces additional alignment tasks, HASTE reshapes the loss on the recom- mendation task and derives attribute targets from the catalogue, requiring no additional task data or prompt engineering. Structured and Auxiliary Supervision. Label structure is well exploited outside recommendation. Hierarchical softmax [24] fac- torises the prediction over a tree, coarse-to-fine curricula [28] or- der the levels in time, and hierarchy-aware penalties [2] charge more for distant errors than for sibling errors. Auxiliary objectives likewise densify supervision: deeply-supervised nets [16] attach classifiers at intermediate layers, MMoE [22] shares experts across tasks in the multi-task architectures common to recommenders, and CLCRec [38] uses a contrastive objective to preserve collab- orative signal in the content representations of cold-start items. Scheduled sampling [1] addresses the exposure bias of autoregres- sive decoders, and applies to semantic IDs, whose levels are defined relative to their predecessors.
