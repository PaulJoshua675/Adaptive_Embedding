E STEP-BALANCED GSPO DETAILS During reinforcement learning, a training sample may contain multiple assistant responses that con- tribute to the policy loss, with each response corresponding to one action step. The optimization unit of a training sample therefore need not coincide with an individual environment action step. In particular, the assistant span associated with a code action step is often substantially longer than that associated with a GUI step. The standard GSPO objective computes one sample-level importance ratio by aggregating token-level log-importance weights over all valid assistant tokens in the training sample (Zheng et al., 2025). Although this prevents a sample from receiving a larger sample-level weight solely because it contains more tokens, it does not equalize the contributions of different ac- tion steps within the same sample. As a result, a long code action can dominate both the sample-level ratio and the clipping decision even when it is only one of several actions in the training sample. This section formalizes the effect and derives a step-balanced aggregation for the GSPO ratio. Our goal is to preserve GSPO’s token-level importance weights and clipping structure while changing their sample-level aggregation to remove the deterministic dependence of an action step’s aggre- gate gradient coefficient on its token count. Recent work has also studied the granularity of im- portance ratios for LLM policy optimization, including sequence-level GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025), subsentence-level ratios (Yang et al., 2025a), cumulative token ratios (Zhang et al., 2026b), and hybrid token/sequence ratios (Min et al., 2026).
