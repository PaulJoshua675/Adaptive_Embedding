Atlas2 along with its distilled versions Atlas2-B and Atlas2-S was used under a research license. Atlas2 is the largest model with ~2 billion parameters pretrained with an adapted DINOv2 recipe on 5,5 million WSI spanning H&E, special stains, and IHC from institutions across Germany and the US (8). To extract the feature vectors each 512×512-pixel patch is resized to a 224×224-pixel RGB image, partitioned into non-overlapping 14×14 pixel tiles, resulting in a sequence of 256 tile tokens, and prepended with a learnable class token (CLS) alongside register tokens (22). This token sequence is passed through the frozen ViT backbone. The tile and register tokens are discarded, and the final layer-normalized CLS embedding z ∈ ℝ^d is taken as the patch descriptor. The dimensions (d) differ by the FM with d = 384 for Atlas2-S, d = 768 for Atlas2-B, d = 1536 for UNI2h, and d = 2048 for Atlas2. ABMIL model To train the gated ABMIL, patch-level features were extracted from tumor positive and negative slides with our standard specifications. Our training data contained 123 negative bags consisting of 294,448 patches and 137 positive bags consisting of 3,158,361 patches. A bag is defined as a matrix H ∈ ℝ^(n×d). For negative bags, n is the number of patches for that slide. For positive bags, n is the number of patches for that case sourced from a varying number of WSIs. To compute patch-level attention weights we use the gated attention formulation (23). Two independent linear projections are applied to H: first, a tanh branch V ∈ ℝ^(d×L), capturing feature relevance; second, a sigmoid branch U ∈ ℝ^(d×L), acting as a gating mechanism to suppress irrelevant patches. These are combined by element-wise multiplication, projected to a scalar attention score per patch via a weight vector, and normalized with softmax across all patches in the bag to yield attention weights aᵢ. The resulting attention-weighted average of patch embeddings forms the bag-level representation M, which is passed through a single linear classifier w to produce the bag-level prediction. The same classifier applied to an individual patch embedding hᵢ yields a per-patch tumor score w·hᵢ, and aᵢ (w·hᵢ) is that patch's contribution to the bag-level decision. Under bag-level supervision alone, attention and this per-patch score can decouple, so in addition to the main bag- level binary cross-entropy (BCE) classification loss we optimize two auxiliary losses. The first is an instance-alignment loss: patches are ranked by the detached per-patch score w·h, and the loss is the negative log of the total attention mass placed on the top-k of them, pushing attention onto the patches the classifier itself reads as tumor while leaving the classifier untouched. This inverts CLAM's instance-level supervision, which ranks patches by attention to generate pseudo-labels for a separate instance classifier (24). The second exploits the fact that every patch of a tumor- free case is a confirmed negative instance. For each positive bag, the top-k patches by w·h are compared against m sampled certain negatives: those exceeding the highest-scoring negative are labeled positive, and a BCE loss is applied against the sampled negatives as ground truth. If no top-k patch exceeds that bar, the loss falls back to negatives-only. Unlike the alignment term, this loss updates the classifier weights w directly. For both terms, k follows max(8, f·n), where f is a tuned fraction that scales selection with bag size, combined with a fixed floor that prevents degenerate selection on small bags. Attention is detached for negative bags, as a tumor-free label provides no supervisory signal for attention placement. Mild class imbalance is addressed through a weighted random sampler; the positive class weight is set to 1.
