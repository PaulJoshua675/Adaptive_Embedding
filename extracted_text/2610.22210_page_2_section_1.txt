Centerline and skeleton supervision. Centerline- and skeleton-based objectives promote connectivity by supervis- ing thin structural representations derived from the predic- tion, target, or both. clDice [20] measures reciprocal overlap between the prediction and target using differentiable soft skeletons. Menten et al. [17] developed topology-preserving differentiable skeletonization for gradient-based losses. clCE [1] applies cross-entropy at centerline locations to improve topology while preserving region accuracy. cbDice [19] in- corporates boundary and radius cues to preserve geometry across varying widths. Skeleton Recall Loss (SkelRecall) [13] uses a dilated, class-labeled target skeleton and avoids prediction-side skeletonization. These methods, however, tie structural supervision to centerline or skeleton support and do not explicitly identify the prediction error that blocks a valid reconnection. TRACE addresses this gap with a topology-preserving carrier that defines the local same-class branches to be connected and extends pointwise supervision to the bottleneck of their easiest valid reconnection. Global topology supervision. Global topology objectives constrain connected components, loops, and cavities across the segmentation. TopoLoss [11] uses persistent homology to identify predictions associated with topological errors. Betti Matching [22] matches topological features between the prediction and target while accounting for their spatial correspondence. Subsequent extensions support multiclass segmentation [3]. Topograph [16] represents prediction– target component relations as a graph to provide efficient binary and multiclass constraints. Whereas these methods constrain global or component-level topology, TRACE tar- gets a required local reconnection and directly supervises its prediction-dependent bottleneck. Pairwise and path supervision. Pairwise- and path-based objectives supervise connectivity through neighboring el- ements, point pairs, or graph paths. MALIS [24] assigns pairwise connectivity errors to the weakest edge along a maximin path in an affinity graph. CAPE [10] penalizes shortest-path costs between selected target-graph nodes on a predicted distance map. SCNP [25] adjusts semantic log- its according to the weakest same-class neighbor. WPRF [28] optimizes widest-path reachability between sampled point pairs on a target-derived support graph using a separate affinity prediction. In contrast, TRACE defines each local reconnection on a class-labeled carrier that preserves the topology of both the foreground union and each semantic class. Removing a carrier point exposes all neighboring same-class branches, which we jointly reconnect via an ex- act search over at most 9 candidate points in 2D or 27 in 3D, using a centered logit margin computed directly from standard multiclass semantic logits. 3. Method TRACE turns target-skeleton supervision from fixed-point enforcement into prediction-adaptive local reconnection by identifying and supervising the bottleneck of the easiest valid reconnection under the current prediction. We first formulate local reconnection as a connectivity bottleneck problem that requires a connected set of target-class points to reach all neighboring branches (Sec. 3.1). We then construct TASC, a thin class-labeled carrier that encodes these connectivity requirements while preserving both foreground and class- specific topology (Sec. 3.2). Next, we define a centered logit-margin score that ranks candidate points by their rival- to-target margin relative to the carrier average (Sec. 3.3). Finally, we combine the pointwise and connectivity penalties into a structural objective (Sec. 3.4). 3.1.
