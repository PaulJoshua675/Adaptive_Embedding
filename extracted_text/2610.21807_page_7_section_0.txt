Results are con­ ditional associations, not causal effects: answerer reputation, question difficulty, and platform recommendation are unmeasured. 4 Results 4.1 Nouns associated with best-answer selection The keyness scatter in Fig. 2 shows a clear pattern: answers chosen as best are distin­ guished by concrete nouns naming logistics operations, while passed-over answers are distinguished by generic, often off-topic nouns. These are answer-level lexical associa­ tions, not evidence that any particular word causes selection. Best-selected answers are characterized overwhelmingly by “logistics” itself (the single strongest noun in either direction by a wide margin, z = 38.1), followed by “distribution” (z = 7.7), “center” (z = 6.2), “warehouse” (z = 5.8), “base” (z = 5.4), “network” (z = 4.3), and “manufacturing” (z = 4.1). Passed-over answers are characterized by “people” (z = − 9.8), “job” (z = − 7.6), “car” (z = − 7.0), “reference” (z = − 7.0), “person” (z = − 6.9), “company” (z = − 5.6), and “party” (z = − 5.0). Inspection of the Japanese originals shows that “ref­ erence” derives mainly from the formulaic 参考 family — “参考までに” (for your ref­ erence) and the platform’s 「参考にした回答」 quoted-answer footer — so it marks deferential or secondhand answers rather than literal citation of external sources. Because this pooled comparison weights threads by their number of answers, the reported nouns were re-tested with the within-thread permutation test. For all fourteen nouns the observed z exceeded every one of the 500 within-thread relabelings (permuta­ tion p =.002, the resolution floor at 500 permutations).
