C Clinical Profile Presenting problems The presenting-problem taxonomy (Table 16) is non-diagnostic. It is grounded in Bakker’s presenting-problem framework (Bakker, 2019) and extended with CORE-A (Evans et al., 2000), the Bern Inventory of Treatment Goals (Grosse Holtforth and Grawe, 2002) and studies of how adults use mental-health apps and AI tools (Luo et al., 2025; Callahan et al., 2026; Ueda et al., 2026). The rate of each problem is fitted from 1,666 annotated patients of the real service (earliest session each, aggregate rates only), and the sampler reproduces those rates given age and gender. Functional impact is kept separate from the problem: it records which part of the patient’s life is most affected (Table 11). Quality of life is a single band that the profile writer chooses last. Formulation vocabularies The rest of the profile follows a 5P formulation: predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating, protective factors, and the presenting problem; and includes a core belief. One call writes all seven sections for one patient; the writer chooses labels only from the vocabularies in Table 17–Table 21 and writes the patient’s own history around them. Screening and selection We generated 1,000 profiles and screened each with an adversarial judge (GLM-5.2) that returns a verdict with every fault quoted: 795 were clean, 142 had minor faults and 63 serious ones.
