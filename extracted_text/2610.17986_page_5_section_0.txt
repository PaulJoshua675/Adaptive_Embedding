By linearity of expectation (which requires no independence), E[rank] = 1 + E[K] = 1 + P i E[Bi] = 1 + Nxq, and more generally the j-th positive (already pre- ceded by j−1 better positives) sits at expected rank j + N xq,j. The map (3) assembles these per-positive ranks into the metric. Whether it equals the realized mAP exactly or only predicts it depends on which hardness it is fed, which we make precise after the proof. The Poisson limit is the standard convergence Binomial(N, xq) →Poisson(Nxq). Monotonicity follows by coupling KN+1 = KN + BN+1 ≥KN, so each rank rj only grows and each j/rj only shrinks. Exact identity vs. prediction. The map (3) is used two ways, and separating them is what makes “exact law” precise. (i) Identity: a benchmark pool is one fixed set, and the j-th positive’s rank is the deterministic rj = j + Kq,j, where Kq,j counts the distractors outscoring it. With the real- ized fraction ¯xq,j := Kq,j/N we have j + N ¯xq,j = rj, so Rk({¯xq,j}, N) is that gallery’s mAP exactly: truncation included, no expectation, no linearization (App. C). (ii) Prediction: the popu- lation hardness xq,j of Eq. (1) differs. Since Kq,j ∼Binomial(N, xq,j), ¯xq,j concentrates on it with standard deviation p xq,j(1−xq,j)/N, so feeding {xq,j} predicts the realized mAP. That pre- diction error is empirically ≤0.006 at 100M. The E[1/r]-vs-1/E[r] gap concerns only the never- reported pool-resampling average E[AP], which we compute exactly (the per-positive Binomial sum P m j j+m Pr[Kq,j=m]) and find sits ≈0.005 above the realized value: since a benchmark reports the realized number, targeting it with the expected-rank map—as we do—is more accurate than targeting E[AP], not less. The expected-rank result 1 + Nxq holds under arbitrary dependence between distrac- tors. The exact (1 −xq)N/Poisson forms assume independence, which holds for i.i.d. random dis- tractors and which we find empirically tight at 100M (Sec. 5). The theorem models N i.i.d. draws from a distractor source. Evaluating on a fixed release (e.g. the official 100M set) is one realiza- tion, for which the law is the with-replacement approximation of the finite-pool (hypergeometric) outcome. The two coincide at sampling fractions ≪1, and the full-pool value is the N →pool-size limit. Everything hinges on the single per-query scalar: the law is an exact identity at the realized exceedance fractions and a concentration-tight predictor at the population hardness (above), and all of the empirical content lives in how that hardness is distributed across queries.
