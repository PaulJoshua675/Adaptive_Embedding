LLM-based deep search agents have become a pow- erful paradigm for knowledge-intensive problem solving, coordinating multi-turn search, evidence inspection, query reformulation, and termination decisions through language-model reasoning (Yao *Corresponding author et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2026a). Unlike con- ventional retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), which typically follows a one-pass retrieve-then- read pipeline (Lewis et al., 2021; Gao et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024), deep search agents iteratively formulate queries, inspect evidence, update beliefs, and decide when to stop (Nakano et al., 2021; Li et al., 2025c). This makes deep search a long- horizon sequential decision problem, where suc- cess depends on adaptive decisions about what to search, what evidence to keep, and when to termi- nate. Because supervised fine-tuning mainly imi- tates surface behavior (Nakano et al., 2021; Schick et al., 2023) and cannot optimize such strategic de- cisions, reinforcement learning has become central to training competitive deep search agents (Guo et al., 2025; Jin et al., 2025; Song et al., 2025). However, applying reinforcement learning to deep search agents encounters three coupled diffi- culties in credit assignment (Figure 1). First, termi- nal outcome rewards used by group-relative meth- ods such as GRPO (Shao et al., 2024; Jin et al., 2025; Song et al., 2025; Lu et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025b; Liu et al., 2025) are uniformly broadcast to every action, so a single final reward cannot reveal which intermediate decisions were useful, redundant, or harmful. Second, recent process- supervision methods such as GiGPO (Feng et al., 2025) that align trajectories by their intermediate states require state recurrence across trajectories, yet deep search rollouts seldom revisit the same ob- servable state even when they acquire the same evi- dence. Third, dense process rewards derived from changes in the model’s predicted answer probabil- ity, as in IGPO (Wang et al., 2026), or from learned critics and rubric-based reward models (Shi et al., 2025; Lee et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025, 2026b; Lu et al., 2026), provide denser signals but are driven by model-internal beliefs or learned proxies rather than by externally verified knowledge.
