However, this modality presents several limitations, including poor separation of objects or surfaces with similar tempera- tures [22], background interference when ambient temperatures approximate those of objects [23], and the absence of colour information, which remains an essential cue for accurate scene parsing and object identification [24]. Despite these constraints, the practical acquisition of thermal data has become increasingly accessible. The commercial miniaturization of Long-Wave Infrared micro- bolometers has rendered these sensors both cost-effective and easy to integrate into real-world au- tonomous systems. In the scope of this study, the thermal modality is technically leveraged to en- hance low-light RGB data by providing a high Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) in environments where visible-spectrum sensors succumb to photon scarcity. Specifically for semantic segmentation, ther- mal data provides an invariant heat-signature prior very helpful for class-specific identification (e.g., distinguishing biological entities from inanimate structures). Improving Segmentation Performance Through Enhancement. VesperMUSE adopts a pre- segmentation thermal-guided enhancement approach, where low-light images are first enhanced using a model trained separately from the semantic segmentation network. This design provides greater modularity, allowing the framework to be extended to other degradations, like haze or blur, by simply replacing the enhancement module. Another advantage is that the training data can be de- coupled, removing the need for low-light, well-lit, and semantic segmentation annotations to coexist within the same dataset. Since the enhancement network is trained independently, its predictions achieve a high degree of visual quality, as the module is optimized to approximate normal lighting conditions. In contrast, jointly trained (one-stage) networks may produce intermediate-enhanced outputs represented in latent space which is not suitable for direct human evaluation. This aspect is particularly important for the present work, as the low-light enhanced RGB map is further used as input for additional representations. Challenge Understanding Through Data.
