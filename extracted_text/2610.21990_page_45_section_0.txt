3.4.6 Fusion Vision Transformer Loss function In the datasets VesperMUSE tests semantic segmentation on, there is both a significant structural and semantic difference between the classes (especially on the CART [25] dataset) and also a very heavy class imbalance. To ensure multi-modal feature alignment and good class representation a weighted hybrid loss function is employed which also acts on multiple levels allowing deep supervi- sion. Considering Lmain(·, ·) as the hybrid loss function, P(0) as the final prediction, and P(s) as the inter- mediate predictions originating from the decoding path and weighted by λs, with Y(0) denoting the ground-truth labels and Y(s) representing the ground-truth labels resized to match the spatial reso- lution of the corresponding deep supervision outputs, the total loss function Ltotal is formulated as follows: Ltotal = Lhybrid (︁ P(0), Y )︁ + ∑︂ s∈S λs Lhybrid(P(s),Y(s)). (3.62) Hybrid Loss. Lhybrid(·, ·) is a weighted combination of the multi-class Focal loss which operates at pixel level and is derived from the cross-entropy objective and the weighted Dice loss, which is region-based and directly optimizes the overlap between the predicted and ground-truth masks. Since the Dice loss is computed from class-wise statistics it is less sensitive to pixel-level imbal- ance and better captures structural and object-level coherence. The relative contribution of the con- stituents of the hybrid loss, Lhybrid, is balanced by the coefficients α and β. Lhybrid = α LFocal + β LDice. (3.63) Multi-class Focal Loss. LFocal(·, ·) is a pixel-wise classification loss designed to mitigate severe class imbalance. The strong pixel-density of the background class in both datasets can overwhelm the gradient signal and hinder learning of minority or hard classes. The Focal loss addresses this issue by introducing a modulating factor that reduces the importance of well-classified samples and emphasizes misclassified or uncertain predictions. Additionally, class-dependent weights are incorporated to further compensate for dataset imbalance.
