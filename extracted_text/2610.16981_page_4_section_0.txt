Inter-Transaction Edges connect receipts and expenditures of the same ac- count and asset across different transactions. For each node (h, a, τ), we calculate the net balance change ∆h,a,τ = X e∈Ein h,a,τ xe − X e∈Eout h,a,τ xe, (1) where Ein h,a,τ and Eout h,a,τ are the incoming and outgoing transfer edges of the node and xe is the transferred amount. Mint edges are excluded from the outgoing amount, and burn edges from the incoming amount. Consequently, ∆h,a,τ > 0 indicates a receipt, ∆h,a,τ < 0 an expenditure, and nodes with ∆h,a,τ = 0 receive no inter-transaction edges. Connecting each receipt to every subsequent expenditure would create a dense set of temporally valid but increasingly implausible edges. Inspired by the FIFO and LIFO allocation conventions used in taint tracking [12], we apply a recency-based cumulative-expenditure cutoff, referred to as the LIFO-cutoff, which approximates rather than implements LIFO accounting. For each receipt ri = ∆hi,a,τ > 0, let hj1, . . . , hjm denote the subsequent transactions with expenditures ∆hjℓ,a,τ < 0, ordered chronologically. (2) If the cumulative expenditure never reaches ri, all m expenditures are retained (c∗ i = m). Thus, each receipt is connected only to the earliest subsequent expen- ditures whose cumulative value is sufficient to exhaust the received amount. This LIFO-cutoff rule assumes received funds are spent soon after receipt, rather than after older holdings. It favors short temporal spans and avoids re- constructing each account’s complete balance history, making it suitable for ego- based tracing that begins from a specific borrowing or transfer event, though it remains an accounting approximation rather than a definitive attribution of fun- gible tokens.
