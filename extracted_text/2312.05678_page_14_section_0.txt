Credible intervals formed from these draws are compared with designated thresholds to inform the deployment of corrective actions. 3.3 SFP-rate inference and sampling plan utility The utility of a sampling plan is directly tied to SFP-rate inference. As with Wickett et al. (2023), we consider two echelons of a larger, more complex supply chain in analyzing sampling plans. Sampling plan n ∈(Z+)|A| is an allocation of samples where na is the allocation to test node a in A. Each PMS iteration has a budget of N samples; thus, P a∈A na ≤N. Consistent with PMS practice, we express the budget by the number of samples instead of the cost to procure samples. Bayesian experimental design uses existing testing data and priors on SFP rates to explore how the value of inference might be improved through different sampling plans in the next PMS iteration (Chaloner and Verdinelli, 1995). Inference improvement is defined by a value function capturing what constitutes a good estimate of SFP rates. Proper characterization of this value function is key to Bayesian experimental design.
