A reconstruction can also reduce a trajectory’s immediate value while creating opportunities for later improvement or exchange. These dependencies motivate the joint choice of how to intervene, where to act, and how search should continue. The resulting control problem links structural targeting with the delayed effects of intervention (Figure 1). Large language models (LLMs) offer complementary capabilities for addressing this problem. They can construct executable heuris- tics from problem descriptions and evaluative feedback [22, 26, 38], and make state-dependent heuristic choices during optimiza- tion [35, 40]. Bringing these capabilities to conflict-graph search requires an actionable connection between contextual judgment and structural change. A useful decision must identify its target, organize the intervention and its continuation, and remain mean- ingful as the solver progresses. This motivates a common control abstraction through which both generated policies and runtime reasoning can direct a continuing search. We propose CIPHEUR, an LLM-guided structural search-control framework for conflict-graph optimization. The framework trans- lates conflict evidence and search experience into coordinated de- cisions about how to redirect an ongoing search. To express these decisions, we introduce the Structured Intervention Unit (SIU), which jointly specifies a structural target, an intervention, and its continuation. Candidate SIUs are grounded in the conflicts affecting current search trajectories. The solution-quality changes observed across executed intervention–recovery periods inform subsequent choices, closing the loop between structural evidence and search behavior. Two working modes realize control over this common SIU space. In the offline mode, an LLM translates problem semantics and the control specification into customized executable policies. These policies select SIUs from evolving observations and may update their internal estimates from accumulated execution feedback. In the online mode, an LLM makes the same type of SIU choice di- rectly from current structural evidence and search history. Offline synthesis provides reusable control logic, while online reasoning retains contextual judgment during a solve. Both modes act through a shared execution interface that preserves the original objective and feasibility constraints; asynchronous online decisions allow native search to progress throughout model inference. The contributions are threefold. First, we propose CIPHEUR, an LLM-guided structural search-control framework for conflict-graph optimization that turns conflict evidence and search experience into adaptive control of ongoing search. Second, we design SIUs that couple structural targets with intervention and recovery, using feed- back from executed intervention–recovery periods to guide subse- quent choices. Third, we develop two policy realizations—offline controller synthesis and online contextual decision-making—over a shared executable interface, with asynchronous online execution sustaining native search and both modes preserving the original objective and feasibility constraints. Experiments on eight constraint views of one satellite–ground scheduling source, with five runs per view and a 900-second eval- uation cap, establish the competitiveness of CIPHEUR across 11 configurations. CIPHEUR-online increases mean scheduled contact duration by 14.84% over the greedy baseline GWMIN. It also out- performs CHILS in all 40 paired runs. A separately frozen, one-shot LLM-generated controller improves the mean objective over CHILS without runtime model calls, while matched mechanism studies and cross-source evaluations further characterize the framework’s effectiveness. 2
