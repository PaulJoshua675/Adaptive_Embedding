Without our distillation, the baselines score 25.9–34.8 on MMLU, close to or modestly above 25% chance accuracy and far below 76.8 at full precision. RoleQ-T retains 47.6 at 1.590 bpw and 62.8 at 2.157 (56.0 before distillation). Perplexity alone therefore gives an incomplete account of the knowledge lost during conversion. Two comparisons delimit this result. First, shared scale distillation does not close the gap: applying it to PT2-LLM’s codes yields 15.51 perplexity at 1.835 bpw, versus 11.33 for RoleQ-T at 1.590 (Table 1). The advantage thus survives the same distillation procedure, although the quantization recipes differ. Second, comparison with learned ternarization entails a budget trade-off. CAT-Q optimises its codes for 60 epochs on 512 C4 samples, 8–64 A100-hours at 8B by its own bracket (Appendix A), and its released checkpoint scores 52.9 MMLU at 1.71 bpw on post-trained Qwen3- 8B on our harness. Our planes reach 61.8 at 1.99 bpw in under ten GPU-hours: +8.9 points for 0.28 additional bits. At CAT-Q’s own 1.71 bpw, the corresponding RoleQ-T-g128 leads it on perplexity (17.00 vs 27.68) and narrowly on zero-shot (61.7 vs 61.0) and ties on MMLU (53.1 vs 52.9). The gains extend to 14B. With planes and scale distillation, RoleQ-T reaches 8.05 perplexity (1.26× full precision), 69.2 zero-shot and 68.3 MMLU at 2.156 bpw, versus 11.78, 56.6 and 47.8 for QTEA’s released checkpoint at 2.26. At the matched PTQ stage, before distillation, the planes run still leads on all three measures (10.11, 65.7 and 62.0), with each gap at least 19× our seed range. This includes WikiText2, QTEA’s calibration corpus. The one-plane run at 1.589 bpw also leads after distillation (9.53 / 65.4 / 59.7), saving 0.67 bpw, but trails at PTQ. The lower-rate advantage therefore depends on distillation; the planes advantage holds at both stages. TWLA (Zhao et al., 2026) is a higher-rate reference: it releases no checkpoint, and its released code, which reproduces its reported Qwen3-8B perplexity, builds a masked two-plane model its paper does not describe, resident at 4.15–4.65 bpw, roughly twice our planes rate (Table 1, Appendix A). Section 5 discusses the stronger trellis-coded results at matched bits on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Appendix Table 10). 4.2 THE PLACEMENT RULE Table 2 isolates scale placement: within each grid and model, the placements share the solver, random Hadamard r1, calibration draw and paired seeds, with no distillation. At ternary, RoleQ has the lowest mean perplexity at every tested size in both families, improving on the best uniform placement by up to 2×, with the same ordering on every seed from 1.7B upward. At 14B, the gain also appears on both task measures: 52.3 versus 45.6 zero-shot and 38.5 versus chance-level 25.4 MMLU for per-OC (Appendix Table 7). The 0.6B and Qwen-8B perplexity gaps remain within the largest seed range. Integer grids show the same mean perplexity ordering, but the margin depends on alphabet size and model. On the four-level grid, the best uniform placement has 1.19× RoleQ’s perplexity at 1.7B and 1.22× on Llama-8B, versus about 1.01× at Qwen-8B and 14B. At INT3 and INT4, the largest gains occur at 1.7B: ratios of 2.19× and 1.14×, respectively. Placement thus matters most at ternary, where the per-unit scale is a unit’s only continuous degree of freedom; here the benefit persists to the largest tested model in each family. Two practical comparisons follow. RoleQ-INT4 matches or improves on MXFP4 (Open Compute Project, 2023; Rouhani et al., 2023) perplexity at 0.6B, 1.7B and 8B while using 8% fewer bits (information bits, Appendix A). The rule also comes within 0.023% of AdaDim’s (Heo et al., 2024) searched H-metric reconstruction error at half the solver work of a two-way GPTQ search, without calibration data for axis selection or stored axis flags. This is a reconstruction comparison, not an end-to-end AdaDim evaluation. Appendix C controls for codes, scale count and fitting procedure, and shows that the rule’s advantage depends on rotation. 4.3 SPARSE SECONDARY PLANES Secondary planes recover much of the knowledge lost in single-plane conversion for a fraction of a bit. At 8B, +0.57 bpw of text-Fisher planes recovers about half the MMLU loss: scores rise from 33.3 to 56.0 at PTQ and from 47.6 to 62.8 after scale distillation, against 76.8 at full pre- cision. After distillation, perplexity improves from 11.33 to 9.30 (Appendix Table 9). At 14B, planes raise MMLU from 44.7 to 62.0 at PTQ and from 59.7 to 68.3 after distillation, with zero- shot gains of 10.2 and 3.8 points.
