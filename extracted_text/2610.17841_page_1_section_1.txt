Evaluating fashion retrieval systems remains challeng- ing due to the limited availability of benchmarks that cap- ture the fine-grained, open-ended queries encountered in real-world search and recommendation. Existing bench- marks often rely on manual curation, predefined tax- onomies, or synthetic prompts, limiting scalability and se- mantic coverage. We introduce FASHION-RQ: Mining Hierarchical Semantic Structure for Automated Evalua- tion of Fashion Retrieval, an automated framework that derives user-type queries and relevance sets directly from catalog structure. Our key insight is that hierarchical dis- crete representations learned by Residual Quantized Vari- ational Autoencoders (RQ-VAE) organize products into se- mantically coherent clusters at progressively finer granu- larities. We leverage this structure to construct FASHION- RQBENCH, a scalable benchmark without manual query annotation or predefined taxonomies. We propose four com- plementary tasks for evaluating ranking, relevance classifi- cation, fine-grained sibling discrimination, and paraphrase robustness, alongside query- and attribute-level relevance metrics. Experiments across recent retrieval models demon- strate that FASHION-RQBENCH differentiates model ca- pabilities across query types and attributes. A human study further shows that benchmark queries achieve comparable naturalness and higher intent clarity than real user queries, supporting their validity for real-world evaluation. The code and FASHION-RQBENCH dataset will be publicly re- leased upon acceptance.
