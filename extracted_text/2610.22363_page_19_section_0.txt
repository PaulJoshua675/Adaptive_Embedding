E.2 SCORING RUBRIC Humans and the VLM use the five dimensions and score anchors in Table 8. Scores 2 and 4 indicate intermediate performance. Action process and instruction adherence are the primary criteria. Reviewers assess the manipulation sequence and its transition toward the specified goal, including the required intermediate actions, visible contact, and consistency of hands and objects. E.3 VLM EVALUATION We use qwen3.8-max (Qwen Team, 2026) at temperature 0 for independent, method-blind video scoring. Each request contains the initial and goal images, the task instruction, and time-ordered frames from one anonymous video. We sample 16 uniformly spaced frames and the final eight consecutive frames, yielding 22 frames after deduplication. The denser sampling near the end captures the transition into the goal state. Frame indices and timestamps accompany the images. The judge applies the shared rubric to each dimension and returns a score, instruction checks, and supporting frame indices. It evaluates the visible action sequence, marks insufficient evidence as unobservable, and flags conflicts between instructions and reference images. Returned frame indices are checked against the supplied frames. Both the human-alignment study and the external-baseline comparison use independent single-video scores. 19
