A Shared Evaluation Inputs The following wrappers are reproduced from the packaged EASY N001 and MEDIUM C001 prompt files. HARD uses the same wrapper as MEDIUM. Only the idea text changes across items. These are the original user-facing retrieval inputs, not instructions to reveal a desig- nated answer. The original files and hashes are retained under datasets/<tier>/prompts/; gold metadata and prior results are evaluated separately and are not runtime inputs. A.1 EASY wrapper Search for the research paper that best matches the idea below. The target has a journal, conference, or workshop publication dated from 2015 through 2025, inclusive. A verified preprint version of the same work is acceptable; its date and title may differ from the published version. Return a ranked list of up to five distinct candidate papers. For each, give the exact title, authors, publication year, venue, a DOI or stable paper URL, and one sentence explaining the match. Rank by the complete idea rather than shared topic words. Verify each paper’s identity against an authoritative record. Do not invent citations. Versions of the same work count as one candidate, but a predecessor or follow-up is not automatically the same paper. If no plausible candidate can be verified, say so. Research idea: {idea_text} A.2 MEDIUM and HARD wrapper Search for the research paper that best matches the idea below. The target has a journal, conference or workshop publication dated from 2015 through 2025, inclusive. A verified preprint of the same work is acceptable; its title or date may differ from the published version. Return up to five distinct candidate works, ranked by the complete idea rather than shared topic words. For each candidate, give its exact title, authors, publication year, venue, DOI or stable paper URL, and one sentence explaining the conceptual match. When a conference paper and an expanded journal article are part of the same work family, identify their relationship and list the versions within one candidate rather than filling multiple ranks with them. Do not merge distinct follow-ups or merely related papers. Verify identities against authoritative records. Do not invent citations. If several papers fit, state the ambiguity rather than inventing a unique answer. If no plausible candidate can be verified, say so. Research idea: {idea_text} A.3 One target expressed at three information levels EASY. Could we infer a neighborhood’s fuzzy edges from residents’ contributed locations rather than force it into a single official boundary? I want maps to distinguish its core from places that only partly belong. MEDIUM. I keep wondering whether a neighborhood has to have a clear edge on a map. Could we represent the way people loosely agree about a place without forcing one exact boundary? HARD. Maybe maps should leave room for disagreement about where a neighborhood begins and ends. This example illustrates reduced identifying information; it is not a calibration proof for every query. Broad descriptions can legitimately fit several works. The designated positive is used for post-retrieval Hit@5, while relevance of the other returned works requires separate assessment.
