We studied multilingual dense retrieval through cross-lingual disagreement: how the rankings dif- ferent languages produce for the same need diverge in isolation and under competition in a shared in- dex. This view separates failures that look iden- tical under relevance metrics. A language may retrieve well alone, preserve its internal ranking under mixing, and still fail in the shared index be- cause its relevant foreign-language documents are demoted beneath the same-language or English ma- terial. The failure is thus one of interference, not absent representation: correct cross-lingual docu- ments are pushed down rather than lost, replaced mostly by non-relevant material whose language follows model-dependent regimes. The same signal also corrects. Among training- free interventions estimated from an external cor- pus, mean removal, subspace removal, rotation, and disagreement-guided steering repair different aspects of the failure: mean removal gives stronger ranking quality, while steering improves recall and reduces own-language crowding. Relevance, agree- ment, and language composition move separately, so multilingual retrieval should be judged not only by how many relevant documents are found but by how languages disagree about what to retrieve. Cross-lingual disagreement offers both a label-free diagnostic of this interference and a practical signal for inference-time correction.
