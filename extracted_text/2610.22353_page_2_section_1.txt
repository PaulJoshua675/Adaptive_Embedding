MoEs. Modern sparse MoEs originated with the sparsely gated MoE layer of Shazeer et al. (2017) and were extended to large-scale Transformers in e.g., Lepikhin et al. (2020); Fedus et al. (2022). Later work focused on improving scalability, stability, and expert specialization. GLaM (Du et al., 2022) demonstrated that sparse language models could scale beyond one trillion parameters, while ST-MoE (Zoph et al., 2022) improved training stability. MoEs have since become widely adopted. Beyond those mentioned earlier, other representative examples include Gemma4, OLMoE and gpt-oss (Team-Gemma, 2026; Muennighoff et al., 2025; Agarwal et al., 2025). Across this development trajectory, two trends have clearly emerged: fine-grained expert segmentation and shared experts. Tension between expressiveness and pretraining. A growing line of work shows that a model’s expressive capacity may not be readily realized without carefully designed training procedures (Lion et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026; Jha and Reagen, 2026; Wei et al., 2025a).
