We relax the free phase to convergence throughout (App. H). The correspondence itself is not the constraint. Where EP estimates the parameter gradient by a finite difference of two nudged equilibria, the implicit solve obtains it directly at equilibrium, so its only error is the O(γ) correspondence bias of Section 4.1. Even in TF32 cos(∆θIFT, ∆θBP) is exact to four decimal places from γ = 10−7 to 10−2, softening only to 0.998 at γ = 10−1, and is equally insensitive to the number of conjugate-gradient steps (App. H). Everything that follows is therefore a test of EP’s finite-difference estimator rather than of the correspondence. γ and β set a window together. Once the free phase has converged, the fidelity of ∆θEP to ∆θBP depends on the size of the displacement the nudge produces, which γ and β control jointly: the two nudged equilibria differ by O(γβ) (Section 3.2.1), so it is their product that matters. Table 3 (App. H) gives cos(∆θEP, ∆θBP) on a γ–β grid, and the usable region is a diagonal band, where each decade lost in γ is recovered by roughly a decade gained in β: γ = 3 × 10−5 needs β ≥10, where γ = 10−3 is clean from β = 0.3. The two edges have different causes. Below the band the displacement is too small to survive TF32, and float64 rescues it completely — 1.0000 against TF32’s 0.77 at the operating γ = 10−4 and β = 0.3 (Figure 7). Above it the nudged equilibria are displaced too far for a linear finite difference to estimate the derivative well; float64 does not rescue that edge, and since the implicit solve carries the same O(γ) bias yet degrades only to 0.998 there, the failure belongs to the nudge rather than to the correspondence or the arithmetic. The band therefore closes at both ends, with no β reaching 0.99 by γ = 10−1; our phase-1 operating point, γ = 10−4 with β = 30, sits comfortably inside it at 0.9998. The window width is due to the linearised nudge: sweeping β alone at the operating γ (App. H), the linear nudge holds cos ≥0.99 from β = 3 to 103, where the quadratic nudge only does so at β = 1 and nowhere above. 5 CIFAR-10 GENERATION BY BACKPROP-FREE TRAINING The previous section established both correspondences at fixed weights. We now train with them end to end, over 105 steps, and measure the generation quality of the resulting model. We train three arms: the feedforward Energy Matching model by backpropagation, and the PCN at γ = 10−4 by the implicit solve and by EP with the linearised nudge. The architecture, optimiser, schedule, batch size, EMA decay and step budget are shared; initialisation and the random draws of training are not. The EP arm’s phase-2 differs further: a smaller γ, a stiffer tether λ and a larger β, starting from its 100k phase-1 checkpoint rather than 145k. Relaxation settings are in Table 6 and the rest of the recipe in App. J. Backprop-free training matches backpropagation. Table 2 gives the three arms. After phase 1, backpropagation reaches an FID of 6.42 at 145k steps and the implicit solve 6.70, while the EP arm reaches 6.23 at the 100k checkpoint its contrastive phase starts from. These span 0.47, more than the ∼0.2 below which we cannot resolve two FID evaluations (App. J), which is unsurprising for runs that share neither an initialisation nor a data realisation. The contrastive phase then brings all three together: 3.54 for backpropagation, 3.64 for the implicit solve, and 3.64 for EP — the one estimator of the three that a neuromorphic substrate could run.
