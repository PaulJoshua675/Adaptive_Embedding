F.5 Optimizer-State Co-Transformation with Lion A hard rebalance changes gradient coordinates while Lion retains a momentum buffer storing an EMA of past gradients. To measure the effect of this state mismatch, we compare Vanilla, hard SAGGS, Soft SAGGS, and the state-covariant variant of Appendix D.5 under matched medium-scale Lion protocols. Design. The state-covariant experiment comprises six runs: λ ∈{0.0, 0.2}, three paired seeds, 101M parameters and L = 24, WikiText-103, Lion η = 2 × 10−4, β = (0.9, 0.99), weight decay 0.1, batch 32, 15K steps, all-pairs reparameterization, and Tbal = 100. The only changed variable relative to hard SAGGS is that the Lion momentum buffer (exp_avg) is multiplied by the same chain-rule factor as the gradient coordinate after every rebalance. The Vanilla, hard SAGGS, and Soft SAGGS baselines use the matched three-seed Lion baselines from the hard-rebalancing comparison. Their means and sample SDs are reported alongside the state-covariant variant in Table 10. Interpretation. At λ = 0, state-covariant SAGGS has a lower mean and less seed variation than hard SAGGS: 24.14 ± 0.15 PPL compared with 30.12 ± 10.56 PPL in Table 10. State-covariant SAGGS still has an observed mean cost of 0.225 PPL relative to Vanilla, with ¯∆Vanilla−State = −0.225 PPL and n = 3. These results support an optimizer-state contribution to the cost of hard rebalancing, while showing that co-transforming the momentum buffer does not eliminate the measured unperturbed mean cost. At λ = 0.2, the state-covariant variant retains the full protective effect: Vanilla minus state-covariant SAGGS is +52.917 ± 31.539 PPL, with d = +1.68 (Table 10), matching the direction and order of magnitude of the matched Vanilla-minus-hard and Vanilla-minus-Soft contrasts in Table 10. State-covariant SAGGS and hard SAGGS differ by only −0.021 PPL on average at λ = 0.2, while Soft SAGGS is slightly lower than state-covariant SAGGS by 0.117 PPL on average.
