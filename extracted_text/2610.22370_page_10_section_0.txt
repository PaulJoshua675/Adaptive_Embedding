Fig. 8 further illustrates how different importance signals affect region preservation. The CFG- based signal [9] is strongly biased toward prompt-aligned foreground regions and therefore neglects unmentioned background content. Using only the temporal signal alleviates this issue by preserving evolving background regions (yellow box), but it still misses structurally important boundaries (black regions), leading to structural distortions (red box). By jointly modeling temporal and spatial signals, our importance map preserves both evolving background content and critical object boundaries, resulting in more faithful image structure and higher overall visual quality. Constraint Token Selection. In Tab. 6, we ablate different definitions of the constraint set. The best results are obtained when the constraint tokens are selected from regions with both high temporal and high spatial responses. In particular, including spatially high-response tokens improves structural preservation, indicating that the spatial axis captures fine object details and structurally salient regions. By contrast, using spatially low-response tokens degrades performance because such tokens are biased toward homogeneous regions and therefore fail to protect meaningful object structure. Effectiveness of the Importance Signal. To isolate the contribution of our importance signal, we keep the entire merging pipeline of [9] unchanged and replace only its CFG-based importance with ours. Since [9] requires a single token ranking, we combine our temporal and spatial rankings using rank-sum. As shown in Tab. 7, replacing only the CFG-based signal with ours improves CLIP-T, HPSv2, and all fidelity metrics, demonstrating that the proposed dynamics-aware importance more effectively identifies tokens to preserve or merge. Using the full DaTMe merging strategy further improves all metrics, indicating that the role-assignment and spatial merging design provides additional complementary benefits. Preference Study. We evaluate perceptual quality at a compression ratio of r = 0.3 using human judgments. For 20 randomly sampled PixArt-α cases, 32 evaluators compare outputs from ToMeSD, CFG [9], and DaTMe without access to the uncompressed reference. Evaluators independently rate text alignment and visual quality, and select their overall preferred result. As shown in Fig. 9, DaTMe achieves the highest ratings for both text alignment and visual quality, while also being the most preferred method overall. 5
