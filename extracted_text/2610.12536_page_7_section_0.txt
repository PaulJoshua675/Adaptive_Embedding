5.2 Limitations and next steps Understanding EliteTable’s components requires abla- tions and comparisons with other systems. The observed improvements cannot yet be attributed specifically to reuse, editing, remixing, retention, or repair. Ten searches per environment quantify variation under fixed episode panels, but do not establish robustness across episode distributions, language models, prompts, or search con- figurations. The tasks are well known, so the model may have encountered descriptions or potential solutions during training. Time-based deadlines can produce different outcomes on different hardware. Hosted-provider variation can also change model outputs and search trajectories, even when the same model route is requested. The final solutions are Python programs available for inspection, but source availability alone does not establish human interpretability. I would prioritize budget-matched comparisons with other program-search algorithms, including a history- independent generation baseline, followed by complete- search ablations and comparisons across language models. Longer budgets, new episode panels, and other task fami- lies would test the limits of the observations. The present results provide development evidence for those designs rather than confirmatory evidence of comparative superi- ority.
