The overall framework of FENAS. The pipeline consists of three decoupled stages: (1) Feature Extraction: raw zero-cost proxy scores are extracted from com- plementary physical dimensions. (2) CAST Feature Engineering: heterogeneous proxy scores are aligned and transformed into a compact feature manifold. (3) Meta- predictor: a lightweight meta-learner uses extremely few-shot labels to output accu- rate performance rankings. Zero-Cost Proxies (ZCPs) offer a promising solution by estimating architec- ture quality at initialization from a single forward or backward pass. They can quickly screen weak candidates before expensive training and thus greatly re- duce search cost. However, individual proxies are often unstable because each one captures only a limited physical property of an architecture, such as ex- pressivity, trainability, or connectivity. As a result, their rank correlations vary significantly across datasets, tasks, and search spaces [23,31], making raw ZCPs difficult to use as universal and reliable performance indicators. Few-shot predictors based on Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) or other topol- ogy encoders [24,32,4] provide another way to reduce evaluation cost. By ex- plicitly modeling architecture graphs and learning topology-aware representa- tions, these methods can improve prediction quality under limited supervision. Nevertheless, they introduce additional overhead from graph construction, mes- sage passing, and structured inference. More importantly, in extreme few-shot regimes, e.g., less than 1% labeled samples, learning high-dimensional topology representations from scratch can be sample-inefficient and prone to overfitting. Our experiments show that on topology-stable benchmarks such as NAS-Bench- 201, a lightweight topology-agnostic predictor can already outperform heavy topology-based methods; with only 78 labeled architectures, FENAS achieves a τ of 0.695 on CIFAR-10, surpassing DCL-ENAS [36] (0.610) and CAP [11] (0.600). To address the tension between the instability of individual ZCPs and the complexity of topology-based predictors, we propose FENAS (Few-shot meta- Ensembling Neural Architecture Search), an efficient and topology-agnostic per- formance prediction framework. Our key insight is that, instead of spending model capacity on explicit topology learning, it is more effective to directly model the intrinsic priors of architectures and learn how to combine them with minimal supervision. Specifically, we extract features from four complementary physical dimensions: expressivity, trainability, connectivity, and parameter ca- pacity.
