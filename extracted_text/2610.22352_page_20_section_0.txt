B Training Data and Optimization B.1 Training Data We train our models using publicly available 3D datasets. The 3D generator uses assets from TexVerse (Zhang et al., 2025a), Objaverse-XL (Deitke et al., 2023), ABO (Collins et al., 2022), HSSD (Khanna et al., 2024), and open synthetic 3D collections, together with human scans from 4D-Dress (Wang et al., 2024), Human4DiT (Shao et al., 2024), THuman2.1 (Yu et al., 2021b), and X-Humans (Shen et al., 2023). These assets provide geometry and material supervision for observation-guided full-surface completion. The 2D material estimator is trained on paired RGB images, material maps, and normal maps rendered from Objaverse assets. All datasets are used in accordance with their respective licenses for non-commercial academic research. B.2 Training F2D We initialize F2D from FireRed-Image-Edit-1.1 adapted to individual PBR and normal prediction tasks, and jointly fine-tune it on aligned RGB images and ground-truth material and normal maps rendered from publicly available 3D assets. Both the task-specific adaptation and joint fine-tuning use these rendered data. Modality-axis attention is inserted at Transformer blocks 29, 39, 49, and 59, with rank 256 and four attention heads. We optimize these adapters and backbone blocks 29–49 using a latent flow-matching objective and AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019), with learning rates of 2 × 10−4 and 5 × 10−7, respectively. The VAE and text encoder remain frozen. By folding the batch dimension, the model processes albedo, metallic-roughness, and an auxiliary normal stream in parallel, inserting lightweight cross-attention adapters at specific transformer layers to exchange features across modality axes at identical spatial coordinates. We train F2D with a flow-matching objective anchored at the fixed maximum-noise endpoint σ = 1.0 using an outlier-masked MSE loss. Elementwise latent-flow residuals with absolute magnitude greater than 50 contribute zero loss but remain in the averaging denominator. Losses are averaged equally across the albedo, metallic-roughness, and normal streams. At inference, we use a single prediction at this endpoint to estimate the clean material latents, which the image VAE decodes into 1024 × 1024 PBR maps. B.3 3D Generator Training Preprocessing for training. We build training observations from ground-truth PBR materials of public 3D assets using the visible-surface observation encoding pipeline. Foreground validity and first-hit visibility determine the observed surface voxels supplied to the sparse material encoder. We follow Pixal3D (Li et al., 2026a) for asset preprocessing and reference-view sampling. During training, we drop the entire observation condition with probability 0.03 while retaining the geometry condition. Optimization. We use random seed 42 throughout our training and evaluation pipelines. The material encoder and decoder are frozen. Generator self-attention is initialized from TRELLIS.2 and the remaining components randomly. We use the flow-matching objective in Eq. equation 10.
