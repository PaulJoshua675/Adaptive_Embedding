Dividing by γ, the visible dynamics ˙x = −γ−1∇xEPCN reproduce those of (a) to O(γ): the backward pass has been performed by the relaxation. 3.2 TRAINING THE PCN BY EQUILIBRIUM PROPAGATION Section 3.1 shows that the PCN’s relaxation reproduces the generation velocity −∇xVθ for any weights θ, whether or not those weights were themselves trained on the PCN. Training them on the PCN requires two more things: that the correspondence hold for the loss’s parameter gradient and not only for the velocity, and a way to measure that parameter gradient without a backward pass. The PCN’s loss is the potential’s loss. In its first phase Energy Matching trains Vθ by flow match- ing: for an interpolant xt between a noise sample and a data sample, with target velocity u, the loss is L(θ) = 1 2∥−∇xVθ(xt) −u∥2, and ∆θBP = −∇θL is its backpropagated gradient. To allow this loss (dependent on an energy gradient) to be trained using EP, we use GradEP’s spring-clamped free phase: rather than fixing x at xt, we tether x near xt with a spring energy term and let x and h relax together on Efree(x, h) = EPCN(x, h) + λ 2 ∥x −xt∥2.
