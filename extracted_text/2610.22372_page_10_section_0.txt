This question evaluates whether policies can compose prefer- ences, decompose a target preference from joint training, and handle preference-preference or task-preference conflicts. Tables 4 and 5 report the results. For composition/decomposition, Eval SR/SA denote task success and target preference satisfaction on the evaluation condition. For conflicts, Pref-Dom. measures the gap between following either preference under a joint prompt; lower values indicate less consistent preference domination. In task-preference conflicts, Follow Task, Follow Preference, and Incomplete denote completing the task while violating the preference, following the preference while violating the task, and satisfying neither, respectively. Finding. Preference composition is partially learnable, whereas decomposition fails. After learning PA and PB separately, the policy can compose them into PA+PB with 25.0% Eval SR/SA. However, when asked to infer PB from PA+PB and PA, the policy obtains 37.5% Eval SR but 0.0% Eval SA, showing task success does not imply successful preference behavior execution. Finding. Conflicting preferences reduce performance and have no dominant preference. Under preference-preference conflicts, conflict-time SR/SA drop to 66.00%/47.75%, and Pref-Dom. is only 11.25%, suggesting no clear preference dominates the other under our either-preference success rule. Under task-preference conflicts, Follow Task and Follow Preference are close, 33.3% vs. 29.2%, with 37.5% incomplete trials, indicating that conflicts often lead to failed execution rather than task- or preference-following behavior. 5
