The non-normality is balanced amplification. Schur-triangularizing J = UTU∗, almost the whole operator lives in the strictly upper-triangular, feed-forward part: that fraction — which equals Henrici’s departure from normality — is 0.99, against 0.71 for a Ginibre matrix and 0.93 for the random core (equivalently, the eigenvalues carry 2.0% of ∥J∥2 F , against 14% for the random core), while the antisymmetric part sits at the random- matrix level. With ρ(J) < 1, this triad of near-maximal feed-forward non-normality, stable eigenvalues and chance-level rotation is the structure Murphy and Miller (2009) identified as balanced amplification: a hidden feed-forward chain that amplifies activity in transit while every mode decays (App. B). 4. Attention follows a shared schedule Where the model attends is a program shared across images, not an image-specific search. Attention is selective: it concentrates on about 96 of the 196 tokens (exponential of its entropy), with its most-attended token carrying 11× the uniform weight. Ablations (Ta- ble 1): freezing the query at its first-tick value, so the model never advances its schedule, removes 34% of accuracy, and flattening attention removes most of it (−80%); handing each image another image’s query at the matching tick costs only 7% (1000 images). The progression is essential; its image-specific content matters far less. Factorization: substi- tuting the population-average query at each tick reproduces the image’s own attention map at cosine 0.936 (Fig. 4) — a generic schedule pointed at the image’s features. Prediction: the current query plus the shared average increment forecasts the next attention map at cosine 0.996, against 0.948 for assuming attention stays put (400 held-out images): the next glance is predictable before the model takes it (App. C). This is a one-step-ahead forecast: the predictor consumes the current query, so it anticipates the next glance from the current one, not the whole scan from the image alone. What the schedule does to the curve. Most of the fixed point’s motion is common to all images: the mean curve over images carries 77% of its squared motion per tick.
