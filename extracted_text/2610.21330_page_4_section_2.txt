For jp-election-26, we use all available collection days and group queries by policy issue. For the U.S. Google-SERP reference, we use 7 evenly spaced days spanning the 106-day collection window and aggregate results by query subcategory. The same sources remain visible longer for more stable topics. Table 2 shows that long-standing policy issues tend to retain more of the same sources across days in generative-engine citations. Social insurance, Nuclear power, and Consumption tax show URL overlap of 7.8–8.8%, whereas more event-driven issues such as China re- lations and Constitutional amendment show lower overlap of 4.1% and 2.7%, respectively. Domain overlap is consistently higher than URL overlap, indicating that the same publishers can remain visible even when specific webpages change. The U.S. Google-SERP refer- ence shows a similar within-setting pattern. More stable election- reference queries tend to retain more of the same webpages across days. For example, Background queries, such as “What controver- sies is Donald Trump the subject of?”, reach 12.5% URL overlap. In contrast, Comparison queries reach 0.8%, while Recent stories, such as “Are there any recent stories about Kamala Harris that may affect the 2024 US presidential election?”, have no URL shared across all sampled days.
