Two primary sub-directions stand out: EA-assisted optimization of RL [33] , where the EA’s population-based evolutionary mechanism generates a more diverse set of experiences to improve DRL training, and RL-assisted optimization of EAs [34] , where RL dynamically guides the EA's search and refinement process. The RL-assisted EA direction still faces notable challenges. First, generalization remains largely unresolved, since many methods are developed for specific target problems, constrained by EA operators suited only to a single problem type. Second, many existing methods offer only limited control over key hyperparameters, typically restricted to mutation and crossover rates [35], because designing RL-assisted dynamic hyperparameter outputs from the actor model requires a deep understanding of both the EA’s internal mechanisms and the target problem’s properties [34]. Moreover, many ERL or DRL-based methods remain focused on purely mathematical or control benchmarks with limited real-world engineering application, and are further hindered by heavy reliance on supervised-style feedback, large EA populations, and long per-episode training. These limitations can leave them without efficient, generalizable inference once training ends, often making them poorly suited to real-world model updating tasks that already carry the added cost of neural network training and/or physical simulation on top of the optimization itself. 1.2 Research focus and key contributions of this study This study is situated within the domain of RL-assisted optimization of EAs and introduces an innovative DRL-governed dynamic DE–CMA-ES Orchestration (DRL-DCO) algorithm. The DRL-DCO algorithm employs an actor-critic orchestration agent, built upon the Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) method [36], to dynamically coordinate the DE and CMA-ES search mechanisms through a multi- dimensional continuous action space governing key hyperparameters and orchestration decisions, guided by a progression-aware state representation and a diversity-informed reward function. Whereas many existing RL-assisted EA approaches confine the RL component to intermittent intervention on a single evolutionary strategy and/or limited landscape or progression awareness, DRL-DCO’s actor-critic agent maintains continuous, progression-aware oversight of two parallel evolutionary processes throughout the search. This persistent, multi-dimensional governance represents a more thorough realization of the RL-assisted EA paradigm. The DRL-DCO algorithm is evaluated across high-dimensional single-objective optimization benchmarks and a practical FEMU task, spanning both synthetic optimization landscapes and practical structural engineering generalization. The key contributions are summarized as follows. 1) Rather than hard-switching between algorithms, the DRL-DCO algorithm performs continuous orchestration of DE and CMA-ES through a compositional control mechanism governed by a continuously learnable balancing parameter. This parameter allocates search effort between the two operators on the non-elite subset of each generation, preserving the global distributional structure tracked by CMA-ES through its covariance model while retaining the aggressive, direction-agnostic local search of DE. By adaptively regulating this balance, the agent reduces over-aggressive search steps and mitigates premature convergence. 2) The DRL-DCO algorithm is equipped with an enhanced landscape-informed state representation that augments the candidate solution vector with additional progression-aware descriptors, including population diversity, distributional shape statistics, and convergence-rate indicators. This enriched state gives the actor a comprehensive, temporally coherent view of the search dynamics and geometry, enabling more informed and timely adjustment of DE and CMA-ES hyperparameters across generations. 3) To further enhance adaptability, the DRL-DCO algorithm incorporates a learnable population-scaling factor together with an elite-preservation mechanism, jointly adjusting the effective population size and the proportion of elite solutions carried forward across generations based on the current search state. This allows the agent to deploy smaller, computationally efficient populations during exploitation-dominant phases and expand population coverage during exploration-demanding phases, improving both efficiency and stability. 4) The DRL-DCO algorithm incorporates a learned restart policy governed by a learnable restart probability, estimated from stall-count and diversity statistics within the state representation, which determines when the population should be reinitialized to escape deceptive local minima on highly multimodal landscapes.
