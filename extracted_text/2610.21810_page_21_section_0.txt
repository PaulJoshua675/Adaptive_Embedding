F Geometry and Error Analysis F.1 Gaussian coordinates with a fixed Gram matrix This construction establishes compatibility be- tween Gaussian coordinates and learned geome- try, not how training produces them. Initialization and intermediate checkpoints for the target mod- els are unavailable. Rotating a fixed table Z by an orthogonal matrix R keeps its Gram matrix, ZR(ZR)⊤= ZZ⊤. If R is Haar-distributed, the normalized direction of any fixed nonzero row is sphere-uniform by rotational invariance. It can be represented as h/∥h∥2, with h ∼N(0, Id). For fixed k, ∥h∥2/ √ d →1 in probability, so the first k coordinates scaled by √ d converge in distribution to N(0, Ik); see Diaconis and Freedman (1987) for finite-dimensional approximation results. The same rotation applies to every row, preserv- ing all learned pairwise geometry and rank. Rows can remain correlated, including a rank-one table, and a unit vector’s full coordinates obey a fixed- norm constraint. The construction is a compatibility argument for source statistics. Actual trained coor- dinates require the empirical checks in Section 4.3; it does not model the optimizer or establish that the complete network is invariant to a coordinate change with the consumer held fixed.
