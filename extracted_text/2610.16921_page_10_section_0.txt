F. Computational efficiency analysis Online MI decoding requires repeated inference on sliding windows, so the computational cost for each window is crit- ical for practical deployment. We compare the computational efficiency of different backbones. A single EEG window of size 22 × 250 (1.0 s at 250 Hz) from BNCI2014001 is forwarded with batch size 1. Table V reports inference time, forward FLOPs, and parameter count. Baseline measures the backbone classifier only, consisting of a feature extractor and a discriminator (FE+Disc). TSE-RL measures the full window optimization pipeline, which adds a history encoder and a policy network on top of FE+Disc. The increase in FLOPs and parameter count from Baseline to TSE-RL primarily results from the additional history encoder and policy network. They process multiple historical windows with intermediate high- dimensional features. Despite this additional computation, the per-window inference time remains low across all backbones. Note that mAtt exhibits longer inference time due to its reliance on eigendecomposition operations, which are less suited to parallel computations on GPUs. Even the slowest configuration remains well below the 0.1 s stride interval and significantly below the 1.0 s window duration. This indicates that TSE-RL introduces only a modest overhead and is compatible with online decoding requirements.
