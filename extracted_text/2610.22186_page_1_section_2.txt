Video captioning [3, 13, 39, 48, 61] aims to extract rich semantic content from videos and generate coherent natural language descriptions. As a fundamental task in video understanding, it underpins various downstream applications, including video retrieval [34, 38, 47, 57], video reasoning [10, 11, 28, 37, 56], and video generation [2, 35, 36, 40, 54]. Recently, advances in Transformer-based end-to-end models have revolutionized the conventional modularized video caption pipelines. Especially, Omni Language Models (OLMs) [7, 20, 29, 46, 52, 53] have driven a paradigm shift from visual-only modeling to an omni-modal paradigm that integrates audio and visual signals, enabling more effective cross-modal reasoning and stronger generalization in real-world audiovisual scenarios. Despite its promise, achieving factually accurate and comprehensive audiovisual captioning remains difficult due to the intrinsic complexity and dense interdependence between visual and auditory content. Existing methods treat video captioning as a text generation problem for OLMs based on caption-level supervision [4, 43]. This overlooks the structured semantics inherent in videos, meaning a generated caption may be linguistically correct but misaligned with the logical structure and event-level correspondences.
