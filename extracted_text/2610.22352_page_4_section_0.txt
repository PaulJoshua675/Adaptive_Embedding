The frozen decoder Dmat converts Z0 into surface PBR materials, lit from the left and right. 3.2 Image-to-Material Translation To reduce the learning burden on the 3D generator, we separate reference-view material estimation from 3D surface completion. Specifically, we train an MLLM-based image editing model F2D to infer reference-view albedo and metallic-roughness (MR) maps directly from the input photograph: bT2D = F2D(I) = (ba, bm, br) , (1) where ba denotes the albedo map, and bm and br denote the metallic and roughness maps. To reuse the 3-channel pretrained image VAE, we pack bm and br with a constant channel into a 3-channel map, which also fulfills the input format expected by the pretrained material encoder. Joint alignment and one-step inference. To coordinate channel predictions while retaining pretrained image priors, F2D adapts a multimodal DiT backbone (Super Intelligence Team, 2026) via factorized modality-axis joint attention, jointly predicting albedo, metallic–roughness, and auxiliary normal maps. This attention exchanges features across output modalities at corresponding spatial locations. We train the 2D material estimator on RGB images paired with ground-truth material and normal maps rendered from Objaverse, using a latent flow-matching objective at the fixed maximum-noise endpoint σ = 1.0. At inference, a single DiT forward pass at this endpoint (1.2 s on one H800) estimates clean material latents, which the VAE decodes into 1024 × 1024 material maps without iterative denoising. This design has two advantages. First, it uses 2D image priors to disentangle illumination from intrinsic materials at full reference resolution. Second, it shields the downstream 3D generator from abstract visual features, letting the 3D model use physical PBR observations to focus on reference preservation and unobserved-surface completion. 3.3 Native Observation Encoding To supply physical observations to the 3D generator in a representation it natively understands, we bridge the estimated reference-view PBR maps bT2D directly to the surface of M using the pretrained material encoder Emat. Filtered surface projection. Through the camera projection π, we associate each foreground pixel u of bT2D with its first visible surface hit p ∈M. At inference, foreground-mask erosion and local depth-discontinuity filtering further remove unreliable boundary correspondences. The retained correspondences form the observation set Hvalid, shared by all PBR channels.
