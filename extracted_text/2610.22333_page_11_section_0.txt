Our harmful prompts are Toxic Matrix items that we machine-translated from English with Claude Opus, so we verify translation quality automatically for every harmful string, complementing the native-speaker review of a small sample. We score with COMET-Kiwi (Rei et al., 2022) (Unbabel/wmt22-cometkiwi- da), a reference-free quality-estimation model that rates a source–translation pair without a human refer- ence; English is the source side of every pair, and the model’s InfoXLM backbone covers all five target languages. Table 5 reports the results. Quality is uniformly high and even across languages, with a mean of 0.866 and every language between 0.858 and 0.881. Any translation scoring below 0.70 was retranslated once and rescored, keeping the earlier score on record; 7 strings went through this step, and 4 still fall just below 0.70. One prompt has no score in Bengali, Tamil, and Kannada. The benign prompts come already translated with IndicTrans2 in Dolly-T, and the cue strings are short author-written or drafted texts whose status is given in Appendix A; neither was rescored for this study. COMET-Kiwi measures adequacy and fluency, not whether a harmful request remains harmful after translation; that property is covered by the native-speaker verification described in Section 3. 11
