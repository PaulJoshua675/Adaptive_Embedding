The root cause lies in its dense activation patterns: with over a thousand non-zero terms per document, both the index footprint and the computational cost of scoring grow proportionally. Our method addresses this limitation through a two-stage training frame- work that combines adaptive sparse supervision with FLOPS regularization. Specifically, dynamic α-truncation is applied only during the alignment stage to guide the learning of compact sparse representations, without requiring addi- tional truncation at inference time. The resulting model achieves an average doc- ument sparsity of L0 = 182, reducing index storage by 6.4× (83GB →13GB) and E2E query latency by over 11× (1046ms →∼90ms) compared with Lion-sp. The substantially fewer activated terms contribute to more efficient inverted-index retrieval, while the compact index further reduces storage requirements. These results demonstrate that our training strategy achieves a favorable effectiveness– efficiency trade-off without introducing additional inference-time sparsification. Despite adopting a larger backbone (LLaMA-1B) than SPLADE++ (BERT- 110M), our approach maintains comparable efficiency, with both storage (13GB) and latency (90ms) remaining in the same order of magnitude as SPLADE++. As shown in Figure 3, our model lies on a favorable Pareto frontier, achieving higher MRR@10 than SPLADE++ while substantially reducing latency relative to Lion-sp. This positions our framework as a practically viable solution for de- ploying LLM-enhanced sparse retrieval in latency-sensitive, resource-constrained environments, where both storage budget and response time are critical con- straints. 5
