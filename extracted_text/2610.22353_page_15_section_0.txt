B.2 Router Variance under Different Levels of Gumbel Noise When evaluating RouterVar𝑙, we replace deterministic top-𝐾(for non-shared MoEs) or top-(𝐾−𝑆) (for shared-expert MoEs) routing with stochastic sampling from the learned routing distribution. Such sampling is equivalent to perturbing the routing logits with unit-scale Gumbel noise (𝛽= 1) and then applying top-𝐾selection (Kool et al., 2019; Nie et al., 2021). We further extend this evaluation by defining RouterVar𝛽 𝑙, where the routing logits are perturbed by Gumbel noise parameterized by other 𝛽s. We consider 𝛽∈{0.5, 2} and report the resulting RouterVar𝛽 𝑙for MoE-498M-A121M model in Fig.6. The same trend persists across these perturbation levels, further showing that shared experts consistently reduce routing-induced variance. B.3 Increasing Exploration during Pretraining Can Be Tricky An alternative way to address the exploration dilemma is to encourage greater exploration during pretraining. However, this can be challenging for MoEs because stochastic routing may introduce unstable router dynamics and increased training variance. To examine this tradeoff, we consider two alternative routing strategies under 𝐾= 8. First, we replace deterministic top-(𝐾−𝑆) routing with stochastic sampling of 𝐾−𝑆experts, denoted as Sto-(𝐾−𝑆) in Tab.5. Second, we deterministically select the top-2 experts and stochastically sample the remaining 𝐾−𝑆−2 experts, denoted as Top-2 + Sto-(𝐾−𝑆−2). As shown in Tab.5, on a MoE-100M-A38M model, deterministic top-(𝐾−𝑆) routing performs best, followed by Top-2+Sto-(𝐾−𝑆−2), while fully stochastic routing performs worst. These results suggest that the additional instability introduced by exploration can outweigh its potential benefits during pretraining. For this reason, MirrorMoE takes the opposite approach by shrinking the exploration space. 15
