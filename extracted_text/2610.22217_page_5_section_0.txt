The resulting skill Sc provides an explicit symbolic representation of the reusable expertise acquired by the capability expert πθc, which will serve as a privileged conditional anchor to regularize the expert during subsequent distillation. Skill-Augmented Distillation. To translate decoupled skills into regularized supervision, SCOPED utilizes Sc as a privileged directional anchor exclusively for each capability expert. This asymmetric conditioning elicits precise reasoning traces and highly stable target distribu- tions (Huang et al., 2026). By distilling from these skill-augmented experts, the unified student receives clean supervision, effectively avoiding the exploratory noise and reward-hacking biases inherent in raw expert policies. For a given sample (x, a) ∈Bc, the unified student policy πθ generates an on-policy response y ∼πθ(· | x). At decoding step t, the predictive distributions of the student and the corresponding capability expert πθc are respectively given by: pθ,t = πθ(· | x, y<t), qθc,t = πθc(· | x, Sc, y<t), (8) where y<t denotes the student-generated prefix. Under this asymmetric conditioning, Sc acts as a directional anchor that regularizes the target distribution qθc,t, guiding the expert along verified reasoning paths while effectively suppressing erroneous or over-specialized tokens. To consolidate the diverse expertise into a single model, we optimize the unified student policy over all C capabilities. The overall skill-augmented distillation loss L is defined as the expected token-level reverse KL divergence aggregated across all capability-specific subsets: L = 1 C C X c=1 E(x,a)∼Bc, y∼πθ(·|x)  1 |y| |y| X t=1 DKL(pθ,t ∥qθc,t)  , (9) where DKL(· ∥·) denotes the reverse Kullback-Leibler divergence, and |y| is the length of the gen- erated response. Minimizing this objective directly embeds the regularized expert knowledge into the student’s weights, yielding a unified model that is entirely self-sufficient and requires no explicit skill conditioning during inference. 4 EXPERIMENTAL METHODOLOGY This section first describes the datasets, evaluation metrics, and baselines. Then, we introduce the implementation details of our experiments. Datasets. The evaluation covers eight benchmarks across four capability groups. Count- BenchQA (Paiss et al., 2023) and PixMo-Count (Deitke et al., 2025) are used for fine-grained perception; CC-OCR V2 (Xu et al., 2026c) and ChartQA (Masry et al., 2022) for OCR and chart un- derstanding; VisualPuzzles (Song et al., 2025) and We-Math (Qiao et al., 2025) for symbolic visual reasoning; and MUIRBench (Wang et al., 2025a) and MMSI-Bench (Yang et al., 2025) for multi- image reasoning. For capability-specific expert training, reinforcement learning data are constructed from six public multimodal datasets, including task-relevant subsets of Orsta-Data-47K (Ma et al., 2025). Detailed dataset composition and statistics are provided in Appendix A.1. Evaluation Metrics. We employ task-specific metrics following the official evaluation protocols of each benchmark. Fine-grained perception, chart understanding, symbolic visual reasoning, and multi-image reasoning tasks are evaluated using accuracy. OCR recognition is evaluated using micro-F1 following CC-OCR V2 (Xu et al., 2026c). All scores are reported as percentages, and the overall score is the arithmetic mean across the eight benchmarks. 5
