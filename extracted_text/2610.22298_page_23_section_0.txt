The mean in panels A and B averages available numeric scores for criteria 1–8; criterion 10 was analyzed separately. 5 Discussion 5.1 Principal Findings This study produced four principal findings. First, model identity separated final-report quality more strongly than response format: GPT-5.6-Sol led every evaluated component, the model ordering was identical across final-report formats, and the best-to-worst spread substantially exceeded all within-model format differences. Second, the complete structured synthesis had higher pooled Judge means than the pre-consilium primary-role opinion for every shared criterion, with the largest gains in problem prioritization, factual grounding, and reasoning coherence. Third, structured final reports achieved higher overall means in seven of eight model pipelines; this broad model-level advantage was driven primarily by a 0.304-point improvement in management, which was also observed in seven pipelines. Fourth, the review protocol reached eventual consensus in 97.8% of reports, whereas longer review trajectories had no final-report score advantage. GPT-5.6-Sol combined the highest report quality with the fewest review rounds, linking strong initial performance with rapid convergence in this protocol. The stage comparison showed a favorable aggregate pattern for the full synthesis pipeline. Its final reports had higher pooled Judge means for every criterion comparable with the structured primary-role output. The largest differences occurred in problem prioritization, grounding, and coherence, consistent with the consilium integrating case evidence and role-specific perspectives into a clearer and more clinically organized account.
