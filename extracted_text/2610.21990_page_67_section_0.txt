To motivate the design of the proposed Fusion Encoder Block, it is compared against a baseline strategy that relies on naive concatenation. In this baseline, multi-modal features are simply concatenated along the channel dimension prior to passing through a standard Transformer block. As shown in Table 4.7, the proposed cross-attention fusion approach yields a substantial performance improvement, outperforming the concatenation baseline by ≈4% mIoU. This performance gap highlights the limitations of early concatenation. While concatenation aggre- gates multi-modal data into a unified tensor, it forces the subsequent layers to implicitly learn the cross-modal alignments from scratch without any structured guidance. In contrast, the proposed fusion mechanism explicitly models the spatial and semantic correlations between the primary and auxiliary modalities. By utilizing cross-attention, the network dynamically queries the auxil- iary streams to enhance the primary features, ensuring that the model selectively attends to relevant multi-modal cues rather than treating all concatenated channels with equal initial weight. Replacing Decoders with Convolutions. As detailed in Section 3.4.4, the core feature refinement component within the VesperMUSE decoder is a lightweight, attention-based Transformer block. This module is specifically designed to refine the multi-scale fused features, utilizing self-attention to dynamically weigh and prioritize spatial information that is most relevant to the current semantic context. To validate this design choice, an ablation study was conducted where these Transformer blocks were replaced with standard pointwise convolutions, effectively removing the global context- aggregation capabilities of the attention mechanism while maintaining a similar channel dimension- ality. 67
