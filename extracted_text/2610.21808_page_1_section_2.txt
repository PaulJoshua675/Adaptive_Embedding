Weight memory and bandwidth constrain language-model deployment on phones and in browsers. At batch size one, decoding is typically bandwidth-bound, making bits per weight a key determinant of latency (Wang et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2025), motivating ultra-low-bit quantization. Below four bits, however, acceptable perplexity can conceal severe losses in factual knowledge and task accu- racy. We therefore evaluate quantization with three complementary measures: WikiText2 perplexity, a seven-task zero-shot average and five-shot MMLU. Ternary weights replace weight multiplications with additions and subtractions. Pretraining (Wang et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2024), quantization-aware training (Liu et al., 2025c), code fine-tuning (Ma- linovskii et al., 2024) and learned ternarization over many epochs (Wang et al., 2026c;b) preserve much of the model’s knowledge at substantial computational cost. Post-training conversion (Zhang et al., 2026; Yan et al., 2026a; Guo et al., 2026) is much cheaper but loses factual knowledge: on Qwen3-8B-Base, these methods score 26–35 on MMLU, versus 77 at full precision. The challenge is to approach training-based retention at post-training conversion cost. Our central insight is that quantization should allocate bits by function. We introduce RoleQ, a format that assigns scales to functional units: vectors that read from the residual stream receive one scale per output unit; vectors that write to it receive one per input unit. This structural rule applies to integer and ternary grids and requires neither calibration data nor stored axis choices. RoleQ-T combines this format with a data-free learned rotation, ternary GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2023) with an exact per-vector step, and brief scale distillation with frozen codes (Figure 2). Qwen3- 8B-Base (Yang et al., 2025) reaches 1.59 bits per weight (bpw), 11.33 perplexity, 60.3 zero-shot accuracy and 47.6 MMLU in a few GPU-hours, a fraction of the tens of A100-hours for CAT-Q’s learned ternarization at this size (Section 4.1). 1
