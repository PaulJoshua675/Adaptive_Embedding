For instance, content-boosted models [30] utilize content features to gen- erate pseudo-ratings for sparse user-item pairs before applying traditional CF. DeepMusic [17] pioneered the use of deep neural networks to extract textual and acoustic features from side attributes and map them into the CF latent space, demonstrating that integrating content features effectively mitigates the cold-start dilemma. Furthermore, weakly supervised methods [31] leverage user content preferences and item attributes to score new items, bridging the gap left by missing interactions. 2.3 Representation Alignment in Recommendation While content features provide a viable path to alleviate cold-start issues, an inherent hetero- geneous gap exists between the topology-based ID space and the attribute-based semantic space. To bridge this discrepancy, frontier research often relies on complex external graph structures for cross-view alignment. For example, DCIB [32] employs a dual contrastive information bottleneck to facilitate topological knowledge transfer from knowledge graphs to collaborative views. Similarly, DFHE [33] injects structural information into the semantic view by fusing meta-graphs and meta-paths. However, these methods depend heavily on complete prior knowl- edge bases, such as high-quality knowledge graphs or predefined heterogeneous information networks. In many practical and stringent industrial scenarios, such external knowledge is often unavailable [34–36], leaving the model to rely solely on highly sparse bipartite graphs and mini- mal item features.
