Outcome uncertainty and its influence on remaining candidates jointly determine predictive improvement. If neither outcome changes remaining predictions, the feedback provides no predictive improvement. Otherwise, even a query without a hit changes remaining predictions and produces a nonzero weight. This explains how TDAL retains the current discovery reward while allowing experiments without hits to contribute to learning through successor value. The decomposition into two outcomes is the special case of one query revealing only a binary label. TDAL observes a batch of continuous phenotypes instead. The general identity for predictive improvement conditions on the complete batch observations 𝑂𝑡and also applies to that feedback under the same assumptions about the joint model.
