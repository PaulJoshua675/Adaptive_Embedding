To rigorously assess the global search capability and robustness of DRL-DCO under highly uncertain prior knowledge, wide uniform priors were assigned to all 12 inter-story stiffness parameters (k(f) 1 , k(f) 2 , and k(f) 3 for f = 1, . . . , 4), covering the range [10, 500] MN/m, which also served as hard bounds during optimization. Spanning nearly two orders of magnitude around the true values, this range intentionally simulates a practically challenging scenario with almost no reliable prior structural information, as may arise from degraded or undocumented structural records or significant environmental and operational variability in real engineering settings. Reliably extracting all 12 modal DOFs of this benchmark structure via system identification methods such as Stochastic Subspace Identification (SSI) is known to be practical, but also can be challenging for the scenarios with large modeling complexity and measurement uncertainty [49, 50]. Following the findings in [50], only modes 1, 2, 3, 7, 9, 10, 11, and 12 can be reliably extracted via SSI, and only these eight modes were therefore included in the FEMU objective function, reflecting the common practical constraint of incomplete modal information. The FEMU objective function follows the weighted modal residual formulation defined in Section 2.1. Convergence is declared when the objective function value falls below a threshold of ϵ = 1 × 10−6, which has been verified to yield sufficiently precise stiffness identification for practical engineering purposes [49]. Further reduction beyond this threshold is neither practically meaningful nor physically justified given the inherent presence of measurement noise and environmental variability in real applications. The convergence performance of DRL-DCO in both its agent-training and agent-inference phases is systematically summarized in Table 6, with representative convergence histories visualized in Figure 7 and the corresponding identified stiffness parameters presented in Figure 8. Three key observations are drawn from these results: 1) The DRL-DCO algorithm demonstrates remarkably efficient and robust convergence on this FEMU problem. In the agent-training phase, full convergence is achieved on average within fewer than 300 generations with low standard deviation across independent runs. More notably, convergence is consistently reached within the first episode of 1,000 generations, entirely within the warm-up stage, and is sustained across all five warm-up episodes without exception. This indicates that, although the identification problem spans a 12-dimensional parameter space with a wide and deliberately uninformative prior range, its optimization landscape is sufficiently well-structured for the DRL-DCO’s adaptive orchestration to reliably locate the global optimum even before the policy has been meaningfully trained, reflecting the algorithm’s intrinsic search effectiveness independent of its RL progress. 2) In the agent-inference phase, based solely on the policy learned in a single agent-training run following the five-episode warm-up stage, DRL-DCO autonomously and consistently locates the global optimum with high precision across all independently initialized runs. More importantly, the generation-wise trajectories of the effective population size (controlled by λ(t)), elite ratio r(t) eli , and orchestration ratio β(t) exhibit notably more stable evolution during inference than during training, indicating that the learned policy has acquired a consistent search strategy rather than relying on continued online adaptation. The trained actor therefore provides reusable control decisions for different initial populations, allowing the optimization to proceed without further critic evaluation, reward feedback, or policy updates. These results demonstrate that the learned policy can retain effective search behavior after training and transfer it across independently initialized instances of the structural identification task. 3) The identified stiffness parameter values across all 12 DOFs are consistently accurate, with mean percentage errors below 0.1% and small standard deviations across independent runs for all parameters. This level of precision is achieved despite the deliberately challenging setup of incomplete modal information involving only 8 available modes, confirming that DRL-DCO can perform high-accuracy FEMU under practically relevant constraints of partial observability and wide prior uncertainty. 20
