Fast action potentials produce an inward sodium current flanked symmetrically by outward potassium currents, creating an opposing quadrupolar current configuration. The electric fields of these quadrupoles decay rapidly with distance as 1/r3, resulting in extensive spatial cancellation and negligible contributions to macroscopic recordings. In contrast, synchronized postsynaptic currents flowing into the aligned apical dendrites of layer V pyramidal neurons (large excitatory projection neurons whose elongated dendrites span multiple cortical layers perpendicular to the brain surface) create an open sink-source current configuration that acts as a current dipole. The electric fields of current dipoles decay more slowly as 1/r2 and sum coherently across large cortical assemblies, dominating both the local field potential (LFP) recorded within tissue and scalp EEG signals [23, 6, 21].
