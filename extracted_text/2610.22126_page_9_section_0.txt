DeepSHAP, by contrast, enriches only for the interferon response, with the remaining pathways enriched by integrated gradients not reaching significance. 5 Limitations and future work Our method requires prior knowledge of the directed causal structure among features, which for biological data can often be sourced from curated knowledge bases such as OmniPath [Türei et al., 2021]. Since such a graph may be imperfect in practice, we test how much this assumption can be violated before performance suffers by removing, adding, and reversing edges: CIG degrades under genuine information loss (removal or reversal) but tolerates spurious additions almost entirely, so the graph need not be exactly correct to retain most of the benefit (Appendix K). A separate concern is whether CIG’s advantage merely reflects graph access, not correct use of it. We rule this out: CIG’s attributions are instance-specific, tracking each cell’s actual transition rather than the static graph alone (Appendix J). Like causal Shapley values [Heskes et al., 2020] and conditional SHAP [Aas et al., 2021], CIG is true to the data rather than to the model: it recovers causal drivers under the supplied structure and is not meant for debugging a model’s idiosyncratic behavior. Extending the method to undirected or partially directed graphs is a natural direction for future work. Our surrogate models each feature’s mechanism as additive across parents (Section 3.3) and so cannot capture non-additive parent interactions. This is a limitation of our chosen surrogate, not of the method, since any differentiable surrogate can be substituted. The one case a different surrogate cannot fix is an unmodeled confounder, whose effect is folded into the additive residual and attributed to xi’s direct effect. In our experiments this biases attribution magnitudes for affected nodes (Appendix L). Our methods scale quadratically in the number of features in the worst case, slower than integrated gra- dients but substantially faster than Shapley-based alternatives; our synthetic and real-data experiments (100–1000 features; 2,320–6,941 genes) already exceed the scale at which existing dependency-aware baselines have been validated (Heskes et al. [2020]: M=7 features; Aas et al. [2021]: 3–10 features, largest real-data example at 28). Finally, the current formulation concentrates attribution on root nodes, potentially underweighting relevant intermediate nodes; future work could explore distributing indirect effects more evenly across the causal path. 9
