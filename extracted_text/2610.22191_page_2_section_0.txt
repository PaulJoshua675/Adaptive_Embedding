The answers are obtained analytically by a signal- to-noise analysis, which in the present setting can be carried out in closed form, and are checked by Monte Carlo simulations. In a nutshell: in [13] the probabil- ity of reconstructing a pixel of the archetype by a ma- jority vote over its M examples is 1 2[1 + erf(1/√2ρ)], and the one-step magnetization of the network has the same error-function form; here we find that the one-step magnetization of an unseen archetype produced by an attention layer is again m(1) = erf(1/√2ρeff), but the dataset entropy ρ is replaced by an attention entropy ρeff(β) that depends on how the softmax distributes its weight over the training set. At intermediate β the load drops out (ρeff →ρ for any α); at small β the crosstalk of the pairwise model is recovered; at large β the softmax condenses, i.e. it assigns essentially all of its weight to a single training example, and the layer memorizes. Seen from the query, the logits of the soft- max are independent Gaussian variables, so that the attention layer is a random energy model (REM), the system of M independent Gaussian energy levels intro- duced by Derrida [26, 27] as the simplest spin glass: the condensation is the freezing transition of that model, located at β p N(1 −r2) ≃ √ 2 ln M, and it is the atten- tion counterpart of the spin-glass phase. The window of generalization between the two edges widens with the system size as √ N. We then test the picture on MNIST and fashion-MNIST, and compare the Hebbian attention with a one-head attention classifier trained by gradient descent on the same datasets. The present model is the p →∞end of the fam- ily of dense Hebbian networks learning from examples of [14, 15]: there the crosstalk is still extensive in the polynomial load αp, here it becomes logarithmic in K, which is what allows the closed-form treatment. Re- lated settings of learning from examples have been con- sidered in [19] for dense p-body networks in the teacher– student scenario, in [20] for the random-features Hop- field model, and in [25] for hierarchical prototypes in polynomial dense memories. For the storage, rather than learning, properties of exponential models we re- fer to [6, 8, 9], and to [10] for the multilayer hetero- associative extension, whose exponential capacity and basins are obtained at β →∞by a large-deviation eval- uation of the noise. 2 Dataset and models We use the vocabulary of attention throughout. The vectors stored in a network are its keys; the state in which the network is prepared is the query; the soft- max of the overlaps between query and keys gives the attention weights, and the state produced by one update is their weighted sum, passed through a sign. A net- work generalizes when, prepared in a query of a given archetype, it produces a state close to that archetype, which it has never seen; it memorizes when it produces a state close to one of its keys, i.e. to a training exam- ple.
