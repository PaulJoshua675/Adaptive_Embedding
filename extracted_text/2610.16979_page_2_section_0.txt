Larger rollout bud- gets incur substantial computation, while beam search introduces a mismatch between selected trajectories and the policy’s sampling distribution. On the other hand, auxiliary rankers—trained strictly on candidates from upstream retrieval stages—provide unreliable scores for medium- and long-tail items explored by the genera- tive policy. Furthermore, practical ranking scores often rely on hand-crafted heuristics tailored to specific scenarios, limiting cross- scenario generalizability and requiring costly adaptation. Thus, denser rewards do not necessarily yield better preference align- ment and may instead inject misleading supervision. To address these challenges, we present WeGR, a unified gen- erative retrieval framework for industrial search systems. WeGR connects behavior-aware SID construction with reward-model-free preference optimization through two components. First, the Col- laborative and Collapse-free Tokenizer (CoCo-VAE) incorpo- rates query-to-item and item-to-item collaborative supervision into continuous representation learning. It further combines first-layer codebook refresh with residual-fidelity and collision regularization, mitigating persistent codeword underutilization while preserving semantic structure across quantization layers. Second, Dynamic Horizon Policy Optimization (DHPO) leverages the hierarchical structure of SIDs for coarse-to-fine GRPO optimization. By pro- gressively expanding the horizons for both reward evaluation and policy optimization, DHPO allows sampled trajectories to receive behavioral rewards through prefix matching during early training, before gradually transitioning to exact item-level preference opti- mization. This enables more effective use of click- and order-based outcome rewards without auxiliary reward models. We further introduce Multi-Positive Supervised Fine-Tuning (MSFT) to learn from multiple positive items and provide stable learning signals when group-relative rewards become uninformative. We evaluate WeGR in large-scale production search. In controlled online A/B tests, the V1.2 deployment removes 80% of candidates from conventional retrieval channels while achieving relative im- provements of 2.539%, 1.508%, and 1.381% in orders, CTR, and CVR, respectively. This reduces the number of candidates entering pre- ranking by 38.10%, yielding CPU and GPU savings of 25.5% and 26.1%. WeGR also reduces average serving latency by 21.7 ms in WeChat Channels Search and 15.4 ms in WeChat Search. Further analyses examine performance across different user groups and query-frequency levels. To validate its cross-scenario applicability, we independently evaluate DHPO on WeChat e-commerce live- stream search, where it consistently outperforms SFT across all Click and Order metrics, achieving relative gains of 5.95%–7.07% in HR@6 and 6.12%–7.82% in MRR@6. These results demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of WeGR in industrial deployment, as well as the applicability of DHPO across different search scenarios. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows: • We introduce CoCo-VAE, a behavior-aware residual quanti- zation framework that integrates collaborative supervision with codebook refresh, residual-fidelity preservation, and collision regularization to mitigate codebook underutiliza- tion without sacrificing SID semantics. • We propose DHPO, a reward-model-free GRPO variant that progressively expands the horizons of reward evaluation and policy optimization along the SID hierarchy, alleviating outcome reward sparsity while maintaining generalizability across diverse search scenarios. • We demonstrate the effectiveness of WeGR via production ex- periments and fine-grained analyses, showing improvements in business metrics with reduction in candidate volume, serv- ing resources, and latency. We further validate DHPO in an independent live-stream search scenario. 2 Related Works 2.1 Generative Retrieval Generative retrieval (GR) formulates retrieval as autoregressive gen- eration, directly predicting target identifiers conditioned on queries or user histories [1, 14, 29, 33]. For item retrieval, each item is repre- sented as a Semantic ID (SID), a sequence of discrete tokens encod- ing its semantic information. Such SIDs are constructed through two paradigms: RQ-VAE-based learnable quantization [17, 25] and RQ-Kmeans-based residual clustering [2, 9]. RQ-Kmeans-based methods. [20] construct SIDs by successively clustering item embeddings and residuals, with explicit assignment control. OneRec [9] uses balanced clustering, while OneSearch [2] combines selective cluster balancing with optimized product quanti- zation of the remaining residual [13]. These designs separate quanti- zation from behavior-aware representation learning. In OneSearch, collaborative signals enter upstream contrastive embeddings, but subsequent clustering does not directly optimize behavioral objec- tives. Preserving these signals in SIDs thus relies on quantization fidelity, not direct behavioral supervision of code assignment. RQ-VAE-based methods. learn representations and codebooks via reconstruction and quantization, allowing task supervision. LC-Rec [38] combines learned indices with collaborative-semantic alignment tasks, while LETTER [32] directly regularizes the tok- enizer via collaborative contrastive alignment and code-assignment diversity. ETEGRec [19] couples tokenization and recommendation via sequence–item and preference–semantic alignment. These ap- proaches differ in whether alignment targets the recommender, the tokenizer, or their joint training, rather than merely in the informa- tion used. Yet task alignment cannot ensure effective codebook uti- lization: hard assignments can undertrain rare codewords, causing codebook collapse [30] [21]. DIGER [11] enables recommendation- guided differentiable assignment with Gumbel-noise exploration to reduce code concentration; R3-VAE [31] stabilizes quantization via reference-vector guidance and scoring-based assignment. CoCo-VAE jointly addresses behavioral alignment and codebook underutilization within the RQ-VAE framework.
