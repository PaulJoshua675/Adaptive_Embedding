23 V. NONABELIAN LATTICE-GAUGE SIMULATION In Hamiltonian lattice gauge theory, space is replaced by a grid. A gauge-field configuration assigns a group element Ui(x) to each directed edge, or link, from site x in direction i. This element describes how the gauge field transports a state between neighboring sites; traversing the link backward contributes its inverse Ui(x)−1. Multiplying the links around a closed path therefore describes the net transport along that loop. Although this product depends on an arbitrary convention at each site (the gauge), the real part of its matrix trace does not, and these loop traces form the magnetic energy [73]. Because the gauge group is nonabelian, its elements need not commute, so the links must be multiplied in the order in which the path traverses them. We consider the group-register formulation of Hamiltonian lattice gauge theory [73], in which a register holds the group element of each link. The Hamiltonian separates into an electric (kinetic) term HE and a magnetic (potential) term HB. A Trotter step evolves these terms separately. Here we implement the magnetic evolution e−i∆tHB for the improved Hamiltonian of Carena et al. [74]. The smallest loop contributing to HB goes around one square face of the lattice and is called a plaquette; the standard magnetic term sums a trace over these four-link loops. The improved Hamiltonian adds weighted 2 × 1 rectangles, which cancel the leading discretization error. Thus the magnetic evolution requires the same basic operation at many locations: follow a short closed path, multiply its links in order, extract a trace, and add the weighted result to the phase. The compilation task studied here is one application of the magnetic evolution to the whole lattice, which we call the magnetic layer. We use a periodic 4 × 4 × 4 lattice [75]. The lattice has 192 links, and the layer contains 192 four-link plaquettes and 384 six-link rectangles. To store each link in a finite register, we restrict its value from the continuous group SU(3) to the 216-element subgroup G = Σ(72 × 3) ⊂SU(3) used by Osorio Perez et al. [37]. We encode these elements using the unique normal form g = ωpCqErV 2s+tXu, p, q, r ∈{0, 1, 2}, s, t, u ∈{0, 1}, (77) where ω = e2πi/3 and C, E, V, X are fixed matrices that generate the group. These six exponents are the coordinates of a group element. Each three-valued exponent uses two qubits and each binary exponent one, for nine qubits per link and 1,728 in total. We write g for the value of one link and g0, . . . , g191 for all of them. Setting u = 0 in Equation (77) gives the 108-element subgroup Σ(36 × 3), while u = 1 gives its other coset.
