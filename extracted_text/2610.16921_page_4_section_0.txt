Temporal structure embedding via FSM-constrained action masking and Transformer-based history encoding As mentioned in Introduction, applying RL to MI-EEG time window optimization faces two significant challenges. First, the agent must respect safety constraints so that selected window sequences remain physiologically meaningful. Sec- ond, the agent has to learn from long sequences of noisy EEG observations in a stable and data-efficient way. The proposed TSE-RL framework addresses these issues through a temporal structure embedding mechanism that combines an FSM-constrained action mask with a Transformer-based history encoder. This design enforces the decision of window optimization alignment with MI neural patterns, while provid- ing a stable architecture for learning. MI events exhibit contralateral desynchronization of the µ (8–13 Hz) and β (13–30 Hz) rhythms during MI execution, and exhibit a rebound of synchronization of the β-rhythm after the event ends [22]. Conventional isolated window optimization methods ignore this temporal pattern, leading to discontin- uous or semantically incomplete window sequences. This consequently reduces classification accuracy and diminishes interpretability. Reward shaping can, in principle, discourage such sequences by penalizing non-contiguous or incomplete window selections. However, this soft constraint can only indirectly influence the policy through policy gradients. During the early stages of training, the policy that is not aligned with physiological patterns can still be explored. Even during inference, a converged policy employing stochastic sampling cannot entirely prevent the occurrence of invalid action se- quences. To ensure that the selected time window consistently consti- tutes a valid MI event, we embed the temporal structure of MI events into the decision process using FSM-constrained action masking. We employ FSM to model the legal phase transitions of events, restricting valid actions Aϕn based on the current phase ϕn. The probability of all invalid actions is set to zero before the action sampling. This hard constraint reduces the policy search space, forcing the agent to follow physiologically plausible trajectories. Huang et al. [27] showed that invalid- action masking yields more effective gradient updates, with benefits increasing as the number of invalid actions grows. Masking scales well when invalid actions are numerous, while penalty-based methods struggle to explore the task. Further- more, Stolz et al. [28] found that focusing exploration on state- specific valid actions significantly improves learning efficiency and the predictability of the agent in safety-critical tasks. Therefore, in EEG decoding, we propose FSM-constrained action masking combined with the reward function described in Section II-A to guide the agent to generate continuous and complete MI windows.
