The three loss terms respectively promote latent prediction, reproducibility across finite FC estimates, and a non-degenerate representation distribution. 4 Results 4.1 Experimental setup CoFlu-Brain was pretrained on 13,444 subjects from eight dataset groups: CHCP, HCP, ABCD, ABCC, HBN, PNC, MDD, and AOMIC PIOP1/2 (Ge et al., 2023; Van Essen et al., 2013; Casey et al., 2018; Feczko et al., 2021; Alexander et al., 2017; Satterthwaite et al., 2014; Yan et al., 2019; Snoek et al., 2021). All inputs were represented as Schaefer-400 cortical time series (Schaefer et al., 2018), and 200-frame segments were used for pretraining. Downstream evaluation comprised two in-cohort tasks from HCP and ABCD, whose unlabeled scans contributed to pretraining, and seven out-of-cohort tasks from SALD, PPMI, ADNI, ABIDE, and ADHD200, which were excluded from pretraining (Van Essen et al., 2013; Casey et al., 2018; Wei et al., 2018; Marek et al., 2011; Weiner et al., 2010; Di Martino et al., 2014; ADHD-200 Consortium, 2012). No downstream labels were used during pretraining. Frozen representations were evaluated with logistic regression for classification and ridge regression for age prediction. For age prediction, nMAE was computed after standardizing the target using the training-fold mean and population standard deviation. Cross-dataset ranks used weighted F1 for classification and nMAE for age prediction. Endpoint ranks were averaged within each cohort, and the dispersion of method mean ranks was evaluated using two million method-label permutations within cohort blocks. Full details are provided in Appendix A. 4.2 Main Results We evaluated CoFlu-Brain against four released fMRI representation models: BrainGFM, BrainMASS, BrainLM, and the Large Connectome Model (Wei et al., 2026a; Yang et al., 2024; Ortega Caro et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2026b).
