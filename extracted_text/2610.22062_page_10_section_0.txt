Most residual discrepancies reflect differing scoring targets rather than misreading of the note We classified every diarrhea discrepancy under the reported configuration (29 of 183 cells) by root cause (Table 4, Figure 2D), assigning each cell by rule from the keyword-passing notes in its own window and the adjacent ones rather than by manual re-reading. Three mechanisms account for all 29, and all three are annotation, data-coverage, or window-placement limitations rather than demonstrable misreadings. The reviewer integrates patient-level context across encounters and grades case by case, while the model is note-local by construction and applies one fixed threshold uniformly. The largest category (14/29, 48%) is a definitional disagreement about what counts as clinically significant diarrhea rather than a model error. It cuts both ways, since the 29 errors are 17 false negatives and 12 false positives, so a single severity threshold would trade one error type for the other. Re-scoring the 14 threshold-disagreement cases as correct yields a 91.8% bounding sensitivity-analysis estimate rather than a corrected performance figure (Supplementary Note S4). The 12 displacement cells are the ones an onset-aligned variant of the predictions addresses (Supplementary Note S7). Errors concentrate at 6 months, which holds 18 of the 29, and 28 of the 29 were also errors under the deployed prompt, so the severity bar shrank the error set rather than relocating it. What this rule-based assignment cannot do is separate a within-note misreading from a window-placement mismatch, since the prompts emit only YES or NO and so record whether a call is right but not the reasoning behind it (Supplementary Note S4).
