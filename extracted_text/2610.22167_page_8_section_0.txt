These findings support CLUE’s ability to identify rele- vant earlier exchanges and use them to explain its judgments. 5.2 Human Evaluation We next ask whether the model’s explanations make clear how conversational evidence supports a prediction, and whether it draws on the context that human readers consider relevant. We com- pare CLUE with base Qwen on 50 paired instances spanning all eight social tasks, selected indepen- dently of correctness. Each pair uses the first sam- pled output from each model in the main evalua- tion. The two graduate students who grew up in mainland China and the United States and are fa- miliar with Japanese language and culture are in- vited for the annotation. Before seeing model outputs, annotators select the conversational lines needed for the judgment. They then rate short reasoning excerpts extracted with the same fixed rule for both models, shown with their predicted answers in randomized or- der and with model identities hidden. An ex- cerpt is grounded when it identifies specific con- versational content through a quotation or precise paraphrase and explains how that content supports the answer. For annotations with at least one se- lected line, Evidence Alignment F1 measures over- lap between human-selected lines and conversa- tion lines matched to quotations in the full reason- ing, accepting any quotation style for both models. Scores are averaged over the available annotators for each item. Agreement on grounded versus not- grounded judgments is 80% on the shared items. Figure 7: Rates of grounded excerpts, Evidence Align- ment F1, and final-answer accuracy on 50 paired in- stances. Values are percentages. As shown in Figure 7, CLUE grounds its expla- nations in specific conversational content substan- tially more often than base Qwen, with 85% of ex- cerpts rated grounded compared with 33%. The improvement concerns the connection between ev- idence and the prediction, since quotation alone does not satisfy the grounding criterion. Evi- dence Alignment F1 also increases overall, indi- cating closer overlap with independently selected evidence. Using the two-sided signed-rank test of Wilcoxon (1945) on paired per-item F1 scores, we obtain p = 0.012, providing statistical support for closer alignment with human-selected evidence. This gain is clearest in-domain; on out-of-domain items, grounding remains higher but the smaller Evidence Alignment F1 difference is not statisti- cally significant. The grounding difference also persists among the 22 instances where both models answer cor- rectly: 95.5% of CLUE’s excerpts are grounded, compared with 50.0% for base Qwen. This shows that CLUE more often makes the conversational support for a judgment explicit even when both models reach a correct answer. 8
