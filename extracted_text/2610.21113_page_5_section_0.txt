RQ1 measures the operational effect of registry growth and SSR-based candidate pre-selection, RQ2 evaluates SSR retrieval effectiveness and efficiency on a large public corpus, and RQ3 compares full-catalog orchestration with SSR and BM25 candidate pre-selection under the same maximum candidate budget. A. SSR-AgentBench Public benchmarks exist for large-scale tool retrieval and MCP-oriented tool use [32]–[34], but, to the best of our knowl- edge, no public benchmark currently combines structured A2A-style Agent Cards, natural-language discovery queries, reproducible query-to-agent references, and controlled registry scaling. We therefore introduce SSR-AgentBench, a synthetic benchmark for controlled agent-discovery experiments. RQ1: Registry-Scaling Overhead RQ1 evaluates how full-catalog LLM orchestration scales with registry size and whether SSR-based candidate pre- selection can maintain a compact candidate context. All 250 SSR-AgentBench queries are evaluated at N ∈{10, 100, 500, 1000}. For each query, deterministic nested registries are constructed such that A10 ⊂A100 ⊂A500 ⊂A1000, with the designated target present at every size. Two configurations are compared: full catalog, which for- wards every Agent Card to the LLM, and SSR pre-selection, which retrieves at most K = 10 candidates before arbitration. Both use the same LLM candidate projection: a one-based index, Agent Card name, and description. SSR does not add non-matching Agent Cards when fewer than K candidates are available. RQ1 reports candidate count, provider-reported input to- kens, estimated inference cost, and online end-to-end latency; candidate recall and final selection accuracy are retained as diagnostics. SSR latency includes query-time retrieval, prompt construction, the LLM request, and response parsing, while registry construction and indexing are excluded. The two configurations are executed in deterministically alternating order across query–registry pairs.
