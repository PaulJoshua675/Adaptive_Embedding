Depth recurrence and architectural retrofitting. Depth recurrence reuses a block of layers mul- tiple times, increasing the effective depth of the model without adding any additional parameters (Dehghani et al., 2019). Previous attempts at creating recurrent LMs include Huginn (Geiping et al., 2025) and Ouro (Zhu et al., 2025). Huginn splits the model into three components: a prelude that embeds the input into a latent state, a recurrent block that iterates on that state, and a coda that de- codes the final state into output logits. Ouro iterates the entire set of layers, and Loopie (Gao et al., 2026) applies each layer recurrently instead of performing block-level looping. In all three cases, the model is trained from scratch without any pre-initialization by existing models. Two findings in- form our design: a looped block benefits from a stable learned connector between successive passes (Prairie et al., 2026), and an iterated layer contributes only a fraction of the capacity of a stored one (Schwethelm et al., 2026), thus bounding what recurrence can recover from pruning. As an alternative to full-corpus pretraining, recurrence can instead be achieved by retrofitting a pretrained model. Within this regime, there exist two lines of work: (1) looping a middle block of the stack without pruning, for latent reasoning (Koishekenov et al., 2025), and (2) looping after reducing the depth, either by keeping a block of the surviving layers (Bae et al., 2025; McLeish et al., 2025) or by merging neighbouring layers into a few blocks that are then looped (Kwon & Park, 2026). We adopt the latter approach, retrofitting recurrence after structural pruning. Specifically, we take inspiration from McLeish et al. (2025), who drop layers 4 to 9 of TinyLlama-1.1B to obtain a 4/8/4 layer allocation looped through a linear adapter. Recurrence in vision-language models. Current works either loop layers of a pretrained VLM without pruning, with a near-constant number of parameters (Xu et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026), or build one on a natively recurrent language backbone (Zhang et al., 2026). HIVE (Zhang et al., 2026) builds a VLM on the pretrained recurrent language model Huginn-3.5B (Geiping et al., 2025) by attaching an InternViT-300M vision encoder and training the combined model on 6.5M training samples in three stages. As such HIVE is based on a significantly larger model and trained on five times more training samples than the proposed model, preventing a direct comparison to those checkpoints. We therefore take inspiration from the pipeline in HIVE to replicate this setting as a baseline to compare to rather than comparing against their model. Beyond standard vision-language tasks, depth recurrence has been explored for robotic action policies, either in the action head of an intact VLM (Tur et al., 2026) or by pruning a VLM and looping a small block (Shen et al., 2026). However, these methods operate in a fundamentally different domain, and the pruned variant of Shen et al. (2026) lacks a prune-only baseline. Our setting departs from those existing approaches by following a prune-then-loop pattern, removing layers before the loop is installed. To the best of our knowledge, this work presents the first prune-then-loop retrofit of a pretrained VLM evaluated on VQA. 3
