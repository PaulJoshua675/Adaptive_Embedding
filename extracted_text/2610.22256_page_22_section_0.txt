BitSparse: Compact Activation Storage E.7 Layer-count trade-offs Figures 7, 8, and 9 give the three remaining activation and sparsity settings for the layer-count sweep of Figure 2. Whenever at least one layer is compressed or checkpointed, BitSparse completes the step faster than checkpointing, because checkpointing pays for recomputation while BitSparse adds only a codec pass. Checkpointing nevertheless attains the lowest VRAM in the two ρ = 0.5 settings, and the margin there is large: for ReLU at 8 layers, BitSparse uses 29% more memory than checkpointing at ρ = 0.5, but 5% less at ρ = 0.9. Both ρ = 0.9 settings therefore cross below checkpointing at the largest layer count. The steepest fall in VRAM occurs at the final layer count, and it is far sharper for ReLU2 than for ReLU: moving from 7 to 8 compressed layers lowers BitSparse’s VRAM by 23% at ρ = 0.9 and 15% at ρ = 0.5 for ReLU2, but by only 7% and 1% for ReLU.
