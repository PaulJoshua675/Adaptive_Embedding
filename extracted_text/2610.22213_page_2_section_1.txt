In this section, we review the related work on RBCD and two major approaches for generalization analysis: the uniform convergence approach and the algorithmic stability approach. Randomized Block Coordinate Descent. Coordinate descent has become the workhorse behind many large-scale optimization tasks, including regularized risk minimization [9, 54], nonnegative ma- trix and tensor factorization [30, 65], and root-finding for equations [59]. A seminal work established fast convergence rates of RBCD to the global minimum under smoothness, convexity, and separability assumptions [47]. This analysis was then extended to composite optimization problems, considering the minimization of the sum of a smooth data-fitting term and a nonsmooth block-separable convex regularizer [42, 51]. Random dynamical systems theory was used to characterize the asymptotic con- vergence of RBCD in nonconvex settings, especially with regard to saddle point problems [12, 13]. Moreover, subsequent work also applied RBCD to distributed optimization [52, 64], primal-dual opti- mization [21, 72], and differential privacy [15, 43]. While these studies provide extensive guarantees on the optimization of RBCD, there is still a gap in theoretically understanding its generalization for nonconvex problems. Uniform Convergence. Uniform convergence is a popular approach to study the generalization gap, which controls the uniform deviation between empirical quantities and their population counterparts over the whole hypothesis space [60]. For convex problems, the uniform convergence was established in terms of function values [4], and combining this with the convergence of empirical risks can lead to comprehensive generalization guarantees. However, optimization algorithms usually fail to find the global minimum for nonconvex problems, making optimization guarantees focus on the convergence of empirical gradients [23]. Motivated by this issue, covering numbers and Rademacher complexities have been used to characterize the complexity of function classes, yielding the uniform convergence of gradients in smooth problems [22, 44]. Furthermore, the lack of well-defined gradients in nons- mooth problems motivated the work on the uniform convergence of gradients of Moreau envelopes [16]. Recently, extensive studies theoretically analyzed the generalization of SGD by the uniform con- vergence approach [35, 36].
