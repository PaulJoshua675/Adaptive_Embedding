Designing Transformers for Continuous Diffusion Language Models 3 These architectures provide useful starting points for methodological research, but their suitability for continuous language generation warrants closer examination. Causal LLMs are designed for next-token prediction, whereas image DiTs operate on continuous visual representations. CDLMs must recover discrete language tokens from noisy continuous states while coordinating noise-dependent computation, vocabulary prediction, and, when used, self-conditioning. These requirements motivate a central question: • What Transformer architecture is best suited to continuous diffusion language models? In this paper, we systematically study Transformer architectures for CDLMs and introduce LangDiT , a backbone designed for continuous language generation. Our central finding is that architectural choices effective in causal LLMs or image diffusion models do not necessarily yield the best per- formance in CDLMs. By varying the architecture within fixed model formulations, we show that architectural design can substantially improve generation performance. We first develop and evaluate LangDiT within LangFlow (Chen et al., 2026), then transfer it to Plaid-style CDLMs (Gulrajani and Hashimoto, 2023; Yang et al., 2026). In each setting, we retain the underlying representation, objective formulation, and inference sampler to study architectural changes within each formulation. The Plaid adaptation reuses a subset of the design while retaining its native prediction interface. We investigate three aspects of CDLM architecture. Within the backbone, we study how feature normalization and noise conditioning affect denoising. At the vocabulary interface, we examine how gradients through the likelihood shortcut affect the learning of token embeddings. For self- conditioning, we investigate how strongly previous predictions should guide subsequent denoising. A cumulative ablation study under shared training and evaluation settings measures the incremental effects of these components, leading to a reusable architecture that improves generation while preserving the underlying model formulation. We evaluate LangDiT on OpenWebText unconditional generation at a sequence length of 1,024. A seven-stage LangFlow ladder tracks successive architectural changes (Figure 1). At 250k updates and 128 NFE under the same schedule, GenPPL decreases from 64.26 to 53.66, while mean per-sample unigram entropy changes from 5.416 to 5.391. Separately, a 660k LangFlow checkpoint and a 520k Plaid checkpoint from 1M-update plans reach GenPPL 21.72 and 11.33 at the larger sampling budget (Table 3). These extended-training scores accompany larger changes in entropy and do not isolate the effect of architecture from training budget. We distinguish these settings throughout the paper (Figure 2). Our contributions are threefold: • We conduct a systematic empirical study of six denoiser-design interventions in CDLMs, using a cumulative LangFlow ablation under shared training and evaluation settings. • We introduce LangDiT , which combines backbone normalization and noise conditioning with likelihood-shortcut gradient routing, calibration, and confidence-gated training self- conditioning. • We demonstrate lower GenPPL in matched LangFlow comparisons and examine the reuse of compatible components in a Plaid-style formulation, reporting sampling cost and unigram entropy alongside generation perplexity. Together, these findings establish architectural design as an important source of progress in CDLMs and provide a reusable foundation for further research on parallel language generation.
