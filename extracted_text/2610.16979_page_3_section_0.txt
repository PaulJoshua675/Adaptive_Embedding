Given predicted semantic-code values, GPR’s Value-Guided Trie-based Beam Search [34] allocates beam widths under trie con- straints without updating the generative policy. Training-based approaches [18] incorporate utility into super- vised or preference objectives. Exponential reward-weighted SFT [7] reweights logged interactions by observed rewards, while VARG [8] combines value-aware supervision with local ordinal learning over value-ordered codes. DPO [24] learns relative candidate preferences; RankGR [12] extends comparisons to lists, and RAD-DPO [6] ad- dresses shared-prefix conflicts and noisy implicit feedback. Reinforcement learning aligns generation with retrieval utility through relevance-feedback training [41], PPO [26], or GRPO [27]. However, exact-match behavioral rewards require the complete SID of a clicked or ordered item, leaving partially correct prefixes unrewarded [35]. Limited rollouts can yield all-zero reward groups and no reward-driven policy gradient. With advantages centered within each group, increasing sample count alone cannot recover a learning signal if all sampled rewards remain identical. Existing methods alleviate reward sparsity by modifying sam- pling or reward design [39]. Larger rollout budgets increase com- putational costs, while HCGRec [35] uses target-prefix hints to recover rewarding trajectories but introduces additional training complexity and a potential training-inference mismatch. VARG [8] provides denser feedback through relevance and ranker-derived rewards, yet its reliance on auxiliary scoring systems may limit cross-scenario generalizability. Hierarchy-aware methods alleviate reward sparsity through finer-grained credit assignment. OneSearch-V2 [3] combines behav- ioral rewards with estimated relevance and CTR, using position- wise advantages and prefix-dependent gating. HRPO [15] estimates prefix utilities from user-group statistics to derive token-level re- wards. However, both methods rely on additional utility estimation or reward design, potentially introducing noisy supervision and limiting generalizability across search scenarios. DHPO alleviates reward sparsity through curriculum learning by progressively expanding reward evaluation and policy optimization horizons from prefix-level to item-level matching. This enables effective behavioral supervision while ensuring accurate credit assignment, without relying on auxiliary reward models. 3
