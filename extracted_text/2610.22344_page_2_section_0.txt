Recently, Arbor [4] and Argus [5] maintain persistent research states and allow models to design, continue, and revise experiments, placing greater demands on the model’s ability to plan experiments and sustain optimization over long interaction horizons. ML-Agent [6] combines multi-turn demon- strations with single-step RL, while Frontis-MA1 [7] trains program evolution operators through SFT and RL and integrates them into an external evolutionary search framework. These approaches primarily train models for single-step decisions or local operations within externally organized pro- cesses. In contrast, we study how to train models to organize experiments and improve solutions over sustained interaction, using experience and execution feedback from real-world MLE tasks. However, training this long-horizon optimization capability poses three challenges. 1) Open-ended MLE requires an interaction environment that preserves experimental states while leaving action selection to the model. 2) High-quality long-horizon supervision is difficult to obtain because MLE tasks are heterogeneous and interaction trajectories may mix useful exploration and failure recovery with degraded decisions. 3) Each costly experiment returns feedback on several preceding decisions before the full run ends; online RL must use this intermediate signal while handling policy mismatch during asynchronous collection. To address these challenges, we propose a post-training method for autonomous MLE that combines a model-directed long-horizon interaction harness, hierarchical agentic SFT, and Windowed Interaction Reinforcement Learning (WIRL). Based on this, we train MLExpert, a 35B Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) agentic model, to learn long-horizon optimization strategies. As shown in Figure 1(b), MLExpert uses sustained tool interaction within the interaction harness to autonomously organize experiments, revise solutions based on execution feedback, and maintain submission candidates. We build the Kaggle-MLE Factory to convert historical Kaggle competitions into executable MLE tasks. Hierarchical agentic SFT transfers general tool-use and coding capabilities to MLE optimization, with execution outcomes guiding the selection of trajectories and segments for supervision. To use this intermediate feedback, WIRL treats each completed experiment as the boundary of an execution window containing decisions since the previous result. It trains those decisions with a shared outcome-based reward, while asynchronous collection and policy correction allow updates as trajectories continue. On MLE-Bench Lite, MLExpert improves the average medal rate of the base model from 37.9% to 72.7%, achieving performance comparable to larger models such as Kimi-K3 and GLM-5.3. MLExpert also improves performance on NatureBench Lite and transfers its optimization capabilities to agent scaffold and algorithm optimization tasks.
