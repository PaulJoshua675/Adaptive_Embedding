Future comparisons should use isolated sessions with memory disabled and a frozen protocol. 5.2 Metrics For n planned queries with designated targets gq, target recovery is Hit@5 = 1 n n X q=1 1[gq ∈Rq]. (1) Identity matching uses normalized persistent identifiers when available and reviewed title/ver- sion associations otherwise. Hit@5 measures recovery of a known positive; it does not assert that other returned works are irrelevant. Strict designated-target recovery should not be silently replaced by a broader work-family match. To examine how results depend on topics and prompt wording, we also measure pairwise agreement. For two systems A, B in a ten-query tier, Coverage(A, B) = P10 q=1 |RA q ∩RB q | 10 × 5 . (2) Only known same-query paper identities count, ignoring rank. The denominator remains 50 planned slots even when fewer papers are returned; an unfilled slot contributes no observed match. This is symmetric fixed-slot coverage, not Jaccard similarity or a denominator based on a variable returned-list size. Agreement complements target recovery but cannot establish that the benchmark is unbiased or representative. 5.3 Target recovery and cross-system coverage Table 1 shows a higher aggregate reported recovery rate for Idea2Prior, with a 10.0-point difference from ChatGPT and a 16.7-point difference from Gemini. This advantage is not uniform: ChatGPT ranks above Idea2Prior on HARD. The small, overlapping target set and historical protocol do not support a broad or statistically established superiority claim. Coverage ranges from 6% to 34% (Table 2). Thus the systems often return different papers for the same idea. This motivates inspecting complementary evidence sets, but low agreement alone does not show that their union is more relevant or that a particular system searches more broadly. 7
