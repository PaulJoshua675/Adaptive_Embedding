This paper introduces TagInsert, a Transformer- based model that challenges the common left- to-right (L2R) generation paradigm in Trans- formers, for sequence labelling tasks where de- pendencies may not naturally align with a L2R order. TagInsert lifts the L2R constraint by re- moving the decoder-side attention mask and enabling the model to decide a preferred order of tag generation at inference time. We exper- iment on the common Part-of-Speech tagging and the more complex Combinatory Categorial Grammar (CCG) supertagging tasks on multi- ple languages and low-resource data settings. We show that, although encoder-only models like BERT remain strong performers for tag- ging tasks, TagInsert frequently outperforms a L2R encoder-decoder model, achieves results comparable to encoder-only models under data- scarce conditions, and shows better results on the long-tail of rare categories. Our analyses show that TagInsert alleviates error propagation typical of autoregressive models by learning to defer difficult predictions to later stages in the decoding process. 1
