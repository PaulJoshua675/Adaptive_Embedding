The incremental gains over TextGrad are 2.74, 1.76, and 4.74 points, respectively: the newest generation has the smallest overall gain but the largest M-step increment. On Qwen3.5-9B, context-only optimization gains just 1.66 points on average, whereas the full method gains 6.40. AIME makes this contrast concrete: Base, TextGrad, and EMO score 25.0, 26.7, and 45.0. Thus, limited gains from context rewrites need not imply limited scope for adaptation: steering can further exploit the distributional changes induced by those rewrites. Family: narrowing gaps at matched model size. Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B allow a cross-family comparison at matched parameter scale. On MMLU-Pro, their accuracy gap shrinks from 18.2 points at Base to 3.6 after applying EMO to both models. The corresponding gaps shrink from 31.8 to 17.4 on MATH-500 and from 9.3 to 2.9 on GSM8K. Steering contributes directly to the MMLU-Pro result: its increment over TextGrad is 10.0 points for Llama versus 2.7 for Qwen. On Llama-3.1-8B/MATH-500, however, TextGrad already reaches 65.4 versus EMO’s 65.6. The narrowed family gaps therefore reflect task- dependent contributions from context refinement and steering. Thinking mode: gains with and without explicit reasoning modes. The non-thinking Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Llama-3.2-3B gain 9.64, 12.22, and 12.96 points on average. Qwen2.5-7B also exceeds TextGrad on all five task groups, showing that M-step gains do not require a native thinking mode. Models with thinking modes also improve: the Qwen3 series gains 7.44–9.42 points, and the reasoning-distilled DR1-Qwen3-8B gains 6.02. DR1 improves over Base on all five task groups, showing that reasoning distillation leaves scope for question-specific adaptation. Yet its M-step increment is −0.64 points on average, with an additional gain only on MMLU-Pro (68.2 vs. 67.0). DR1 and Qwen3.5-9B therefore have similar overall gains (6.02 vs. 6.40) but contrasting M-step contributions (−0.64 vs. +4.74). One possible explanation is that distillation reshapes context-conditioned distributions and their sensitivity to logit interventions, altering the benefit of a fixed steering rule. Establishing this mechanism requires matched pre-/post-distillation controls. Where the M-step is most effective. Figure 1 shows how the advantage develops across rounds on Qwen3-14B/GPQA- D. From a shared 52.5 baseline, EMO continues improving to 65.2, while TextGrad levels off near 56–59 and ends at 58.1.
