This avoids an additional matrix-vector product and the computation of A−1. 5.3 Active Update Active-CMA-ES (Jastrebski and Arnold, 2006) extends the standard CMA-ES by incorporating information from both successful and unsuccessful candidate solutions into the covariance matrix adaptation process. While traditional CMA-ES increases variances in directions associated with successful steps and allows other directions to decay passively, Active-CMA-ES actively reduces variances in directions associated with poor solutions. This is accomplished by modifying the rank-µ update term to include negative contributions from the worst-performing individuals. Specifically, the update in line 16 from Algorithm 1 C ←[. . . ] + cµ µ X i=0 wiyi:λ(yi:λ)⊤ (10) is extended to C ←[. . . ] + cµ λ X i=0 wiyi:λ(yi:λ)⊤ (11) where the weights wi assigned to the λ −µ worst individuals are negative. This active update accelerates the adaptation of the covariance matrix and has been shown to empirically improve performance on standard benchmark problems (Jastrebski and Arnold, 2006). Note that in Hansen (2016), this variant is treated as the default; here, we consider it an optional modification. For canonical xNES, active update is the standard, since the update of ∇M is originally defined as a sum over all λ individuals (Wierstra et al., 2014). Here, the default is to sum over µ, and with active update enabled, to sum over λ. 6 Restarting When the optimization process stagnates, the CMA-ES can be restarted using a restart strategy (Auger et al., 2004). By default, the restart criteria specified in Hansen (2016) are used. These include checks on the internal parameters, such as σ and C, and stagnation of the observed f(x) values. In ModCMA, any of these criteria can be disabled or their tolerances modified to suit the user’s needs. Moreover, custom restart criteria can be specified, and potentially combined, providing greater flexibility. If any of these criteria evaluates to true, the CMA-ES will be stopped or restarted. What happens following a restart is controlled by the next two modules. 6.1 Restart Strategy The restart strategy specifies how the algorithm proceeds once a restart condition is triggered.
