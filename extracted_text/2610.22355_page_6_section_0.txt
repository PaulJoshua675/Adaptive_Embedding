For the decomposition-based scoring sample, the verifier takes the textual prompt c and the generated image y as input, and is trained to predict the global alignment score induced by the decomposition. Given the predicted score ˆS ∈[0, 1] and the ground-truth score S∗∈[0, 1], the reward is defined as Rscore = 1 −| ˆS −S∗|. For each fine-grained judgment sample, the verifier takes the k-th sub-question Qk and the generated image y as input, and is trained to predict the binary answer associated with this sub-question. Given the predicted answer ˆAk ∈{0, 1} and the ground-truth answer A∗ k ∈{0, 1}, the reward is defined as Rjudgment = 1[ ˆAk = A∗ k]. During reinforcement learning, we mix these two types of samples and optimize them with their respective rewards, forming a decoupled RL training scheme that jointly optimizes sub-question-level binary judgment and global alignment scoring. Precise 0/1 feedback on individual sub-questions enables the verifier to develop fine-grained verification capabilities that provide a foundation for reliable continuous scoring. Experiment Analysis. Table 1 shows that the Decomposed Graded Verifier achieves stronger gener- alization than the Discrete Binary Verifier on ViVerBench, a comprehensive and challenging benchmark for evaluating multimodal reasoning and critique. Notably, the Decomposed Graded Verifier requires supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to learn the structured decomposition paradigm. By contrast, the Discrete Binary Verifier only needs to produce a binary True/False verdict with a corresponding textual explanation, and can therefore be directly optimized through reinforcement learning. For a fair comparison, we evaluate the Discrete Binary Verifier in both RL-only and SFT+RL settings. The results show that the Discrete Binary Verifier consistently underperforms the Decomposed Graded Verifier, regardless of whether it is first adapted with SFT. This indicates that the key factor is not the training pipeline itself, but the nature of the supervision signal. Binary supervision is highly compressed and sparse, which encourages the model to rely on shallow patterns rather than capturing complex semantic structures. In contrast, the Decomposed Graded Verifier decomposes complex tasks into multiple sub-questions and performs fine- grained judgments, thereby explicitly enforcing a structured reasoning process. This mechanism provides richer and more informative feedback signals, effectively mitigating reward hacking and substantially improving the model’s multimodal reasoning and critique capabilities. Moreover, our Decoupled RL training strategy jointly optimizes sub-question-level binary judgment and global alignment scoring, using precise 0/1 feedback on individual sub-questions to strengthen fine-grained verification capabilities and thereby provide a foundation for robust generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. 4 Verifier-Guided Reinforcement Learning for Generative Models In this section, we investigate how the Decomposed Graded Verifier guides generative model optimization through reinforcement learning. We begin with traditional text-to-image generation task, and then focus on studying how to adapt the verifier to provide more reliable reward signals for rule-governed world modeling scenarios. 4.1 Verifier-Guided RL for Traditional Text-to-Image Generation Given a textual prompt c and its generated image y, the Decomposed Graded Verifier first decomposes c into a set of fine-grained sub-questions Q = {q1, q2, . . . , qK} and then produces a continuous score by aggregating their verification results: S = 1 K PK k=1 J (qk, y), where J (qk, y) ∈{0, 1} denotes a binary judgment function that evaluates whether the generated image y satisfies sub-question qk.
