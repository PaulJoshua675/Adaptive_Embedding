Controlled scheduling comparison on the historical 72K corpus. Local grouping reduces padding but leaves more rank imbalance; global grouping and balancing deliver the largest throughput gain. A cross-update buffer reduces padding slightly further without increasing throughput. Local grouping increases throughput from 99.06 to 116.91 QA/s and reduces padding from 35.17% to 12.36%. However, mean gradient-ready spread between ranks increases from 115.0 to 306.6 ms, and tail communica- tion/wait time from 39.5 to 157.6 ms. Minimizing padding independently on each rank can therefore leave unequal workloads at synchronization. Global grouping plus balancing reduces padding to 1.77%, reduces ready-time spread relative to local grouping to 145.7 ms, and reaches 129.54 QA/s: a 30.78% throughput in- crease or 23.53% less measured time for equal QA volume. Ready-time spread and tail time remain above the original-order values, so this gain reflects the combined scheduling changes rather than an isolated reduction in synchronization waiting. The 128-QA buffer lowers padding to 1.03% but achieves 129.39 QA/s. Its step-time coefficient of variation increases from 6.07% to 19.77%; even after aggregating pairs of updates into equal 512-QA units, variation is 6.30% versus 4.61% without the buffer. This identifies diminishing returns from further packing and supports the unbuffered final layout.
