Because retained conversation counts could dif- fer between conditions, the filtered entropy es- timates may be sensitive to unequal exposure. Among paired users, the mean numbers of retained conversations were 2.60 for FULL and 2.62 for STATISTICALONLY with KBRD, and 1.66 and 1.80, respectively, with UniCRS. Pooling more conversations can expose more aspect categories and thereby affect entropy, so the filtered estimates may reflect both condition effects and retained- exposure differences. We therefore use the all- conversation analysis below, which retains all four opening strategies in both conditions for all 120 users, as a sensitivity check. Table 7 and Figure 11 show a positive primary T3 effect for both recommenders and both eval- uation judges. The all-conversation sensitivity analysis yielded effects of +0.275 and +0.371 for KBRD, and +0.560 and +0.534 for UniCRS, un- der GPT-4o-mini and DeepSeek-chat, respectively (all p < 0.001). Here, “technically completed” means that a complete conversation record was pro- duced without a transport or generation error; it does not require natural acceptance and includes max-turn terminations. Per-user T3 scores were strongly correlated be- tween the two evaluation judges: Spearman’s ρ = 0.77 in the primary analysis and ρ = 0.72 in the all-conversation sensitivity analysis. The consistent direction makes unequal exposure an unlikely sole explanation of the T3 result, but does not establish overall simulator quality or fidelity to individual real users. F Human Evaluation Protocol F.1 Annotation Task Each annotation task presented an annotator with a single claim (one of the six attribute values from a FULL-enriched user profile), the user’s behavioral profile (Stage 1–2 statistics), and the user’s in- terview transcript (Stage 3 output, including any turns the auditor had flagged). Annotators were asked to judge whether the claim was supported by the interview evidence. F.2 Rating Scales Support (4-class): • Supported: the claim is directly justified by specific content in the interview. • Partially supported: the interview contains relevant content but does not fully justify the specific claim (e.g., the claim is more specific or stronger than the evidence). • Unsupported: the interview does not contain evidence relevant to the claim, even though the claim is consistent with the profile. 16
