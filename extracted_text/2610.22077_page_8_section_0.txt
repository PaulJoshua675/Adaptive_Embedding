FGN and GenCast have similar negative peak-wind biases at short and intermediate leads despite their different CRPS. Separating dependence from intensity. To isolate the dependence structure from marginal biases, we rank wind and pressure separately within each dataset and transform the ranks to standard normal scores. This rank-based transformation renders the marginal distributions identical across datasets, while preserving their joint dependence structure, represented by the copula. The copulas are found to be consistent across models and references (Figure 6b): FGN and GenCast have rank correlations of −0.83 and −0.84, compared with −0.88 for HRES-fc0. The qualitative result also holds in the earlier evaluation (Appendix B.2) and across separate lead-time ranges. IBTrACS supplies observational context, although its sustained winds differ from gridded instantaneous winds. FGN therefore reproduces a wind–pressure dependence structure comparable to that of GenCast, despite being trained with CRPS applied separately to each variable and grid point, which constrains their conditional marginal distributions but not their joint dependence. The coexistence of negatively biased wind speeds and pressure minima with a realistic wind–pressure association suggests that intensity errors and dependence errors are distinct. While this does not imply that the models have learned an explicit physical mechanism, nor that wind speed is correctly predicted at a given pressure level, it allows intensity biases to be clearly separated from errors in the dependence structure. 6
