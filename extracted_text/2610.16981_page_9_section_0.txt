The results show the shortest path uses tele- portation as a shortcut rather than longer sequences on swap and transfer edges. This reliance increases as LIFO-cutoff or ego-only restrictions remove alternative inter-transaction connections. Weighting produces a markedly different composition (Fig. 2(b)). Without teleportation, it raises the swap share from 5.62% to 17.89% (all accounts, all edges) and from 5.67% to 18.23% (LIFO-cutoff). In contrast, alter-account inter- transaction edges fall from 10.30% to 3.78% and from 17.34% to 6.63%, respec- tively, reflecting the low cost of swap edges and the added penalty on alter- account edges. Weighting also reduces teleportation reliance: The ego-account teleport share drops from 20.75%–47.98% (unweighted) to 5.35%–21.24% (weigh- ted), while swap edges rise from near zero to 13.37%–17.68%. Teleport share re- mains highest under ego-only and LIFO-cutoff, indicating that no lower-cost alternative exists for some recovered pairs. Overall, weighting retains teleportation as a fallback while favoring transfer, swap, and ego-account inter-transaction edges, resulting in longer path lengths.
