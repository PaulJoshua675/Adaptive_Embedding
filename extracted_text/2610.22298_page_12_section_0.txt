Continuous-variable SMDs use means and pooled standard deviations although medians and IQRs are displayed, binary-variable SMDs are standardized prevalence differences. SMDs are descriptive and no cohort-comparison hypothesis tests were performed. All models completed both response-format pipelines for all 190 held-out cases (100% coverage). The 1,520 unique case–model panels contained 7,271 role slots (mean, 4.78 per panel); reuse of each panel across the free-form and structured conditions yielded 14,542 role-specific opinions and 3,040 final consilium opinions. These artifact counts describe coverage, whereas the DOI remains the inferential unit. Panel selection was strongly concentrated at the configured ceiling. Among 1,520 case–model panels, 8 (0.5%) contained two roles, 53 (3.5%) contained three, 199 (13.1%) contained four, and 1,260 (82.9%) contained five (Figure 2b). 7 models had a median of five roles, Gemma-4-31B had a median of four. Because the selector could assign at most five roles, this concentration shows that most configurations frequently exhausted the panel capacity, not that five was an empirically optimal panel size, it also provided little empirical variation for assessing whether panel cardinality adapted to the case. The 7,271 assignments used 696 exact model-generated role labels, including 212 exact labels among the 1,520 primary designations. This lexical variety largely reflected differences in naming granularity rather than hundreds of clinically distinct disciplines. After mutually exclusive harmonization, infectious disease, radiology, and neurology were the most frequent categories, whereas neurology (245/1,520, 16.1%) and infectious disease (199/1,520, 13.1%) were the most frequent primary roles (Figure 2c). Radiology-containing labels appeared in 729 assignments but received only 3 primary designations (0.4% of radiology assignments), indicating that the selector usually prioritized another discipline as the principal case perspective. 12
