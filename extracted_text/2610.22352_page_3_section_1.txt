Multi-view texture generation. Most texturing pipelines synthesize appearance in 2D views and fuse the predictions onto the mesh. Early approaches paint the mesh view-by-view with depth-guided diffusion and inpaint unobserved UV regions (Richardson et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023; Zeng et al., 2024a). TexFusion aggregates view-wise denoising predictions in a shared latent texture map (Cao et al., 2023). Subsequent works improve cross-view coherence by synchronizing multi-view denoising (Liu et al., 2024b; Cheng et al., 2025; Bensadoun et al., 2024) or generating views jointly via geometry-conditioned diffusion (Huang et al., 2025b; Shao et al., 2025; Liang et al., 2026; Han et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026), a recipe also adopted by large image-to-3D systems (Zhang et al., 2024a; Li et al., 2025b; Tencent Hunyuan3D Team, 2025b). Reference-guided methods also explicitly target appearance preservation: EASI-Tex combines mesh-edge guidance with image features for single-image texturing (Perla et al., 2024), while AlignTex aligns multi-view artwork with the mesh before projection and texture refinement (Zhang et al., 2025b). Optimization-based approaches such as DreamMat distill geometry- and light-aware diffusion priors into PBR materials (Zhang et al., 2024b). Paint-it instead optimizes convolutionally parameterized PBR texture maps with score distillation (Youwang et al., 2024). To produce relightable assets, multi-view generation is extended to PBR channels using lighting-aware priors, material latent representations, or alternating view-space baking (Deng et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025a; He et al., 2025a; Yeo et al., 2026; Feng et al., 2025; Tencent Hunyuan3D Team, 2025a; Lai et al., 2025; Bao et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2026b), often aided by single-image intrinsic delighting (Kocsis et al., 2024; Zeng et al., 2024b; Ke et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024; He et al., 2025b; Careaga & Aksoy, 2024). Nevertheless, residual inconsistencies between synthesized views can blur or ghost sharp reference details during projection and blending. In contrast, SUPRA-Tex completes materials in native 3D space, using projected observations as conditioning anchors rather than directly fusing synthesized views into the final texture. Native texture generation. Driven by the rise of large-scale 3D pretraining, native texture generation has emerged as an alternative paradigm. Existing approaches explore diffusion over point, octree, and UV representations (Yu et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024a; Yu et al., 2024) and PBR prediction with octree-aligned Gaussians (Xiong et al., 2025). Latent texture generators, such as NaTex, LaFiTe, and TEXTRIX, learn structured latent spaces for surface colors or attributes (Lai et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026; Zeng et al., 2026), while native 3D foundation models jointly represent geometry and appearance (Chen et al., 2025; Xiang et al., 2025; 2026). Beyond global conditioning, partial observations and spatially aligned features have also been explored. At scene scale, WorldGrow (Li et al., 2026b) extends TRELLIS with context-conditioned 3D block inpainting for progressive scene generation. TEXGen back-projects partial UV maps (Yu et al., 2024), NaTex encodes initial textures for refinement (Lai et al., 2026), and TEXTRIX encodes projected appearance with its pretrained attribute VAE in the generation target’s latent space (Zeng et al., 2026). For spatial feature alignment, camera projection techniques originally developed for implicit reconstruction (Saito et al., 2019; Yu et al., 2021a) have been adapted to 3D generative models, such as CUPID’s pose-aligned features and Pixal3D’s camera-frame back-projected multi-scale features (Huang et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026a). Appendices A and B provide implementation and training details; Appendix G.1 describes additional-view conditioning and texture editing. 3.1 Preliminaries Surface PBR and material latents. We represent surface appearance through physically based rendering (PBR) properties over the mesh M. At any surface location p ∈M, these properties comprise albedo a ∈R3, metallic m ∈[0, 1], and roughness r ∈[0, 1]. We build on the O-Voxel representation of TRELLIS.2 (Xiang et al., 2026), which additionally includes opacity α ∈[0, 1], giving the material tuple T(p) = (a(p), m(p), r(p), α(p)). Separate sparse VAEs compress 3D geometry and surface materials into geometry latents G = {gs}s∈S and material latents Z = {zs}s∈S over active coordinates S. For sparse surface attributes P, the pretrained material VAE encoder Emat yields Z; conditioned on G, the material generation stage models PBR materials directly in the native 3D space by generating Z, which the decoder Dmat restores onto the mesh surface. Geometry foundation and camera projection. Our framework takes a mesh M and camera projection π, either provided as ground truth or predicted from the reference image. We use the open-source TRELLIS.2-based Pixal3D variant (Li et al., 2026a; Xiang et al., 2026), denoted Pixal3D⋆, which supports geometry and material generation.
