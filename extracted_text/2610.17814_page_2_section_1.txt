Event linking and item admission. Event linking grounds se- lected mentions to news archives or knowledge-base events [17, 26], with event arguments helping disambiguation [10]. Entity-linking research addresses incomplete inventories, NIL prediction, and language-model disambiguation [16, 25, 27, 29]. These tasks es- tablish a mention’s target or its absence. Admission concerns the role of an event in the complete item: a linkable historical refer- ence can be background, while a new event can be central without a known target. Target availability and item eligibility therefore define different decisions. Detection, salience, and organization. Web event detection ranges from real-time social sensing [23] to graph-based detection [20] and retrieval-augmented event discovery and evolution [14]. Salience identifies events carrying a document’s central information [12, 15]. Admission additionally asks whether that information establishes an organizing target: a salient hypothetical can be rejected, while a concrete reported action can qualify without being globally im- portant. Timeline construction, background summarization, and cross-document coreference organize or connect event informa- tion [4, 5, 13, 19]. We examine the item-level boundary at which information enters such workflows. Selective prediction and calibration. Reject-option and selective- prediction methods study predictive risk as a system withholds decisions [6–8]. Our distinction is between a threshold rejecting an available score and upstream processing preventing that score from existing. Probability calibration concerns the reliability of predicted probabilities [9]. We instead study development-selected operating thresholds, including targets that unavailable scores make infeasible. Work on unanswerable RAG queries examines related output decisions [18]. Tracking pre-score losses makes the inputs unavailable to the final decision rule explicit. Evaluation populations. Benchmark construction, retrieval-based sampling, and impression logs influence which examples are evalu- ated [1, 2, 24]. Full-stage ranking addresses selection across retrieval and ranking stages [28], while recent information-extraction work examines sampling and evaluation sensitivity [11]. Here, selection also arises during inference on a fixed incoming cohort. The same inputs yield different scored subsets across systems and execution conditions. We connect this population change to both the mean- ing of ranking comparisons and the feasibility of complete-stream operating targets. 2
