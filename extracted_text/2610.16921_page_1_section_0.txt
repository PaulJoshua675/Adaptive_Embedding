PREPRINT 1 Time Window Optimization for Motor Imagery Classification via Temporal Structure Embedded Reinforcement Learning Huan Luo, Na Lu, Xiaopeng Wang, and Ziyang Shang Abstract— Online motor imagery-based brain-computer interfaces (MI-BCIs) usually processes continuous EEG signal with fixed-length sliding windows, which is inflexible, human intervened, and misaligned with neural events. Time window optimization has shown promising improvement as a substitute solution. However, the existing optimiza- tion methods typically evaluate windows isolatedly and fail to model temporal dependencies across consecutive windows, which may produce fragmented and physiologi- cally implausible sequences. To address this limitation, we propose a novel Temporal Structure Embedded Reinforce- ment Learning (TSE-RL) framework. This framework em- beds prior temporal-structure knowledge of MI events into a stable RL formulation, reformulating window optimization as a constrained Markov Decision Process with ordinal re- lation enforcement of neural event. First, the evaluation and action selection upon each window has been changed from simply “retain/discard” to semantic alignment between win- dow and neural events as Discard/Onset/sustain/Offset. Second, action masking is imposed by a finite-state ma- chine (FSM) which enforces physiologically plausible de- cision orders with constraint of neural activity progress in term of planning/execution/recovery, enhancing both rationality, interpretability and classification performance. Third, a Transformer-based history encoder is designed to capture long-range inter-window dependencies in parallel, improving training stability and efficiency compared with existing recurrent architectures. Besides, TSE-RL adopts a plug-and-play design that can be integrated with diverse MI classifiers. Extensive experiments on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that TSE-RL consistently improves decoding performance in both cross-session and cross- subject settings. Visualization analyses further suggest that the learned policy of window optimization aligns with neurophysiological principles. Index Terms— Brain computer interface, electroen- cephalography, motor imagery, reinforcement learning
