We presented an open midtraining recipe for agentic coding, with full details of how each data source is built, filtered, and weighted and how the models are trained. After identical SFT, the recipe gives clear gains as midtraining scales to 500B tokens on N-3.5 Lightning, and it carries over to the larger N-3.5 Super, where it raises DeepSWE, the hardest benchmark we evaluate, from 11.7 to 15.7 and also improves SWE-bench Verified and BrowseComp. On both models, midtraining improves results on every agent harness we tested. Our experiments show that agentic traces are essential, since natural software-engineering data alone gives much smaller gains after SFT, and that a short SFT run gives a more direct post-training signal than base-model post-trainability metrics, which remain useful to track. Midtraining at 256K also works as an intermediate long-context stage, and we explain our other modeling choices, such as tool-response masking and pretraining replay. Limitations and future work. Agentic benchmarks are noisy, and each comparison is a single run, so differences of one or two points, especially on Terminal-Bench, may come from run-to-run variation. We did not compare against spending the same tokens on longer pretraining or SFT, since midtraining adds a separate stage that complements SFT rather than replacing it. We tested the recipe only on Nemotron models, not on other model families, and mainly on agentic coding. Our SFT evaluations use an agentic-focused SFT blend rather than a full general-purpose post-training blend. Our RL results are also limited, since clear RL gains on top of a strong SFT checkpoint were difficult to get at the N-3.5 Lightning scale and RL was too compute intensive at the N-3.5 Super scale, so we report only a short, single-seed run on N-3.5 Lightning-8K (Appendix D). For future work, since traces drove most of the gains, we plan to scale trace data with more diversity, and we have already expanded our trace corpus to about 90B tokens. We also plan to extend the agentic data to more capabilities and to move more post-training data, such as reasoning and chat data, into midtraining.
