2.1 Generative Verifier or Reward Models Generative reward models (Mahan et al., 2024; Malik et al., 2025; Tan et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2026c) differ fundamentally from traditional reward models (Xu et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024a; Ouyang et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2024c) in that they produce explicit rationales rather than merely assigning scalar scores. OmniVerifier (Zhang et al., 2025b) first introduces the concept of Generative Universal Verifier and presents ViVerBench, a comprehensive and challenging benchmark for evaluating multimodal reasoning and critique capabilities. DeepSeekMath-V2 (Shao et al., 2025) further proposes meta-verification and integrates it into verifier training to enhance faithfulness and reliability. OmniVerifier-M1 (Zhang et al., 2026d) extends meta- verification to multimodal scenarios by introducing symbolic spatial annotations, such as bounding boxes, points or lines as verifiable rationales. These rule-based supervision signals substantially alleviate reward hacking and enhance verifier generalization. Despite their improved interpretability, existing generative verifiers still rely on coarse binary decisions as final judgments, and thus struggle to provide dense and optimization-friendly reward signals for reinforcement learning. In this paper, we introduce a Decoupled RL training strategy to train the Decomposed Graded Verifier, which provides reliable continuous scores through explicit fine-grained decomposition. 2.2 Generative Model as World Reasoner Multimodal generative models have made remarkable progress (Lin et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026b;a; Zhang et al., 2026c) in semantic alignment and aesthetic quality. Beyond visual fidelity, they are increasingly expected to serve as reasoning-capable world simulators (OpenAI, 2025b; Google, 2025; 2024). To quantify the world reasoning capabilities of current generative models, several benchmarks (Li et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025; Han et al., 2026) have been proposed. WISE (Niu et al., 2025) focuses on evaluating generative models’ understanding of cultural commonsense and scientific knowledge. MMGR (Cai et al., 2025) is designed to probe generative models’ spatial, temporal, physical, and logical reasoning capabilities. Motivated by these challenges, recent studies (Tan et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026a; 2025a) have explored new training paradigms and input modalities to learn the underlying dynamics of world evolution. Emu3.5 (Cui et al., 2025) advances world modeling by learning native next-state prediction over long-horizon interleaved vision-language sequences. UnityVideo (Huang et al., 2025) improves world-aware video generation through unified multi-modal and multi-task learning across diverse visual representations. In contrast to these efforts, we focus on improving the reliability and accuracy of reinforcement learning rewards for visual generation in rule-governed world-modeling scenarios, thereby encouraging generative models to develop stronger and more generalizable world reasoning capabilities. 3 OmniVerifier-C1: Decomposed Graded Verifier In this section, we first analyze the theoretical advantages of the graded verifier over the binary verifier as a generative reward model. We then empirically demonstrate that our verifier develops fine-grained and generalizable multimodal verification capabilities through the Decoupled RL strategy, which jointly optimizes sub-question-level binary judgment and global alignment scoring.
