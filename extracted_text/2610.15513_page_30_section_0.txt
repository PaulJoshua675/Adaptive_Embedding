A behavior whose initial population already contains a successful attack terminates at iteration zero, in which case the target-query cost equals exactly 𝑃(24 for default-configured runs, 16 for legacy 𝑃= 16 runs). The median cost of 24 target queries across all 211 behaviors indicates that more than half of all behaviors are solved by the initial population alone, before the evolutionary loop activates. This reflects the combined effect of the curated seed library, the high-temperature bootstrapping strategy, and the amortization cache (Section 3.5), which together produce a sufficiently diverse initial population to cover a majority of behaviors without iteration. The evolutionary loop is therefore primarily responsible for solving the harder tail of behaviors that resist initial-population coverage. The worst observed case (gpt-4o-mini at 266 iterations, 5589 queries) remains well below the per-behavior budget reported by gray-box random-search methods: Andriushchenko et al. [4] report using up to 10,000 random-search queries per behavior for prompt suffix optimization under their gray-box setup, making REPRO’s median cost roughly 417× more efficient and its worst observed case roughly 1.8× more efficient. The cross-benchmark contrast for gpt-4o-mini also illustrates that benchmark difficulty affects cost: the same target model requires a median of 24 queries on AdvBench-50 versus 66 queries on JBB-50, and the mean roughly doubles (231 vs. 411 queries), reflecting that JBB-50’s behaviors more frequently resist the initial population and require evolutionary iteration. Scope. Table 12 reports query cost on a representative subset of our experiments covering 211 behaviors across five target models drawn from two benchmarks (AdvBench-50 and JBB-50). All attack-success-rate results reported elsewhere in the paper are from the full experiment matrix; this query-cost analysis uses the subset because per-behavior query logs were retained only for these runs.
