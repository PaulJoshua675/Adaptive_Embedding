The controlled tests confirm each prediction: freshness alone leaves changing decisions stale, delivery is sufficient for change and recall follows what the ledger indexes, misleading deliveries cost correct decisions in proportion to weak judgment, and the residual error is mostly temporal. The theory yields three design princi- ples for LLM agents: record each decision with its times and what it depends on; route news to it until its revision deadline, through an index written when the decision is made; and revalidate it by checking when each change is in effect. Limitations Controlled streams. The decision streams are synthetic by design: exact control over timing, ref- erence and labels is what lets each prediction be tested in isolation. Real news is longer and nois- ier, dependencies can be implicit, and relevance can be uncertain, so the magnitudes we report be- long to these streams while the predictions concern mechanisms. Testing the theory on logged agent decisions, and in languages beyond English, is the next step. Scope of the theory. The theory models a deci- sion as a choice among options with one revision deadline and a binary outcome, and the freshness ceiling assumes a constant hazard rate. Real com- mitments are staged, partly reversible and graded in utility, and facts change in bursts. The factori- sation of Eq. 3 carries over, because information must reach a decision before it can change it, but its terms then depend on time and on the cost of revising. Reach of the ledger. Routing recall is bounded by the dependencies the ledger records at decision time, and the ledger treats routed news as trustwor- thy. Discovering dependencies after the decision and weighing the credibility of sources extend the ledger beyond this paper. Ethics Statement The decision streams use fictional entities and con- tain no personal data, and hosted models were queried on this synthetic data only. Systems that revise decisions on a user’s behalf can act on false or adversarial news; routing should be paired with source verification and, for consequential decisions, with user confirmation.
