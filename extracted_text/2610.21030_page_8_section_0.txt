We compare AURA with an LLM- confidence rule based on the mean token entropy of the current reasoning step, tuning both the thresh- old and stopping direction on development data. Its strongest variant obtains 32.8 nDCG@10 at 3.2 average steps, compared with AURA’s 34.7 at 2.8 steps. Thus, generation confidence alone does not reliably identify the reasoning state that is most discriminative for retrieval. Q6: When should reasoning stop? Early stop- ping consistently outperforms using the full reason- ing trajectory (33.0), validating that over-reasoning degrades retrieval quality. Among stopping strate- gies, patience p=1 (34.7) performs best; greedy peak stopping (p=0) is comparable (34.6), while increasing patience to p=3 (33.7) allows too many low-utility steps through. The performance gap be- tween p=1 and no early stopping (+1.7) confirms that adaptive termination is essential. 4.9 Inference-Time Compute Efficiency Table 5 compares inference-time reasoning cost. To isolate the effect of utility-guided stopping, we first compare AURA with and without early stop- ping under the same Qwen3-4B backbone, hard- ware, batch size, and software stack. Without early stopping, AURA generates 332 tokens (9.5 steps) per query, takes 4.6 seconds, and achieves 33.0 nDCG@10. Utility-guided stopping reduces gen- eration to 96 tokens (2.8 steps) and latency to 0.76 seconds, corresponding to a 3.5× token reduction and approximately 6× speedup, while improving performance to 34.7 (+1.7). The utility estimator itself adds approximately 1 millisecond per query. For reference, the strongest external reasoning base- line (DIVER + GPT-4 expansion) generates 479 tokens per query using a separate reasoning model yet achieves lower performance (32.1).
