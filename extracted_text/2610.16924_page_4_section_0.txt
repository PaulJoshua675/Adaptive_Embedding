Input-Dependent Neuron Parameters To enhance the expressive capacity of the neuron, we make the time constant τi and resistance Ri adaptive to the recent input context, which is inspired by recent studies in sequence models (Gu and Dao 2024; Peng et al. 2023). Let Ci = (ci−n+1, . . . , ci) denote a causal window containing the most recent n inputs. A small causal parameter generator gψ gives (∆τi, ∆Ri) = gψ(Ci). (21) To constrain the range of parameters and improve training stability, we define the final dynamics parameters as follows: τi = τmin + (τmax −τmin)σ(τbase + ∆τi), Ri = Rmin + (Rmax −Rmin)σ(Rbase + ∆Ri). (22) The bounds τmin, τmax, Rmin, Rmax and the base values τbase, Rbase are tunable hyperparameters; we always con- strain τi > 0 so that ρi = exp(−∆t/τi) < 1, preserving the membrane potential decay characteristic of the LIF model. All {τi, Ri}T i=1 are generated from causal input contexts before the iterative solve, so no future inputs are required and step-by-step inference is preserved. These parameters remain fixed throughout the K Newton iterations; therefore, they do not introduce additional temporal dependencies into the solver, and the same parallel scan formulation remains applicable. During backpropagation, gradients with respect to τi and Ri are computed by the adjoint backpropagation derived above, and gradients are then propagated through gψ using standard automatic differentiation.
