C.4 COMPLETE INTERVENTION RESULTS Table 9 expands the intervention analysis from Section 5.3 by reporting both absolute accuracy and the percentage-point change relative to clean performance. Unlike the main analysis, which is re- stricted to the 2,020 scale- and sampling-invariant items, this table covers all 15 TSRBench tasks. We additionally report two temporal-symmetry transformations, temporal reversal and sign inver- sion. Unlike the other interventions, these can legitimately change the gold answer (e.g., reversing an increasing trend), so they illustrate that invariance is not always desirable: a model that is insensitive to these transformations may be ignoring information the task requires. The full results make the distinct sensitivity profiles particularly clear. Delay only changes by at most 0.8 percentage points under the five affine interventions, including z-score normalization, which is consistent with the invariance induced by its per-series normalization. In contrast, Plot only and our model remain sensitive to transformations of the measurement frame. Delay only and Plot only also exhibit the smallest average degradation under noise and smoothing. Our model is not uniformly the most invariant representation, but retains substantially higher absolute accuracy across all reported interventions. Under changes in sampling horizon, its category-average accuracy is virtually unchanged from clean performance. C.5 ALTERNATIVE SECONDARY REPRESENTATIONS The main model uses a normalized delay representation as the complementary view. To test whether the observed gains follow simply from introducing any second representation, we additionally re- place the delay view with a short-time Fourier transform (STFT) representation while retaining the line-plot stream. We evaluate this alternative both in a controlled setting and after full training. Controlled representation comparison. We first compare delay and STFT representations under the same controlled TSExam setting. Table 10 reports overall accuracy for the secondary represen- tation alone and when combined with the line-plot representation. Both raw and normalized STFT variants are included. The results show that the three secondary representations are similar when combined with the line plot on this evaluation, indicating that TSExam alone does not strongly distinguish among them. Robustness to evaluation-time transformations. We further apply deterministic transformations to the TSExam inputs of the single-representation (“Only”) models from Table 10. As shown in Table 11, the representations exhibit different robustness profiles. The delay embedding and the z- normalized STFT are nearly invariant to scaling and offsets, whereas the raw STFT degrades under mean shifts.
