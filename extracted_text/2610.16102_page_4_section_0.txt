Polanyi’s pianist, who falters when he attends to his fingers (Polanyi 1958, 56), has an exact algebraic counterpart. The trade-off separates contingently tacit structure, which a new query brings out, from regime-bound tacitness, which explication destroys. It also shows why the debate over tacit knowledge has lasted so long: Polanyi, Nonaka and Takeuchi, Tsoukas and Collins each describe part of the substrate correctly and are narrow in a characteristic way; even on the continuum of Nonaka and von Krogh (2009), knowledge conversion cannot tell the two classes apart. 3. A stratified substrate account of Collins’s three tacit knowledges (§5). Rela- tional, somatic and collective tacit knowledge map onto bound structure, substrate valua- tion and value binding, the last absent from the substrate; the two hippocampal pathways give a biological precedent for keeping both readouts. 4. The mentor–student DAG as a tacit-knowledge transmission graph (§6). The Leibnizian hourglass, the Kuhnian shift and the Monastery Wall are reread as events in how tacit knowledge travels, and the Newton–Leibniz case as the historical prototype of the follow-up gap. 5. A diagnosis and a remedy for the follow-up gap (§§7–8). Rootless explicit knowl- edge is defined, current evidence on LLM self-explanation is reviewed, and a three-layer division of labour—LLM generation, VaCoAl/PyVaCoAl trace and confidence, community valuation—is proposed as a way to re-root AI results. Virtue epistemology explains why the third layer stays human: the intellectual virtues exercised there cannot be offloaded to technology (Pritchard 2023). Section 2 reviews the tacit-knowledge debate, the VaCoAl programme and recent claims about tacit knowledge in language models; Section 9 states testable predictions and limitations. We use “tacit” in the structural sense defined in §4: information that shapes retrieval and inference without being represented as a proposition the system can state. Whether a memory substrate can be a knower in Polanyi’s “personal” sense (Polanyi 1958) lies outside our claims. Our claim is narrower and testable.
