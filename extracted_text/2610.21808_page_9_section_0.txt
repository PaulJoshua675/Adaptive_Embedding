RoleQ-T outperforms the tested ternary conversion baselines at matched or lower memory-resident rates, with the largest gains in factual knowledge, which perplexity alone does not capture. Fisher-guided secondary planes further recover knowledge by directing a fraction of a bit to loss-sensitive units. Together, these results offer a practical route to lower memory use and multiplication-free ternary matrix products while preserving useful model capabilities. These findings have a defined scope. The placement rule is supported in a rotated basis; its advantage does not extend to native coordinates. Four Qwen3 sizes and one Llama model do not establish a general scaling trend, and the best uniform placement varies with size and family. Fisher selection’s advantage over random placement narrows with increasing budget in the 1.7B sweep. The gains from adding planes are also generally smaller at 14B than at 8B at the same total rate. At 2 bpw on Llama, QTIP’s trellis codes, alone and with GuidedQuant’s Hessian (Tseng et al., 2024b; Kim et al., 2025), remain ahead (Appendix Table 10), so we do not claim parity with this codebook class. Our evaluations do not cover generation or reasoning, including long chains of thought, where quantization introduces distinct failure modes (Liu et al., 2025a). Our results are weight-only (A16) and measure storage and quality, not inference speed. The ternary inner loop requires no multiplier, codebook or group metadata, making evaluation with lookup-table kernels (Wang et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2025) a natural next step (Appendix A). The broader lesson concerns allocation. At very low precision, both scale sharing and the placement of additional bits shape knowledge retention. Under tight budgets, allocating precision to the right units can matter more than increasing the overall rate. A low-bit format should therefore be judged by both how many bits it spends and where it spends them. 9
