Dexterity from Supervised Play Botao He1,2, Yiming Dou1, Yiannis Aloimonos2, Jitendra Malik1, Akash Sharma1, and Ruoshi Liu1,2 Abstract— How can robots autonomously collect the contact- rich experience needed to learn dexterous manipulation? We present DSP: Dexterity from supervised play, a framework in which a robot learns a play policy from minutes of human play through behavior cloning, then deploys it autonomously to collect hours of interaction data to train a world model. During inference, the same play policy then guides model-predictive control (MPC) by proposing candidate action sequences for evaluation under the learned world model. To accelerate infer- ence, we also introduce a method for distilling a closed-loop, goal-conditioned policy through self-play within the learned world model. We evaluate this framework on underactuated pushing and any-to-any in-hand pose reorientation with a mul- tiﬁngered hand, in simulation and real world. In real world ex- periment, our method outperforms hindsight behavior cloning by 69.6% in success rate and achieves comparable success to reinforcement learning with 490× less interaction data. These results demonstrate that supervised play can autonomously collect interaction data, guide model-based planning, and enable policy learning without any task-speciﬁc demonstrations or sim- to-real transfer.
