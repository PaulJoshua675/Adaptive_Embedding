The benchmark thus establishes both the breadth and the remaining limitations of current 3D verification. What capabilities and supervision are needed to strengthen 3D verification? Our analysis shows that recognizing an object or action does not ensure verification of the decisive attribute, target, or geometric condition. Identity preservation, grasp validity, and motion compliance are particularly weak, while explanation checks reveal that even correct rejections can miss the reference contradiction. These findings motivate supervision that links a decision to a specific fact and its evidence. Motivated also by prior work on training multimodal verifiers (Zhang et al., 2026b), we develop an automated pipeline for large-scale verification data curation, combining multi-view fact extraction, controlled requirement and candidate changes, and native task annotations. With this pipeline, we produce a large-scale dataset with 14,126 samples across diverse tasks, which provides a data foundation for scaling domain-specific verification supervision. How can a 3D generation model reflect by verifying reliably? We examine both selection among existing candidates and recursive refinement of unsatisfied requirements. In human-labeled sixteen-candidate pools, oracle selection improves complete factual success by 63.3 percentage points. In a separate eight-input study, human-guided recursive refinement satisfies all seven requirements for every input, compared with three under parallel Oracle@8. Together, these results demonstrate substantial downstream headroom for reliable verification, connecting the capability gap and data foundation to the goal of reflection in 3D generation. 2
