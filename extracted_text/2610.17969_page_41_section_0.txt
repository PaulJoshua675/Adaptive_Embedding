H.6 Task-specific and audit protocols Parameter-gauge audit predictions. GCT nondimensionalizes every probed output. The architecture applies C to q, k, and the gate preactivation, and applies C ◦P ⊥to both branch proposals. All seven roles are null. Normalizing every weight row in nGPT’s forward pass makes all roles null. QK-Norm applies per-head RMSNorm only to q and k. In NormFormer, LayerNorm after the attention out-projection types v and the out-projection. NormFormer’s mid-MLP LayerNorm acts after the GELU, leaving the GELU’s input scale uncorrected. Pre-LN, LLaMA-style, and LayerScale have no nulls because none places a nondimensionalizer between a probed matrix and the residual stream. In evaluation mode, UCT’s live-RMS residual adapter types both branch outputs, covering v, the attention output, the up-projection, and the MLP output. The q, k, and gate interfaces retain radial sensitivity because the corrections at these interfaces use frozen EMA statistics. Parameter-gauge audit protocol. We use fp32 evaluation mode and the first 8 fixed validation batches. One backward pass yields every ⟨W, ∇W L⟩, with dot products accumulated in 64-bit floating-point precision and reported as the maximum over layers for each role. A central finite difference at the middle layer uses c = e±0.1 and cross-checks two roles per architecture. This secant over ±0.1 log-units includes curvature. For nonnull cells, the secant checks the sign and order of magnitude, while for null cells, the pairing and secant both vanish. The identical audit runs on the five trained 1B models, yielding 34 cells because UCT is absent at 1B and Pre-LN’s GELU MLP has no up-projection. Blocks that realize the same activation rescaling must carry equal pairings. The q and k rows both rescale the logits, the v rows and out-projection both rescale the attention write, and the up- and down-projections both rescale the MLP write. The measured columns agree, providing an internal consistency check on the pairing measurement. For stop-gradient scale estimators, as in UCT with batch statistics active, the finite difference and the autograd pairing measure function invariance and the optimizer-visible gradient, respectively. The two measurements are reported separately in App. I.2. PINN details. Both problems use the standard two-term split between the PDE residual and combined supervision to give the adaptive balancers a well-conditioned pair. Burgers uses Nf = 104 uniformly sampled interior points and combines 200 initial-condition points with 200 boundary-condition points into one supervision term. The Burgers reference is computed by Cole–Hopf with 100-node Gauss–Hermite quadrature on a 256 × 101 grid. Helmholtz uses Nf = 104, 400 boundary points, and the manufactured solution sin(πx) sin(4πy). The adaptive baselines follow the published method specifications. LRA follows Wang et al. (2021). Every 10 steps, λsup tracks ∥∇θLpde∥/∥∇θLsup∥as an EMA with weight 0.1, clamped to [10−2, 102]. Uncertainty weighting optimizes P i e−siLi/2 + si/2. NTK balancing follows Wang et al. (2022). Every 100 steps, λk := tr(K)/ tr(Kk), with tr(Kk) estimated as Nk× the mean squared parameter-gradient norm of 16 randomly sampled per-point residuals of term k.
