Every hidden state of a language model has a na- tive reference frame, fixed at the two ends of the residual stream and adaptive in between, and lex- ical reference frames approximate it to a stated order without fitting to the model’s output. In two model families LEXFRAME[1] recovers the model’s lexical readouts and output from mid-depth on, be- yond the reach of any method with one map per layer, and it attributes and steers predictions. The frame explains why a fixed lens fails at mid-depth, increasingly so along training, and why the point cloud reveals little about how the model uses a state. To interpret a state, read it as the model does: through the layers that remain, with the context it sees, and in its own native semantics, a reading that can stand in for lenses. 9
