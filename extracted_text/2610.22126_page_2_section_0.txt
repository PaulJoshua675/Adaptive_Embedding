Some approaches have been proposed to incorporate feature dependencies into attribution methods. Methods based on Shapley values, such as conditional SHAP [Aas et al., 2021] and causal Shapley values [Heskes et al., 2020], can account for dependencies but rely on sampling-based computations that become prohibitively expensive in high-dimensional settings. Conversely, gradient-based meth- ods like integrated gradients (IG) [Sundararajan et al., 2017] and expected gradients [Erion et al., 2021] offer computational efficiency but fundamentally assume feature independence. To address this gap, we propose causal integrated gradients (CIG) and causal expected gradients (CEG), principled extensions of integrated gradients that incorporate causal structure among input features. Like causal Shapley values [Heskes et al., 2020], our methods require the structure of a causal graph over the inputs; unlike them, we need only a deterministic, off-the-shelf surrogate rather than a full conditional distribution to sample from. Conditional SHAP needs no causal graph but does need that conditional distribution, while standard integrated and expected gradients need neither. In return for this graph, our methods keep the efficiency of integrated gradients and, under conditions on the causal graph and surrogate, its axiomatic guarantees. They scale to high-dimensional settings, recovering upstream causal drivers that graph-agnostic methods miss, as in gene regulation (Figure 1). The main contributions of this paper are as follows: • We propose causal integrated and expected gradients (CIG and CEG), extending integrated gradients to incorporate causal structure among input features. Both methods are applicable to any differentiable model for which causal dependencies among inputs are available. • We evaluate the proposed methods on a synthetic benchmark with known causal effects and compare against the state-of-the-art (causal Shapley values [Heskes et al., 2020] and condi- tional SHAP [Aas et al., 2021]), demonstrating improved causal attribution performance at substantially lower computational cost — reducing runtimes from hours to minutes. • We demonstrate the real-world applicability of our method on two high-dimensional single- cell datasets: identifying transcription factors driving cell differentiation in simulated data, and prioritizing transcription factors that are most sensitive to interferon-β stimulation. Across both tasks, our method outperforms existing approaches in recovering causal tran- scription factors, while scaling efficiently to high-dimensional RNA expression data. 2
