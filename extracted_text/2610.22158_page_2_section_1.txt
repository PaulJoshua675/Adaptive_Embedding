2.1 Unanswerable and Ambiguous Queries in NL2SQL Prior work has explored unanswerable and ambigu- ous queries in NL2SQL. TriageSQL (Zhang et al., 2020) formulates the problem as question intention classification, while Know What I Don’t Know (Wang et al., 2023) further categorizes ambiguity and unanswerability cases. More recent work ex- tends beyond classification-oriented formulations. TrustSQL (Lee et al., 2024) introduces infeasible queries and proposes a reliability-oriented evalua- tion metric for trustworthy NL2SQL systems. Com- plementarily, NL2SQL-BUGs (Liu et al., 2025) focuses on errors in NL2SQL translation and cat- egorizes semantic errors into fine-grained types. However, NL2SQL still lacks a fine-grained and comprehensive examination of diverse premise er- rors and the varying challenges they pose to models. PremiseSQL addresses this gap through a unified premise-error taxonomy. 2.2 Robustness and False Premise Handling in LLMs Beyond NL2SQL, prior work studies LLM robust- ness under adversarial, noisy, or logically flawed inputs. For example, SQuADRUN (Rajpurkar et al., 2018) introduces unanswerable questions, high- lighting models’ tendency to produce confident but incorrect responses when no valid answer exists. AbstentionBench (Kirichenko et al., 2025) provides a large-scale benchmark across 20 diverse datasets to assess abstention behavior.
