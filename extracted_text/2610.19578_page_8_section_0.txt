However, when a novel item 𝑖new ∈Icold enters the system during inference, traditional GCF models encounter a dual structural failure. First, due to the constraints of the transductive parameterized lookup table mechanism, un- seen items 𝑖new lack pre-learned ID embeddings. This results in an immediate representation void in the initial collaborative space, causing a collapse in model performance. Second, the absence of historical interactions implies that the degree of 𝑖new in the interaction graph G is strictly zero. Consequently, the corresponding row and column vectors in the normalized adja- cency matrix ˜A are null, leading to a structural fracture in the topology-dependent neighborhood aggregation mechanism. As a result, cold-start items are significantly marginalized during vector retrieval. As illustrated in Table 1, on the Amazon-Book2023 dataset, pure ID- driven models such as NGCF and LightGCN experience a catastrophic collapse of over 97% in Recall@20 and NDCG@20 metrics when transitioning from warm items to strict cold-start scenarios. Since topological isolation severs the acquisition of collaborative signals for cold items, leveraging graph-independent prior knowledge to bridge the representation gap becomes es- sential.
