The fine-tuned models’ wrong answers follow the Pre-linearity probe more than they follow Recall (Figure 4). We credit each error on a held-out subject to the expla- nations that name its wrong answer, split equally when several do. The explanations are the three answers the probe ranks highest, the three the pretrained model scores highest (the ranking behind Recall), and three others defined in Appendix G. An explanation can name a wrong answer by co- incidence, so we compare each share with two baselines: the same attribution with each fine-tuned model’s wrong answers shuffled among themselves, and with each wrong answer replaced by a uni- formly random wrong label of its relation. The shuffle keeps which wrong labels a model favors and breaks only their link to the subject, which makes it the stricter baseline. The probe’s three highest-ranked answers take 21.5% of the errors, against 8.8% shuffled; the pretrained model’s three highest-ranked answers take 11.5%, against 7.3%. The correlations above compare relations; this analysis looks inside them, at single subjects. There too, the fine-tuned models follow the probe, which suggests that fine-tuning builds on the structure the probe reads. 6
