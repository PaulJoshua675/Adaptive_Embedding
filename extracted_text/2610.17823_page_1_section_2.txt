Modern recommender systems are typically optimized to maximize ranking or accuracy metrics such as NDCG and Recall. However, this optimization often leads to popularity bias, the systematic over-exposure of head items and under-representation of the long tail [14, 22]. As a result, recommendations tend to concentrate on already popular content, limiting diversity, reinforcing exposure feedback loops, and providing a suboptimal experience for users with niche interests. The problem is particularly relevant in sequen- tial recommendations, where next-item prediction is performed over large, highly imbalanced catalogs using standard likelihood- based training objectives. A range of approaches has been proposed to mitigate popularity bias, including re-weighting or re-sampling training data [9, 10, 36], incorporating regularization terms that penalize exposure imbal- ance [1, 6, 47], applying logit adjustment based on item priors [7, 27], and post-processing ranking lists to promote long-tail items [21, 47]. While these methods can reduce the dominance of head items in recommendations, they often introduce a trade-off between bias reduction and relevance. In practice, suppressing popular items may lead to degraded ranking accuracy, as popularity is often cor- related with relevance signal. Consequently, mitigating popularity bias without sacrificing recommendation performance remains a challenging problem. We propose PR-entmax, a method for reducing popularity bias in sequential recommendation models that are typically trained with objectives based on softmax or its sampled variants [18, 38, 39, 42]. Our central idea is to make popularity correction context-dependent.
