Autoregressive language models generate text by predicting tokens sequentially. Continuous diffusion language models (CDLMs) offer an alternative: they jointly refine continuous representations of a token sequence through iterative denoising or flow dynamics, allowing multiple token positions to be updated in parallel. Recent studies have explored a range of representations, training objectives, and generative dynamics, including one-hot flows, learned token embeddings, and latent-space models (Lee et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026; Hu et al., 2026; Meshchaninov et al., 2026; Guo et al., 2026; Liang et al., 2026). Despite their different formulations, these models share a central computational component: a neural backbone that maps noisy continuous states to predictions for language generation. Much of the progress in CDLMs has focused on the formulation and learning of continuous generative processes, while the Transformer architectures that implement them have received comparatively less systematic attention.
