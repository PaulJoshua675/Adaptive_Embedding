By inspecting failed trajectories, the first author identified three failure modes and developed an annotation codebook specifying operational definitions, inclusion criteria, and exclusion rules. For lack of temporal awareness (RT-Deliberate), we annotated failures under TL and TH to examine whether models accounted for the temporal cost of deliberation under standardized token-based timing; Paused Clock removes this cost, whereas Wall Clock additionally reflects deployment- dependent latency. For failure to track the evolving state (RT-Perceive) and misjudging whether action is needed (RT-Act), we annotated failures under PH, which pauses the environment during generation and uses high reasoning effort, allowing us to examine state tracking and action necessity judgments without environment changes during inference. We then prompted Codex to apply the codebook to the recorded action trajectories, timing information, available model reasoning, and observations. All failed trajectories in the selected conditions were screened, with targeted inspec- tion of video frames to verify candidate events rather than exhaustive frame-by-frame review. The second author checked the resulting annotations against the source evidence. C.2 ANNOTATION CODEBOOK. Prompt 1: Failure-mode annotation codebook ANNOTATION SCOPE Annotate valid failed episodes from the selected runs. Assess RT-Deliberate only in TL and TH, where charged output tokens advance simulated task time. Assess RT-Perceive and RT-Act only in PH. An episode–category pair outside these settings is outside the selected annotation scope, not a negative judgment. FAILURE MODES 1. Lack of temporal awareness (RT-Deliberate). Generation latency is mismatched to the avail- able response window, consuming time needed to observe, act, or complete a required se- quence. Qualifying evidence includes generation that exhausts a model-visible or task-stated deadline, leaves insufficient time for necessary subsequent steps, or delays a response to a vis- ibly evolving hazard. Cumulative generation costs may qualify when they contribute before the opportunity becomes irrecoverable. A timeout, long generation, or final rejected action alone is insufficient. Do not infer a usable response window solely from hidden backend timing when the visible deadline has already expired. Short generation does not qualify merely because more time remained. Distinguish charged output tokens from reasoning tokens. Generation-related latency does not apply in PH; explicit waiting there is assessed under RT-Act. 2. Failure to track the evolving state (RT-Perceive). The model fails to incorporate task- relevant current information and subsequently behaves according to a stale or incorrect state. Examples include overlooking an updated request, reverting to superseded preferences, misidentifying the currently active game object, and incorrectly assuming that a workflow has completed. Both external changes and action-induced interface changes qualify. 20
