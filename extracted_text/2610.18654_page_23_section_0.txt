Together with local context, these blocks retain 87.6–97.3% of the dense attention mass while accessing only 31.5% of the original-text positions. Continued pretraining of Hybrid KSA. Be- cause our adapted models receive additional training while the reported baselines do not, we examine whether continued pretraining of the unchanged Hybrid KSA backbone would provide a stronger quality reference. Table 11 reports a full-parameter continuation experiment from the same 128K checkpoint, using a global batch size of 256, a peak learning rate of 4 × 10−4, and a warmup–stable–decay schedule. Across five eval- uated checkpoints covering 5.7–51.3B additional tokens, the six-length RULER mean remains be- low the original checkpoint’s 72.60. The 128K score improves at 28.5B tokens but falls back at later checkpoints. Under the tested recipe and data budgets, continued pretraining does not yield a consistent improvement in Hybrid KSA’s RULER performance. This supports using the original checkpoint as the quality reference for assessing whether architectural adaptation can preserve its capabilities at lower inference cost.
