Clinical Syndromes and Involved Mechanisms The dominant imaging-supported syndrome is a unilateral, benign-appearing retroareolar breast mass. This is confirmed by the sonographic findings of a well-circumscribed, oval, hypoechoic, mobile mass with smooth margins, through-transmission, parallel orientation, and minimal peripheral flow (DOI: 10.1056/NEJMcpc1503831, Figure 1). The mechanism involves hormone-sensitive proliferation of breast tissue; however, the discrete, nodular morphology suggests a localized fibroepithelial proliferation (such as a fibroadenoma) rather than the diffuse ductal and stromal proliferation typical of gynecomastia. A second relevant syndrome is early adrenarche, supported by the presence of fine, straight, lightly pigmented pubic hairs and scant axillary hairs at age 8, alongside prepubertal testes (3 mL) and a prepubertal phallus (DOI: 10.1056/NEJMcpc1503831). This is realized through early adrenal androgen production, which can be peripherally aromatized to estrogen, potentially stimulating localized breast tissue growth. The concordance of bone age with chronologic age (8 years) argues against a long-standing, advanced systemic sex-steroid effect. Considered but rejected is true central precocious puberty, as the testes remain prepubertal in volume and the bone age is not advanced. Arguments against: rare in prepubertal males. What would confirm: histology showing biphasic proliferation of stroma and epithelium. Diagnostic and Treatment Tactics Diagnostics: The primary goal is to confirm the benign nature of the mass and exclude rare pathologic hormone sources. Scrotal ultrasound with color Doppler is the next essential imaging step to exclude a small, non-palpable estrogen-secreting testicular tumor (e.g., Leydig cell tumor). If scrotal ultrasound is normal, adrenal imaging (MRI or contrast-enhanced CT) should be considered if pending laboratory values (Table 1) suggest an adrenal source of estrogen excess. Follow-up breast ultrasound in 3 to 6 months is recommended to document interval stability; development of irregular margins, increased vascularity, or calcifications would prompt ultrasound-guided core biopsy. Treatment: Conservative observation is the appropriate initial approach given the benign imaging phenotype.
