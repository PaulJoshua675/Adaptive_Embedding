Figure 2 displays the relative velocity deviation ϵ = ∥vPCN −vFFN∥/∥vFFN∥as a function of γ, for our replication of the CIFAR-10 Energy Matching model (Balcerak et al., 2025). The deviation decays proportional to γ over six decades, from γ = 0.1 down to γ = 10−7, in both its direction and magnitude components (App. F), as predicted by the derivation in App. A. At small γ each arithmetic saturates at its own precision floor (App. F); the highest is the ∼2 × 10−4 of the TF32 tensor-core mode our runs use. Above γ ∼10−2 the curves coincide, the finite-γ bias having grown past every floor. At the γ = 10−4 of our CIFAR-10 runs the float64 total relative deviation is 1.3 × 10−5 (a direction cosine similarity of cos(vFFN, vPCN) = 1 to nine decimal places), which lies below the TF32 floor. The correspondence error for our runs is therefore dominated by standard GPU arithmetic precision rather than by the finite-γ bias of the relaxation, so smaller γ buys no accuracy in this arithmetic. Repeating these measurements on the authors’ released ImageNet-32 checkpoints gives the same behaviour (App. G), so the correspondence is not specific to this dataset or trained potential.
