Multilingual dense retrievers place all lan- guages in one shared space so that a question finds relevant documents, regardless of which language they are written in. We show that the same question, asked in different languages, re- trieves substantially different documents, and this disagreement is not because of weak lan- guage understanding. Each language retrieves well on its own and ranks candidates consis- tently with the other languages; divergence ap- pears only when languages share an index. We trace the mechanism: each language keeps its own ranking, but in the shared index, its correct answers are pushed far down, buried under non- relevant documents that are either in the query’s own language or in English, depending on the model. Because this disagreement is structured, it can also serve as a correction signal. We in- troduce disagreement-guided steering: for each language, we contrast the documents that other languages agree on for a question against those that language retrieves idiosyncratically, and take the difference of their representations as a language-specific direction, added to that lan- guage’s queries at inference. Against baselines of language-mean removal, subspace removal, and per-language rotation, it most strongly re- duces same-language crowding while remain- ing competitive on recall, trading off against mean removal, which better preserves ranking quality. 1
