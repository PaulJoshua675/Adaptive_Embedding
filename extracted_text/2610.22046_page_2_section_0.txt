A final label does not distinguish these failure types, and a model’s stated confidence is an unreliable guide to correctness: instruction-tuned models asked for a confidence score often assign high scores to incorrect answers [Zhao et al., 2026a]. A monitor therefore needs to identify the failure type and, for a trace that becomes unrecoverable, the step at which recovery is lost. A trace is the reasoning the model generates in one attempt at a problem, and a prefix is a trace up to a given step. A prefix is recoverable if a correct answer remains reachable from it. Monte Carlo rollouts from each evaluated prefix estimate the probability that a continuation from it reaches a correct answer. An incorrect trace is termed a capability gap if it is unrecoverable from the initial problem state, and a near miss if it remains recoverable at every evaluated prefix. A PIT is an incorrect trace whose first unrecoverable prefix occurs partway through [cf. Setlur et al., 2024]; the step that ends that prefix is its PIT step. We test the ability of hidden-state probes to distinguish these failure types and locate the PIT step without additional rollouts at deployment time. Hidden states carry information about the truth of a statement or an answer [Azaria and Mitchell, 2023, Marks and Tegmark, 2024, Kadavath et al., 2022], and a probe on the hidden states of a language model can estimate the correctness of its reasoning steps [Ni et al., 2026]. Final labels record only final-answer correctness and do not directly supervise the failure type or the PIT step. We train probes on the hidden states at a fixed set of layers, the read site, with three kinds of supervision: final labels; rollout rates, the fraction of rollouts from a prefix that reach a correct answer; and the PIT step itself (Table 3). We evaluate Qwen3 in thinking mode at 4B and 8B on hard mathematics problems from DeepMath- 103k [He et al., 2026] and the training split of Hendrycks MATH [Hendrycks et al., 2021]. We use this model family because it provides open-weight reasoning models at two scales with a thinking mode [Yang et al., 2025]. Appendix B gives the difficulty levels, the sampling parameters, and the seed and interval conventions of each measurement. Findings. 1. The confidence probe, trained on final labels, misses capability gaps and PITs more often than near misses. At the final step, the probe predicts the final label and remains calibrated at both model scales, but it misses 81% of PITs and 47% of near misses at 8B; a trace is missed when its predicted correctness at the final step is at or above a cutoff of 0.5. After controls for four variables of the trace and the problem, the difference between PITs and near misses exceeds the prespecified minimum effect of 0.08 at 8B. At 4B the difference is nonzero and below the minimum effect (Section 3). 2. The combined probe reverses the failure-type ordering. The combined probe, trained on rollout rates of incorrect traces, reads the same hidden states at the same read site. At 8B the confidence probe gives a randomly chosen PIT a higher final-step score than a randomly chosen near miss with probability 0.76 (the AUROC of PITs against near misses), and the combined probe does so with probability 0.40. The two probes differ in training labels, attention mask, step weighting and training schedule, so the reversal cannot be attributed to the labels alone (Section 3). 3. At 8B, a failure-type classifier on transcoder activations meets the margin. The failure- type classifier reads the transcoder activations of one completed incorrect trace per problem and separates problems labelled capability gap from problems labelled PIT. It exceeds the strongest tested baseline that uses no hidden states by 0.055 AUROC at 8B and 0.049 at 4B, so the 8B result meets the prespecified margin of 0.05 and the 4B result does not (Section 3). 4. No single probe exceeds the length rule at locating the PIT step. The length rule predicts a late step from trace length alone. At 4B, the pointer probe, trained to predict the PIT step, exceeds the length rule in 0 of 10 runs on identical held-out PITs. The ensemble, the average of the five pointer probes, exceeds the length rule under one of the two weightings of its failure-type loss. At 8B only the value probe, trained on rollout rates of recoverable traces, was tested, and it is below the length rule in each of five training seeds (Section 4).
