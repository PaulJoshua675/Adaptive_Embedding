Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) provides a non-invasive view of large-scale functional brain organization and its relationships with cognition, behavior, and disease. As large multi-site fMRI collections continue to grow, self-supervised pretraining offers a path toward transferable brain representations without requiring downstream task labels during pretraining (Ortega Caro et al., 2024; Dong et al., 2024; Gijsen et al., 2026). A common route from fMRI measurements to brain-network representations is to parcellate the brain into regions of interest (ROIs), extract a regional BOLD time series from each ROI, and estimate the statistical dependence between regional signals. Pearson correlation organizes these pairwise dependencies into a compact and interpretable functional connectivity (FC) matrix. By explicitly representing interregional functional coupling, FC has become a widely used input for brain-network modeling and connectome-based pretraining (Kan et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2026a). An observed Pearson-FC matrix, however, is not a sampling-independent brain graph but a scan-level statistical estimate formed from a finite BOLD sequence.
