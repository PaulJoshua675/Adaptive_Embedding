Between pretraining and post-training, frontier coding models such as Qwen3-Coder-Next (Cao et al., 2026), GLM-5 (GLM-5 Team, 2026), Kimi-Dev (Yang et al., 2026), and Composer 2 (Cursor Research, 2026) include a midtraining stage of hundreds of billions of tokens of agentic, long- context data on top of a converged base model. Technical reports describe this stage at a high level, but rarely explain how its data is constructed, filtered, and weighted, or measure the impact of each design choice. Following Qwen-Coder (Hui et al., 2024; Cao et al., 2026), we present an open midtraining recipe for agentic coding, developed and demonstrated on Nemotron base models. We describe how each data source is built, and we report the ablations that guided our training choices. Base models are usually pretrained at short sequence lengths, typically 4K to 8K tokens for compute efficiency (DeepSeek-AI, 2024; Qwen Team, 2025), and then extended to long context in a short fi- nal stage before SFT. Nemotron models, for example, are pretrained at 8K and extended to 1M. The data that matters most for software-engineering agents does not fit this pipeline. Pretraining cor- pora usually contain almost no agent interactions, as in the Nemotron pretraining corpus (NVIDIA, 2025), and the natural data around software engineering, such as whole repositories, code evolution through commit histories, pull requests, and build logs, often runs to tens or hundreds of thousands of tokens, too long for pretraining and too large for a short long-context stage. Adding agent traces to pretraining is also not straightforward, since pretraining typically applies no loss masking, and it is unclear how the tool responses (environment outputs) in those traces should be handled.
