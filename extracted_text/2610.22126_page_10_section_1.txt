We introduced causal integrated gradients and causal expected gradients, which extend integrated gradients to incorporate known causal structure among input features. They apply to any differentiable model and, under conditions on the causal graph and surrogate, retain the axiomatic guarantees of integrated gradients. On a synthetic benchmark, CIG best matches ground-truth causal effects at a fraction of the cost of causal Shapley values; on simulated and real single-cell data, it best recovers the transcription factors driving the response while scaling to thousands of genes. Ethics statement This work uses only public, de-identified data: the real perturbation dataset [Kang et al., 2018] was collected and published by others, and no new human-subject data was collected for this study. The attributions produced by our method are intended to prioritize candidate causal genes for downstream experimental follow-up, not to be interpreted as definitive causal or clinical claims; any prioritized targets should be validated experimentally before drawing biological or clinical conclusions. Reproducibility statement Our implementation of CIG/CEG and the code for the synthetic CATE benchmark (Section 4.1) are available at https://github.com/sifrimlab/CausalIG. Full implementation and training details for the Dyngen and IFN-β experiments — including preprocessing, hyperparameters, and dataset statistics — are given in Appendix E and the corresponding experiment-specific appendices (Appendices G and H); both the Dyngen simulator and the IFN-β dataset [Kang et al., 2018] are themselves publicly available. Complete proofs of all theoretical claims, including the axiomatic properties and complexity analysis, are provided in Appendices C and D.
