B Overview Figure 3 summarises our approach. Given each agent’s objective and a target solution concept, we compile the path formulas into reward automata and form the product stochastic game. We train a single joint profile using model-free MARL, with optional counterfactual replay, and evaluate its satisfaction probabilities and NashConv≈. C Environments We consider 8 benchmark instances drawn from five environment families. Table 2 summarises the complete suite. Figure 4 shows the spatial environments. Observations. Agents observe structured symbolic grids: each grid records, cell by cell, the identity of one layer of the scene (terrain, avatars and their orientations, coins, apples, claims, beams, etc), alongside scalar side channels. The grids are flattened and scaled to [0, 1], and the index of the agent’s reward-automaton state is appended alongside spatial coordinates. C.1 Stochastic Pursuit-Evasion We provide a stochastic variation of the Pursuit Evasion environment in the literature [56], which is also referred to as Guard Gridworld. The slippery gridworld is instantiated with one guard, player0, and either one or two intruders. In the classic task, the guard satisfies G¬goal_reached, whereas every intruder satisfies Fgoal_reached ∧G¬collision_ever. In the capture task, the guard satisfies Fguard_intruder_collision, whereas each intruder satisfies G¬guard_intruder_collision ∧ G¬collision_ever. Each move is subject to a role-specific slip probability, so the environment remains stochastic under a fixed joint policy. Handcrafted rewards.
