Few-shot prompting lets users specify model behavior through demonstrations, without updating model parameters (Brown et al., 2020; Garg et al., 2022). This is useful for test-time guidance, especially when instructions or rewards are difficult to specify (Kwon et al., 2023; Zamfirescu- Pereira et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023). For instance, in editing by example, a demonstrated formatting change or API migration can illustrate a transformation to apply elsewhere (Gupta et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2024). When a demonstrated edit must be applied at multiple sites, a natural approach is to generate the tokens in place within a shared canvas containing all demonstrations and relevant context. We call this multi-target few-shot prompting. However, causal autoregressive models generate outputs as continuations, making it awkward to infill a target site using right-hand-side context. Incorporating such context requires rearranging the prompt or using specialized infilling formats (Bavarian et al., 2022; Fried et al., 2022), a process that becomes more cumbersome when editing multiple sites. Moreover, pretrained associations and sensitivity to prompt design can interfere with the model’s adherence to demonstrations (Min et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2021). Although guidance methods have been developed to mitigate these issues, they are primarily designed for autoregressive models and do not directly apply to in-place editing (Peng et al., 2025; Shi et al., 2024b). Diffusion language models (DLMs) offer a natural interface for these tasks because they are not restricted to next-token prediction and can generate tokens anywhere on the shared canvas (Austin et al., 2021; Sahoo et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024a). As illustrated in Figure 1, the missing output regions are initially masked and progressively filled, while demonstrations and surrounding context remain visible. Each generated token can therefore condition on both left and right context, avoiding the specialized prompting harnesses often needed in causal autoregressive generation (Ou et al., 2025). Moreover, jointly infilling multiple sites allows tokens revealed at one site to inform subsequent predictions at others, helping maintain consistency (Kim et al., 2026). Yet several challenges remain in using DLMs for multi-target few-shot prompting. First, infilling requires allocating masked positions before generation, even though the required output lengths are generally unknown (Xu et al., 2026a; Zhang et al., 2025). Second, although DLMs condition on demonstrations, they may still fail to follow them reliably. Context-aware decoding suggests that contrasting predictions across contexts could strengthen adherence to demonstrations (Shi et al., 2024b; Whitammer et al., 2022), but extending such guidance to complete DLM infills requires joint likelihoods that are often intractable or decoder-specific (Turok et al., 2026).
