It is notewor- thy that each candidate neighborhood node ui, will further be passed through the causal reasoning mod- ule C before the agent chooses its next action. 3.2 Causal Reasoning Module Neighbors retrieval identifies nodes that are struc- turally adjacent and textually similar to the current exploration state. However, neither graph adja- cency nor textual similarity guarantees that a can- didate node provides evidence that materially con- tributes to answering the query. Therefore we in- troduce a causal reasoning module C that evaluates each candidate according to its consequentiality and essentiality for resolving the information need. Given the candidate set Nt = {u1, . . . , uk} re- turned by the Neighbors tool, the module receives the query Q, the current node vt, the candidate nodes Nt, their connecting relations, and the accu- mulated paths Pt+1. Unlike literal causal reasoning that seeks cause-effect relations in the underlying domain, our notion of causal relevance is retrieval- oriented: evidence is considered causally relevant when its presence or absence materially affects the ability to support an answer to Q. This opera- tionalization is motivated by two complementary properties: consequentiality and essentiality. For each candidate ui, the module C estimates a consequentiality score ci ∈[0, 1] and an essential- ity score ei ∈[0, 1]. Consequentiality asks whether incorporating the candidate node and its associated hop materially changes the evidence available for answering the query. It captures the candidate’s in- cremental answer contribution, rather than simply its textual similarity. The LLM is therefore asked whether adding ui, together with its relation to vt, introduces informa- tion that substantially strengthens or advances the answer. Essentiality instead asks whether removing the candidate evidence from the currently available evidence would materially reduce the ability to justify an answer. More directly, the LLM is prompted to reason counterfactually: if this candidate node and its con- necting relation were unavailable, would an im- portant part of the evidence needed to answer the query be lost? A high ei therefore indicates that the candidate carries information that is difficult to replace with evidence already contained in the current trajectory. We combine these two judgments into a node- level causal relevance score: caui = λci + (1 −λ)ei, (3) where λ ∈[0, 1] controls the relative emphasis on consequentiality and essentiality. Importantly, caui should not be interpreted as a probability that a literal causal relationship exists between vt and ui. Rather, it measures whether the candidate evidence has a materially incremental and counterfactual contribution to answering Q. Causal relevance alone, however, evaluates a can- didate primarily with respect to the query and the evidence currently available. In multi-hop retrieval, a locally useful candidate may still lead the agent away from the reasoning trajectory developed so far. We therefore introduce a separate path consistency score pi ∈[0, 1]. Unlike caui, which measures the node-level contribution to answering the query, pi evaluates whether the transition vt →ui forms a coherent continuation of the accumulated path developed so far. In particular, the module exam- ines whether the relation connecting vt and ui is compatible with the information need represented by the current trajectory and whether following this candidate preserves a plausible chain of evidence toward the answer. The final score for candidate node ui is: st(ui) = αcaui + (1 −α)pi, (4) where α ∈[0, 1] controls the relative importance of node-level causal relevance and the trajectory-level consistency. The highest-scoring candidates are returned to the retrieval agent. Importantly, the causal reasoning module C is an assessment module rather than an autonomous agent. It does not independently invoke Glob- alSearch or Neighbors. Instead, its assessments enhance the information presented to the retrieval agent and therefore influence subsequent search de- cisions. Based on the causal and path-consistency assessments, the agent may continue along a promising neighborhood path with Neighbors, re- formulate the subquery, modify node or relation filters, re-anchor through GlobalSearch, Append nodes to the retrieved set, or terminate with Finish when additional exploration is unlikely to provide consequential evidence. In this way, causal rea- soning affects not merely the reranking of already retrieved nodes, but the direction and termination of the retrieval process itself. 4
