This is equivalent to projecting the gradient and, after each step, re-projecting the total change from the initial anchor, W −Wref. To isolate this choice, we fine-tune Qwen2.5-0.5B for 60 Adam steps on a fixed eight-example next-token prediction task. We use r=0.99 and apply the constraint to the attention and MLP linear maps. Because the task is deliberately easy, every method reaches a final training loss of approximately 10−5. We can therefore compare their collateral effects using two measurements: the increase in NLL on held-out generic sentences, and the fraction of the total weight change that falls outside the allowed free-to-free block. Our results are in Figure 4. Unconstrained fine-tuning increases the held-out NLL by 13.0, with 98% of the weight change entering protected directions. Projecting the gradient reduces the NLL increase to 0.72, but Adam reintroduces substantial leakage: 69% of the final change still leaks into the protected directions. Re-projecting the cumulative change eliminates leakage, but raises the held-out NLL by 3.28. Projecting the applied update is the only scheme that both eliminates leakage and keeps the probe nearly unchanged, with an NLL increase of just 0.13. We therefore design SST to project the final applied update. The full procedure is given in Algorithm 1 in Appendix B.8. 2.3 Refreshing the Protected Basis The default variant computes Uk, Vk once from the pretrained weights and keeps them fixed. During longer runs, the leading singular subspaces of the current weights can rotate away from this initial basis. SST can therefore re-anchor Wref to the current weights and recompute Uk, Vk from it every K optimizer steps, using a Gram-based eigendecomposition for efficiency (Appendix B.13 discusses the minimal time overhead of SST compared to SFT). A refresh defines the free complement relative to the current weights and opens new directions for adaptation. As the singular vectors rotate, a direction protected by the initial basis can acquire a component in a later free complement. Refresh can improve continued task learning, but it mildly weakens protection relative to the original model because SST now preserves a sequence of updated principal subspaces rather than one fixed pretrained subspace. We therefore treat K as an adaptation–retention control and report no refresh, R100 (K=100), and R10 (K=10) through many of our experiments. 5
