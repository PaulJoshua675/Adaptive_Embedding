Please refer to the Supplementary for more details about architecture (section A), task definitions (section C), baseline implementation (section D), and compute efficiency comparison (section E.4). 4.2 SIMULATION EXPERIMENTS Single-Factor Environmental Variation. We first evaluate T2-Z under individual unobserved environmental factors, spanning continuous physical properties, mass-dependent interactions, and discrete human-defined rules. As shown in table 1, T2-Z achieves the highest action success on all five tasks, increasing the five-task average from 36.9% for the Standard World Model to 76.6%, while reducing average LPIPS from 0.0469 to 0.0325. It also substantially improves task-relevant prediction, reducing mean error from 11.06 to 3.85 pixels on Friction, 21.93 to 6.45 pixels on Mass Collision, and 1.18◦to 0.64◦on Mass Balance. These gains arise because T2-Z directly optimizes an environment latent from observed interactions, capturing environment-specific variation more flexibly than non-adaptive or amortized baselines. 7
