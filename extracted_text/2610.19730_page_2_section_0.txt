This article aims to design and analyze different degrees of temporal representation in neural networks. This per- spective enables a systematic evaluation of how the degree of temporal representation affects both performance and computational efficiency. Experiments on demanding event-based datasets show that increasing the degree of temporal representation improves both performance and computational efficiency (Perez-Nieves et al., 2021; Fang et al., 2021; Bittar and Garner, 2022; Yu et al., 2026). Indeed, to solve a temporal dataset, models typically need neurons able to integrate information on different timescales. For example, on an audio signal, there is information on the millisecond timescale (pitch periods and formant transitions), while phonems need tenth of milliseconds to occur, and syllables and words a few hundreds. Moreover, it allows the analysis of the quantitative differences in computational trajectories induced by temporal versus non-temporal activation functions. We show that, if two activation functions can be mapped together, it is not a sufficient condition to consider the two functions to be of the same nature (spatial vs temporal). More specifically, we study two activation functions, one temporal and one spatial and show a mapping between the two after learning. However, we show that the trajectories of the two networks during learning take different paths. To study this continuum of temporal degrees, we compare three activation mechanisms under a fixed deep archi- tecture and fixed event-based datasets: (i) weighted-sum activations used in conventional ANNs; (ii) binary spiking ac- tivations used in SNNs; and (iii) spike-time–delayed activations used in Delay Neural Networks (DeNNs) (Gattepaille and Muzy, 2025). This isolates the effect of explicit temporal variables in the network functions from architectural confounds. Analyzing the temporal nature of learning functions, through the gradient-based update rule, is challenging due to the entanglement between spatial and temporal dimensions within a deep architecture (Werbos, 2002; Meng et al., 2023). Most time-dependent models are trained with Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) (Werbos, 2002; Eshraghian et al., 2023). The BPTT is the extension of the backpropagation algorithm to models with recurrent mechanisms, so that the gradient can be computed recursively through all the timesteps. However, it can be shown that, in BPTT, spatial components dominate the learning process, while the time components (i.e. the backward computation from the end of the sequence to some timestep t) can be dropped (Meng et al., 2023). Finally, the choice of encoding and decoding representations depends on the type of activation function used. For ANN with weighted-sum activation functions, information from event-based videos is encoded using pixel brightness levels and decoded via a softmax function applied to the network’s output values. For SNN with spiking activation functions, information is encoded through the number of input events, while decoding is again performed using a softmax function applied to the output spiking frequencies. For delay-based neural networks (DeNNs) with temporal activation functions, information is encoded using the period of neurons, and decoded using a softmin function applied to the network’s output spike times. Contributions. This work makes the following contributions: • We propose a formalization for designing and analysing different degrees of temporal representation in neural networks. • We introduce and design a family of activation functions with increasing degrees of temporal representation, independently of the network architecture. • We show that increasing the degree of temporal representation improves both performance and computational efficiency on event-based datasets. • We provide a formal comparison analysis of the quantitative differences between temporal and non-temporal computational trajectories in both forward and backward passes. • We show how full temporal representation induces gradients that depend on the history of event times. • On the Google Speech Commands dataset (Warden, 2018), we reach State-Of-The-Art performances, above spiking models and transformers. 2 Design of temporal representations Various degrees of temporal representations are described here for ANN, SNN (Maass, 1997a; Gerstner and Kistler, 2002; Gerstner et al., 2014) and DeNN (Gattepaille and Muzy, 2025).
