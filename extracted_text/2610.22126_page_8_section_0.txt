Error bars denote the standard deviation over 5 seeds. 4.3 Perturbed gene discovery in the IFN-β stimulation dataset We next evaluate our method on real single-cell RNA-sequencing data from Kang et al. [2018], in which peripheral blood mononuclear cells were stimulated with interferon-β (IFN-β) or left as controls. A fully connected neural network is trained to predict whether a cell was exposed to IFN-β stimulation. Unlike the previous two experiments, no true causal graph is available here, so ground truth is only approximate: we rely on curated prior knowledge (OmniPath) and state-of-the-art regulatory network inference methods (SCENIC) to test whether our method still identifies the transcription factors driving the interferon response. We compare our method against DeepSHAP [Lundberg and Lee, 2017] and integrated gradients [Sundararajan et al., 2017] (zero baseline). We derive a directed acyclic graph from OmniPath [Türei et al., 2021], used both for feature ordering and to train the surrogate. We compute attributions for all genes in held-out test cells across five seeds, summarized as the mean absolute attribution per gene, and evaluate rank by hop distance from the roots of both the OmniPath prior and a SCENIC-inferred regulatory network [Aibar et al., 2017]. SCENIC leverages motif- and TF-curated references to derive gene regulatory networks from the data while OmniPath is not data-specific. Dataset, model, graph construction, surrogate, and evaluation details are given in Appendix H. Next, we test whether the methods recover stimulation-responsive regulators: by ranking genes per cell type by their attribution difference between stimulated and control cells and testing whether SCENIC-responsive TFs rank above the rest, using rank- and top-k-enrichment tests (one-sided Mann–Whitney U test and hypergeometric test, respectively). As an orthogonal readout, we evaluate recovery of per-cell-type pseudobulk differentially expressed genes (DEGs). For the three cell types most responsive to IFN-β stimulation [Kang et al., 2018] (CD14+/FCGR3A+ monocytes, dendritic cells), we also run over-representation analysis (ORA) [Fang et al., 2023] on each method’s unique top-100 genes against MSigDB Hallmark gene sets [Liberzon et al., 2015]. Results and discussion. The distribution of attribution ranks across hop-distance groups shows the CIG advantage is concentrated at the top of the regulatory hierarchy: on the OmniPath prior, CIG ranks root genes higher than DeepSHAP and integrated gradients, with a smaller advantage one hop downstream (Figure I.1); the effect also holds on the SCENIC graph (Figure 5A), where DeepSHAP and integrated gradients rank roots below genes one to three hops downstream. Rankings are reproducible across seeds, with CIG the most stable of the three at SCENIC roots (Tables I.1 and I.2).
