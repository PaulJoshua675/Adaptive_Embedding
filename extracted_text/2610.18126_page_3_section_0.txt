Agent benchmarks evaluate plans and actions executed after reasoning (Yao et al., 2023; Xie et al., 2024b; Yao et al., 2025). Time-aware simulations score how agents sched- ule work (Zhang et al., 2024), and agents misjudge when elapsed time calls for fresh tool calls (Cheng et al., 2026). Proactive agents decide when to in- tervene (Lu et al., 2025; Tang et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026; Xiaohongshu Dots Studio and Evolvent AI, 2026), maintain long-term intents through trigger conditions (Shi et al., 2026), and accept user correc- tions during execution (Zhai et al., 2026). We study many concurrent decisions with revision deadlines, a shared stream of world news, and the cost of deciding which news reaches which decision. Memory and retrieval. Agent memories store and retrieve past interactions (Park et al., 2023; Packer et al., 2023; Chhikara et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025), invalidate outdated facts in temporal graphs (Rasmussen et al., 2025), index memories by time and topic (Tian et al., 2026), and are evaluated on long-term recall (Wu et al., 2025; Du, 2026). Persistent, evidence-grounded state with tempo- ral validity is a design requirement for systems that work across sessions (Bakkali and Merimi, 2026); the decision ledger applies it to commit- ments. Watch conditions are document expansion (Nogueira et al., 2019) and generated queries (Gao et al., 2023) written once, at decision time. BM25 remains a strong zero-shot retriever next to dense retrieval (Karpukhin et al., 2020; Thakur et al., 2021), and long contexts degrade reasoning when relevant content is buried or lacks literal overlap (Shi et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024; Modarressi et al., 2025). 3 A Theory of Decisions Over Time Six timestamps. Evidence is written at tw and last verified at tv. The model decides at td with evidence verified at tv ≤td. The decision can be revised until the revision deadline tr, is carried out at ta, and its outcome is observed at te, with td < tr ≤ta ≤te. The revision deadline captures commitment: an order ships, a table is taken, a train departs. The freshness ceiling. Let s(t) be the state of the world and U(a, s) the utility of action a in state s. The model chooses ˆa at td, and the outcome is U(ˆa, s(ta)), so the target is the best action at ta. The age of the evidence at action time decomposes as ta −tv = (td −tv) | {z } ∆fresh + (ta −td) | {z } ∆act . (1) Retrieval and knowledge updates shrink the fresh- ness gap ∆fresh; the execution gap ∆act is set by the task. If a fact f changes at a constant hazard rate λf, it still holds τ time units after verification with probability e−λfτ, so Pr[f holds at ta] = e−λf∆fresh e−λf∆act. (2) Proposition 1 (Freshness ceiling). For any fact f and execution gap ∆act, the probability that f holds at action time is at most e−λf∆act, for every degree of freshness. Appendix A proves the proposition and extends it to decisions with several dependencies. Fresh- ness is a property of the evidence; validity at action time is a property of the whole pipeline. The ceil- ing locates the residual risk in the execution gap and in the volatility of the evidence, and only infor- mation that arrives after td can reduce it: a notice published inside the execution gap that reaches the decision before tr. Post-decision routing. A ledger holds open de- cisions. Decision i has a chosen option ai, alter- natives, evidence Ei, times ti d < ti r ≤ti a and an activity [ti a, ti a + hi]. A stream delivers items xj, each written and published at tj w. Item j is deci- sive for decision i if it is published in the revision window, ti d < tj w < ti r, concerns ai or an entity ai depends on, and blocks ai over an interval Ij that overlaps the activity, so that ai stops being the best option. An item about ai or its dependencies that is not decisive is related: a change outside the activity, or news that leaves ai usable. A router assigns each item to a subset of the decisions open at tj w, and at ti r the model reads Ei with the items routed to i and keeps or replaces ai. Routing and judgment. Let π be the share of decisions that should change. For those, let ρ be the routing recall, the probability that a decisive item reaches the decision; S the probability that the model switches when it does; and L the probability that it switches when it does not.
