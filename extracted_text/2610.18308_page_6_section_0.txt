Higher aspect entropy indicates the sim- ulator articulates preferences along a wider range of behavioral dimensions rather than collapsing to a single template. T4 — Linguistic surface variety. Per-user met- rics over user turns: corpus-level type-token ratio (TTR), n-gram monotony (mean Jaccard overlap of bigrams/trigrams between turn pairs), and SBERT semantic diversity (Reimers and Gurevych, 2019). TTR and n-gram monotony measure lexical variety, SBERT diversity measures the semantic spread of requests, and T3 captures semantic-content variety; these can move oppositely. 4.3 Results Table 2 summarizes the matched 100-user com- parisons. Relative to STATISTICALONLY, FULL achieves higher T3 aspect entropy (∆= +0.760) but lower T1 item diversity (∆= −0.375), both significant after Holm correction. FULL also ex- ceeds CSHI on T1, T3, and T4. T2 correlations are reported descriptively. T1 — Item diversity. FULL exhibits a more con- centrated movie-mention distribution than STA- TISTICALONLY (padj < 0.001), and a marginally higher but significant JSD from ReDial (0.681 vs. 0.676). These results characterize the breadth of movie mentions rather than the fidelity of simulated preferences. T2 — Popularity alignment. In the full 300- user evaluation, SimInter exhibits a moderate pos- itive correlation between simulated movie prefer- ences and MovieLens-25M mean ratings (Pearson r = 0.347, n = 275 movies). This suggests that the preferences expressed in simulated dialogue capture meaningful population-level rating patterns. On the matched 100-user subset, correlations re- main positive for FULL (r = 0.171) and STATIS- TICALONLY (r = 0.133). As the movie sets differ across conditions, these estimates are descriptive rather than paired comparisons and do not estab- lish individual-level preference fidelity.
