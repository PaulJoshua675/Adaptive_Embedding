We presented ModCMA, a modular framework for constructing and studying CMA-ES variants from interchangeable algorithmic components. By exposing modules for sampling, selection, step-size adaptation, matrix adaptation, and restarting within a unified implementation, ModCMA enables the systematic combination, benchmarking, and configuration of a large space of CMA-ES variants. Through a focused analysis of matrix adaptation, we showed that different adaptation mechanisms exhibit clear trade-offs between computational cost and optimization performance. In particular, alternatives such as Cholesky- and Matrix-based adaptation can substantially reduce runtime while retaining competitive performance on many benchmark problems. We further demonstrated the usefulness of the framework for automated configuration with SMAC Lindauer et al. (2022), where per-function tuning on BBOB produced specialized variants that substantially improved over the default configuration and outperformed the established bbob2009 reference set Hansen et al. (2010) on half of all the functions. Finally, the SHAP-based analysis demonstrated that the resulting tuning data can be used to interpret the roles of individual modules and options, revealing both global trends and problem-specific effects. Overall, these results show that ModCMA is not only a practical implementation of many CMA-ES variants, but also a tool for studying algorithmic design choices and their interactions.
