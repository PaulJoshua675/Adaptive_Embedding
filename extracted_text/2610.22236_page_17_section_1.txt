Mathematics benchmarks use temperature 0.7, top-p 0.9 and at most 31,744 new tokens. AIME24, AIME25 and HMMT25 have thirty problems each; four answers are sampled per problem and the mean correctness over all answers is reported. MATH-500 and OlympiadBench (674 problems) use one sampled answer per problem. An answer is correct if the content of its last \boxed{} expression, or of its final “Answer:” line, matches the reference after normalization or is symbolically equal to it; answers these rules reject are re-checked by CompassVerifier-3B. Code benchmarks use temperature 1.0, top-p 1.0 and at most 32,768 new tokens, with one sampled answer per problem; the last Python code block that defines the required entry point is extracted and scored by EvalPlus on the base and additional tests, and an answer that reaches the length limit counts as failed. Released checkpoints are evaluated in bfloat16 and fused models in float16. For the study of Section 4.5 the mathematics specialist is evaluated in float16 as well (Table 5). Compute. All runs use 48 GB GPUs, one per process. Table 4 lists the cost of each step. Generating the rollouts dominates, the teacher-forced pass and the solve are short, and no step computes a gradient or keeps optimizer state. C Ablations and diagnostics All fused models of this appendix use the solve of Section 3 with α = 0.9, are stored in float32 and evaluated in float16, and differ only in the statistics they are given. Differences between two models are paired over problems, with 95% intervals from 10,000 bootstrap resamples. Weight-only rules. Task arithmetic adds both updates to the base weights. The low-rank variant takes the singular value decomposition of each update, treats singular values above 1.05 times the Marchenko–Pastur edge as the low-rank part, sums the low-rank parts of the two specialists and averages the rest. Amount of rollouts. Table 3 compares two amounts of rollouts: 128 training problems per domain with a limit of 16k new tokens, and 256 other problems per domain at the limits of evaluation.
