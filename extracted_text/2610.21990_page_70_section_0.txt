Qualitative maps highlighting the noise and excessive degradation observed in few synthetically low-light adapted images. 5.1 Known Limitations Synthetic Low-Light Adaptation Algorithm Strong Degradation. In the current configuration, the main issue that is persistent within the low-light synthetic pipeline is that a small sample of the low-light samples are excessively degraded so that the LLIE module cannot recover lost informa- tion. The source of this issue remains unknown, however it is suspected that the issue comes from increasing the variability of illumination in the low-light conditions. As a result, a very few number of samples from the final light distribution are affected by this issue. Figure 5.1 presents a part of the affected samples with some managing to capture the overall structure of the image keeping some of the objects observable while others represent a totally noise and artifacts-covered representation which cannot be correctly evaluated by the segmentation module. A solution to this problem would be either removing the affected samples or further tuning the syn- thetic low-light adaptation algorithm to diagnose problematic scenarios and increase the illumina- tion threshold while keeping the noise levels lower than for other samples. A comparative analysis of the global intensity dis- tributions (Figure 5.2) reveals that the LLIE module introduces luminance-dependent chromatic aberrations via non-linear channel uncoupling. Specifically, in the shadow and mid-tone regimes (0 ≤I ≤150), the model disproportionately attenuates the blue channel relative to the source distribution, inducing an unnatural warm colour cast.
