Analyst prompt (simple) # Context You are a leading expert in spiking neural networks (SNNs) and in decoding outputs firing rates into class labels for the MNIST task, with over 20 years of practical experience. ↪ You are provided with: - Output spike sequences from 10 output neurons for the training and test datasets; - A JSON table containing experiments conducted using various decoding methods. The goal of this research is to develop a new decoding method that achieves an accuracy of 0.9 or higher (90%+) using a vector of firing rates from 10 output neurons. ↪ Important feature of the encoding: Input sequences are encoded by randomly selecting spikes without replacement from a given reference spike sequence. Therefore, the temporal structure of individual spike sequences is unstable across runs and should not be used directly. The informative object for decoding is the vector of firing rates of the 10 output neurons. ↪ ↪ ↪ # Your task Develop a method for decoding the firing-rate vector of the output neurons (dimension: 10) that is not included in the provided JSON experiment table, with the goal of achieving an accuracy of 0.9 or higher. ↪ **Available data and abilities:** 1. Output spike sequences vector (zeros and ones) for the training dataset - train_spikes. 2. Output spike sequences vector (zeros and ones) for the test dataset - test_spikes. 3. Correct labels for the training dataset - y_train. 4. You may construct new features and new decoding rules based on firing_rates. When developing a method, clearly distinguish between: - the feature-extraction step from the firing rate vector; - the decision rule step based on these features. Examples of acceptable method classes (which may be combined or extended, but are not limited to): - Normalized argmax over firing rates, optionally with neuron-specific weights and biases; - Rules based on the difference between the two highest firing rates (margin-based); - Class calibration using firing rate statistics for each class (e.g., mean firing rates for each neuron and class) and calss-specific score calculations; ↪ - Simple threshold-based and piecewise rules (e.g., small if/then/else/ decision trees based on firing rate thresholds and their combinations). ↪ **Limitations:** 1. The output spike trains have already been recorded. You cannot modify the network architecture or encoding method to record different sequences. ↪ 2. You cannot use backpropagation to train the proposed method. 3. You may fit method parameters only on the training data (train_spikes and y_train). The test data must be used exclusively for evaluation. ↪ 4.
