Each plot shows two Pareto fronts of MASK honesty against AlpacaEval LC win rate, one with CATCH trained with an on-policy utility dataset, and the other trained with UltraChat, an off-policy utility dataset; everything else fixed. The MASK honesty score represents the proportion of honest answers out of all scenarios with elicited belief. Among Us Figure 3 reports two measures over 100 games per method, with the intervened model playing the Impostor. Prompting and SFT-t barely change either. CATCH lowers the Impostor win rate from 39% to 20% on Llama-3.3-70B and from 48% to 12% on Qwen3.6-27B, and raises the confession rate from 5% to 84% and from 19% to 85%. SFT-tau increases confession rates to 14% and 58%, better than SFT-t but still below the full CATCH. Additional measures, including checks that the Impostors are still playing the game, are in Appendix A.5. Audit Bench Figure 4 shows the results of CAT applied to the Llama-3.3-70B model trained with hidden quirks in AuditBench. On the SFT-adversarial organism, the default auditor uncovers the hidden behaviour in 43% of audits; after CAT, the same auditor reaches 79% (55/70). On the KTO- adversarial organism, where no tool scores higher than 50%, CAT reaches 93% (65/70). CAT is above every tool (except scaffolding) reported in the AuditBench paper on both settings.3 CAT is on par with Herbster et al. (2026)’s SOTA steering baseline, where StTP reaches 83% on the SFT organism, above CAT, and 91% on the KTO organism, just below it. Utility preservation Figure 5 shows whether each intervention leaves general instruction follow- ing intact. On AlpacaEval, CATCH is at parity with the base model on both architectures, at 51.3% and 51.8%. Under the honest-persona prompts, both models open their replies with commentary on the “honest-only” mode before answering, and win rates collapse to 9-12%. Steering barely affects Qwen (45.2%) but drops Llama to 8.6%. CATCH also stays within one point of base on MMLU and at or slightly above it on MT-Bench (Appendix A.6). Utility data ablation Figure 6 shows MASK honesty against the AlpacaEval LC win rate over the course of training, with one Pareto curve per utility corpus. The runs are otherwise identical, using the same adversarial data, the same objective, and the same hyperparameters. For both models, both the on-policy (Magpie) and off-policy (UltraChat) utility data reach a similar level of honesty (73.9 vs 73.6), but they differ substantially in capability measured by AlpacaEval. Specifically, reaching comparable honesty scores on Llama-3.3-70B, the on-policy data maintains the AlpacaEval win rate at 51.3, while UltraChat drops it to 22.0. Similarly, on Qwen3.6-27B, though the UltraChat run scored slightly higher in honesty score (80.6) than Magpie-Qwen3.6 (74.1), its win rate decreases to 28.7, while Magpie-Qwen3.6 maintains it at 51.8. Off-policy data may do better when it comes from a more capable model. Training Llama-3.3-70B with Magpie-Qwen3.6, which is generated by a newer and stronger model, raises its win rate to 85.3, consistent with the utility term acting as distillation from a stronger teacher (Appendix A.7).
