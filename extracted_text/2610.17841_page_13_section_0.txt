We initialize the model from a checkpoint [28] pre- trained on sentence similarity, and represent product text as "name |brand |gender |color |materials |description". The model is trained with binary cross-entropy loss, mean pool- ing, label smoothing (ϵ = 0.1), and batch size 512. On our internal test set of 2,353 unique queries and 12,619 query- product pairs (positive rate 0.632), with threshold=0.5, the cross-encoder achieves 0.818 ROC-AUC and a Brier score of 0.162, versus 0.233 for a baseline predicting the marginal positive rate for every pair. We validate the oracle on a manually constructed san- ity set of 100 query–product examples covering multiple attributes. The model achieves a relevant–irrelevant prob- ability margin of 0.616 and pairwise ranking accuracy of 97.0%, exceeding our validation criteria of 0.15 and 75%, respectively. Attribute annotations. Item-level attributes are obtained independently from the public Myntra fashion catalog [2]. Product type, gender, age group, color, and usage/occasion are obtained from structured metadata, while material and pattern are extracted from the attribute text. Price is dis- cretized into budget, mid-range, and premium categories. Query-side attributes are obtained from naturally occurring enriched queries. Query and item attributes are canonical- ized using the same normalization procedure before com- puting attribute matches. Matched-pair ablation. For each attribute a, we con- struct matched “twin” item pairs that agree on relevant con- trol attributes but differ in a. As an independent validation, we construct query–product pairs from real queries and en- code each attribute as fa ∈{1, 0, 0.5} for match, mis- match, and unspecified, respectively. After standardizing the ten attribute features, we fit a Ridge regression [46] to the cross-encoder logits using N = 6,205 pairs. The regression achieves R2 = 0.490. Variance inflation fac- tors (VIFs) [26] between 1.00 and 1.10 indicate negligible multicollinearity among the attribute features. For each method, negative esti- mates are clipped to zero and the resulting values are nor- malized to sum to one. We compute the Spearman rank cor- relation between the two sets of raw estimates as an agree- ment measure. The final attribute weight wa is obtained by averaging the two normalized weight vectors and renor- malizing the result to sum to one.
