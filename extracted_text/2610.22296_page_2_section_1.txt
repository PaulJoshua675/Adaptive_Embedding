Deep-learning-based audio watermarking jointly trains an embedder and a detector to balance imperceptibility and robustness. RobustDNN [3] introduced the basic recipe, WavMark [4], AudioSeal [5], Timbre Watermarking [6], and SilentCipher [7] subsequently improved robustness to common signal-processing attacks. VoiceMark [8] extends robustness to zero-shot voice-cloning attacks. All of these systems train and deploy embedders and detectors as fixed pairs and provide no explicit mechanism for cross-version compatibility. Petrov et al. [9] study the coexistence and ensembling of independently trained watermarks. Their setting differs from ours in two important ways: embedder-detector pairs are trained independently (with no shared parameters or training signal), and there is no obligation for a later detector to recognize watermarks from an earlier one. As a consequence, their reported interference is minimal. 2
