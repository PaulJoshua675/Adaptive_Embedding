Preprint Algorithm 1 Predicting the web-scale metric from a small distractor sample. A correct answer’s rank grows by the number of distractors that outscore it—about N times its per-query hardness x, the fraction of all distractors scoring above the answer. Estimating x from a small sample therefore fixes mAP at any gallery size N, with no full-scale indexing. When extrapolation is needed. The estimator’s job is to recover the unobserved hard tail, so its value scales with how much that tail matters. With a hard pool (large decay), the count estimate is badly optimistic and tail modeling is essential. With an easy pool (little decay), there is almost nothing to extrapolate and counting already suffices. Across our datasets a random small sample recovers mAP to within ∼0.01. 5 EXPERIMENTS Setup. We evaluate on three benchmarks across two distractor pools. ILIAS (Kordopatis-Zilos et al., 2025): 1,232 instance queries against 100M YFCC distractors, using the official precomputed features for 5 backbones (DINOv2-L, DINOv3-L, SigLIP-L, SigLIP2-L, PE-core-L), which we re- produce to cosine 1.00; we report the official mAP@1000. INSTRE (Wang & Jiang, 2015): 1,250 object queries with the same 100M pool appended. Google Landmarks v2 (Weyand et al., 2020): 1,129 retrieval queries against the 759k-image index pool—a different domain, its own ground truth, and a same-domain (landmark-vs-landmark) distractor distribution—encoded with the same five backbones at their native preprocessing; we report the official mAP@100.
