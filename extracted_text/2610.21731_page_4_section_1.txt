We study why search-augmented LLMs fail to use available evidence correctly. We find that models often retrieve relevant evidence but answer from search snippets without opening the source pages. Such answers are more likely to be stale and to carry unfaithful citations. Providing relevant evi- dence directly reduces failures but does not elim- inate them. To understand the remaining failures, we analyse how models process retrieved evidence across layers. The retrieved answer can gain an early advantage in the upper layers but lose it to the outdated answer in the final layers. Future work can explore strategies that help models make more effective use of source pages when needed. Causal interventions and latent debate (Chen et al., 2026) can be used to better understand how retrieved evi- dence competes with outdated knowledge and how to improve it.
