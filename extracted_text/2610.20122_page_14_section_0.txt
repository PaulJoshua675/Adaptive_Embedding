Accordingly, this stratified result is a complementary head- focused diagnostic rather than direct evidence about the exact default overlap feature. The low-agreement group shows significant gains for both nDCG@10 and MAP, whereas the medium- and high-agreement groups do not. This result narrows the mechanism claim: XViewRank is most useful when the retrieval views expose different candidate rankings that fixed rank fusion cannot resolve as effectively. Low agreement is an observed condition under which learned fusion has more room to help; it should not be interpreted as a generation objective, because deliberately forcing unrelated views can instead introduce query drift. Does the learned scorer transfer to other collections? We further test zero-shot transfer to two BEIR datasets. The XViewRank scorer is still trained on MS MARCO and is directly applied to FiQA and NFCorpus without dataset- specific tuning. RRF, CombSUM, and XViewRank use the same original, hy- pothesis, and step-back views for each target dataset, while Exp4Fuse follows its original two-view setting with the original and hypothesis views. Table 10 reports nDCG@10. XViewRank obtains the highest score among the reported baselines on both datasets. We treat this as a limited transfer check rather than a claim of universal cross-collection robustness. XViewRank is a retrieval-side fusion method and does not jointly encode query and passage text. A matched-budget neural-reranking reference is there- fore reported separately in Appendix B, rather than being treated as part of the main method.
