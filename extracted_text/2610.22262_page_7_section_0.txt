For each ground-truth training field, TTK computes the persistence diagram once, offline, and stores the two endpoint vertices of each supervised persistence pair together with the ground-truth birth value b, death value d, and persistence p. During training, the predicted speed ˆs is read at those fixed vertices; LCV penalizes endpoint-value errors and Lpers penalizes the error in the predicted persistence ˆp = | ˆd −ˆb|. Gradients flow only through ordinary scalar-field values. MT distance is computed on the same crops with TTK’s merge-tree distance between join trees (merge trees of sublevel sets); for readability we call it MT distance throughout. All four distances are lower-is-better. Persistence thresholding is used only to reduce clutter in qualitative figures. Validation of the PD distances. Because the PD distances are our own implementation, we validated them along two in- dependent paths. First, GUDHI’s distance functions, applied to the same TTK-extracted D0 and D1 diagrams across 51 archived reconstruction runs and 168 fields per run (8568 ground-truth– reconstruction comparisons), reproduce our bottleneck distances exactly; the maximum absolute discrepancy is 1.02 × 10−12 for W2,∞and 6.40 × 10−14 for W2,2. This validates the distance com- putation rather than TTK’s diagram construction. Second, to test sensitivity to the diagram-construction backend, we rebuilt the di- agrams of the pretrained CNN, the standard fine-tuning control, and the final model with GUDHI’s cubical complexes for all 168 fields. These diagrams are not identical to TTK’s, because the two libraries use different discretization and pairing conventions, but they preserve our conclusions: the ranking of the three mod- els agrees on 161, 165, and 164 of 168 fields for dB, W2,∞, and W2,2, and the direction of the final-model-versus-CNN comparison agrees on 98.8%, 100%, and 99.4% of fields. For merge trees, an in- dependent join-tree construction reproduces TTK’s trees, after root normalization, for all 504 SR trees examined. This validates tree construction but not TTK’s merge-tree distance itself, which we did not re-implement. Win counts. When we say that one method wins on a field, it has the higher PSNR/SSIM or the lower error or distance on that field; win counts are out of the 168 benchmark fields. We report means in the main text; medians (Appendix D) lead to the same conclusions. 6 RESULTS 6.1 Pretrained CNN–GAN trade-off The pretrained models expose the evaluation tension that moti- vates this work. Table 2 reports per-field win counts for the com- plete evaluation suite. The CNN dominates direct errors, WPD MAE/RMSE, and gradient MAE. The GAN is preferred on PSD log-L2, gradient Wasserstein distance, and slightly more often at the p95 and p99 exceedance thresholds. The topology rows do not reduce to a single verdict either. MT distance prefers the CNN (148 of 168 fields), and so does bottleneck PD distance (129 of 168; mean dB 3.124 versus 5.059), whereas both Wasserstein PD distances prefer the GAN (155 and 158 of 168; mean W2,2 17.46 versus 24.55). One reading, consistent with the GAN’s sharper output and its behavior on the gradient and spectral measures, is that the GAN better matches the aggregate distribu- tion of birth–death pairs that the Wasserstein distances accumulate, while producing larger worst-case discrepancies and a less faith- ful merge hierarchy. We offer this as an interpretation rather than an established mechanism. Either way, the preferred reconstruc- tion depends on whether the evaluation emphasizes pointwise er- ror, distributional sharpness, accumulated or worst-case persistence mismatch, or merge hierarchy. 6.2 Persistence gains beyond ordinary fine-tuning Table 3 compares the final objective with the pretrained CNN and the standard fine-tuning control. Fine-tuning with the reconstruc- tion loss alone makes persistence agreement worse: the control in- creases all three mean PD distances relative to the pretrained CNN, by 5.2% (dB), 10.0% (W2,∞), and 12.0% (W2,2), and lowers all three on only 11 of 168 fields. Adding the auxiliary losses reverses this. The final model lowers mean dB, W2,∞, and W2,2 by 31.2%, 25.2%, and 27.4% relative to the CNN and by 34.6%, 32.0%, and 35.2% relative to the control.
