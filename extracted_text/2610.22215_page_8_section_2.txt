We introduced FutureLift, a backbone-agnostic trajectory refiner that turns multimodal proposals into explicit hetero- geneous future graphs. By representing proposal segments as spatially grounded nodes connected to history, map, modal, and for coherent joint proposals to social context, FutureLift exposes the complete proposed future before cor- rection. Its bidirectional temporal relations allow later ev- idence to revise earlier segments in one graph pass, while its proposal-only interface supports structurally different forecasting backbones without backbone-specific adapta- tion. Across Argoverse 2 and WOMD, separately trained FutureLift models improve frozen proposals in marginal and joint forecasting and outperform native and special- ized refiners in within-backbone comparisons. On the lo- cally evaluated AV2 test split, FutureLift is competitive with the strongest listed marginal methods and achieves the low- est listed avgMinADE and avgMinFDE in joint prediction. On the WOMD test leaderboard, the single-model Future- Lift system ranks first in horizon-averaged minADE and minFDE among the listed entries with valid reported val- ues and achieves the lowest listed 8-second minFDE; it out- performs all listed ensembles on these displacement metrics and exceeds two of the three ensembles on Soft mAP. Com- ponent analysis shows that one bidirectional sweep outper- forms up to three causal sweeps, with a larger advantage at longer WOMD horizons. Our evaluation is limited to offline motion-forecasting benchmarks with HD-map context, so it does not yet establish gains for map-light settings or downstream closed-loop planning. Future work should extend future-graph lifting to less structured scene context and evaluate whether the refined forecasts translate into safer planning decisions under closed-loop interaction.
