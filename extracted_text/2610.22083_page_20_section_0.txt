Sergio Davies is a senior lecturer in AI in the Department of Computing and Mathematics at Manchester Metropolitan University. He received his Laurea (equivalent to MSc Eng) in Telecom- munication Engineering from the University “Fed- erico II” in Napoli, Italy in 2006. Following his Ph.D. in Computer Science at the University of Manchester in 2012, Sergio continued his research on spiking neural networks as a postdoc in the Human Brain Project until 2016. Transitioning to industry, he took leadership roles in strategic ad- vising as a consultant, and then managed various research projects covering computer hardware de- sign, embedded systems architecture, software de- velopment and system networking. In 2019, Sergio returned to academia, initially at Sheffield Hallam University before joining Manchester Metropolitan University. His current research focuses on practi- cal application of spiking neural networks in real- world scenarios. Muhammad Aitsam is a Research Fellow in Artifi- cial Intelligence and Robotics at Sheffield Hallam University, UK. He received his PhD in Com- puting from the same institution, where his doc- toral research focused on neuromorphic comput- ing and event-based vision for intelligent robotic systems. His research interests include neuromor- phic computing, event-based vision, spiking neu- ral networks, human-robot interaction, and in- teractive and autonomous robotics. Dr Aitsam has contributed to several UK and European re- search projects, including the EU-funded PERSEO project, the EPSRC-funded ALDENS project, and the Innovate UK-funded PRIMI project. He also contributes to the research community through peer review and programme committee activities for international conferences and journals in arti- ficial intelligence, computer vision, neuromorphic computing, and robotics. Morgan-Tyler Stone is a PhD candidate in the Department of Computing and Mathematics at Manchester Metropolitan University, UK. They re- ceived a BSc in Computer Science for Games from Sheffield Hallam University. Their wider interests include neuromorphic and edge computing, cyber- security, and energy-efficient real-time intelligent systems.
