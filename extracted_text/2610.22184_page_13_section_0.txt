The lower body and waist are welded, so the torso frame is exactly static and the arms are the only actuated degrees of freedom. Arm stiffness is kp = 20; damping is derived per joint as kv ∝ p kp. B. Rates, Episodes, Terminations Physics runs at 200 Hz with a decimation of four, giving a 50 Hz control rate. Training episodes last 40 s (2000 control steps), and rewards are not scaled by the timestep. Contact is read from a sensor between all links and the object. An episode ends on time-out, which is bootstrapped, or on one of six failures: the object falling below 0.5 m in the world frame; contact lost for longer than a grace window of 24 control steps (0.48 s), where contact is maintained if the arm contacts required by either the source or the target grasp mode are present, and torso contact alone does not count; the object leaving a 0.55 m workspace radius or exceeding 6 m/s or 40 rad/s; the object coming to rest on the legs (disabled for large objects); a non-finite state; or a dead end in the goal graph. The sparse failure penalty fires on the first five of these; dead ends and time-outs are not penalized. Each episode is initialized directly from the grasp dataset, with the arm configuration and object pose written exactly from the sampled grasp and zero velocity. The servo target is set instantly to that grasp’s pressed target with no ramp. Grasps are sampled as in Section III-B. The only continuous randomization at reset is a ±5 mm jitter on movement goal positions; hold goals are exact. C. Goal Completion Criteria We define the object as settled when its linear and angular speeds are below 0.12 m/s and 1.5 rad/s, respectively. A commanded goal is completed when all of the following hold at once: the object is within 0.02 m of the commanded position, the object is settled, the arms required by the goal grasp mode are in contact, and these conditions persist for 10 control steps for movement goals or 25 for hold goals. A single-arm goal additionally requires the other arm to register no object contact for five consecutive steps. Training and evaluation both apply this requirement. D. Reward Details Table V lists the weights applied to the reward terms defined in Section III-B and below. Reward weights are fixed for the whole of training; the two curricula modify the environment rather than the objective. For progress, both distance errors refer to the same commanded goal. Assigning a new goal resets the distance baseline without generating progress. The object’s linear and angular speeds are denoted by vt and ωt. Let at be the raw policy action, bt = tanh(at) the bounded action before scaling to joint increments, ct the arm joint target, and qt the measured arm joint positions. The five regularization penalties are: • Unsupported motion, (1−ρt) max(vt−5 cm/s, 0)2: penal- izes linear object speeds above the threshold when ρt = 0, discouraging unsupported motion. • Speed cap, max(vt −0.35 m/s, 0)2 + 0.02 max(ωt − 3 rad/s, 0)2: penalizes linear and angular speeds above their respective thresholds. • Action magnitude, ∥bt∥2 2: penalizes large bounded actions. • Action rate, ∥bt −bt−1∥2 2: penalizes changes between consecutive bounded actions. • Servo lead, P14 j=1[|ct,j −qt,j|−0.25 rad]2 +: penalizes joint targets more than 0.25 rad from the measured positions.
