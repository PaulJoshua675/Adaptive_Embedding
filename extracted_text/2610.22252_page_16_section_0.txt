Their guarantees depend on an expected-disagreement quantity that captures the overlap among target policies. They first analyze this procedure under a sampling oracle and then construct an approximate oracle through interaction with the environment. Our approach exploits a different source of shared information: under linear reward structure, an observation can inform the values of policies taking different actions through their feature representations. We capture this relationship through policy feature means and second moments. The work closest to ours is Chen et al. [9], who propose CAESAR for multiple-policy evaluation in tabu- lar MDPs. Their method first obtains coarse estimates of policy visitation distributions and uses them to construct a shared sampling distribution. It then estimates marginal importance ratios through quadratic objectives inspired by DualDICE and uses these ratios to evaluate the target policies. Their sample guar- antee depends on an optimized occupancy-based measure of the difficulty of covering the target policies. Our method follows a related separation between designing a sampling distribution and collecting data for evaluation. In our setting, the design is expressed through feature moments, and its quality is measured by the largest quadratic uncertainty among the target feature means. Moreover, contexts follow a policy- independent distribution, so we can estimate these moments by querying all policies on shared contexts without observing rewards. We use a regularized empirical design and a single ridge regression model to obtain value estimates, accounting explicitly for moment-estimation error and regularization bias. Liu et al. [27] also design a shared behavior policy to evaluate multiple target policies. Their approach uses per-decision importance sampling and derives behavior-policy choices through objectives that aggregate es- timator variances across target policies. They establish unbiasedness and give conditions on policy similarity under which their estimators improve upon separate on-policy evaluation. Their implementation estimates the value and second-moment quantities needed for behavior-policy construction from offline transition and reward data, using fitted policy evaluation. They also demonstrate the approach in Gridworld and MuJoCo environments. Our objective instead controls the maximum uncertainty across target policies, matching the requirement that every value estimate satisfy the same accuracy guarantee. Our planning procedure uses reward-free feature queries, and our analysis accounts for the samples needed to learn the design as part of a simultaneous high-probability evaluation guarantee. These works address sequential decision-making in MDPs, where actions affect future state distributions. We focus on stochastic contextual bandits with linear conditional mean rewards and a known feature map. This structure allows us to share information across context–action pairs and obtain sample bounds governed by feature dimension and the instance-dependent quantity ρ⋆. Our guarantees avoid explicit dependence on the cardinalities of the context and action spaces and require no numerical lower bound on second-moment eigenvalues. The distinction between context draws, policy queries, and reward observations is essential: the shared contexts reduce the context budget, while querying every target policy still requires K policy queries per context.
