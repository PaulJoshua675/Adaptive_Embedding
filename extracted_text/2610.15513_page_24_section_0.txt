These are the targets used in the main benchmark evaluation, the transfer experiment, and the ablation study, and the alternative judges back REPRO’s labels at substantial-to-near-perfect agreement on all of them except the Qwen3-8B-vs-GPT-4 pair on JBB gpt-4o-mini (𝜅= 0.58, moderate agreement), where the larger sample size (𝑛= 200) exposes more borderline cases than the smaller AdvBench slices. The grok-4-1-fast slice shows the weakest agreement in the validation (𝜅= 0.37–0.46, 61% three-way agreement, 𝑛= 33); the three judges pull apart on borderline replies for this target, and we treat the REPRO labels for this slice as the noisiest in our dataset. Implications for REPRO-vs.-baseline comparisons. Baselines in this paper are reported with their original judges for faithfulness to published numbers, so the REPRO-vs.-baseline comparisons in subsequent tables are not strictly like-for-like in the judge dimension. The cross-validation above bounds the size of this discrepancy on the REPRO side: our reported ASR values are robust to the choice of judge, with the direction of any judge-induced discrepancy favoring stricter rather than more lenient assessment of attack success.
