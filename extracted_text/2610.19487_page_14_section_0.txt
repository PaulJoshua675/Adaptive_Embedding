These exponents should not be interpreted as biolog- ical universality classes, nor as consequences of a puta- tive dendritic fractal dimension. They characterize this particular nested sigma–pi ensemble. The more gen- eral conclusion does not depend on their numerical val- ues. Once nonlinear subunits are unresolved, the coarse model is confined to the approximation space generated by the variables that survive the projection. Resolving additional subunits exposes additional interaction coor- dinates and thereby enlarges that space. A point-node description can therefore be an excellent effective model while remaining computationally lower-dimensional than the biological object it represents. This completes the inversion of the argument devel- oped in Sections V and VI. Coarse-graining showed how fine interaction structure can disappear from an effective description. Refinement shows the complementary op- eration: restoring spatially organized nonlinear degrees of freedom expands the set of functions available to that description. What appears as unexplained variation at the coarse ruler can become representable structure once the appropriate internal coordinates are resolved. III. DISCUSSION A. Effective descriptions need a scale The adequacy of a low-order statistical model in ex- plaining observed neural activity is a joint property of the underlying generative mechanism and the ruler through which it is observed. Pairwise maximum-entropy models can explain collective activity in small, sparsely active population of neurons7, but this measure permits accu- rate fits even when the underlying interactions are not pairwise8. Common input can be a source of higher- order correlations24, and larger neural populations can have explicit higher-order terms9. The finding that di- rect, pairwise dependencies explain much of the output entropy of recorded neurons11 therefore establishes an ef- fective description at an observation scale rather identify- ing the underlying fine-scale computational mechanism. It does not provide a window to the computation im- plemented by intracellular voltages, channel states and contact locations absent from that description. Our prior work illustrate why both the observable and the measure scale matter. In human cortex, pair- wise models that exclude inhibitory neurons substan- tially overestimate excitatory synchrony10. In multielec- trode recordings from cats, monkeys and humans, neu- ral avalanche statistics differ between spikes and field- potential, cautioning against interpreting apparent scal- ing as a unique criticality signature of the underlying generative mechanism25. Spike avalanches show no clear power-law scaling, and the apparent scaling of local-field- potential negative peaks is shared by positive peaks un- related to firing.
