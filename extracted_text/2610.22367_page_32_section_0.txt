Where every sampled input uses a position at a given step, the schedule marks the position test-free, and the circuit performs its arithmetic unconditionally. Counter tests remain at the other positions inside the window, near the moving boundaries of the values. On an input that does not use a test-free position, the unconditional arithmetic produces a wrong result. The two approximations remove different costs. A fitted window deletes both the test and the arithmetic at the positions outside it. A test-free position keeps the arithmetic and deletes the test. Figure S2(c) shows the resulting per-step cost. Merged scans and measured carries. The baseline circuit compiles the scans within a step separately, so neighboring scans repeat some of the same comparisons and compute some of the same control bits. Compiling the complete step as one operation removes this repetition; we call this change merged scans. The clearest instance is the remainder update. The comparison that decides whether a subtraction applies and the subtraction itself run over the same two remainders, and the baseline compiles them as two passes. The discovered circuit merges them into one compare-and-subtract scan whose intermediate bits serve both operations. It then erases those bits by measurement, which needs no Toffolis, instead of uncomputing them. The baseline also shifts a register’s contents, by an amount that depends on the input, as the values change size. The discovered circuit renumbers the positions instead of moving the qubits, which costs no gates. In the additions that remain, a carry that only one position needs is likewise erased by measurement (measured carries). These changes are exact. Multiplier arithmetic. The coordinate update also requires modular squaring and multiplication. The discovered circuit specializes these operations to the sparse prime p.
