Despite these magnitude biases, we next examine whether the predicted winds and pressures co-vary consistently with the reference data, by analysing their joint distributions. 5 DO THE MODELS REPRODUCE THE WIND–PRESSURE RELATIONSHIP? Stronger tropical cyclones are generally characterised by higher wind speeds and lower central pressure. We therefore examine whether the models reproduce this wind-pressure relationship, even when the predicted storm intensity is systematically underestimated. This is particularly relevant to FGN: its CRPS training objective evaluates each variable and location separately, without directly enforcing their joint dependence. GenCast, by contrast, relies on diffusion to learn the joint distribution of atmospheric fields (Alet et al., 2025; Price et al., 2024). Comparing predicted storms. At each forecast time, we take the strongest 10m wind and lowest sea-level pressure within the cyclone region, giving one wind–pressure pair per ensemble member. These extrema depend on how the predicted fields vary across space; their pairing also tests how wind and pressure vary together. We compare their distributions to ERA5, HRES-fc0, and IBTrACS at times with valid observed wind and pressure. The main figure pools storms, members, and lead times; Appendix A gives the sampling details. FGN and GenCast produce weaker winds and shallower pressure minima than HRES-fc0, with distributions closer to ERA5 despite operational fine-tuning (Figure 6a).
