• Error analysis. We decompose the KL-divergence between the data distribution and the output of SIRS into a clipping-induced error, the error of the proposal, and the error of the acceptance probability, where the latter two vanish when the score function is exact. Parallel sampling, which corresponds to B = 0, incurs the full correlation error, i.e., the KL-divergence between the joint distribution of a block and the product of its marginals. SIRS corrects part of this error: its clipping-induced error is controlled only by the parts of the increments that exceed the clipping range [−B, B], and vanishes once B exceeds all increments. We further show that the Rao-Blackwellized increment yields a smaller bound on the clipping-induced error. • Experiments. On an Ising chain and a P´olya urn, where the score function is exact and the KL-divergence can be computed, SIRS with the Rao-Blackwellized increment achieves a lower KL-divergence than parallel sampling with the same number of score queries when the dependence within a block is weak, while parallel sampling with smaller blocks remains cheaper under strong dependence. Notations. We use normal-face letters for scalars, boldface lower-case letters for vectors, boldface upper-case letters for matrices, and italic boldface upper-case letters for random vectors. For N ∈N+, [N] denotes the set {1, 2, . . . , N}. The clipping function is denoted by clipB(x) = min(B, max(−B, x)). We use (z)+ and (z)−to denote max(z, 0) and min(z, 0), respectively. We use KL(·∥·) to denote the KL-divergence, DH(·, ·) to denote the Hellinger distance, and χ2(·∥·) to denote the χ2-distance. 2
