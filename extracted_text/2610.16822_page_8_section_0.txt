The “w/o Directed Node Fea- tures” variant retains operation embeddings but omits in-degree, out-degree, forward-depth, and reverse-depth features from node initialization. Direction-aware DTA-GT outperforms its undirected counterpart across the reported metrics, supporting the importance of directional information in architecture representations. Removing direction-aware components at individual encoder stages also degrades performance, supporting our central principle that directionality should be preserved throughout the encoder. These results support maintaining directional information from node ini- tialization and global spectral encoding through pairwise interaction and feature update. Removing directed node features or Magnetic spectral encoding reduces performance, supporting the use of both local directed-role information and a global orientation-sensitive spectral prior. Removing structural attention or MoE-FFN also weakens performance, supporting the contribution of directional relations during node interaction and predecessor/successor contexts during feature updates. CLS readout performs worse than attention pooling, which aggregates existing nodes without introducing virtual graph connections that would alter directional structural relations and Magnetic Laplacian encoding. The MSE-only control also underperforms the proposed training objective. Combining regression with ranking supervision is appropriate for predictor-based NAS: MSE fits numerical attribute values, while the ranking term explicitly constrains their relative order for candidate selection. 5.3 Main Results Table 3 compares all methods across the four tasks and training budgets. DTA-GT achieves the highest Kendall’s τ and the lowest MAPE across all four tasks and training budgets, showing consistent improvements in both candidate ranking and attribute estimation. These gains support the value of pre- serving directed computational semantics in architecture representations. Operator composition along directed paths, branch splitting and merging, and reachability-related dependencies describe how computations are organized within an architecture. DTA-GT explicitly models upstream/downstream roles and directional relations across its encoder stages, providing an inductive bias for capturing these computational semantics in both accuracy and latency prediction. Its consistent gains in both accuracy and latency prediction support encoder-wide directional modeling across architecture attributes. 5.4 Magnetic Spectral Encoding Analysis Table 4 isolates the spectral-encoding stage by comparing no directional encoding, symmetric Laplacian controls, and Magnetic Laplacian encoding in the directed encoder. Magnetic Laplacian encoding performs best on all reported metrics, whereas symmetric Laplacian encoding gives limited and task-dependent gains. Replacing Magnetic Laplacian with symmetric Laplacian inside the directed encoder also weakens performance, suggesting that the spectral benefit mainly comes from preserving edge-orientation asymmetry rather than adding generic undirected spectral information.
