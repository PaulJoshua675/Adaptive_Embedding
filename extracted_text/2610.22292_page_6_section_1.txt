We evaluate the honesty-intervention methods on three benchmarks where dishonesty manifests differently, as well as capability benchmarks. MASK benchmark. MASK (Ren et al., 2025) asks whether a model contradicts itself under pres- sure. For each scenario, it first elicits the model’s belief about a proposition in a neutral context, then puts the model under pressure, and compares the model’s answers under the two. Every pressured response with an elicited belief falls into one of three classes: it asserts what the model believes (honest), asserts the opposite (dishonest), or asserts neither, by hedging or refusing (ambiguous). In the literature, the third class is usually folded into the first category, with the honesty score calculated as 100 −dishonest rate, so a model that simply stops making claims or refuses most times scores well. Thus, we report all three instead of collapsing them into one number. As for accuracy, some propositions yield no consistent belief. Yet if we do not know what the model thinks, we cannot say whether it contradicted itself. We therefore drop those items before computing honesty rates.
