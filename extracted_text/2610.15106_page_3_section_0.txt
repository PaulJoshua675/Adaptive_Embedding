Directed edge representation. RELAGRAM encodes a CVRP instance as sparse directed edge tokens. Each customer i connects to the depot and its k −1 nearest customers, while the depot connects to every customer; opposite directions are encoded independently. For each edge e = (i, j), the encoder collects nine features: the coordinates, demands, and depot indicators of i and j, and their Euclidean distance. A bias-free linear layer projects them to xe ∈Rd and scales by √ d. Recursion over edge tokens. RELAGRAM maintains answer and workspace states yt, zt, initial- ized as fixed random vectors y0, z0 ∈Rd. The network Fθ is a two-block transformer, each block applying self-attention and SwiGLU [10] sublayers with post-norm residuals and RMSNorm [11] without learned scale, as in TRM. At each depth, it updates z then y: zt = Fθ(zt−1 + yt−1 + x), yt = Fθ(yt−1 + zt). (1) A bias-free linear head maps yt,e to Ht,ij, the score of moving from i to j. Edges absent from the sparse encoding receive a logit of −10, providing a fallback when every encoded move is masked. Route decoding. Starting at the depot, the decoder constructs a route vertex by vertex. The row of Ht scores each move after masking visited and capacity-infeasible customers. Stochastic decoding samples the remaining logits; greedy takes the largest. The heatmap remains fixed. Learning and the self-imitation auxiliary. At each training depth t, RELAGRAM draws S = 16 routes from the masked autoregressive policy. Route τt,s receives reward Rt,s = −C(τt,s); their mean ¯Rt = S−1 P s Rt,s is the same-instance REINFORCE baseline [8]. The loss is Lt = −1 S S X s=1 (Rt,s −¯Rt) log πΘ(τt,s | Ht) | {z } REINFORCE + λSI Ledge(Ht, τ elite t ) | {z } Self-imitation . (2) REINFORCE favours routes shorter than the same-instance mean. Self-imitation pulls the unmasked heatmap toward the shortest sampled route τ elite t using cross-entropy loss. We set λSI = 0.3; Θ represents trainable parameters. Every recursion depth receives one optimizer update, after which its latent states are detached, so gradients never propagate down the recursive chain. 4 Experiments Experimental design. We train on CVRP-100 in the RL4CO CVRPEnv [12], whose generator samples fresh unit-square instances for every batch. We use a neighbour mask of k = 20. The model has 0.53M parameters and is trained at four depths. Runs use a batch size of 16 with 16 sampled routes per instance, which across the four recursion depths yields 1,024 routes per batch. We evaluate RELAGRAM under two training budgets: 40,000 optimizer updates with a training time of 2.98 hours to compare RELAGRAM with DeepACO; and 160,000 optimizer updates with a training time of 11.94 hours to assess performance and its scaling under a larger training budget. Training uses a 10 GB A100 GPU slice. DeepACO is chosen as our baseline because it is a competitive neural combinatorial optimizer with a similar design: a learned edge heatmap guides a separate solution search. DeepACO iterates through pheromone updates, whereas RELAGRAM updates latent states. We train it as a 0.52M-parameter model with the same k = 20 neighbour mask and optimizer updates matched to RELAGRAM’s 40,000. The models use similar numbers of training trajectories (9.60M for DeepACO and 10.24M for RELAGRAM), but the comparison is not compute-matched: DeepACO sees twice as many base graphs and takes 1.87 times longer to train. Every model is evaluated on the same 256 fixed instances per size. References are PyVRP 0.13.4 [13] under per-instance time limits of 5 s at n = 100, 20 s at n = 500, and 40 s at n = 1000. We evaluate three decoding configurations: greedy (one route), eightfold symmetry augmentation, and the 256-candidate combination of symmetry, per-step latent noise [14], and forced different starts. Solution quality across graph sizes. We evaluate RELAGRAM trained for 160,000 updates on CVRP-100. The model reaches an 8.30% gap on CVRP-100 under 256-candidate decoding (Table 1). Without retraining, the model achieves gaps of 15.64% and 21.65% on CVRP-500 and CVRP-1000 under 256-candidate decoding. On CVRP-1000, expanding one greedy decode to 256 candidates improves the gap from 29.91% to 21.65%. The decoder therefore offers test-time scaling that strengthens with instance size, worth 5.10 points on CVRP-100 and 8.26 on CVRP-1000. 3
