Long legal documents are often represented as single retrieval units, which can dilute rele- vant content and limit retrieval effectiveness. This study investigates structural segmentation as a document representation strategy to improve Brazilian legal document retrieval. Using the hierarchical structure of legal acts, we represent each document as a collection of chunks. We evaluate four representation strategies: a multi-layered hierarchy representation and three simplified versions based on core chunks (articles and non-normative document components), with varying contextual details. We test these strategies on two publicly available Brazilian legal datasets containing normative acts and legislative proposals, using both lexical and dense retrieval models. Results show that structural segmentation improves dense retrieval com- pared with whole-document indexing, despite substantially increasing the number of indexed units. However, it does not consistently benefit lexical retrieval. Finally, we find that full hier- archicy representation is not necessary: core chunks enriched with document metadata yield comparable or better results. Keywords: legal information retrieval, document segmentation, dense retrieval, Brazilian legal acts
