Ad hoc methods have been proposed to mitigate these weaknesses, such as completing marginal quantiles under independence or comonotone assumptions (Perez-Diaz et al., 2025), or learning copula structures on top of marginal forecasts (Drouin et al., 2022; Ashok et al., 2024). On the other hand, diffusion- or flow-matching-based generative time-series models are naturally well-suited for capturing joint trajectory distributions even without these ad hoc methods, as they can generate complete future paths while maintaining temporal and cross-variable dependencies. Unfortunately, existing generative models (Kollovieh et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025) achieve neither high marginal accuracy nor efficient sampling, limiting their practical utility in real-world forecasting scenarios. This leads us to ask: Can we construct a generative time-series model that achieves both high marginal accuracy and efficient sampling while also preserving trajectory dependence? We introduce PathFM to combine joint and multi-mode trajectory modeling with accurate marginal forecasting and efficient sampling. The model learns temporal and cross-variable dependence through conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022), generating complete future paths conditioned on history and known future covariates. Parallel decoding distillation (Shaul et al., 2026) and history attention caching enable faster sampling with one or two model evaluations. To narrow the marginal accuracy gap, we add a calibration loss that directly optimizes fair CRPS on generated forecasts. The distilled sampler makes this optimization practical by allowing calibration gradients to pass through its short rollout and refine the forecast distribution.
