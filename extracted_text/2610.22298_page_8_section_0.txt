Complete interactive-consilium reports for the same calibration case are provided for the free-form and structured conditions in Appendices C and D, respectively. 3.5 Construction of the Physician-Annotation Corpus The physician-annotation corpus comprised 110 cases. Each case was processed through both the free-form and structured pipelines using the same finalized role panel. Before generating the corpus, we screened open-weight models offered through OpenRouter in July 2026 [OpenRouter, 2026]. Eligibility required public weights, support for structured outputs or reasoning, sufficient context for the longest prompts in our pipeline, reliable provider access, and a feasible projected cost. We selected the four highest-ranked eligible models when the OpenRouter catalogue was sorted by the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. GLM-5.2 [Z.ai, 2026] ranked highest and combined a long context window with broad provider availability, so it was assigned to combine the workers’ outputs and conduct the interactive consilium. MiniMax M3 and DeepSeek-V4-Pro [MiniMaxAI, 2026, DeepSeek-AI, 2026] were the next two eligible models and offered long context windows at comparatively low inference cost. Kimi K2.6 [Moonshot AI, 2026] was the next-ranked eligible model and added a third independent developer and model family to the worker panel. Thus, the strongest eligible model performed the final synthesis, while the next three provided independent opinions without relying on a single model family. Role assignment and role-specific pre-consilium generation used a 3+1 procedure: the three worker models independently completed each task, and GLM-5.2 consolidated their outputs. In both pipelines, the pre-consilium opinions were then passed to the interactive consilium. GLM-5.2 generated one response at each step, with a maximum of five review rounds. This produced 110 final consilium conclusions per pipeline. 3.6 Single-Model Evaluation The 190-case held-out cohort was generated separately with eight candidate models: DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Gemma-4-31B, GLM-5.2, GPT-5.6-Sol, GPT-OSS-120B, Qwen3.5-27B, Qwen3-235B, and Qwen3-Next-80B. Within each model- specific configuration, the same candidate model performed role assignment, generated every role-specific opinion, and conducted every review, revision, and finalization call. The role assignment for a case was reused by its free-form and structured pipelines. All models received the same ordered held-out case set and the same stage- and response-format-specific prompts and schemas. Generation used temperature 0, top-p 1, seed 42, and at most five review rounds. Model-specific maximum output allowances were retained: 1,024, 2,048, or 4,096 tokens for role assignment, 16,000 or 16,384 tokens for pre-consilium generation and 16,000 or 32,768 tokens for the interactive consilium. Endpoint routing and additional decoding controls were also fixed per model and recorded in the run manifests. The descriptive process and Judge-scored analyses used these eight configurations. Role-level Judge scoring was performed only for the primary role in the structured pipeline, whereas final consilium conclusions were scored in both the free-form and structured pipelines; role-level Judge scores were not produced for free-form outputs. 3.7 Evaluation Criteria We used a physician-developed measurement framework comprising eight response-quality criteria and three separately interpreted panel- or case-level ratings (Table 1). The framework was informed by literature on multidisciplinary clinical review [Lamb et al., 2011, Basta et al., 2017, Koˇco et al., 2021, Soukup et al., 2020], clinical LLM assessment [Tang et al., 2024, Kocaman et al., 2025, McCoy et al., 2025, Brodeur et al., 2026], uncertainty [Savage et al., 2025], and safety [Asgari et al., 2025]. The eight response-quality criteria covered factual grounding, problem prioritization, data use, reasoning coherence, differential diagnosis, the final clinical conclusion, management, and safety. Their applicability was determined by the component being assessed. The eight response-quality criteria used a 0–3 ordinal scale: 0 - indicated that the criterion was not met, 1 - a major deficiency, 2 - a mostly adequate response with a noticeable limitation and 3 - full and clinically convincing satisfaction.
