5 Each query vector is searched against the corresponding FAISS index. The closest clusters to the input image are found via k-means. The exact similarity within this cluster is then calculated via inner product similarity. The number of probed Voronoi cells is dynamically set to min(max(200, ⌊clusters/5⌋), 1500), balancing recall against search latency. A patch-level adaptable candidate pool is retrieved per query (default = 150,000). Retrieved patches are grouped by their source case. Each candidate is ranked by a sparse Chamfer score computed only over the patches retrieved. Chamfer distance scores two sets by summing each element's distance to its nearest neighbor in the other set, in both directions. Because patch embeddings are L2-normalized, nearest-neighbor matching under Euclidean distance and under cosine similarity is equivalent. The two principal terms are asymmetric as the numbers of patches per query and retrieved case vary. Thus, we use a normalized forward and backward term. The forward term averages, over query vectors, each query vector's cosine similarity to its single best- matching retrieved patch, measuring whether the case covers the query. The backward term averages, over the retrieved patches, each patch's cosine similarity to its single best-matching query vector, measuring whether the query is supported across the retrieved patches rather than by one coincidental match. This factor gets adjusted by a saturating term (1 − e−n/5) favoring more retrieved patches and saturating at around 15 patches. The final ranking score is the sum of the forward and the adjusted backward term.
