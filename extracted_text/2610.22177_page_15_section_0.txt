In each pair, the upper image shows reconstruction without gravity injection, and the lower image shows reconstruction with gravity injection. 4.4.3 Gravity Prior Analysis To evaluate the effect of gravity injection, we train two models under iden- tical settings: one with gravity prior and one without it. Figure 5 shows the reconstructed point maps predicted by the two models. The model trained with gravity prior produces more stable global structures, where ground sur- faces appear flatter and walls are more consistently aligned with the vertical direction. This observation suggests that gravity prior mainly improves reconstruc- tion by enforcing a global orientation reference rather than altering local ge- ometric predictions. By anchoring the reconstruction to a physically mean- ingful direction, gravity helps reduce structural drift and leads to more ge- ometrically coherent scene reconstruction. 4.5 Analysis We discuss the proposed priors from three perspectives: view-density behav- ior, architecture-dependent injection preference, and prior complementarity. First, the gain over the baseline increases with the number of input views. This trend indicates that the guidance of motion and gravity priors does not simply compensate for sparse observations; instead, they improve the consistency of cross-view geometric aggregation.
