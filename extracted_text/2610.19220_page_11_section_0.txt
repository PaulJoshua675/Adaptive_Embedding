Accumulating one repeated state makes the readout confident but wrong: the decision is read from the spread of states along the trajectory, not from any single state. (ii) Shuffled ticks. Feeding the same states to the memory in random order changes intermediate entropy but not the final readout (0.72 either way), as expected with inert decays: the memory is an order-free average of co- activations. (iii) Fixed norm. Rescaling the logits to a common norm removes the descent: normalized entropy stays between 0.03 and 0.12 from the first tick on. The entropy fall is therefore largely the growth of the accumulated readout’s magnitude, better read as a growing gain than as evidence accumulating. The decision itself settles earlier: the predicted class still differs from its final value for 38% of images at tick 4, 18% at tick 8, 12% at tick 16 and 8% at tick 40. Without attention, every image receives the same class (here “football helmet”), since z1 is a learned constant and no image information enters. 8. Smaller models Two D=128 models, one on MNIST and one on CIFAR-10, separate what is intrinsic to CTM inference from what ImageNet elicits. Both have a dense synchronization matrix: unlike the ImageNet checkpoint’s random pairing, every tracked entry is a pair among the first 16 neurons, giving action and output slices of 136 entries each. The CIFAR model uses a ResNet-9 backbone, which lifts its accuracy to 0.84 from 0.64 with the original backbone and lets us treat it as a competent classifier rather than a toy. The curve is universal, the transient amplification is not. Measured the same way as the ImageNet model, both have a spectral radius near a tenth at the fixed point (ρ(J) = 0.10 on MNIST, 0.068 on CIFAR), but a largest singular value below one throughout on MNIST (σmax(J) = 0.49, max 0.77) and in the median on CIFAR (0.36, max 3.06; about one CIFAR fixed point in ten does stretch)—these maps are Euclidean contractions, in contrast to the ImageNet map’s several-fold stretch (Table 2). They are nonetheless non-normal: already at initialization (normal share P |λ|2/∥J∥2 F ≈0.29, against 0.50 for a Ginibre matrix), and more so after training (0.16 on MNIST, 0.095 on CIFAR, against 0.020 on ImageNet). Training thus increases non-normality at every scale, more at larger scale, but only on ImageNet does it reach transient amplification. 11
