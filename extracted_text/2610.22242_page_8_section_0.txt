Baseline hyperparameters were selected using a budget of up to 50 random search steps per dataset, minimizing the integrated Brier score on an event-stratified 80/20 split within the seed-0 training partition. The selected configuration was reused across all ten evaluation splits, with each esti- mator refitted on the corresponding training split. Methods without tunable hyperparameters were evaluated directly. Search spaces and method-specific budgets are detailed in Appendix B.1. Cox regression uses an ℓ2 penalty to regularize estimation, including when p > n. SurvICL uses each dataset’s training split as context, with at most 4,096 labeled observations se- lected by event-stratified subsampling, without fine-tuning or dataset-specific hyperparameter tun- ing. Benchmark results for all methods and metrics are in Appendix D. Baseline runtimes measure fitting and survival-curve prediction, excluding hyperparameter search and metric computation. 6.4 RESULTS: SURVICL IS BEST OVERALL SurvICL is the best survival model overall. On the 63 held-out datasets, SurvICL wins 82.9% of pairwise comparisons in IBS, against 71.3% for the runner-up, the Cox model (Figure 1), and the dif- ference is statistically significant (Figure 4a, ranks and winrates are related by an affine transform). It also achieves the highest win rate in C-index, 77.7% against 71.6% for random survival forests (Figures 4b and 12). All existing survival TFMs trail behind classical methods. That the runners-up are the Cox model and random survival forests, with few parameters and strong inductive biases, underlines how hard survival analysis is for flexible models, which overfit small censored datasets. SurvICL is the first model to rank above Cox on small datasets. 69.8% of our held-out datasets have fewer than 1,000 samples, making this regime central and not a niche. SurvICL is the first to rank above Cox in IBS (win rate 84.9% against 77.2%, Figure 23) and it matches Cox in C-index, although these differences are not statistically significant (Figures 22 and 26). On large datasets, boosting remains strongest. On held-out datasets with more than 1,000 sam- ples, SurvivalBoost is clearly the best method in IBS (Figure 37, 86.9% winrate). SurvICL ranks second in average IBS and first in C-index 50th quantile, ahead of TabSurv-WSA, RSF, and all other TFMs, although it wins fewer pairwise comparisons than on small datasets (Figure 38). 8
