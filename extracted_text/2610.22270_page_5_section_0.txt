B. Evaluation Protocol For each configuration, Table II reports the number of clusters K selected by the Silhouette and Elbow criteria, together with the Shannon entropy of the distribution of RTP buses across clusters, defined as H = − K X k=1 pk log2 pk, (6) where pk denotes the fraction of all ground-truth RTP buses assigned to cluster k. The entropy equals zero when all RTP buses are grouped into a single cluster and increases as they become dispersed across multiple clusters. In addition, accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score were computed by treating the cluster containing the largest number of RTP buses as the predicted RTP class and comparing its members against the manually annotated labels. C. Quantitative Analysis The early fusion configuration with α = 0.65 achieved the best overall performance, with the highest accuracy (0.9521), precision (0.9507), and F1-score (0.7516) among all meth- ods. Compared with the morphological baseline, incorporating color information increased precision by 18 percentage points (from 0.7703 to 0.9507) and the F1-score by 7.6 points (from 0.6759 to 0.7516), while also reducing the entropy from 1.175 to 1.059 bits. In contrast, recall improved only marginally (from 0.6022 to 0.6215). This indicates that the main contribution of the color descriptor is to increase cluster purity: the dominant RTP cluster obtained through fusion contains far fewer non-RTP vehicles, most likely buses from other routes that share a similar body structure but differ in livery, which morphological features alone cannot reliably distinguish. Nevertheless, the recall of approximately 0.62 obtained by both the baseline and the best fusion configuration shows that a substantial fraction of RTP buses (about 38%) is assigned to clusters other than the dominant one. This is consistent with the nonzero entropy values and suggests that the RTP class is not homogeneous in the feature space, possibly due to variations in viewpoint, illumination, partial occlusions, or differences among RTP vehicle models and paint schemes. Performance did not vary monotonically with the color weight. With α = 0.50, precision remained high (0.8988), but recall dropped to 0.4774 and the entropy reached its highest value (1.680 bits), indicating that the RTP buses were fragmented across a larger number of clusters (K = 20). As a result, the F1-score of this configuration (0.6236) fell below that of the morphological baseline. It should be noted that, because both descriptors are unit-normalized and the weights act quadratically on the squared Euclidean distance, α = 0.50 assigns equal influence to both descriptors, whereas α = 0.35 gives color roughly 3.4 times the influence of morphology. The configuration with α = 0.35 illustrates the failure mode of an excessive color weight. Although it yielded the lowest entropy (0.012 bits) and a near-perfect recall (0.9989), these values do not reflect a successful separation. With only three clusters, virtually all RTP buses fall into a single cluster, but this cluster also contains a large number of non-RTP vehicles, as reflected by a precision of only 0.2606 and the lowest accuracy and F1-score of all configurations (0.6692 and 0.4133, respectively). In other words, the low entropy is a consequence of the collapse of the clustering space rather than of an accurate grouping of the target class. This case highlights that entropy and recall must be interpreted jointly with precision, since each of them can be trivially optimized by a degenerate solution. Accuracy should also be interpreted with caution. Because RTP buses represent a minority of the dataset, a trivial classi- fier that labels every image as non-RTP would already achieve a high accuracy. The F1-score and precision are therefore more informative indicators of the quality of the RTP cluster. Overall, these results show that, with an appropriate fusion weight, the proposed methodology groups the majority of the target class into a single, highly pure cluster. This supports our hypothesis and suggests that the approach can facilitate the detection of public transport vehicles without the need for large annotated datasets. D. Qualitative Analysis Visual inspection of the resulting clusters supports the quantitative findings. With α = 0.65, the Silhouette analysis indicated an optimal value of K = 14, and the clusters were highly homogeneous: vehicles with distinct shapes were correctly separated, while buses belonging to the same transit route remained chromatically consistent within their clusters. Fig. 3 shows twelve randomly selected members of the dom- inant RTP cluster. Increasing the color weight to α = 0.50 shifted the optimal number of clusters to K = 20. In this configuration, cohesive groups were split into fragmented sub-clusters that appeared to be driven primarily by minor variations in lighting or saturation rather than by differences in vehicle structure.
