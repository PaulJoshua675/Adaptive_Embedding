Since ˆPx,s is discrete and Px,s is not, the Wasserstein distance [27, Def. 2] and the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) distance [28, Def. 2.1] are the typical choices of d(·, ·) to construct Ux,s. When ˆPx,s and Px,s are parametric distributions (e.g., Gaussian, exponential family), divergences such as the Kullback-–Leibler (KL) divergence, or more general ϕ-divergence, are also applicable to particularize d(·, ·) because parameters can be estimated using samples.
