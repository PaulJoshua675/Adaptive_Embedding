Although cryptocurrency flow tracing is central to blockchain forensics, multi- token representations and tracing strategies remain underexplored. Early work has focused on Bitcoin, applying taint-allocation rules such as Poison, Haircut, FIFO, and LIFO [12], or representing taint flows as dynamic graphs [13]. For account-based blockchains, TRacer formulates tracing as a graph search over directed, weighted, temporal, and multi-relational networks [19]. Cryptocurrency flow is widely used to detect fraudulent and illicit activ- ity. On Bitcoin, MFScope traces funds linked to Dark Web addresses [8], while graph learning methods classify illicit Bitcoin transactions [16]. On Ethereum, DenseFlow identifies Ethereum laundering groups and pathways [9], and Wu et al. trace heist assets through token swaps and service providers [18]. Recent works incorporate transaction semantics for EVM and cross-chain tracing [4, 6, 17]. These methods rely on black-box machine learning models to flag suspi- cious activity from known illicit accounts, whereas our interpretable approach reconstructs multi-token paths, with forensic use as a possible extension. Ethereum token transfers have also been studied as account-level networks [2, 10, 15]. Within DeFi, prior network work has examined protocol compositions [7] and used token transfer motifs to identify transaction methods [14]. Studies of decentralized lending investigate motivations behind using Aave [3], liquidation risks [11], and time to repayments [5]. We complement this literature by con- necting transaction-specific account–asset states to trace how borrowed assets are used and transformed between Aave borrowing and repayment events. 3 Material and Methods 3.1 Data Acquisition and Preprocessing For each ego account and tracing instance, we retrieve the relevant history of native-currency, ERC-20 (fungible), ERC-721 (non-fungible, NFTs), and ERC- 1155 (multi-token) transfers from the Alchemy Transfer API [1]. Each transfer event records the block number, transaction hash, sender, recipient, token con- tract, asset symbol, transfer category, and the amount transferred.
