We initialize the single-mode and unified models from Wan2.2-Fun A14B-Control and evaluate them at 5,000 total training steps, with all other training variables held fixed. Evaluation covers EgoDex (Hoque et al., 2026), ARCTIC (Fan et al., 2023), HOT3D (Banerjee et al., 2025), and EgoLive (Li et al., 2026b). Tables 1 and 2 compare perception, prediction, and planning under these matched training conditions. Unified training improves both global hand placement and local articulation in PERCEIVE, reducing camera-space, root-relative, and PA errors by 23.46%, 14.23%, and 5.69%, respectively. In PREDICT, PSNR increases from 17.356 to 18.138 dB and FVD decreases from 1105.95 to 1009.84, indicating gains in both pixel accuracy and video feature similarity. The PLAN-only baseline is trained with visual subgoals. At evaluation, both models receive the same initial image, instruction, and goal image. We use matched generation settings and score the complete video, including the goal-conditioned ending, for both models. Unified training improves PLAN’s VLM scores across all five dimensions (Table 2). The largest gains are in temporal/identity consistency (2.15 to 3.57) and goal transition (1.98 to 3.29), while higher action-process and physical-interaction scores reflect more coherent manipulation sequences. Appendices C, E, and G.1 give mode exposure, evaluation details, supplementary human ratings, and qualitative comparisons. 4.2 PERCEIVE EVALUATION We compare video-based 3D hand reconstruction with HaMeR (Pavlakos et al., 2024) and Dyn- HaMR (Yu et al., 2025) on the same four datasets. Following ViDiHand (Wang et al., 2026a), we report presence-aware joint errors in camera space, after wrist alignment (RR), and after Procrustes alignment (PA) to assess global placement and local pose, using the protocol in Appendix D.3. Our model achieves the lowest presence-aware reconstruction error on all four datasets (Table 3). Average camera-space MPJPE-p is 63.05 mm, compared with 145.34 mm for HaMeR and 231.54 mm for Dyn-HaMR. Root-relative error is 22.54 mm, compared with 46.61 mm for HaMeR and 45.25 mm for Dyn-HaMR, while PA error is 11.79 mm, compared with 16.75 and 32.94 mm, respectively. Our model also achieves the highest hand-visibility F1 on all four datasets. Appendix F.1 separates joint accuracy from missing-hand penalties, and Appendix F.2 evaluates reconstruction against the original EgoDex joints. Qualitative results.
