4. Results 4.1. Datasets Table 4 summarizes the four Copernicus-Bench (Wang et al., 2025b) S1GRD tasks used for evaluation, each built from its own established source dataset: EuroSAT (Helber et al., 2019) (EuroSAT-S1), BigEarthNet (Sumbul et al., 2019) (BigEarthNet-S1), the 2020 IEEE GRSS Data Fusion Contest dataset (Robinson et al., 2021) (DFC2020-S1), and the Kuro Siwo flood mapping dataset (Bountos et al., 2024) (Flood-S1). The validation split is used solely for checkpoint selection (best validation metric). No hy- perparameters were tuned on the validation set after the pretraining configuration was fixed. 4.2. Baselines We compare against: (i) a non-pretrained ConvNeXt-RS baseline finetuned with identical protocol, isolating the pretraining contribution; (ii) a standard ConvNeXt (7×7, stride 32×) pre- trained with SparK-SAR at matched learning rate (lr=10−3), isolating the resolution contribution; (iii) four published frozen-encoder results drawn directly from their source papers: Copernicus- FM ViT-B/16 (139M parameters) (Wang et al., 2025b), SoftCon ViT-B/14 (86M) (Wang et al., 2024), CROMA ViT-B/8 (86M) (Fuller et al., 2023), and DOFA ViT-B/16 (111M) (Xiong et al., 2024). As ConvNeXt-RS is a novel domain-adapted architectural variant, no pre-existing ImageNet- pretrained weights are available with the 2× stem stride and 2-channel (VV, VH) S1GRD input. The non-pretrained random initialization baseline therefore serves as the appropriate control for isolating the SAR-specific pretraining contribution. 4.3. Main Results Table 5 presents our main results. SparK-SAR with ConvNeXt-RS and LLRD finetun- ing improves over the non-pretrained baseline by +4.7 pp, +5.0 pp, +5.8 pp, and +1.4 pp on EuroSAT-S1, BigEarthNet-S1, DFC2020-S1, and Flood-S1 respectively (UNet-128 decoder). The ConvNeXt-512 decoder achieves comparable performance while reducing pretraining VRAM by 7.5–8.5% per GPU, depending on hardware. Despite having roughly 5× fewer parameters than Copernicus-FM ViT (27.8M vs. 139M, frozen encoder), our finetuned model still comes out ahead on three of the four tasks: EuroSAT- S1 (88.49±0.16 vs. 87.2±0.1%), DFC2020-S1 (56.00±0.08 vs. 52.4±0.1%), and Flood-S1 (80.21±0.10 vs. 77.7±0.0%). On BigEarthNet-S1, SparK-SAR (77.03±0.40) falls marginally below Copernicus-FM (77.9±0.0%) and SoftCon (78.7±0.0%) under frozen evaluation. We note that Copernicus-FM incorporates tile geographic coordinates as an additional input modality via hypernetwork encodings (Wang et al., 2025b). BigEarthNet-S1 labels are derived from the CORINE Land Cover map (Sumbul et al., 2019; European Environment Agency, 2020), which exhibits strong geographic cluster- ing of land-cover classes across Europe. It is therefore possible that Copernicus-FM’s frozen score on this task partly reflects a geographic coordinate prior rather than purely learned SAR representations. SoftCon’s strong result likely reflects its multi-label land-cover guidance during pretraining (Wang et al., 2024), which directly benefits this multi-label classification task. These hypotheses are not formally tested and are presented as caveats. Table 6 reports frozen encoder evaluation results for SparK-SAR.
