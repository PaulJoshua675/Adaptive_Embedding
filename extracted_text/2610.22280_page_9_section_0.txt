Uncertainty Estimation We compare output probability-based uncertainty estimates with three representation-based alter- natives: Euclidean distance to class centroids, kNN over reference representations, and our pooled LDA using covariance-aware Mahalanobis distance. The probability-based baseline considers several uncertainty metrics, including negative log-probability, chosen-token probability, predictive entropy, and nucleus-set size under different aggregation schemes (Gupta et al., 2024), and reports the strongest one in each setting. As shown in Table 3, the effectiveness of each uncertainty estimator (i.e., how well it predicts task or sub-goal failure) varies across both backbones and execution stages. At the initial query-based uncertainty estimation stage, pooled LDA performs best for Nemotron, whereas probability-based baseline is stronger for Qwen3.8-27B. At the runtime step-wise estimation stage, our pooled LDA score achieves the highest AUROC for both backbones, although the overall effectiveness remains moderate. Overall, these results suggest that representation-based uncertainty can provide a training-free signal for runtime monitoring, particularly highlighting the effectiveness of our LDA-based estimation, while its effectiveness remains dependent on the backbone and execution stage. Meanwhile, the uncertainty estimator is modular in LEASH, allowing alternative, better uncer- tainty signals to be substituted without changing the escalation workflow depending on available resources or data (Park et al., 2026a,b). 4.4. Ablation Study Component-wise ablation. As shown in Table 4, the largest gain comes from enabling uncertainty- guided escalation: task success increases from 41.15% with the Manager to 67.94%, at a higher inference cost. Adding Shadow monitoring and checkpoint intervention further improves success to 73.66% with only a small additional cost, revealing an isolated effectiveness of the post-handoff intervention.
