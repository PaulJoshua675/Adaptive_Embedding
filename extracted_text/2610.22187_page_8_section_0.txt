For every design element, sensitivity under rubric prompting is within 0.007 of sensitiv- ity under zero-shot prompting, and all interaction terms are 0.006 or smaller (Table 6). The small differences that appear run in opposite directions across tasks. Rubrics slightly increase semantic sensitivity for political leaning and sentiment and slightly decrease it for toxicity. For RQ4, model choice affects scale sensitivity more than prompting strategy but much less than task. All five models are sensitive to changes in all three design elements in the same way (see Figure 4). Qwen3.8 27B is the most sensitive model and Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite the least sensitive. Within each model, sensitivity to the three design elements differs by at most five percentage points. 4.3 Relative Importance of Each Design Choice Regarding RQ5, the variance decomposition places all factors on a common scale (Figure 5) and shows that scale design and the task explain different aspects of sensitivity. Scale design explains the largest share of numerical sensitivity (40.3%) but only 8.9% of semantic sensitivity. The task ex- plains 29.7% of numerical sensitivity and 58.9% of semantic sensitivity. The model and the prompting strategy explain little of either. The model accounts for 2.6% of numerical and 1.3% of semantic variation, and the prompting strategy for less than 0.1%. Their inter- actions with scale design add 3.4 to 4.1% for the model and 0.2 to 0.3% for the prompting strategy. The second largest source of semantic variation is instead the interaction between scale design and task (16.0%), which captures combinations such as signed scales for toxicity. 4.4 Additional Analyses Moreover, we conduct two supplemental analyses.
