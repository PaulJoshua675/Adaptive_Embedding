The four outputs are the speed of each wheel, the signal transmission, and the control bid, which is only used within EASA 3.3.2 EASA Architecture EASA implements the basal ganglia model of Prescott et al. [10], based on Gurney et al. [22], with the same neurons, constants, and connections. We verified that our version reproduces the core of EASA with a maximum difference of 5 × 10-15 over 500 steps. Figure 3 shows the system organization. In this work, the basal ganglia choose among three channels. Two are innate. The exploration channel moves forward with a random turn that changes every one to three seconds, and the avoidance channel turns the robot away from the nearest obstacle. The third channel is the Res- GRU. The salience of each channel is a weighted sum of an obstacle detector, network bid, fear, and hunger. The basal ganglia disinhibit the channel with the highest salience, and a thalamocortical loop provides persistence for the selection. A channel passes its commands to the motor when its output from the internal globus pallidus is less than 0.4, and the commands from the selected channels are summed. Communication is not innate in any variant. Only the network can transmit or receive the signal. We used three variants of EASA. In the first, with manually set weights, the innate channels only keep the robot safe. The second adds an innate feeding channel that stops the robot over food.
