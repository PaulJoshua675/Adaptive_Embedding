String rules that graded semantics produced a plausible aggregate reward; semantic conditions belong in judge rubrics, not substring code. A “read all” that silently paginated made the bank entry count plateau at a default limit; pagination and token accounting must be tested explicitly. Failed writes checkpointed as empty were marked complete; platform faults must be retryable and excluded from denominators. Sandboxes that shared a trajectory identity completed requests attached to the wrong rollout; every request must validate bank and trajectory identity. Malformed output treated as an environment error made bad actions disappear from training; policy mistakes should remain valid low-reward trajectories. The governing rule is: a bad policy action must receive low reward; a platform failure must not be misrepresented as policy behavior. 10 Limitations and Future Work Attribution is evidence-based rather than counterfactual: recovered gold facts do not prove that a specific write caused an answer. Credit is truncated across optimizer steps, so earlier-bank errors cannot receive retrospective gradient. One-vote grouping fixes the baseline, but multi-call chunks can still carry more loss mass. Rubric rewards reduce sparsity but require human-agreement and judge-sensitivity checks. The reward artifact covers Chat, Working, and Coding but not every long-term-memory workload; EverMemOS is only one held-out framework. The primary matrix uses deterministic 20% subsamples, so 0.01–0.02 point margins are not statistically significant; repeated training seeds remain future work. Finally, pooled scores can hide whether gains come from extraction, update, deletion, reconciliation, or retrieval-facing representation. 11
