AI Consilium: a Benchmark of LLM Clinical Multidisciplinary Second Opinions A PREPRINT Example Scoring implication The source case contains no leukocytosis, C-reactive protein value, procalcitonin level, creatine kinase value, troponin value, or acute kidney injury, but the consensus report presents these findings as facts and uses them to support a diagnosis. This is not a minor inaccuracy. Factual accuracy should receive 0 points. Reasoning, differential diag- nosis, the final conclusion, and management strategy should receive no more than 1 point if they rely on these data. The consensus report states that C-reactive protein may be elevated and should be checked, although no C-reactive protein result is available. This is a hypothesis rather than a fact. It is permis- sible if it is clearly framed as a hypothesis and not presented as an already available test result. Procedure for scoring one case row 1. Identify the case number in the spreadsheet. 2. Read the clinical case description, including the patient characteristics, main complaints, important laboratory results, imaging, clinical course, treatment, and safety risks. 3. In the pre-consensus stage, identify the block corresponding to the rater’s specialty. 4. Assign eight scores for the “Reasoning” component in columns B–I. 5. In the same specialty-specific block, identify the “Final conclusion” section and assign eight scores for the “First-stage final judgment” component in columns J–Q. 6. Proceed to the overall consensus stage and assign eight scores for the “Consensus conclusion” component in columns R–Y. 7. Assign the whole-case scores: column Z for adequacy of the specialist composition, column AA for clinical complexity, and column AB for the overall quality of all stages. The complexity score is the rater’s expert judgment from the perspective of the rater’s specialty. 8. Complete the general comment in column AC. Scores of 0 or 1 should be explained whenever possible. Criterion-specific scoring rules Factual accuracy and sources Assess whether the facts in the consensus report match the source case, including symptoms, dates, laboratory values, test results, diagnoses, procedures, and treatments. Score Assign this score when 0 A fabricated or distorted clinically significant fact is present and could alter the diagnosis, risk assessment, or management strategy; or the conclusion directly contradicts the source data. 1 There are several factual errors or one important unsupported interpretation; the sources are unclear; however, the entire conclusion does not depend exclusively on fabricated data. 2 The main facts are reported correctly and no clinically significant fabricated facts are present, but there are minor inaccuracies or incomplete source references. 3 All important facts, numerical values, dates, and investigations are accurate; their sources are clear; and hypotheses are distinguished from established facts. Identification and prioritization of clinical problems Assess whether the report identifies what is clinically central, what is secondary, and what represents an immediate threat.
