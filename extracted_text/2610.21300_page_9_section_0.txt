A route is the way a model is reached (a provider’s API, a cloud platform or a self-hosted server) and a deployment is a model reached through a partic- ular route. The configuration Θ is everything other than the inputs that can change an output: the extraction, chunking and embedding components, the retrieval parameters, the prompt template and label set, the model weights, the inference engine’s ver- sion and settings, including its prefill chunk budget and concurrency limit, and the hardware class (GPU model with its driver and CUDA versions); the inputs are the document bytes and the assessment items, and a manifest is the recorded listing of Θ. Byte- level reproducibility means that, for a fixed Θ, every intermediate result and output is identical byte for byte across runs that differ only by operations that should not matter: repeating, restarting, interrupt- ing and resuming, reordering, changing concurrency, and adding and deleting a document. We use repro- ducibility in the computational sense; in the terms of FDA’s draft guidance on AI-enabled device software [69], repeating a request under the same conditions tests repeatability and agreement across days, orders, concurrency and machines tests reproducibility under pre-specified changed conditions. Documents and assessment items. The main corpus is the public FDA documents for over-the-counter blood glucose test systems (prod- uct code NBW; in vitro diagnostic devices for self- testing in the EU) with a decision date from 2015 to 2025 in openFDA and a published 510(k) summary: 133 510(k) summaries, 132 decision summaries and 72 predicate summaries, 337 PDFs. The assessment items are the 11 sentences of FDA’s 2020 guidance for these systems that state a numeric minimum of a counted item (“at least” or “a minimum of” followed by a numeral and a count of meters, strips, vials, lots, days, measurements or concentrations). An item is paired with a device when the device’s decision sum- mary has a section whose heading matches the item’s topic, so that every pair has a section in which to look for evidence; 83 devices had such a section, giv- ing 776 pairs, of which the reference run labelled 356 Cannot determine (324 by the model and 32 by the verification rule), 380 Satisfied and 40 Not satisfied. We restricted the pairs in this way because the hard case for reproducibility is a judgment that has to find and quote evidence. The second corpus is the 43 FDA 510(k) summaries for infusion pumps (product code FRN) cleared in 2015–2025 and the 42 predi- cate summaries they cite, 85 PDFs; its 12 items are every 8.9th of the 107 sentences containing “should” or “recommend” in Section 5 of FDA’s infusion pump guidance (Supplementary Table S9), each assessed for every device (516 pairs). Both pairing rules were fixed before any assessment. Experiment 1: commercial deployments. The 70 prompts were built from the 776 pairs, ordered by 510(k) number and item, by a fixed rule without randomness: every 38th pair for the 20 short prompts (the 510(k) summary; 12–47 thousand characters), every 19th for the 40 mid prompts (the device’s docu- ment set; 31–119 thousand) and every 77th for the 10 long prompts (the set plus other devices’ summaries; 483–502 thousand), skipping pairs whose summary had under 1,000 characters of text; the series overlap, so the 70 prompts come from 49 distinct pairs. Each prompt was sent five times on each of two consecutive calendar days (JST), one request at a time, from a virtual machine in Google Cloud us-central1; the days differ by deployment (Supplementary Table S2), and the same-day route comparisons re-sent the OpenAI API’s short prompts and claude-opus-5-5 through the Claude API on the days of their counterparts. The size of the experiment was set by cost; five iden- tical runs exclude only a per-run change probability above 45% at the one-sided 95% level, so a zero in Table 2 means that no change was observed, not that the deployment is stable. Requests were sent by Python’s standard HTTP client without vendor SDKs, asking for temperature 0, seed 0 and JSON- schema output wherever accepted, with no reasoning parameter and an output limit of 16,384 tokens; set- tings a provider rejected were removed and recorded. Both OpenAI models, and claude-opus-5-5 and claude-haiku-5-5, rejected temperature; the Gem- ini deployments accept the field but ignore it [24]. Reasoning tokens were reported on every call of the Gemini deployments and of gpt-6-luna, on most calls of gpt-6-astra and the Claude 5.5 models, and on none of claude-haiku-4-5. Of the calls reported, 78 to the Claude API failed when a credit balance ran out and were resent, 12 to Azure were retried after a rate-limit response, and 39 of gpt-6-luna and gpt-6-astra reached the output limit with- out parsable JSON and count as differing in every field. The system prompt, the user prompt format and the JSON schema are reproduced in Supplemen- tary Note 1; the 70 prompts, their hashes and every response with its headers, timestamps and usage fields are in the supplementary material, and Supple- mentary Table S8 gives every deployment, date, start and end time and estimated cost ($1,291 in all, from provider-reported tokens at list prices).
