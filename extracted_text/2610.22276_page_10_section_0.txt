Numerical discretization. For numerical estimation, we examine whether finer candidate intervals im- prove prediction accuracy. Keeping the same coarse intervals, we reduce the positive leaf cells from 960 to 216 by replacing 32 fine bins per interval with the 4/8-bin design. Although the coarser codebook has higher quantization error, it reduces VST-300 dev MAE from 0.3277 to 0.3202 m (Table 4a). The result shows that finer representational precision does not necessarily translate into better learned predictions. Using the same 216-cell model, replacing argmax decoding with a conditional expectation increases MAE to 0.3300 m (Table 4b). Point prediction requires both a precise output representation and enough visual detail to distinguish nearby locations. We investigate whether original-image context or reused features can replace fresh crop encodings while retaining localization accuracy. Removing crops from the trained model reduces RefSpatial accuracy from 51.00% to 9.00%; training a model without crops recovers 46.50%, showing the importance of adapting training to the chosen visual input (Tables 5 and 18). Crop re-encoding nevertheless achieves the highest RefSpatial and Point-Bench scores among the four visual strategies, reaching 51.00% and 56.84%, respectively. Replaying visual tokens or attention K/V does not recover these scores. We therefore retain crop re-encoding to supply the local visual evidence needed by successive region decisions. We also evaluate direct visual-token pointing, which replaces the three-stage hierarchy with a single decision over the original image’s visual tokens. For the native Q/K model, connected-component decoding improves counting success from 12.76% with argmax to 32.14%. Selecting the decoder according to the task raises the overall Point-Bench score from 50.76% with argmax to 54.63% (Table 19). The hierarchical crop model retains stronger RefSpatial and Point-Bench performance (Table 20), while these results show how adapting the decoder makes the one-stage alternative more effective.
