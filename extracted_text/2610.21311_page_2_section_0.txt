The core idea is to enhance overall performance by transferring knowledge gained from solving one (often simpler) task to assist in solving another, structurally sim- ilar but more complex task. Their seminal work demonstrated the efficacy of the proposed Multifactorial Evolutionary Algorithm (MFEA) on a set of combinatorial optimization problems (two quadratic assignment problems and one capacitated vehicle routing problem), showing faster convergence and a lower makespan (total time to complete all tasks) compared to sequen- tial single-task optimization. For the general more complex multi-objective scenarios, incorporating the total order defined by the non-dominated sort- ing and the crowding distance from the NSGA-II (the most intensively used multi-objective evolutionary algorithm) [DPAM02], Gupta et al. [GOFT17] further extended this MFEA framework to a multi-objective version, called MO-MFEA. Similar empirical evidences of the advantage of the multitask optimization were also shown for the composite manufacturing processes (resin transfer molding and injection/compression liquid composite mold- ing). Since then, the evolutionary multitask optimization attracts more and more attention for the complex problem solving, like the point cloud regis- tration [WSD+24], the jop shop scheduling [CLW+25], etc. One could refer to [WWZ+22] for more evolutionary multitask optimization algorithms and applications. Despite empirical success, a rigorous theoretical understanding of EMTO, particularly its runtime behavior, has lagged far behind. The first theoretical runtime analyses were conducted by Lian et al. [LHZC19], where a simplified (4+2) MFEA was analyzed with 4 for the population size and 2 for the number of tasks. They proved the runtime for the single-objective Jump can be improved from O(nk−1) to O(n2 + 2k) if it is solved with Leading- Ones simultaneously, and the runtime for LeadingOnes remains O(n2). LeadingOnes and OneMax task pair and TrailingZeros and OneMax task pair were further analyzed in [HCZ20]. They proved that (4+2) MFEA achieved a speedup of Θ(n/ log n) for the LeadingOnes in the former pair, but TrailingZeros cannot be sped up in the latter pair. The reason is that the two tasks in the latter pair are with less similarity. Crucially, all (only two) existing theoretical results are for the domain of single-objective optimization. The extension to the more complex multi- objective domain presents substantial new challenges. As introduced before, the MO-MFEA (the multi-objective variant of the most representative evo- lutionary multitask algorithm MFEA) incorporates sophisticated the total order defined in the NSGA-II, while the first mathematical runtime analysis of the NSGA-II was only recently conducted [ZLD22, ZD23a].
