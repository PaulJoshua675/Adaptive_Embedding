App. C.1 gives the fixed ablation sampling protocol, and App. E lists the sampling settings used for the main comparisons. Architecture and training. Tab. 9 lists the generator architectures and training hyperparameters, which follow BAR (Yu et al., 2026). The class label enters as repeated condition tokens, and for global-augmented generation the global token is likewise repeated after them. For classifier-free guidance, a fixed fraction of training samples drop their conditions. Most of these drop both the class label and the global token, and the rest drop only one of them, so the generator also learns each condition on its own. Following BAR, training starts with randomly permuted token orders, linearly anneals the probability of a random order to zero, and ends with raster order. The global-token loss weight λglobal of Eq. 6 depends on the encoder and, for SigLIP, on the generator size, with λpatch = 1 −λglobal. The value 1/257 weights the global token roughly like each of the 256 patch tokens, and the ablation generators use λglobal = 0.5. Generators train on tokens precomputed with the EMA weights of their tokenizers, on NVIDIA H200 GPUs. Generator at512×512. At 512×512, we train a single BAR-L generator on the tokens of the 512×512 tokenizer (Tab. 8). It models a sequence of one global token followed by 1024 patch tokens of 10 bits each. Following the 512×512 recipe of BAR (Yu et al., 2026), it trains for 200 epochs, half the 256×256 schedule, with warmup and random-order annealing scaled accordingly. We set λglobal = 1/1025, which weights the global token like each of the 1024 patch tokens. Tab. 10 lists all settings and marks those that differ from the BAR-L column of Tab. 9.
