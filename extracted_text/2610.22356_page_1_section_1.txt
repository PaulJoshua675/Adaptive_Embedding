Spatial reasoning connects visual understanding to agentic action in the physical world (Chen et al., 2024, Su et al., 2026, Huang et al., 2025, Zhang et al., 2025a, Driess et al., 2023, Huang et al., 2023). Tasks like multi-view question answering, long-video understanding, robot manipulation, and embodied planning require more than recognizing visual content (Yang et al., 2025a, Mangalam et al., 2023). Specifically, a vision-language model (VLM) must also perceive 3D relations across views and, as the observer moves, keep updating the position and heading on which its subsequent spatial judgments depend (Chen et al., 2024, Yang et al., 2025a). Despite strong progress in semantic recognition and language reasoning, VLMs remain unreliable at estimating distance, direction, camera motion, and cross-view correspondence, and weaker still on tasks such as route planning that chain several such judgments (Yang et al., 2025a, Zhang et al., 2026b). Existing work attempts to enhance spatial reasoning by introducing 3D priors.
