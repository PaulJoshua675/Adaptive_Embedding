Every architecture in a controlled comparison uses weight decay 0.1 on matrices. The effect of weight decay can nevertheless differ across architectures, because nGPT normalizes weight rows and GCT canonicalizes branch proposals. On scale-invariant parameter blocks, weight decay affects the effective learning rate through the parameter norm (van Laarhoven, 2017; Li and Arora, 2020). We assess sensitivity to this shared setting with weight-decay controls. Re-running nGPT without weight decay, as recommended by Loshchilov et al. (2025), changes nGPT’s 51M loss by +0.012 nats, and setting weight decay to zero changes the other 51M baselines by at most 0.004 nats, with −0.004 for Pre-LN and +0.001 for LLaMA-style, while GCT changes by +0.003, leaving the clean-loss ordering unchanged. Removing both weight decay and warmup, as in nGPT’s original recipe, while retaining our other settings increases nGPT’s best loss at the two tested high learning rates by 0.093 nats, from 3.500 to 3.593. These controls do not indicate a disadvantage from the shared recipe in this setting. UCT corrective modules. UCT makes residual additions unit-consistent and gives softmax and gates dimensionless inputs through three added modules: an RMS-matching residual adapter h+αℓ[RMS(h)/ RMS(a)]a, the empirical attention temperature from Eq. 3, and normalized gates. We use UCT to test whether these three local corrections improve a conventional Transformer’s performance and robustness to changes in scale. UCT details. Empirical attention scales sQ and sK are per-head stop-gradient RMS batch statistics with an exponential moving average (EMA) of momentum 0.99 used at evaluation. The gate scale sa is a per-layer scalar EMA. The residual adapter uses per-token RMS matching with a learnable scalar αℓfor each branch. The pre-normalizers and configured converter denominators use fixed numerical floors of ϵ = 10−6. Both these floors and the evaluation-time EMA scales stay numerically fixed under an injected rescaling. When the hidden state is scaled down, the fixed floors contribute more to the normalization denominators, and the stored EMA scales no longer match the activations. Dividing rescaled activations by these unchanged quantities does not undo the rescaling, which explains the loss of equivariance under the most severe down-scaling in Figure 5. GCT details. Canonicalizer floors are fixed numerically at 10−12 inside the square root. The floors have the formal squared unit u(ϵx) = u(x)2. The numerical canonicalizer floors stay fixed under an injected rescaling, producing the finite defect in Eq. F.1. Exact invariance refers to the zero-floor or co-transforming-floor statement. The implementation uses the projection denominator ∥¯h∥2 + ϵ⊥with ϵ⊥= 10−6, whereas the exact orthogonality and rotation results use ϵ⊥= 0 as stated in App. F.1. Per-head gains and the logit gain initialize at 1.0. Attention and MLP sub-blocks are sequential, and each sub-block has a separate αℓ. Embeddings are canonicalized at entry, followed by a final canonicalization before the tied head. Heads, scales, and parameters. The classification head produces dimensionless logits from C(¯hL) with a learnable dimensionless logit gain. For regression and PINN predictions that require units, the head first produces a dimensionless value ˆ¯y. Multiplying by an explicit output scale gives ˆy := syˆ¯y, which restores the magnitude and units. We parameterize each learned scale as s := s0eℓs, where the fixed reference s0 has the required unit and the trainable log multiplier ℓs is dimensionless. Within each block, linear maps mix dimensionless coordinates using dimensionless weights. Any required unit conversion is performed by an explicit scale factor, such as sy at the output. For tasks with dimensionless outputs, such as language modeling, the computation does not depend on the scale state, and we implement the residual stream using only canonical coordinates.
