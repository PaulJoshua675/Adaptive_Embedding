When Inputs Receive No Score 3 Admission and Complete-Stream Evaluation 3.1 The item and its score An input 𝑥𝑖is a complete Web item with its displayed text and recorded context. Its Gold label is Admissible (𝑦𝑖= 1) when the evidence foregrounds an identifiable real-world occurrence, action, change, result, or institutional act suitable for organization. Other- wise it is Reject (𝑦𝑖= 0). Historical and personal events can qualify when central to the item. A hypothetical or a background reference does not qualify on that basis alone. Annotation uses the displayed body, timestamp, and reply, quote, and media-presence indicators, without opening links or independently checking factual truth. A known target, exact date, or named location is not required. A candidate-dependent route extracts event-like expressions and may retrieve targets before constructing a score. Direct admission scores the item without extraction or retrieval, although direct scoring can also fail. Extraction deliberately includes expressions broader than the admission policy, including hypothetical and back- ground references. This makes the same evaluation answer both how scores discriminate and what happens to inputs outside the scored popu- lation. 4 Data and Experimental Design 4.1 Human evaluation and the layered resource The resource contains 3,290 records. Its human-annotated layer con- tains 872: 602 held-out evaluation items, 100 independent calibra- tion items, and 170 retained development items. Original-302 con- tains 209 Admissible and 93 Reject items from targeted collection. Random-200 contains 32/168, sampled uniformly after deduplica- tion from a prespecified ten-minute Bluesky window. Challenge-100 contains 31/69, sampled from the same window using semantic- boundary cues for background references, hypotheticals, personal narratives, and opinions. Random tests transfer to an independently sampled live-Web cohort, while Challenge tests difficult admission boundaries. Independent annotation produced 239/302 initial agreements on Original, with 63 disagreements adjudicated. The combined live-Web package produced 284/300 agreements (94.7%, 𝜅= 0.845), with all 16 disagreements adjudicated. Evaluation uses the result- ing human Gold. DEV30 has 12 Admissible and 18 Reject items within the retained development material. Independent DEV100 and the derived DEV130 union support Qwen calibration sensitivity. Evaluation, development, and reserve allocations are separated by item-ID, normalized-text, external-URL, and near-duplicate checks. The separate AI-assisted observational layer contains 818 topic- targeted Twitter items (555 Admissible, 263 Reject) and 1,600 ran- dom live-Web items (322/1,278). The latter draws 800 from an un- used original-window pool and 800 from an independent second window. Two AI annotators agreed on 1,543 items, and humans adjudicated all 57 disagreements. Consensus cases retain AI labels. This layer broadens the resource but supplies neither Gold EVAL nor calibration labels. 4.2 Strategies, backbones, and paired precision We compare three strategies under the same admission policy.
