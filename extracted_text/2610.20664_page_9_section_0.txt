IG-OPD raises the hit rate of the first search from 12.9% to 16.9% and of any search from 34.1% to 39.1% by step 40, with the gap widening as αk ramps up, while the number of searches per rollout stays essentially unchanged (4.35 vs. 4.26). Calibrated supervision therefore improves the queries the student issues rather than their number, consistent with the 4B student’s advantage over its teacher on the multi-hop benchmarks in Sec. 5.2. Evidence calibration adds modest training overhead. Figure 4(c) compares training costs of OPD and IG-OPD on the same hardware. Time per step rises from 397 to 443 seconds (+11.6%), while peak GPU memory increases from 68.1 to 75.3 GB (+10.6%). This cost arises during training, where post-retrieval information gain is evaluated to calibrate supervision. The increase is moderate relative to the performance gains reported above. Early on, a positive jump from low potential Φt is decisive; later, small fluctuations are mostly verifica- tion noise, while large drops signal misleading evidence. The cosine ramp prevents early search suppression. Early in distillation, weak student retrievals yield predom- inantly negative IG. Unramped IQC (αmax at step 0) turns this into a general search penalty, dropping average roll- out length from 6.06 to 5.29 turns over the first 40 steps on training set (Figure 5). The cosine ramp keeps IQC weak initially while the student acquires teacher search behavior, ensuring IG refines rather than suppresses search once fully active (preserving 5.78 turns).
