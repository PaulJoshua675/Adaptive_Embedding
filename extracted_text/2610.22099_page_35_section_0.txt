The total response length remains 256 tokens, and the global decoder budget is divided equally across blocks, including one deterministic finalization per block. Consequently, increasing the number of blocks changes both the visible-context structure and the number of learned decisions available within each block. Transfer is strongest when sufficient within-block compute remains available. At T = 64, the original full- canvas and transferred two-block configurations reach 62.67% and 62.89% micro-accuracy, respectively; the four- and eight-block configurations retain 59.81% and 55.25%. At T = 16, however, accuracy decreases from 47.00% on the full canvas to 22.43% with eight blocks. In the latter case, each block receives only two calls: one learned decision and one finalization. The results therefore show useful transfer to smaller blocks, particularly at larger budgets, while also exposing the cost of reducing the number of decisions per block.
