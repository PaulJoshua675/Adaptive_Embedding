Java length predictor. We train a Java-specific length predictor using the LLaDA-1.5 architecture and objective of Section B.4, with edits from 21 training and four development repositories in the 28-repository corpus; the remaining three repositories and all SWE-Refactor repositories are excluded. Training prompts contain an optional helper declaration, D accepted <old>/<new> demonstrations, and J targets with visible <old> spans and one placeholder per missing <new> span. We use seeded, disjoint site assignments, retain up to 20 context lines before and 12 after each edit, and exclude empty replacements, lengths above 256 tokens, and prompts exceeding 2,048 tokens. Reference lengths are withheld from the inputs, with prompt-invariance checks to detect leakage. This yields helper-only and all-multi-site corpora with 1,887/370 and 13,543/3,598 training/development prompts, respectively, where the latter includes the former. Training alternates between the corpora equally for 313 updates (batch size 64, 16 warm-up updates, cosine decay), with other settings following Section B.4. We also train an alternative on the all-multi-site corpus alone and select by exact-length probability mass F0 on 488 helper-only development targets (0.0664 versus 0.0660), although within-two-token mass F2 would favor the alternative (0.317 versus 0.328); SWE-Refactor outcomes are not used for selection. Length evaluation. At evaluation, the selected predictor runs once per task and independently samples target lengths for each of K = 16 rewrites, without temperature adjustment or truncation. Oracle lengths equal the developer-reference token counts; 3.3% of predicted per-target lengths exceed the benchmark’s 80-token reference-length eligibility limit. We compare length fidelity against the empirical distribution of 2,467 replacement lengths in the helper-only training corpus, distinct from the demonstration-length prior in Section 4.3. Across 80 unique target sites, the predictor assigns 26.7% probability mass within two tokens of the reference length ([20.1, 33.8]), compared with 15.2% for this prior; exact-length mass is 6.8% versus 3.1%. However, only 6.2% of learned-length joint samples allocate every target exactly. Among learned-length samples, 95% with exact joint allocations are judged correct, compared with 13% for near-but-incorrect allocations and 8% otherwise. These descriptive associations help explain the large oracle-versus- learned accuracy gap in Table 1, while highlighting the importance of accurate joint length allocation.
