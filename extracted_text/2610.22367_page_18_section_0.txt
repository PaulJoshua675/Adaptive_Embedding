SYNDROME DECODING FOR DQI Consider an optimization problem over a finite field Fq. An assignment x ∈Fn q produces m linear values Bx. Given a set Fi ⊂Fq of acceptable values for each constraint, let f(x) = |{i : (Bx)i ∈Fi}| count the constraints satisfied by x; the goal is to maximize f(x). This family, known as max-LINSAT, includes linear constraint-satisfaction problems such as Max-Cut and max-XORSAT, as well as nearest-codeword problems and polynomial fitting over finite fields. Decoded quantum interferometry (DQI) is a quantum algorithm that aims to solve these optimization problems by arranging for constructive interference on assignments satisfying many constraints and destructive interference on assignments satisfying fewer [1]. DQI prepares a superposition over sparse vectors e ∈Fm q , each supported on at most ℓconstraints. Here H = BT can be viewed as a parity-check matrix, making e a low-weight error and He its syndrome. If the associated code has minimum distance dmin ≥2ℓ+ 1, a decoder can recover e uniquely from that syndrome. The inverse quantum Fourier transform (QFT) then produces amplitudes Ax that favor assignments satisfying more constraints. The dominant cost in Equation (23) is uncomputing e, which requires solving a classical decoding problem; DQI thus reduces optimization to decoding. The space-efficient construction of Khattar et al. [13] replaces the middle decoding-and-uncomputation step with a phase correction. It generates one field symbol ei at a time, adds its contribution to s, and then removes it with an X-basis measurement. This avoids retaining all m symbols of e, but the measurement outcomes ci leave a known phase. We expand each symbol of e ∈Fm q in the fixed binary basis used by the circuit, so that c and e are bit strings of equal length; the phase is then (−1)⟨c,e⟩2, where ⟨·, ·⟩2 denotes the binary inner product. We call c the mask. In this section, we use a fixed, randomly chosen mask. A reversible decoder must remove this phase using s, after which the inverse Fourier transform produces the desired interference over assignments x.
