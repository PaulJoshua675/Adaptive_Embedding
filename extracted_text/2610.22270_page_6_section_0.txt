The model became largely insensitive to structural differences, grouping morphologically distinct vehicles, such as vans and full-size buses, into the same cluster simply because they shared dominant colors. Overall, these results confirm that color is a highly discrim- inative cue for identifying public transport routes, but that it must complement, rather than dominate, the morphological representation. A moderate color weight improves the purity of the target cluster, whereas over-prioritizing color degrades the structural understanding of the vehicles and leads either to over-segmentation or to the collapse of the clustering space. V. CONCLUSIONS This study presented an unsupervised pipeline for clustering public transport vehicles captured by a surveillance camera, in which frozen DINOv2 morphological features and HSV color histograms are combined through weighted early fusion and grouped with UMAP and K-Means. Without using any labels during feature extraction or clustering, the best configuration isolated RTP buses in a cluster with higher precision and F1- score than morphological features alone. These results confirm that color provides information that self-supervised features alone do not capture well, since it separates buses that share a body structure but belong to different routes. The ablation study also showed that the fusion weight is crit- ical. A balanced weight fragmented the target class into many small clusters, whereas a color-dominant weight collapsed the space into three clusters that mixed vans, microbuses, and full- size buses. Color should therefore complement, not replace, the morphological representation. The approach has limitations. Some RTP buses were still assigned to secondary clusters and the number of clusters was selected with internal criteria that do not guarantee semantic correspondence. Future work will explore late fusion based on descriptor-specific distances combined with density-based algorithms such as HDBSCAN, which do not require the number of clusters to be fixed in advance. Evaluation on multiple cameras and routes will also be necessary to assess the generality of the method.
