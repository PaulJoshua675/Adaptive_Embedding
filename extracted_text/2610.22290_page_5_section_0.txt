For evaluation, we use BIRD dev to measure in-distribution perfor- mance and Spider 1.0 dev (Yu et al., 2018) to as- sess out-of-distribution generalization on databases disjoint from those used in training. On both bench- marks, correctness is measured by execution ac- curacy, comparing the results of generated SQL queries with those of the gold queries on their re- spective databases (Appendix B.4). To examine the post-training effects in an un- related domain, we also evaluate on MATH-500 (Hendrycks et al., 2021). Finally, to assess the prac- tical value of preserving diverse solutions, we eval- uate on AmbiQT (Bhaskar et al., 2023), a bench- mark derived from Spider where each question ad- mits two valid interpretations due to ambiguity (e.g. in column or table names, join paths, etc). We draw n = 128 samples per question. Baselines. We compare ELITEREPLAY against standard GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and three meth- ods aimed at preserving diversity or improving pass@k. MaxRL (Tajwar et al., 2026) upweights hard questions through a maximum-likelihood objective, while DPH-F (Li et al., 2026b) uses forward-KL regularization to preserve the base model’s behavior on questions it already solves reliably. SetPO (Li et al., 2026a) encourages diver- sity by adding to each rollout’s advantage a bonus for how much it increases the semantic diversity of its sampled group. All methods are trained on the same data for 100 steps, with ELITEREPLAY using a replay weight of µ = 0.05 (Appendix C.3).
