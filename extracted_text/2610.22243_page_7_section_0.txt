5 Cue edits can change guard decisions We use each system as a pre-execution step judge over inert text. Released TS-Guard plus six hosted configurations supply the original-input screen; Q8 TS-Guard, one-draw GPT-5.5, and repeated Haiku and Luna runs score the paired edits. For Haiku, both audited cue edits materially change blocking and remain distinct from the formatting placebo. 5.1 Association selects the intervention target Among gold-safe AgentDojo rows, the literal marker is observationally associated with blocking. Released TS-Guard blocks 9.66% of 321 marked safe rows versus 6.03% of 547 unmarked safe rows, a 3.62 pp clustered difference [0.09, 7.33] pp. GPT-5.5 blocks 17/320 valid marked-safe rows (one marked-safe row is invalid) versus 6/547 unmarked-safe rows, a 4.22 pp difference [1.70, 7.05] pp. Claude Haiku 4.5 shows the strongest association in the seven-configuration screen: 74.45% versus 0.37%, a 74.09 pp difference [68.67, 79.75] pp. GPT-5.6 Luna also has a positive observational gap of 10.48 pp [7.17, 14.11] pp. These comparisons are confounded: marked and unmarked records differ in request, history, argu- ments, and risk. The Haiku association therefore nominates a guard for intervention; it is not itself an estimate of marker use. 5.2 Repeated intervention reveals Haiku cue sensitivity All 960 Haiku calls parse, resolve to the requested model and Anthropic provider, and use one attempt.
