We report regional route completion to characterize the effect of this selection using the same completion requirements as the expert evaluations. A. Escapability Scoring For a grasp g, escapability is the fraction of transitions attempted from g, each to a single commanded goal, that reach the commanded position and grasp mode under the requirements of Appendix IV. Targets are drawn over the eligible mode-compatible position voxels 6–8 cm from the source voxel’s representative position, with a budget of 3 s (150 control steps) per attempt. The score is specific to the frozen WBD-EX expert. We evaluated all 2,510,864 grasps across the fifteen objects with 128 sampled targets per grasp, for 3.2 × 108 single-goal transition trials. Figure 12 shows that the distribution’s shape, not just its mean, varies strongly with geometry. The two most slender objects (the ∅120 × 220 mm cylinder and the 80 × 160 × 220 mm prism) have nearly flat distributions with mean escapability near 40% and only about 1% of their grasps in the top band. Sphere grasps are increasingly concentrated at high escapability with diameter, from 28% of grasps in the top band at ∅120 to 62% at ∅220, with means of 64% and 86%. Thus, grasps that pass grasp simulation filtering can still have low transition success under their fully trained expert, and the distribution of this success varies across the object-specific grasp datasets. B. Removal Threshold Selection Within each original 2 cm position voxel, we remove the lowest-scoring grasps up to a nominal percentage cap, keeping at least twenty grasps where available and preserving every original voxel–grasp-mode pair. We use the twelve non-loop evaluation routes of Table VII. We re-score each expert on the saved evaluation trials whose starts survive the cut, sweeping the cap from 0 to 90%. Figure 13 shows mean regional route completion for each object across removal caps. We use 2,048 bootstrap resamples of evaluation trials, shared across routes and removal caps. At the adopted cuts, the per-object means in Fig. 13 improve by 1.2–13.3 percentage points over the full grasp dataset baseline across all fifteen objects. The cut preserves every occupied voxel–grasp-mode pair and preserves at least 98.8% of each object’s average per-joint span at every cap up to 70%. It preserves spatial and grasp-mode coverage while changing the distribution of grasp configurations within each voxel. The removal each object tolerates tracks the shape of its escapability distribution. Objects with flat distributions take small cuts for small gains: 10% removal for 1.2 points on the slender prism and 30% for 4.0 points on the slender cylinder, against 20–60% removal for 5 to 13 points on the spheres. Where escapability is uniformly moderate, there is little low-escapability tail to remove, and the curve for the slender prism is nearly flat across the whole sweep. At larger removal caps, completion for the slender cylinder and the two largest spheres peaks and then declines.
