Our results show that graph structure can improve evidence retrieval without graph access at query time. Graph-modified embeddings guide the search for additional evidence, while the original embeddings maintain semantic relevance to the query. This supports developing graph-modified embeddings specifically for multi-stage retrieval and evaluating their contribution to the complete retrieval process. Retrieval should adapt as evidence accumulates. The question and earlier results should guide when to use graph information, how to allocate the remaining search budget, and when enough evidence has been found to support an answer. Future evaluation should test the approach on natural corpora, additional embedding models, and graphs with missing or incorrect relations. It should also establish whether smaller retrieved contexts offset the cost of additional searches. The broader goal is to design retrieval with the final answer in mind, using semantic similarity and graph structure together to provide the evidence needed within practical computational limits.
