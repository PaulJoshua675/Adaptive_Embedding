Round 9 is the best on both benchmarks and no later round improves either one. Beyond the settings in Section 3.1, all rounds share micro batch size 1 and minimum learning rate 10−7. 32 rounds are recorded as reweight and one as replay. Both are changes of proportion, which is why Section 3.1 places the whole run in mixture. Round 24 is the replay, and its records show why the distinction does not survive from-base retraining: it asks to reuse round 9’s row selection, but no round inherits anything from an earlier one, so the request is served by reassembling a recipe from the same pool. The result is 34,283 rows against round 9’s 34,184, with 599 row instances missing and 698 added. Replaying a category and restoring its proportion produce the same kind of recipe. Condensed trace Figure 10 condenses the run into the rounds that carry something the main text relies on. Two of them are worth naming here. Round 15 is the only round whose recipe matches an earlier one exactly, and the agent did not set out to repeat anything: it was moving the seed forward from round 14 and landed back on round 9’s configuration by accident, which is what makes the pair usable as a measure of how much a repeated round moves. Rounds 19 and 24 are the two places where what the agent says it is doing and what its plan does come apart, which is why we label actions from the difference between consecutive recipes rather than from the agent’s description of a round.
