Multi-PersonaIF. This dataset targets instruction following under explicit constraints, following the methodology of AllenAI’s Persona IF (Lambert et al., 2024). Instructions are generated by ran- domly sampling 1–3 constraints from a predefined list derived from IFEval (Zhou et al., 2023b), us- ing persona descriptions from a multilingual ex- tension of PersonaHub (Ge et al., 2024) to diver- sify instruction styles. Responses are generated by the same model immediately after instruction generation. We replicated the full pipeline using Qwen3.5-122B-A10B and translated and adapted all prompts, seed instructions, and constraint defi- nitions for the five target languages. One method- ological modification was introduced: the original constraint sampling procedure can yield logically incompatible combinations (e.g., write in lower- case and include a minimum number of capitalized words). We revised the sampling logic to prevent such conflicts. Multi-Safety. A small dataset of harmful request– safe response pairs was constructed internally: harmful prompts were authored by team mem- bers, and safe responses were generated using Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct. English annotation was performed only for datasets where English content was generated from scratch, i.e., Multi-PersonaIF and multi-smol_summarize. 3.5 Quality Assessment Metrics. Inter-annotator agreement was com- puted using Gwet’s AC2 (Gwet, 2008) with quadratic weights on the collapsed three-way la- bel set (A/B vs. C vs. D). AC2 was preferred over Cohen’s κ (Cohen, 1960) because the annotation distributions are strongly skewed toward the posi- tive category (A/B), a condition under which κ is known to produce paradoxically low values even when observed agreement is high. The quadratic weighting scheme is appropriate for ordinal scales, where the distance between non-adjacent categories (e.g., A/B and D) should be penalised more heavily than disagreements between adjacent ones. Results. Agreement results are reported in Ta- ble 4. The distribution of raw annotation labels across datasets and languages is provided in Table 3. Agreement is consistently high across datasets and languages, with values exceeding 0.90 in the major- ity of cases. This indicates reliable interpretation of the annotation schema and stable quality sig- nals. Three datasets show lower but still substan- tial agreement: Multi-FLAN (FR: 0.93, DE: 0.90), Multi-PersonaIF (FR: 0.88), and Multi-TableGPT (ES: 0.87), reflecting known task-inherent chal- lenges discussed in Appendix C.
