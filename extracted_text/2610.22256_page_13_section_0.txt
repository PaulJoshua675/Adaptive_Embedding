Using the mean observed sparsity ρ = 0.9273 as an illustrative cache sparsity, Equation (11) reduces one set of expanded BF16 MLP activations from 3.9375 GiB to approximately 0.534 GiB without sign-bit packing or 0.516 GiB with packing. These estimates include 0.246 GiB of masks and 0.00192 GiB of tile-prefix metadata. Using the minimum batch-wise mean sparsity ρ = 0.9176 instead gives 0.572 and 0.552 GiB, respectively. The sparsity measurements were taken after squaring, while the cache stores unsquared ReLU values, so these are estimates rather than exact cache measurements. Reconstruction can still require a dense temporary, and the attention, Mamba, residual, and output-head tensors are not compressed by this method. Scaling and relation to the benchmark. At fixed precision, parameter-related storage is independent of input length and microbatch size. The linear-width activation footprints above scale by M/4096, where M counts all tokens in the microbatch; materialized attention scores instead scale with batch size times squared sequence length. Wider dense projections increase parameter counts approximately quadratically, while the listed feature tensors grow linearly with width when expansion ratios and head dimensions are fixed. The measurements in Appendix D.4 omit optimizer updates and release parameter gradients as they are produced. They therefore exclude optimizer state and a retained full-model gradient buffer, but transient gradients and operator workspaces still contribute to the measured peak. Inference KV and recurrent-state caches are disabled for these forward/backward runs. Since tensor lifetimes overlap only partially and allocator reservation may exceed live storage, the rows above should not be summed into a predicted peak-memory total.
