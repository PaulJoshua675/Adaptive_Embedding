LLM multi-agent systems. Multi-agent frame- works divide tasks among LLM agents with distinct roles (Guo et al., 2024b). For instance, AutoGen supports configurable conversations that combine LLMs, tools, and human input (Wu et al., 2024). MetaGPT encodes standard operating procedures as prompt sequences to coordinate software devel- opment and check intermediate results (Hong et al., 2024). Our system, ReviewPilot, leverages the po- tential of multi-agent workflow to review stages. Deep research and literature synthesis. Deep re- search tools in ChatGPT (OpenAI, 2025), Gemini (Citron, 2024), and Perplexity (Perplexity, 2025) iteratively search and analyze sources to produce reports. ChatGPT or Gemini can link citations and allows users to revise its research plan. For scien- tific literature, OpenScholar combines passage re- trieval, cited generation, and iterative self-feedback (Asai et al., 2026), while AutoSurvey retrieves pa- pers, constructs an outline, and delegates subsec- tion drafting to specialized LLMs (Wang et al., 2024b). These systems can synthesize reports but do not document the paper filtering process based on PRISMA 2020 (Page et al., 2021). Systematic review tools. Some tools support indi- vidual review stages. ASReview prioritizes records for human screening through active learning from relevance judgments (van de Schoot et al., 2021); LGAR ranks abstracts zero-shot using graded, criterion-based LLM scores and dense reranking (Jaumann et al., 2025). RobotReviewer extracts trial characteristics and supports risk-of-bias assess- ment with sentences linked to the source document (Marshall et al., 2017). Integrated workflows extend assistance across stages. AgentSLR pairs an epidemiological litera- ture review workflow with stage-specific evaluation and describes an interface for expert verification of extracted values (Padarha et al., 2026).
