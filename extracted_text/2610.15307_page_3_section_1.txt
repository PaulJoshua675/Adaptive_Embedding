2.1 Neural Architecture Search (NAS) Neural Architecture Search (NAS) aims to automate the design of high-performing neural networks, but its practical use is often limited by the high cost of evalu- ating candidate architectures in large search spaces. Early NAS methods based on reinforcement learning and evolutionary algorithms [37,26] typically require training or partially training many architectures, leading to substantial com- putational overhead. To improve efficiency, differentiable and low-cost search strategies [15] have been proposed to reduce the reliance on exhaustive evalua- tion. In parallel, benchmark suites such as NAS-Bench-201 [7] provide standard- ized search spaces with ground-truth performance records, enabling controlled analysis of efficient NAS algorithms.
