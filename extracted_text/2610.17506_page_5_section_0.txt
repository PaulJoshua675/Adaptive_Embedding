2 Reservoir Computing and Echo State Networks Reservoir computing is an approach to neural network modeling that leverages the dynamics of a fixed, recurrent network of artificial neurons to perform general-purpose computations. When the dynamical memory of the reservoir balances signal retention with signal separation over time, the projection of inputs onto the nonlinear reservoir may be used to learn a linear "read-out" function, analogous to how biological intelligences may flexibly exploit naturally- occurring intrinsic dynamics for cognition without requiring task-specific wiring (Maass et al., 2002). Because the read-out function is linear, it can be solved directly using a least mean squares algorithm instead of a learning algorithm like backpropagation that requires gradual weight updates over repeated input presentations. As a result, reservoir networks offer simple, dynamical models of processing that have been applied to modeling the brain (Enel et al., 2016), chaotic time series forecasting (Jaeger & Haas, 2004; Pathak et al., 2018), speech recognition (Verstraeten et al., 2006), and many other classification and prediction tasks. We employ ESNs as a dynamical model of decision-making that can be readily extended to collective processing among two or more networks. 2.1 Modeling Methods We trained pairs of ESNs to classify speech signals from the Japanese Vowels dataset (Kudo et al., 1999) that consists of 640 recordings of nine native, male Japanese speakers pronouncing the vowel cluster /ae/ repeatedly. We randomly sample 28 recordings for each of the nine speakers for training data, which were cepstrum-coded to produce 12 linear prediction coefficients (LPCs) that encode the auditory signal on each time step, 25.6 msecs per step, with frames shifted by 6.4 msecs. Twenty-four additional recordings per speaker were sampled for the testing set. The first 10 LPC time steps of each recording were 𝑧-score transformed (recordings less than 10 time steps in length were excluded) and used as sequential inputs to the reservoir. The speaker identification task was coded by nine read-out units, where each unit represented one of the nine speakers and target vectors were one-hot coded. Each ESN was composed of three layers: An input layer, a recurrent reservoir network, and a read-out (output) layer. Input nodes were randomly connected to reservoir nodes with a probability of 𝑝𝑖𝑛𝑙𝑖𝑛𝑘, and input weights were randomly set to 1 or -1 with equal probability. Symmetric weights serve two primary functions. First, zero-mean input weights (paired with zero-mean inputs) prevent a systematic directional bias in how external inputs perturb the reservoir. Second, high variance input weights increase the effective rank of the reservoir state matrix. This reduces correlations among reservoir nodes and maintains diversity in the reservoir state trajectories, which improves the linear separability of the resulting reservoir features.
