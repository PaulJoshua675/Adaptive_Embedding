We replay all 18 retained-parent predictors on CPU, first verifying exact agreement with the original aggregate counts for every sentence. Interior scalar gaps are then partitioned using the recorded Python runtime’s Unicode whitespace predicate. The four disjoint strata have whitespace only to the right (before whitespace), only to the left (after whitespace), on both sides (between whitespace), or on neither side; sentence endpoints are excluded. Table 21 gives the gap counts and target-cut frequencies. Whitespace constitutes 21.86% of Korean scalars and 2.44% of Chinese scalars, so the Chinese text contains whitespace despite its lack of regular word spacing. Table 22 reports missed cuts plus false cuts as a percentage of each stratum’s gaps, averaged across seeds. Within-scalar errors remain part of the primary metric but lie outside this scalar-gap partition. The analysis was chosen after the aggregate results and describes errors on the original inputs; it neither removes whitespace nor evaluates linguistic word segmentation. Every before-whitespace gap is a positive target in these texts, while most after-whitespace gaps are negative, making the distinction between the two sides consequential. At gaps with no adjacent whitespace, UBE’s k = 1 error is lower than both controls in every seed of each language.
