Iterative retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) builds on standard RAG (Lewis et al., 2020; Fan et al., 2024) by interleaving retrieval and reason- ing to answer complex questions (Trivedi et al., 2023). Intermediate findings identify new infor- mation needs and guide subsequent searches. Re- cent methods organize acquired knowledge into evolving representations. KAIR maintains an in- dex of salient keywords and their relations (Wu et al., 2026), MGranRAG combines evidence mem- ory with graph-calibrated passage rankings (Hu et al., 2026), and CIRAG integrates candidate triples using retrieval history (Wei et al., 2026). This knowledge-state updating preserves and prior- itizes acquired information for subsequent retrieval and answer generation. Knowledge-state updating represents facts and relations from retrieved evidence to guide retrieval and generation. As retrieval progresses, newly acquired evidence can establish entity correspon- dences and relational dependencies that change how earlier facts support compositional reason- ing. However, accumulating relevant facts does not ensure that these emerging relations inform sub- sequent retrieval decisions and answer derivation. Figure 1 illustrates this issue with an example from MuSiQue. This raises an open question. How can iterative RAG turn progressively acquired knowl- edge into reliable cross-round reasoning? We propose RCER, an iterative RAG method that complements knowledge-state updating with cross-evidence relational reconstruction to estab- lish how accumulated facts jointly support reason- ing for the current question. Inter-Round Rela- tional Evidence Acquisition jointly analyzes earlier and newly acquired passages to establish entity correspondences and relations. These judgments specify or revise the next information need, guiding dynamic problem decomposition and subsequent retrieval. After acquisition, Reasoning Context Re- construction independently analyzes the final can- didate pool to select complementary passages and derive cross-document inferences. Cross-Evidence Compositional Reasoning uses these inferences alongside the selected sources to bind entities and compose relations under the question’s conditions. The main contributions are as follows. • We propose Inter-Round Relational Evidence Acquisition, which jointly analyzes accu- mulated source passages to update earlier facts’ inferential roles with new evidence.
