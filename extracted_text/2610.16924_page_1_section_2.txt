Spiking neural networks (SNNs) integrate stateful neuronal dynamics with sparse, event-driven communication (Maass 1997; Roy, Jaiswal, and Panda 2019), enabling highly energy- efficient computation on neuromorphic hardware. With re- cent advances in surrogate-gradient learning, backpropaga- tion through time (BPTT) (Werbos 1990) can be effectively applied to SNN training. This has allowed SNNs to achieve competitive performance across a growing range of static or ∗Corresponding author. short-sequence tasks, such as image classification and object tracking (Neftci, Mostafa, and Zenke 2019; Wu et al. 2018). Despite the great potential, training SNNs for long- sequence tasks remains an open challenge due to linearly increasing computational and memory costs. Take the stan- dard leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) spiking neurons as an ex- ample. Although the subthreshold dynamics of LIF neurons are linear and theoretically parallelizable, threshold-based spike generation introduces resets and alters the subsequent evolution. Since we cannot obtain the spikes and resets in ad- vance, forward propagation has to proceed sequentially over time, resulting in training costs that grow with the number of time steps. To improve SNN capabilities for long-sequence learning, several parallel training approaches have been explored in recent years. For instance, some methods, such as Parallel Spiking Neurons (PSN) (Fang et al. 2023), achieve paral- lelization by completely removing the reset mechanism to decoupletemporal dependencies.Alternatively,ForwardPar- allel Training (FPT) (Feng et al. 2025) treats the soft-reset LIF neuron as a trajectory-solving problem, evaluating all time steps in parallel via iterations. However, these previous works face significant trade-offs: modifying the core spiking dynamics sacrifices the temporal expressivity and hard-reset nature of standard SNNs, while existing iterative solvers re- quire backpropagating through all solver iterations, incurring excessive memory overhead. Moreover, most of these meth- ods sacrifice incremental step-by-step inference capabilities while achieving parallelization, which is disadvantageous for deployment. Therefore, achieving efficient parallel training without compromising the fidelity of recurrent LIF dynamics or deployment inference remains largely unsolved. To address these limitations, we propose FidLIF, a frame- work for reconciling parallel forward trajectory recovery and temporal credit assignment with high-fidelity recurrent hard-reset LIF neurons. In forward propagation, FidLIF ap- plies Newton iterations to a differentiable trajectory equation, where the Heaviside function is relaxed by a sigmoid during the iterative solve. At each iteration, linearizing the causal LIF dynamics yields associative temporal updates, allowing all time steps to be updated in parallel with a scan. After the trajectory is recovered, binary spikes are produced using the original Heaviside gate, preserving binary spike commu- nication.
