The mixing coefficient ω balances outcome and process supervision. Table 3 sweeps ω ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.5}. Performance peaks at ω = 0.3 across all four splits. Setting ω = 0 disables step-level supervision yet still outperforms GRPO on BrowseComp-ZH and GAIA, suggesting that grouping by knowledge state implicitly regularizes learning even before any step reward is applied. Setting ω = 0.5 overweights process supervision and degrades on three of four splits, reflecting that excessive local optimization can pull the policy away from the terminal objective. We use ω = 0.3 throughout the rest of the paper. Both incremental gain and knowledge-state grouping are individually necessary. Table 4 decomposes KnowGPO against GRPO. Adding incremental knowledge gain to GRPO (+∆KG, ap- plied without knowledge-state grouping) improves over GRPO by 1.0% to 2.8% across benchmarks, confirming that knowledge-grounded step rewards are useful even without proper grouping. Further introducing knowledge-state grouping (KnowGPO, full) yields an additional 1.0% to 4.2%, with the largest gain (+4.2%) on BrowseComp-ZH. The role of grouping is to restrict normalization to steps that share the same prior knowledge state, so that within-group comparison contrasts decisions made from the same epistemic position rather than across mixed positions; the ablation isolates this effect from the choice of process reward. Incremental knowledge gain outperforms dis- counted future returns as a process reward. Within the same knowledge-grouped framework, replacing incremental gain with the discounted future return Gt used by GiGPO drops accu- racy by 3.0%, 6.0%, and 3.4% on Xbench-2505, BrowseComp-ZH, and GAIA respectively (Table 4, row "KnowGPO w/ Gt"). The two rewards differ in how much downstream stochasticity they absorb: Gt couples a step’s credit to all subsequent deci- sions and is sensitive to long-horizon noise, while incremental knowledge gain is a local, immediate signal that does not require waiting for the trajec- tory to end. The gap shows that, even when group- ing is fixed, the choice of step reward materially matters for long-horizon credit assignment. 4.4 Why Knowledge-Based Grouping Works The ablation shows that knowledge-state group- ing matters in aggregate. We now examine why it works by directly measuring the grouping behavior of three candidate anchors on real rollouts. Setup. We sample 500 queries from Xbench and BrowseComp (Wei et al., 2025) and rollout the SFT checkpoint up to 16 times per query, pro- ducing 5,922 valid trajectories with 55,571 steps after filtering. We compare three grouping an- chors: observation-anchored, matching steps by BGE-large cosine similarity over retrieved contents at threshold 0.9 (the protocol used by GiGPO); action-anchored, matching steps by similarity of generated queries; and knowledge-anchored (ours), matching steps by exact equality of accumulated knowledge state. Grouping quality is measured by the effective step ratio (ESR, fraction of steps in non-singleton groups), the singleton ratio, the mean non-singleton group size, and the cross-rollout cov- erage (average number of distinct rollouts per non- singleton group). Formal definitions are in Ap- pendix D. Knowledge state aligns far more steps than ob- servation or action. Table 5 reports the five met- rics.
