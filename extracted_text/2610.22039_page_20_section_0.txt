GRPO is critic-free: for each task we sample G = 8 rollouts, score each with RWEAVE, and take the advantage of a rollout to be its reward centered and scaled by the mean and standard deviation within its own group. No value network, and hence no generalized advantage estimation, is used. Group-relative normalization makes the gradient invariant to task difficulty, so that easy and hard tasks contribute comparably. Group-relative normalization also explains why reward density matters here beyond the usual sparse-reward argu- ment: a group whose G rollouts all receive the same re- ward contributes no gradient at all, so a sparse reward wastes rollout budget outright rather than merely learning slowly. Because Graph Credit is continuous and dependency- conditioned, its groups almost always exhibit within-group spread. The empirical consequence is quantified in §D.3. Three-phase curriculum. RL training follows a DAG- complexity curriculum over the 3,200-task pool. Phases are cumulative—each admits all tasks from the previous phase— and are allocated equal thirds of the 400 policy updates: • Phase 1 (updates 1–132): DAGs with depth ≤2 and |V | ≤ 3. Eligible tasks: 1,048. • Phase 2 (updates 133–264): DAGs with depth ≤3 and |V | ≤5. Eligible tasks: 1,864. • Phase 3 (updates 265–400): all tasks, sampled uniformly. Eligible tasks: 3,200. Phase membership follows directly from the WEAVE-Train node and depth distributions (Table 20); depth is measured as the number of edges on the longest path, consistent with §B.3.
