H Per-Language Correction Effects The main results report intervention effects aver- aged over query languages, which can hide which languages a correction actually helps. Figure 1 breaks the effect down by language, comparing baseline, mean removal, and disagreement-guided steering on Recall@20 for an encoder (mE5-large) and a decoder (Qwen3-8B), on the parallel Bele- bele collection and a subset of the non-parallel Multi-EuP. On Belebele, both corrections lift the lowest- scoring languages most, compressing the spread across languages, and the two behave similarly. The non-parallel Multi-EuP subset separates them sharply and reveals where steering matters. For the encoder, mean removal collapses on several lan- guages, dropping recall to 16 on Portuguese and 24 on Irish, precisely the cases where its per-language mean is least reliable on non-parallel data. Steering does not: it restores recall to 80 on Portuguese and 58 on Irish, and improves the low-resource Maltese from 50 to 81. On the remaining languages, the two are close, with mean removal holding a small edge on a few. Steering thus rescues exactly the languages the alternative abandons. The decoder behaves differently. Its baseline re- call is already high and uniform across languages, so there is little for either correction to recover, and mean removal is marginally better throughout. The per-language view, therefore, mirrors the aggregate trade-off at its extreme: steering is most valuable where the model and setting are hardest, the en- coder on non-parallel data, and least needed where retrieval is already strong. J Agreement Under Interventions We report cross-lingual agreement under each cor- rection: set agreement A1 (Jaccard overlap of top- K lists) and rank agreement A2 (Spearman over shared documents), averaged over languages on Belebele (Table 12). Both corrections generally raise agreement over the baseline, most on the self- crowding models, where interference was largest; on bge-m3, whose baseline agreement is already high, steering leaves it roughly unchanged. The de- crowding, therefore, reflects convergence toward the cross-lingual consensus rather than added noise.
