The crossover is sharpest on mnist: single-tree search is 1.8× faster than dual-tree search at k=1 but 3.1–3.5× slower at every k≥5. Corel shows the same pattern at smaller scale (single-tree 1.7× faster at k=1, dual-tree 1.2–1.6× faster at k≥5); on optdigits single-tree leads through k=10, the two tie at k=25, and dual-tree leads at k=50. On covtype dual-tree search is best at every k, and the largest mode-choice penalty in the table is there: single-tree search is 7.0–7.5× slower at every k. Batch single-tree is never the fastest mode on these four datasets; at k≥5 it is within 30% of the best only on optdigits. On mnist and covtype the gaps are well outside the confidence intervals; on corel they overlap slightly at k=5, and on optdigits the modes are within their intervals throughout. Single-tree search runs one independent descent per query, pruning against that query’s current kth-best distance. At k=1 that radius is the nearest-neighbor distance, which is small, and the dedicated k=1 path keeps the per-candidate bookkeeping to a single comparison: on mnist the single-tree all-NN issues 6× fewer distance computations than the dual-tree traversal (312M vs. 1.87B, Table 9). As k grows, the pruning radius widens to the kth- nearest distance and every one of the n independent descents pays for it separately: from k=1 to k=5, single-tree time on mnist grows 6.5× while dual-tree time grows 1.2× (Table 4). Dual-tree traversal [39] amortizes that cost by pruning entire (query-subtree, reference-subtree) pairs, but it pays a per-pair overhead in exchange. When both nodes are internal it runs a three-phase expansion, producing O(|childrenq|×|childrenr|) candidate pairs per internal node—typically 9–25 at base 1.3, each a recursive call—and it maintains a per-query-node bound cache with epoch inval- idation, and computes phase-1/2 distances that are wasted whenever the pairs they would pre-filter are pruned anyway.
