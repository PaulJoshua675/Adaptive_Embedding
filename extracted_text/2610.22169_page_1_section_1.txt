The vocabulary of a language model is its na- tive semantics: the embedding and the unem- bedding fix the meaning of a state at the two ends of the residual stream. Between them, ex- isting methods either reuse a fixed lens or treat the states as a point cloud, and neither reads a state the way the model does. We argue that every hidden state has a native reference frame, induced by the layers that read it next and adap- tive to the context. Lexical reference frames approximate it by reading a state through the model’s own remaining layers with a stated number of rounds of attention between posi- tions and a typical attention output for the rest, without fitting to the model’s output. No lens, with fixed directions per layer, can exceed the square root of the native frame’s coherence, 0.30 and 0.35 at mid-depth of SmolLM3-3B and OLMo3-7B, whereas the first-order frame, LEXFRAME[1], reaches 0.97 and 0.97 and reads a state within 0.16 nats of the model’s output. Its coefficients attribute predictions more faith- fully than attribution patching, its lexical basis steers a prediction while sparing other tokens, and its confident readings would let an early exit skip 0.45 and 0.42 of the layers, where lenses skip almost none. In this way, lexical reference frames make every hidden state of the model readable, attributable and steerable.1 1
