2.1 STREAMING MULTIMODAL MODELS AND BENCHMARKS Video multimodal models have progressively evolved from offline video understanding (Maaz et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024b;a; 2023) toward real-time streaming interaction, where models continuously perceive incoming observations and decide when and how to respond as the stream unfolds (Chen et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025b; Qian et al., 2025; Cui et al., 2026). Existing training and evaluation formulate real-time interaction as responding to a continuously arriving but sparsely sampled audiovisual stream, commonly at around 1–2 FPS (Chen et al., 2024; Qian et al., 2025; Yan et al., 2026; Lu et al., 2026b). Under this setting, existing benchmarks focus on evaluating the abilities of streaming perception (Lin et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2025; Shi et al., 2026), response timing (Wang et al., 2025a; He et al., 2026; Lu et al., 2026b), interruption handling (Lin et al., 2025; Lu et al., 2026b), and conversational quality (He et al., 2026; Lu et al., 2026b) in scenarios such as surveillance anomaly detection (Wang et al., 2025a), question answering during gym exercises and shopping, and step-by-step task guidance (Lu et al., 2026b), leaving untested whether models can act effectively in closed-loop environments where the timing of their decisions affects subsequent states and task outcomes. In contrast, Realtime-Bench evaluates closed-loop interaction in web environments and games, where task success depends on both what actions models take and when they take them. 2.2 REAL-TIME EVALUATION IN INTERACTIVE ENVIRONMENTS Existing interactive evaluations assess asynchronous vision-language agents in games that pause during model inference, allowing their perception, reasoning, and planning abilities to be evaluated independently of response speed (Tan et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026; Tan et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2026; Ouyang et al., 2026). To evaluate agents under real-time pressure, VideoGameBench (Zhang et al., 2026), GameWorld-RT (Ouyang et al., 2026), GPTNT (Parekh et al., 2026), and Gaia2 (Froger et al., 2026) allow game or web environments to continue evolving during inference, making task success depend on both decision quality and wall-clock latency. Although such wall-clock capture the consequences of slow responses, they also tie evaluation to deployment-specific inference speed. Similar to Real-Time Reasoning Gym (Wen et al., 2026), we adopt Token Clock, which maps gener- ated tokens to environment time at a shared rate, preserving the temporal cost of deliberation while decoupling evaluation from physical serving speed. 3 SETTING FORMULATION 3.1 REAL-TIME STREAMING CAPABILITIES We define real-time streaming capability as the ability to perceive, reason, and act effectively while the environment continues to evolve.
