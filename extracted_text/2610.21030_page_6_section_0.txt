Setting III evaluates endogenous reasoning, the new paradigm of AURA, where reasoning and em- bedding formation are performed within the same model. Our method further introduces utility es- timation to adaptively allocate inference-time rea- soning and avoid unnecessary refinement. 4.5 Main Results Table 1 reports retrieval performance of differ- ent models measured by nDCG@10 on BRIGHT benchmark (Hongjin et al., 2025). First, Setting II consistently outperforms Set- ting I across retrievers. This result is aligned with recent reasoning-intensive retrieval literature and suggests that augmenting retrieval with external query rewriting can substantially improve retrieval quality. Notably, Setting II baselines use a sepa- rate external reasoning model (GPT-4) for query expansion while keeping the retriever unchanged. Across methods, this external reasoning strategy appears to provide the largest improvements for lexical retrievers such as BM25. AURA achieves the best overall performance among the compared systems with an average nDCG@10 of 34.7. Despite not relying on any ex- ternal reasoning model, AURA outperforms all Set- ting II baselines that are powered by GPT-4 query expansion. The gains are especially pronounced in the theorem-based subsets, where AURA shows the largest margin over existing approaches. The strongest external reasoning baseline, DIVER- Retriever-4B, achieves 32.1 with GPT-4 expanded queries, while AURA improves this to 34.7 (+2.6) using reasoning generated entirely within the re- trieval model itself. Compared with Setting I, the advantage of adap- tive reasoning is even larger.
