Dataset aggregation. In multi-step environments, let Ht = (p, y1, o1, . . . , yt−1, ot−1) denote the trajectory history at step t, comprising the instruction prompt p, past actions yi, and observations oi. At each step, both models propose actions: the weak model generates K sampled actions {yw 1 , . . . , yw K} and K sampled actions {ys 1, . . . , ys K} from the strong model. (K = 10 across all environments). For simplicity and consistency across agentic baselines, we fix the weak-model candidate selection policy ϕ(Ht) to execute the first sampled action (yw 1 ). Using the task-specific transition agreement indicator A defined in Section 5, we compute empirical disagreement targets bd(Ht, yw i ) and bg(Ht). We record the weak model’s hidden states: the final history token representation h(Ht) for the self-disagreement probe bfg, and the mean hidden-state representation over action tokens h(Ht, yw i ) for the weak–strong disagreement probe bfd. Stochastic routing during collection. In the first phase, the weak model acts at every step. In subsequent phases, we fit the probes on the accumulated training data and keep them fixed throughout collection.
