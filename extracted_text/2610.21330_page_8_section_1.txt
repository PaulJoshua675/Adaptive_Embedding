In this work, we introduce VoteGEO-Bench to investigate the po- tential risks of GEO in voting scenarios. We find that generative engines frequently cite lower-cost source channels and that some webpages remain visible across days, motivating our subsequent evaluation of how webpage rewriting affects electoral exposure. Our experiments show that explicitly specifying the target is im- portant for effective rewriting, while Structure and Argumentation strategies show stronger gains across engines and elections. We fur- ther find that rewriting gains remain broadly positive across voter personas, while partisan information can strongly shift baseline exposure. Future work may explore ethically controlled end-to-end evaluation and further investigate contextual sentiment, factual quality, semantic fidelity, and human persuasion. 8 Limitations and Ethics Statement Offline evaluation. Our rewriting experiments use fixed historical webpage pools with web retrieval disabled, leaving the effects of rewriting on live retrieval and citation selection unexamined. A live end-to-end evaluation would typically require publishing rewritten political content for crawling and indexing by search and generative engines. Even after removal, such content could persist in caches or archived snapshots and potentially reach real voters. To avoid these risks, we conduct all rewriting experiments offline without publishing rewritten political content to live websites. Exposure and factuality. Our evaluation measures changes in candidate or party exposure without distinguishing favorable from unfavorable mentions or assessing factual accuracy and voter per- suasion. Moreover, we do not systematically verify the factual cor- rectness or semantic fidelity of rewritten webpages. Some estab- lished GEO Evidence strategies may introduce supporting material not present in the original webpage, while our Structure and Argu- mentation prompts instruct the rewriter to preserve the original fac- tual information or core position. We therefore interpret observed gains only as changes in exposure, rather than improvements in information quality or evidence of voter persuasion. Privacy and dual-use risks. The persona analysis uses previously collected datasets and reports aggregate results without target- ing individual voters. Nevertheless, releasing rewriting strategies and their effectiveness comparisons carries dual-use risks, as these findings could be used to influence political exposure in deployed systems. We hope these findings help researchers understand the effectiveness and risks of GEO and inform safeguards against ma- nipulation through retrieved web content.
