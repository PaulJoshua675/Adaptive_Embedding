Progress on extreme weather forecasting with AI models should consequently be assessed beyond aggregate global scores. Evaluation across additional seasons, including periods without observed cyclones, would test the robustness of these gains and assess the calibration of cyclone-occurrence probabilities. Decomposing forecast performance into location, intensity, uncertainty, and dependence makes such evaluations actionable for model development: it identifies what needs improvement and what should be preserved, while providing more specific evidence about what learned forecasts capture of extreme weather. ETHICS STATEMENT This work evaluates publicly released models and archived forecasts on past storms; it involves no human subjects or personal data. All forecasts, reanalyses, and observations are used under the licenses listed in Appendix A. Tropical cyclone forecasts inform evacuation and preparation decisions, so our results should not be read as guidance on using any of these models for operational warnings: they are a retrospective evaluation of selected storms, and operational use would require evaluation under operational conditions, including cases without cyclones.
