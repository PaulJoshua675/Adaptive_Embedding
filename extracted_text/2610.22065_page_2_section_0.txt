Motivated by these observations, we propose ABNAT (Attention- Based Noise-Aware Training), an attention-based knowledge dis- tillation (KD) framework for preserving semantic routes under CIM noise. ABNAT uses a clean, frozen copy of the target deploy- ment model as the teacher and its noise-perturbed counterpart as the student. Rather than relying only on answer-level supervi- sion, ABNAT transfers the content understanding encoded in the teacher’s attention routes by aligning teacher and student attention distributions. Figure 1 shows that ABNAT restores noise-distorted attention maps toward the clean reference across diverse inputs, indicating more faithful preservation of semantic attention routes. We further develop head- and block-selective variants that retain near-full robustness at lower training cost, and validate ABNAT across vision, language, and vision-language Transformers. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows: • We identify attention drift as a key robustness bottleneck in CIM-deployed ViTs and empirically establish its strong correlation with accuracy degradation under noise. • We propose ABNAT, an attention-based KD framework that preserves semantic attention routes under CIM noise by aligning headwise attention maps between a clean teacher and a noisy student model. • We introduce selective alignment variants at head and block granularity that reduce training cost while retaining accu- racy close to full alignment. • Extensive experiments spanning multiple noise levels, model scales, and datasets across vision, language, and multimodal tasks demonstrate that ABNAT consistently outperforms ex- isting NAT baselines, achieving up to 11.28 percentage points higher noise-degraded accuracy and an 85.77% reduction in attention deviation. 2 Preliminaries 2.1 Transformers and Attention Mechanisms Transformers represent vision, language, and multimodal inputs as token sequences processed by 𝐿stacked blocks [24]. The same mechanism is shared by vision, language, and vision-language Transformers [3, 9, 18]. 2.2 Analog CIM Hardware Analog CIM accelerators perform MVMs in situ by encoding weight matrices as analog device conductances and exploiting Ohm’s law for multiplication and Kirchhoff’s current law for summation [7, 23]. This in-memory computation eliminates costly data movement be- tween memory and processing units, enabling orders-of-magnitude improvements in energy efficiency compared to conventional digi- tal accelerators [6, 11, 22]. These properties make CIM an attractive substrate for energy-efficient Transformer deployment [13, 14, 16]. However, analog device non-idealities introduce unavoidable perturbations to the stored weights [2, 4]. These arise from multi- ple physical sources: conductance programming variation, thermal read noise, inter-cell interference, and long-term conductance drift. The detailed bit-level noise formulation used in our experiments is introduced in Sec. 4. For Transformers, noise corrupts the projec- tion matrices W𝑄, W𝐾, and W𝑉at every layer, perturbing attention computation throughout the network. Because Transformer repre- sentations are built compositionally across depth, these per-layer errors accumulate rather than cancel, causing severe attention drift in deeper layers where semantic concentration is most critical. 2.3 Noise-Aware Training NAT improves CIM robustness by injecting hardware noise during training, optimizing: min W E𝝐  LCE(𝑓ˆW(x),𝑦)  , (2) where LCE is the cross-entropy loss and 𝑓ˆW denotes the noisy model. NAT has demonstrated effectiveness for CNNs [5, 15, 26], but remains output-centric: supervising only final predictions places no explicit constraint on intermediate attention routes. The model may therefore learn shortcut solutions that produce correct, highly confident predictions through severely distorted attention maps. Such confidence–route inconsistency can generalize poorly across deployment noise levels and degrade clean accuracy because the semantic attention routes central to Transformer inference remain unconstrained. KD transfers knowledge from a pretrained teacher 𝑓W to a stu- dent 𝑓ˆW by minimizing discrepancies in their outputs or intermedi- ate representations, and has shown strong results in model com- pression and transfer learning [10, 21]. Prior studies have incorpo- rated KD into NAT for CIM robustness: Charan et al. [5] distill the teacher’s output predictions, whereas Zhou et al. [27] additionally align intermediate feature maps in RRAM-based ViTs. Despite us- ing knowledge distillation, both approaches essentially teach the noisy student to reproduce answers while overlooking attention drift, without teaching it to understand the content under noise. In contrast, ABNAT transfers the clean teacher’s attention distribu- tions, teaching the noisy student to preserve content understanding under hardware noise.
