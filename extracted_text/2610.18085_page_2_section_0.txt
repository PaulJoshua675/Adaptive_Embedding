Standard optimizers already include mechanisms such as adaptive gradient normalization, but these mecha- nisms still do not remove this dependence on the param- eterization. Adam normalizes gradients using moving averages of gradients and squared gradients (Kingma and Ba, 2015), which cancels constant-factor scaling of the gradients: Both models receive the same numerical update (Figure 1(2)). To keep the two models equiva- lent, however, the update of the enlarged matrix would have to be c times larger and the update of its shrunken partner c times smaller. The same update is too small for the enlarged matrix and too large for its shrunken partner. Transforming Adam’s moment estimates along with the weights does not fix this mismatch, because both models still use the same learning rate (Sec. 3). Appendix A discusses in detail how this work relates to and differs from prior studies of rescaling-aware op- timization, weight balancing, and symmetry-induced learning dynamics (Neyshabur et al., 2015; Stock et al., 2019; Kunin et al., 2021). To remove the scale imbalance between paired matrices without changing the function, we introduce Symmetry- Aware Gradient Geometry Stabilization (SAGGS), which periodically rescales each exact symmetry pair so that its two matrices have equal Frobenius norms (Sec. 4). To avoid disrupting optimizer state that has adapted to imbalanced scales, Soft SAGGS applies only a fraction of this correction at each rebalancing step. Both operations leave the current function un- changed and only rebalance the weights from which training continues. SAGGS also gives an exact restart procedure: Applied together with a matching trans- formation of the moment estimates, SAGGS moves any two checkpoints on the same symmetry orbit to the same balanced state, from which training follows the same trajectory (Corollary 4). We evaluate both variants in deliberate rescaling tests, which rescale the pairs of layer ℓby cℓ= eλℓto impose pair-scale im- balance without changing the function, as well as in checkpoint-resume workflows and in training without such rescaling (Sec. 5). In our seven-seed comparisons, rebalancing restores near-baseline PPL after an im- posed rescaling, including a checkpoint edit, and leaves PPL nearly unchanged when no imbalance is imposed. Our contributions are fourfold. • We identify exact symmetry orbits in Transformer architectures (Sec. 2) and show their effect on training. • Our theoretical analysis proves that, under a shared learning rate, no standard optimizer, includ- ing gradient descent, AdamW, and Lion, keeps two equivalent models equivalent during training (The- orem 5). Even with correctly transformed moment estimates, AdamW does so only if its learning rate, ϵ, and weight decay are also rescaled coordinate by coordinate (Theorem 2 and Example 3).
