Vision and physics-informed neural network (PINN) studies validate the same principles beyond language modeling (App. I). 2 NEURAL UNIT CONSISTENCY This section formulates local rules for managing scale on a neural network’s computation graph. The rules specify which quantities can be added and which inputs must be dimensionless. The rules also determine how loss weights must compensate for changes in loss units and how gradients must rescale when activations or parameters are rescaled. These rules provide the basis for the diagnostics in Sec. 3 and the architecture in Sec. 4. 2.1 Units as transformation laws Let G be a computation graph whose node v carries a tensor xv in a finite-dimensional real inner-product space Vv. We declare a rescaling group G := (R+)K, with one factor for each independent internal scale degree of freedom. A neural unit of node v is a repre- sentation ρv : G →GL(Vv), xv 7→ρv(g)xv. (1) A tensor is dimensionless if and only if ρv ≡I. The scalar units considered here are the continuous char- acters χu(g) := QK k=1 guk k , u ∈RK, with ρv = χuvI. We then write u(x) := χux and use the multiplicative notation u(xy) = u(x)u(y). The unit type u(x) specifies how x transforms under G, whereas the empirical scale sx measures magnitude and shares that type, making x/sx dimensionless. The root-mean-square (RMS) value is one such scale on a shared-unit block; mixed-unit tensors require separate scales or a unit-compatible metric (App. F.7). For an operation y := f(x1, . . . , xn), the declared trans- formation laws are ρ1, . . . , ρn for the input tensors and ρy for the output tensor. We call the operation unit- consistent when the following equivariance identity holds for every g ∈G: f(ρ1(g)x1, . . . , ρn(g)xn) = ρy(g)f(x1, . . . , xn). (2) In words, rescaling the inputs before the operation must give the same result as rescaling the output afterward. Parameters follow the same rule. In y := fϕ(x), we treat ϕ as another input and declare the parameter transformation ϕ 7→ρϕ(g)ϕ. App. F.7 details linear maps and explicit scale converters. We also need gradient diagnostics that can be compared across blocks with different units. The next proposition shows that the forward unit declarations fix how every backpropagated gradient transforms. Proposition 1 (Reverse-mode dual typing). Let G be a directed acyclic graph (DAG) whose nodes carry declared units ρv and whose every primitive operation is unit-consistent with respect to those declarations. If the loss node is typed dimensionless, then, at every point where the relevant derivatives exist, every reverse-mode gradient node transforms by the dual law ρ−⊤ v . The proof is given in App. G.1. Under the declared scalar rescaling, x 7→cx gives ∇xL 7→c−1∇xL, meaning that multiplying the activa- tion by c divides the activation gradient by c. These factors cancel in the primal–dual RMS product in Eq. 5, making the product comparable across rescalings of a shared scalar-unit block. To keep a scalar diagnostic unchanged under a change of units, construct the di- agnostic’s dimensionless output from unit-consistent operations. 2.2 Local typing rules Addition and residuals. z := x+y requires u(x) = u(y) = u(z).
