Nevertheless, PR-entmax consistently reduces exposure concentration on the most popular items while maintaining exposure levels for tail items comparable to the strongest competing approaches. 5.6 Sensitivity to the Sparsity Parameter Tsallis entropy, and consequently the proposed variational objective, introduces the hyperparameter 𝛼, which controls the sparsity of the resulting probability mapping. In all previous experiments, we used 𝛼= 1.5. In this section, we analyze how different values of 𝛼affect recommendation relevance and popularity debiasing, and justify this choice empirically. Figure 4 illustrates the dependence of NDCG@10 and Novelty@10 on the sparsity parameter for 𝛼values in the interval [1.05, 2]. In the limiting case 𝛼→1, the mapping approaches softmax, while 𝛼= 2 corresponds to sparsemax. The figure reports the results both for Entmax without prior correction and for the proposed PR- entmax objective. The standard Cross-Entropy baseline is shown as a dotted horizontal line. Across most values of𝛼, Entmax remains above the cross-entropy baseline on both NDCG@10 and Novelty@10, except for several points near the low- and high-sparsity extremes. However, the re- sulting novelty improvements remain relatively modest. In contrast, PR-entmax achieves substantially stronger debiasing gains across the entire range of sparsity values. For highly sparse solutions, Novelty@10 increases dramatically, but this improvement comes at the cost of a substantial reduction in ranking quality. At the same time, there exists a relatively broad interval of 𝛼values, approximately between 1.2 and 1.6, where PR-entmax simultaneously improves NDCG@10 while maintaining strong debiasing performance across all datasets. This behavior in- dicates that the proposed approach is stable and not overly sensitive to the precise choice of the sparsity parameter. The selected value 𝛼= 1.5 achieves near-optimal ranking quality while preserving substantial debiasing improvements. 6
