Generative recommendation models have shown promising re- sults by representing items as semantic IDs and generating these IDs autoregressively. We introduce an approach showing that ex- plicitly incorporating hierarchical and attribute-level supervision into generative recommendation can simultaneously improve train- ing efficiency and long-tail recommendation performance. Exist- ing approaches typically optimize semantic ID prediction with a standard causal cross-entropy objective, which does not explic- itly exploit the hierarchical structure of semantic IDs and can re- quire substantial training to learn effective item representations. Moreover, data-sparse tail items often suffer from poor represen- tations due to limited supervision, resulting in weaker long-tail recommendation performance. We address these challenges with HASTE (Hierarchical and Attribute-aware Supervision for gen- eraTive rEcommendation), a unified training framework that in- troduces hierarchical and attribute-specific supervision. HASTE’s Hierarchical Loss explicitly exploits the multi-level structure of semantic IDs, providing supervision at each level of the item hi- erarchy and substantially accelerating training convergence. Its Attribute-Specific Losses encourage the model to preserve fine- grained item attributes, leveraging the language model’s inherent semantic knowledge to improve representations and recommenda- tions for tail items. We extensively evaluate HASTE on a fashion recommendation benchmark and across multiple domains of the Amazon dataset. HASTE improves recommendation performance across datasets and item-frequency regimes. Notably, the Hierar- chical Loss achieves approximately 2× faster convergence than the standard causal cross-entropy objective, substantially improv- ing training efficiency while maintaining strong recommendation quality.
