Figure 2: Architecture Overview. Injection Locations. The encoded privileged information can be injected at different stages of the transformer-based aggregation module, as illus- trated in Fig. 2. Specifically, residual flow features are fused with patch tokens, while gravity embeddings are combined with camera tokens when such tokens are present. For reconstruction architectures that do not explicitly maintain camera tokens (e.g., π3), gravity embeddings are instead injected before the camera decoder to influence the camera prediction stage. The framework supports multiple potential injection points throughout the aggregator layers, as shown in Fig. 2. Empirically, our experiments show that the optimal injection position depends on the backbone architecture, with different models favoring different stages of the aggregator. 3.3 Finetuning Our training objectives include local and global point maps, log-space depth maps, pixel ray directions, and pairwise camera translations and rotations. Additionally, for synthetic datasets, we incorporate surface normal and depth-gradient signals to provide finer geometric supervision. All loss terms are computed using the Generalized Robust Loss [29], with the final ob- jective being a weighted sum of these individual components.
