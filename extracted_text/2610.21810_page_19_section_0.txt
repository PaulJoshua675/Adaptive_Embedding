To test whether low rank alone favors fitted codebooks, a separate synthetic rank sweep uses tables of 16,384 rows with d ∈ {160, 256} and three seeds. A rank-k table has Gaussian latents in a random k-dimensional basis, prescribed row norms and a common Haar rotation, stored in bf16. The equivalence margin of .05 on mean row kurtosis, mean max/RMS and pooled quantiles was fixed before the run. Every basis and codebook is fitted on half of the rows and evalu- ated on the other half. Byte counts include padding to whole bytes and all shared decoder state; each family’s best configuration at or below the trellis budget is chosen on the fit rows. At d = 256, rank-k tables pass the Gaussian- coordinate equivalence test for k ≥16, yet a PCA-basis code reaches .013 of the trellis MSE for k = 64 at the 2.06 bpp budget. For spiked full- rank tables with 16 strong directions, the structured- code/trellis MSE ratio is 1.19 when those directions hold a third of the energy, and .94 when they hold half. The crossover lies near a spike energy frac- tion of .45–.5 in this codec grid. The most-read real 2-gram rows place 10–33% of their energy in 16 held-out directions (Appendix D.1), consis- tent with the trellis advantage in the trained-table comparisons.
