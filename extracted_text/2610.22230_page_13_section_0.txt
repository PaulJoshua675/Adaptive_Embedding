Kernel Category Analysis To understand the sources of performance differences, we analyze GPU traces collected during training to categorize kernel latency by type: GEMM, Triton Inductor, CUDA embed- ding, elementwise, collectives, and hand-written Triton kernels. Fig. 16 shows the per-model breakdown of kernel latency ratio for each category. Where MI350X excels. Hand-written Triton kernels show con- sistent MI350X advantages, delivering a 6% to 52% reduction in execution latency through the optimizations discussed in Section IV-A. Model-H benefits particularly from faster HSTU kernels, achieving 2.1× speedup for the Triton category. Embed- ding operations show mixed results but generally favor MI350X (0.61×–1.04×), leveraging the CUDA kernel optimizations detailed in Section IV-B. Overall, collective performance is comparable between the two GPU types, with MI350X outperforming B200 in multi- host, small-message AllToAll collectives, specifically benefit- ing Models C and J. Note that overlapped kernel execution presents a challenge for latency analysis: due to the multi- stream nature on GPUs, concurrent compute and communi- cation kernels complicate the measurement of their individual contributions to end-to-end latency. For instance, collectives in Models E and F take longer to execute but are masked by compute kernels in the B200 traces, resulting in no net impact on throughput. Consequently, total kernel latency may overstate the true impact of collectives, leading to discrepancies between Fig. 16 and measured throughput in Table III. Where B200 leads. GEMM operations consistently favor B200, with MI350X showing 1.3×–1.7× higher latency. MI350X lacks native hardware support for TF32, instead emulating them with three BF16 GEMMs at a performance penalty. Model- A’s overall 18% performance deficit is largely attributable to TF32 GEMM operations. While this is a notable limitation, modern models are increasingly moving toward BF16 or lower precision. We expect continued model modernization and improvements to emulated TF32 performance to further narrow the overall gap. Additionally, cuBLAS outperforms hipBLAS due to its relative maturity, especially in the case of irregular matrix dimensions. Triton Inductor kernels show model-dependent results: some models achieve parity (0.99×– 1.08×) while others show MI350X incurring 1.22× higher latency than B200, reflecting ongoing efforts to refine Inductor and eliminate heuristics specifically tailored for NVIDIA GPUs (Section IV-D). C. Operational Considerations Integrating a second GPU vendor into a previously NVIDIA- only fleet raised operational challenges in power, reliability, and observability. In addition to performance, operational maturity is just as critical to adopting a new vendor, and much of the tooling and fleet-management machinery we built generalizes to future accelerators. Reliability. We track MI350X on the same metrics as our NVIDIA fleet (training-failure rate, unplanned unavailability, and hardware-swap rate), and it is steadily converging toward NVIDIA-fleet levels: the training-failure rate has more than halved from its peak as we resolved new at-scale failure modes, namely HBM RAS, multi-chiplet memory-access faults, and silent data corruption (SDC). SDC, the bit-flips that corrupt re- sults without crashing, grows with cluster size and is the hardest to tame, so we address it end-to-end: a model-level checksum- and-replay check flags non-deterministic steps (distinguishing on-chip corruption from communication non-determinism); an all-pairs vote across a host’s GPUs pinpoints the culprit device (the one that disagrees with all its peers) and auto-quarantines the host; and the same check runs during host qualification and burn-in to screen SDC-prone hosts before they enter the training pool, catching over 2× the SDC of the vendor’s field-health tool. These mitigations preserve correctness: across the tens of models we train on both vendors, results reach numerical parity (training-loss difference within 0.3% and NE within ∼10−4 at multi-thousand-GPU scale).
