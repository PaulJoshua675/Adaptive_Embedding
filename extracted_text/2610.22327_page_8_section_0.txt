Our central hypothesis is that exposing the language model to complementary representations pro- vides benefits that cannot be obtained from either view alone. We therefore compare the full dual- view model against Plot-only and Delay-only variants, as well as two same-view controls that retain the complete dual-tower architecture but feed both towers the same representation. In Plot+Plot, the DINOv3 tower receives the line plot in place of the delay map; in Delay+Delay, the Qwen3- VL tower receives the delay map in place of the line plot. Both controls keep the same encoders, adapters, per-tower token budgets, and two-stage training procedure as our model, so any difference from the full model reflects whether the two streams carry distinct or identical views, rather than dif- ferences in architecture or capacity. Table 3 reports results across all 15 TSRBench tasks, together with representative dedicated TSLMs and general-purpose models. The results show that the benefit of complementary representations is concentrated beyond basic perception. Plot only already achieves 87.4% on Perception, and adding the delay view leaves this essentially unchanged at 86.4%. In contrast, the dual-view model improves Reasoning from 35.8% to 40.1%, Prediction from 38.0% to 61.7%, and Decision-Making from 36.4% to 37.6%, raising overall accuracy from 45.2% to 53.2%. The task-level results further show that the contribution of the complementary view varies across tasks, rather than producing a uniform improvement across the benchmark. Importantly, simply duplicating a view does not reproduce the gain: Plot+Plot reaches 44.8% overall and Delay+Delay reaches 42.6%, compared with our 53.2%. This suggests that the improvement is not explained merely by providing a second visual stream, but is associated with combining representations that preserve different properties of the underlying signal. Our model also exceeds the evaluated dedicated TSLMs, whose strongest overall TSRBench result is 36.7%. For context, Table 3 additionally reports GPT-5 results; these models are substantially larger proprietary systems and are therefore not size-matched comparisons. They nevertheless provide a useful reference point: our model reaches 53.2% overall, surpassing GPT-5 with visual input (52.4%) and approaching GPT-5 with text and visual input (55.6%), while exhibiting a markedly different task-level performance profile. 5.3 HOW DO THE REPRESENTATIONS RESPOND TO DISTRIBUTION SHIFTS? To directly probe the inductive biases induced by the visual representations, we apply controlled transformations to TSRBench inputs at evaluation time. We consider affine changes to amplitude and offset, observational noise and smoothing, and changes in sampling horizon. Tasks whose gold targets depend intrinsically on absolute coordinate values or sampling rates are excluded from this analysis, leaving 2,020 items across invariant reasoning, perception, and event prediction tasks.
