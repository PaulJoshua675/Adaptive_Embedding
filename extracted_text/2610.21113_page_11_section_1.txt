This work studied candidate-context scalability in LLM- based agent discovery: the separation between registry-scale retrieval and the compact candidate context presented to the LLM orchestrator. We propose the Structural Signal Retriever (SSR), a deterministic, training-free method for Agent Card discovery based on field-derived lexical signals, inverse doc- ument frequency (IDF), and an Inverted Signal Index (ISI). The three experiments provide complementary evidence. On SSR-AgentBench, SSR reduces mean LLM input by 98.8% and mean successful orchestration latency by 70.9% at N = 1000 relative to full-catalog orchestration. On the MS MARCO adaptation, IDF improves ranking effectiveness within the structural representation, while the ISI preserves the positive-scoring Structural+IDF ranking and reduces explicitly scored passages by 96.8%, yielding a 74.2× mean retrieval- latency speedup over exhaustive scoring. BM25 achieves higher raw retrieval effectiveness, so SSR is not presented as a universally superior lexical retriever. In end-to-end evaluation, SSR and BM25 candidate pre-selection show comparable observed effectiveness under the same maximum candidate budget K = 20; no statistically detectable difference in end-to-end correctness is observed after Holm correction. At N = 5000, both candidate-pre-selection pipelines remain operational, whereas none of the 200 full-catalog LLM re- quests completes successfully under the evaluated model/API configuration. These results show that the main candidate-context reduc- tion arises from effective candidate pre-selection rather than from SSR scoring alone. The contribution of this work is the formulation and systems-level characterization of candidate- context scalability for Agent Card discovery, together with SSR as an Agent-Card-native, deterministic, training-free, field-derived, and score-traceable retrieval mechanism. The conclusions remain limited by the synthetic nature of SSR- AgentBench, the document-derived Agent Card abstraction used with MS MARCO, retrospective validation of the fixed candidate budget, a single LLM/API configuration, and the exclusion of offline index construction from online latency. Future work should evaluate native Agent Card collections, prospective budget selection, query-adaptive and hybrid re- trieval, and dynamic or distributed registries.
