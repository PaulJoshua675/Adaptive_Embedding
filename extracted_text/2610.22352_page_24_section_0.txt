E.2 Partial and Complete Encoding: Diagnostic Protocol The results and reconstruction examples appear in Sec. 4.4. Diagnostic protocol. Figure 5 reports the paired GT partial-versus-complete encoding analysis and three illustrative material reconstructions. The diagnostic uses each object’s designated reference view, a two-pixel foreground erosion, and a 3 × 3 depth-discontinuity filter with relative threshold 0.04; projection IoU must be at least 0.90. The latent summary weights objects equally and uses 10,000 object-bootstrap resamples. The decoded examples are the objects nearest the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles of object-level latent RMS. Their 128 × 128 crops maximize GT albedo gradient on a 16-pixel candidate grid with at least 80% valid pixels. Material values are converted to [0, 1] and clamped as in the production decoder path; PSNR uses the same retained GT surface voxels for both reconstructions. Geometry support is supplied by each encoder’s spatial correspondence cache, so coordinate coverage is not evidence of independent geometry recovery. E.3 Observed-Support Encoding versus Zero Filling As discussed in Sec. 3.3, zero-padding unobserved regions assigns the missing values concrete material semantics, such as black albedo. In the zero-filled formulation, we observed a pronounced dark bias on unseen surfaces. Figure 9 illustrates this behavior: the back of the calculator becomes nearly black and retains spurious front-side characters, while the dish develops dark mottling on its red side and white underside. With observed-support-only encoding, SUPRA-Tex instead produces a green calculator back and preserves the dish’s red-and-white color regions.
