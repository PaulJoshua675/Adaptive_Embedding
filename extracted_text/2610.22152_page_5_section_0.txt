Since the agent carries at most one unit of dirt at a time, at most one unit is unresolved at episode end, so these three cases partition the dirt cells without double-counting. In each case the per-cell proxy contribution equals the sum of that cell’s true and cost contributions. Summing over all dirt cells gives Eq. 6. Proposition 1 shows that costtotal, as defined in Eq. 4, is not merely correlated with the proxy/true gap; it measures the gap exactly. Penalizing cost in Eq. 5 is equivalent, in the exact accounting sense of Eq. 6, to penalizing proxytotal −truetotal directly, despite the agent never observing truetotal during training. Corollary 1 (Deterministic bound under a hard cost constraint). If training enforces costtotal ≤K for a fixed K – for instance via early episode termination or action-masking once K gaming events have occurred – then truetotal ≥proxytotal −K (7) holds with certainty, for every episode, rather than in expectation. Corollary 1 follows immediately from rearranging Eq. 6. It provides the hard-constrained variant of our method with a guarantee of a different character than the fixed-λ Lagrangian penalty used in the present experiments (Eq. 5): rather than encouraging low cost on average, a hard cap on costtotal translates, via Proposition 1, into a deterministic floor on true-objective performance relative to whatever proxy score the policy achieves. We verify Proposition 1 empirically against both trained agents in Section 5.4, confirming that the identity holds exactly on the implemented system and not only in the idealized derivation above. The present experiments use the fixed-λ relaxation rather than the hard-constrained variant of Corollary 1; we discuss this as a direction for future work in Section 7. 5 Experiments and Results 5.1 Sanity Check: Confirming the Proxy/True Gap Before training any agent, we verified that the environment’s constructed proxy/true gap was real and not an artifact of a single layout.
