Large-scale perturbational screens deepen the understanding of disease mechanisms and help validate therapeutic targets (Schmidt et al., 2022; Zhuang et al., 2019; Sanchez et al., 2021). The human genome comprises nearly 20,000 single-gene candidates, roughly 200 million pairs, and one trillion triples in terms of perturbation combinations. The costs of testing each perturbation and measuring its phenotype make exhaustive screening infeasible (Mehrjou et al., 2022; Pacchiano et al., 2023). Closed-loop systems have shown that model-guided experiment selection can improve biological discovery under limited resources (Xu et al., 2026; DeMeo et al., 2025). It is thus important to develop strategies that maximize cumulative discoveries within a fixed experimental budget. Active learning addresses this challenge by iteratively fitting a surrogate model to measured outcomes and using an acquisition function to select subsequent experiments (Figure 1a). GeneDisco evaluates active learning methods for perturbation screening and reports substantial variation in their performance across experimental settings (Mehrjou et al., 2022). Existing methods employ different selection criteria to guide perturbation discovery. OAE (Pacchiano et al., 2023) seeks near-optimal perturbations, DiscoBAX (Lyle et al., 2023) considers response strength and mechanistic diversity, and Probability-of-Hit (PoH) (Rubbi et al., 2026) prioritizes perturbations that exceed a predefined phenotypic threshold.
