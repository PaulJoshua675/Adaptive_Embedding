Aligning LLMs with Sparse Retrieval via Adaptive Sparse Supervision Tongna Chen , Minghan Li ⋆, and Guodong Zhou School of Computer Science and Technology, Soochow University, Suzhou 215031, China tnchentnchen@stu.suda.edu.cn mhli@suda.edu.cn gdzhou@suda.edu.cn Abstract. While large language models (LLMs) offer strong semantic representations, their application to first-stage retrieval incurs substan- tial latency and storage costs. Neural sparse retrievers such as SPLADE provide efficient retrieval but rely on relatively compact encoders with limited representational capacity. To bridge this gap, we propose an LLM-based sparse retrieval framework with input-adaptive sparse align- ment. Specifically, we introduce a dynamic α-truncation strategy that selects input-specific supervision supports based on the cumulative acti- vation mass of SPLADE representations. This strategy guides the LLM to preserve salient lexical signals while suppressing less informative acti- vations, without relying on a fixed support budget. Subsequent retrieval fine-tuning combines contrastive learning, knowledge distillation, and sparsity regularization to improve retrieval effectiveness. Experiments on MS MARCO and six selected BEIR datasets demonstrate improvements over SPLADE++ while maintaining competitive effectiveness against LLM-based sparse retrievers. Compared with Lion-sp, our method re- duces index storage by 6.4× and end-to-end query latency by over 11×, achieving a favorable effectiveness–efficiency trade-off. Keywords: Neural Sparse Retrieval; Large Language Models; Adaptive Sparsification; Efficiency–Effectiveness Trade-off 1
