FDE-Gym measures an agent’s delivery the way a customer does: not by whether a system was delivered, but by whether the customer’s business improved. Measured in this way, the last mile of AI delivery remains long. The best of the thirteen configurations has an average FDE-Gym score of 0.56 and resolves about one task in seven. The gap is not where we expected it to be. Leading models show little performance loss when the sponsor’s request is misdirected, and they obtain most of the facts held by stakeholders. Their principal performance losses arise when translating those facts into an effective system and from the stakeholder contact costs incurred during delivery. Weaker models lose more: on most tasks, the customer would have been better served by no delivery at all. These are the capabilities an experienced engineer brings to a customer. Closing the gap is therefore a question of judgment in delivery, and FDE-Gym makes that judgment measurable: one number per task, on the customer’s own metric, for people and agents under the same rules.
