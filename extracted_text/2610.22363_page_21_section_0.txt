Starting from a generated manipulation video, we reconstruct the relevant objects and scene and recover their motion over time. The reconstructed hand motion is then retargeted to the robot hand to obtain an initial robot trajectory. The resulting action sequence is then deployed on the physical robot without further modification. Thus, the complete evaluation pipeline is generated video →scene and object reconstruction → robot retargeting →real-world execution. This follows do-as-I-do Paliwal et al. (2026) evaluation paradigm, in which a visual demonstration is converted into an executable robot behavior. For both experiments, we report whether execution reproduces the intended manipulation process and reaches the specified task outcome. These real-world trials complement the human and VLM evaluations above: whereas the perceptual metrics measure whether an action appears correct and physically plausible in the generated video, real-world execution provides a direct test of whether the predicted interaction can be grounded into a physically executable manipulation trajectory.
