The distance is one-sided because the input views need not cover the full reference scan. Each method discards its own least confident 5 % of points across the whole union. This one-sided point-to-scan distance measures geometric agreement across time, though proximity to the scan alone cannot establish exact point identity. We assess temporal matching through point tracking on Panoptic Studio Humans (Sec. 4.2) and discuss the limits of these evaluations in Sec. 5. Results. Our union lies less than 1 cm from the measured surface on average: 0.86 cm, compared with 2.31 cm for V-DPM and 4.84 cm for 4RC (Tab. 2). Even with identical person-centred crops and little global translation, Dense4D achieves substantially more accurate surface localisation than the evaluated dynamic scene models. Points from non-reference frames are nearly as accurate as those from the reference frame (0.87 versus 0.80 cm), and only 6.8 % lie more than 2 cm from the surface, compared with 41.5 % and 53.4 % for the baselines. For both baselines, reference-frame predictions have lower error than those from other frames: 1.64 versus 2.36 cm for V-DPM and 4.07 versus 4.89 cm for 4RC. Figure 5 shows how error varies with time and confidence. Our error stays within approximately 0.1 cm of the reference-frame value across the plotted temporal range. V-DPM’s error rises by about 0.8 cm within the first second and then levels off, while 4RC’s grows gradually, by about 1.3 cm over the first two seconds. Increasing the confidence cut from 0 to 50 % reduces 4RC’s error from 5.86 to 2.78 cm and V-DPM’s from 2.70 to 1.85 cm, while ours changes from 0.93 to 0.77 cm. Confidence filtering therefore reduces the baselines’ errors more strongly, while our unfiltered predictions already lie within 1 cm of the surface on average. Figure 7 summarises this visual advantage with matched forward-transfer overlays and reference-time unions, where our predictions form a more coherent human surface. Additional sequences illustrate body rotation and clothing deformation (Figs. 1, 6 and 13). The same reference-space matching supports tracking over long intervals: Dense4D processes 1,287 person crops from an 86 s video in one forward pass (Fig. 12). The comparisons distinguish plausible reconstruction in each frame from consistent transfer of the same surface points across time.
