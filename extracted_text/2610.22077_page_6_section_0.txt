An important note is that ERA5 itself underestimates cyclone maximum winds (Dulac et al., 2024). Agreement with ERA5 consequently does not establish realistic intensity, nor does IFS-ENS’s positive bias establish that its storms are too strong. We next examine whether models fine-tuned on operational analyses improve cyclone tracking and whether their intensity biases persist against HRES-fc0, while assessing spatial agreement separately from intensity. 4 OPERATIONAL SETTING: TRACKING, INTENSITY, AND SPATIAL AGREEMENT FOR MODELS FINE-TUNED ON OPERATIONAL ANALYSES Cyclone tracking. FGN assigns a greater probability density to the observed storm locations than IFS-ENS on approximately 90% of the 41 retained storms and achieves the lowest mean NLL (Table 2). Operational GenCast also outperforms IFS-ENS on 78% of storms, while FGN’s advantage over GenCast is smaller, at 61%. FGN ranks first among all three models on 59% of storms, compared with 34% for GenCast and 7% for IFS-ENS. Both AI models therefore improve tracking on most evaluated storms, with FGN ahead of GenCast. The detection rates follow the NLL ordering, with both AI models ahead of IFS-ENS. Unlike in 2021–2023, where detection rates were close, the operational comparison shows a clear separation. This reflects both higher detection for operational GenCast and lower detection for IFS-ENS on the 2025 storms. The changing numerical baseline also prevents attributing differences between settings to AI fine-tuning alone. Whether its lower detection rate reflects the storm sample, forecast uncertainty, or differences in forecast structure and tracking remains unresolved. We next examine whether the models locate the strongest winds and lowest pressures more accurately. Spatial agreement. A forecast may correctly locate the strongest winds while underestimating their intensity. To evaluate placement independently of magnitude, we compare the regions of strongest winds and lowest pressures across forecasts against those in HRES-fc0. These regions are defined using percentile thresholds computed from the pooled values of all ensemble members for a given model, ensuring that a systematic intensity bias does not influence the comparison. The resulting masks therefore capture the relative placement and shape of extreme features, rather than areas exceeding a fixed hazardous wind speed threshold.
