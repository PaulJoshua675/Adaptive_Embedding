In summary, by training on a broad synthetic population constructed without the fitted patients, we attain on these patients a performance comparable to that obtained with a population calibrated to them, in line with Theorem 2.1, and retain the benefits of both random-perturbation RL and kernel-robust RL. 4 Concluding Remarks In this manuscript, we study adaptive therapy through the lens of robust contextual MDPs, in which the patient is modeled as a hidden context drawn from a population. To account for the uncertainty beyond the variation across patients, we further train the policy under random perturbations of the measured biomarker and against an adversary acting on the transition kernel. Within a model of abiraterone therapy fitted to clinical data, we find that (1) a policy learned within this formulation substantially outperforms AT50 and generalizes to the fitted patients from a broad population constructed without them, (2) errors in PSA of realistic magnitude substantially reduce the benefit of policies that do not account for them, while by training under such errors we retain the benefit of adaptive therapy, and (3) we attain robustness to shifts in the patient dynamics at almost no price in the nominal model, with the resulting policy nearly Pareto- dominating nominal RL and AT50 across all the errors and shifts we consider. In view of this, we believe that robust RL offers a principled route toward adaptive treatment policies that remain reliable beyond the model in which they are learned.
