F Comparison using conventional gFID and PRDC metrics. In the main paper, we report results in gFDr6 (Yang et al., 2026). We compare our method with baselines using gFID (Heusel et al., 2017) and Precision, Recall (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2019), Density, and Coverage (Naeem et al., 2020) in Tab. 14. Per conventions, we use different hyperparameters during sampling for gFID evaluation than gFDr6. gFID measures the Fréchet distance in the feature space of an Inception-v3 network (Szegedy et al., 2016) trained for ImageNet classification. Its sensitivity to ImageNet class information means that it can be lowered substantially without improving the images (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2023), and Inception features represent the content of modern generators poorly, so gFID often disagrees with human raters (Stein et al., 2023; Jayasumana et al., 2024). These problems are most severe at the low values reached by current ImageNet generators. ImageNet validation images score a gFID of 1.68 against the training set (Yang et al., 2026), so generators below 2.0, and especially below 1.68, appear as close to the training data as real images, although their samples remain clearly distinguishable from real ones (Yang et al., 2026). In this regime, gFID can also be optimized directly: post-training a generator on Inception features lowers its gFID from 3.31 to 0.81 without producing the best samples, whereas post-training on modern representations improves object structure while gFID becomes worse (Yang et al., 2026). gFDr6 addresses these issues by averaging Fréchet distances over six representation spaces, each normalized by the distance between the training and validation sets, so no single feature space dominates and a value of 1 means as close to the training data as real validation images. Tab. 14 provides additional evidence. At these low-gFID operating points, improvements in Inception Fréchet distance do not consistently agree with the fidelity diagnostics. Across the ten BAR and FeaTok models, the guided configuration has lower gFID and higher recall in every case, but lower precision in seven and lower density in nine. For example, BAR-B’s gFID improves from 1.64 to 1.13, while precision falls from 0.800 to 0.775 and density from 1.153 to 1.060. Recall rises from 0.614 to 0.660. Thus, rather than demonstrating a uniform fidelity gain, these selected configurations often show a fidelity–recall trade-off in the opposite direction to the usual motivation for guidance. Coverage remains near saturation (0.994–0.997). The results caution against interpreting a small gFID improvement as an unambiguous improvement in generation quality, motivating evaluation across multiple representations and complementary reward measures.
