Each club is scored at the answer slot. 5 Mechanistic Analysis of Evidence Use Providing evidence directly reduces failures but does not eliminate them. We investigate why mod- els may still give outdated answers when the correct evidence is available. We use player transfer facts from the sports do- main and three open-weight models: gpt-oss-20b, gemma-4-31b and qwen3-8b. For each fact, we construct two prompts that assert an outdated club and ask for the player’s current club. One prompt also includes the player’s current Wikipedia page (Figure 3). Comparing the two prompts shows how models choose between the outdated and cur- rent answers. We use the logit lens (nostalgebraist, 2020) and the Jacobian lens (J-lens; Gurnee et al., 2026) to probe how the model represents the two candidate answers across layers. Both methods project intermediate hidden states at the answer position onto token-level logits. Each club’s score is the highest logit among selected first tokens of its name, so a higher score indicates stronger sup- port for that club. We track the ranks of both clubs across layers and compare their scores with and without the page (Table 12 in Appendix J). Figure 2 shows how the two candidate answers compete across layers in qwen3-8b. In success- ful cases, the retrieved club gains an advantage in the upper layers and keeps it until the final lay- ers. In failed cases, the retrieved club ranks above the outdated club early in the upper layers, but the outdated club overtakes it in the final layers. This suggests that retrieved evidence can influence in- ternal predictions even when the model fails. The same crossover appears in gpt-oss-20b, while in gemma-4-31b the outdated club rises first and stays ahead (Appendix J). Averaged over the upper third of layers, the page still promotes the retrieved club in failures, though less than in updates (∆new > 0, p ≤0.04), but suppresses the outdated club far less, and in gemma-4-31b not at all (Table 13). 6
