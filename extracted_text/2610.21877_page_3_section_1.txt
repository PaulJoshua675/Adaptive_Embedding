Neural Sparse Retrieval. Learned sparse retrievers such as DeepImpact [20], uniCOIL [19], and SPLADE [9,8] improve classical lexical matching by learning term importance within the vocabulary space. These models retain compatibility with inverted indices while achieving substantial gains over traditional methods like BM25. Dense Retrieval. Dense retrieval methods leverage pre-trained language models to encode queries and documents into continuous embeddings [14,12,10]. Beyond representation learning, self-supervised domain adaptation through pseudo- relevance labeling has also been explored to improve dense and conversational dense retrieval across domains [15]. While effective at modeling semantic simi- larity, they require approximate nearest neighbor search for scalability [13], and sacrifice the transparency and efficiency of lexical inverted indexing. LLM-based Retrieval. Recent work explores scaling decoder-only LLMs for sparse retrieval, demonstrating strong effectiveness and zero-shot generaliza- tion [26,5]. Another line of work studies how to align neural representations with interpretable lexical spaces. For example, MILCO [21] introduces a multilingual connector that maps queries and documents into a shared English lexical space for multilingual and cross-lingual learned sparse retrieval. While these methods highlight the importance of lexical-space alignment, existing LLM-based sparse retrievers often produce highly dense activations, resulting in substantial storage and latency overhead. Another line of research explores LLM-based query expan- sion to enhance lexical matching and retrieval effectiveness. Li et al [16] provide a comprehensive survey of query expansion techniques in the PLM and LLM era. Recent studies further investigate prompt-based expansion and rank fusion [17], as well as automatic exemplar construction and multi-LLM expansion re- finement [18]. Unlike these generation-oriented approaches, our method directly learns sparse vocabulary representations through LLM-to-SPLADE alignment, without explicitly generating expansion text. Our work focuses on aligning LLM representations with the SPLADE vocabulary space while explicitly controlling sparsity through input-adaptive α-truncation.
