After 20 tasks, pruning had removed all but 1.1% of the lateral weights, and the network matched the plain MLP (ACC 32.3% against 32.6%), while the parameter-matched wider MLP did slightly better (35.1%). The surviving density depends on the L1 penalty, the pruning threshold, and the length of training, since a 3-task pilot kept 37%, so it says nothing about how many connections biology uses. Without consolidation all three networks forgot almost everything, with BWT between −65 and −68 points. Consolidation dominated everything else, lifting ACC from about 33% to between 73% and 80%. On top of it, links between co-active units changed ACC by less than half a point at every 𝜆with 𝐿= 200, and by at most 0.8 points at any lifetime. Random links improved ACC by 2.6, 2.0, and 2.4 points at 𝜆= 0.003, 0.01, and 0.03, through better retention (BWT improved by 2.8 to 4.2 points) at a small cost in plasticity (LA fell by 0.2 to 1.6 points). In the lifetime sweep, random links did best when short-lived and degraded steadily as their lifetime grew, from 81.0% at 𝐿= 25 to 79.5% at 𝐿= 2000. The biologically motivated rule never helped, and the control that was meant to be neutral did. 5.2 Study 2, random links act as shrinkage Figure 2 and Table 2 report Study 2.
