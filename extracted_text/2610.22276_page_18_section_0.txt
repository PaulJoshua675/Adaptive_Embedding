Backbone boundary. Qwen3.5-4B has 32 language layers with hidden width D = 2560. Full attention occurs at L4, L8, . . . , L32, and the other 24 layers are Gated DeltaNet. Qwen’s multimodal rotary embedding (Wang et al., 2024) rotates the first 64 coordinates of each head and leaves the other 192 unchanged. Each group of four query heads shares one key head, as in grouped-query attention (Ainslie et al., 2023). The score is a learned, position-aware scaled inner product. We average the head logits and apply a single softmax over the legal candidates. The candidate probability is Equation (1), with only the current alternatives in the denominator. Each logit thus decomposes into 16 head contributions, which the intervention analysis in Section E.4 exploits. Reusing the pretrained projections. Qwen’s Q projection is a bias-free 2560 →8192 matrix whose 512 output rows per head hold a 256-dimensional query followed by a 256-dimensional output gate. The readout uses the 256 query dimensions of each head, giving WQ ∈R4096×2560. The key projection WK ∈R1024×2560 and both head norms are kept as they are. All of these parameters start from the official pretrained checkpoint. Removing unused computation. Since the readout sits before the value aggregation and residual up- date of L32, the V projection, output-gate rows, output projection, post-attention norm, and SwiGLU FFN (Shazeer, 2020) of L32, together with the final language-model norm, have no path to the loss and are removed. Earlier layers stay intact. The readout gathers the S decision states and P t Kt option-end states before projecting them, so it forms neither a full-sequence Q/K projection nor an L × L attention matrix. Its candidate-comparison work is O(P t KtHd), while image encoding and the L1–L31 prefill still scale with the full input length. The retained language model has 4,111,374,336 trainable parameters, 95,688,192 fewer than the complete- language KEV model; the removed FFN alone accounts for 70,778,880. The Q/K projections and their head norms, 13,107,712 parameters, form the readout optimizer group, and the input norm stays in the backbone group. Trainable weights, gradients, Adam (Kingma and Ba, 2015) states, and all readout operations use FP32, while the retained language layers compute in BF16, following standard mixed-precision training (Mi- cikevicius et al., 2018; Kalamkar et al., 2019). The vision encoder and merger stay frozen in BF16. This keeps the small candidate comparison numerically stable while the backbone computation, which dominates the cost, runs in mixed precision.
