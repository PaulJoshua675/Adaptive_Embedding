Correlation-based Time Window Selection (CTWS) [17] adjusts the onset of window by correlating sliding windows with reference signals. Approaches based on Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) [18] and Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA) [19] employ meta-learning and heuristic searches to identify optimal configurations in time-frequency parameter space. However, these techniques typically evaluate and select candidate windows independently, treating each as an isolated “good/bad” decision based on the quality of the window , with configurations searched offline and applied uniformly across trials [20], [21]. This limitation can result in fragmented or physiologically implausible window sequences that fail to capture the coherent evolution of MI events. In contrast, our method optimizes the time window sequence, conditioning each decision on cross-window history and on the constraint of MI phases. MI events exhibit contralateral desynchronization of the µ (8–13 Hz) and β (13–30 Hz) rhythms during MI execution, and a subsequent rebound of synchronization of the β-rhythm after the event ends [22]. Such an evolution suggests distinct MI phases from onset to sustained imagery and finally to offset. Reinforcement Learning (RL) provides an ideal framework to overcome this limitation, as it inherently models sequential decision-making processes with uncertainty. By formulating the problem of time window optimization in MI-EEG as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), an agent makes adaptive decisions at each time step based on the current state and historical context (e.g., whether to retain a window and what temporal role it plays in the MI event) to maximize cumulative rewards through interactions with the environment. It also avoids brute-force search over window combinations through exploration–exploitation. In addition, Safe Reinforce- ment Learning [23] also inspires us to incorporate neural event ordinal information, where temporal-structure patterns must be embedded to constrain the action space. Notably, preliminary studies have demonstrated the potential of RL in improving BCI adaptivity. Aung et al. [24] use RL to decide whether to classify or skip the current time point, and Zhang et al. [25] use RL to adaptively focus on features at varying scales. In both, RL improves the adaptivity of the BCI decoding: the action optimizes one component inside the decoding pipeline, while the analyzed window remains fixed and predefined. Our work instead optimizes the window itself, taking each incoming window as the decision unit, assigning it an explicit semantic role, and constraining the action space by the structure of MI events. Therefore, we propose a Temporal Structure Embedded Reinforcement Learning (TSE-RL) framework for adaptive time window optimization in MI-EEG decoding. The core innovation of this framework is to embed prior temporal- structure knowledge of MI events into a stable RL framework, transforming window optimization from isolated “good/bad” judgments to semantic alignment of complete neural events. Specifically, TSE-RL incorporates three mechanisms to make this idea practical and reliable. The first mechanism transforms the action selection upon each time window from simply “retain/discard” to semantic alignment between window and neural events as Discard/Onset/sustain/Offset. This seman- tic alignment mechanism imposes physiological patterns on window optimization of EEG signal. The second mechanism adopts Finite State Machine (FSM) for constrained action masking which enables safe reinforcement learning in re- stricted action space. Without proper constraints, the RL agent may generate physiologically implausible window sequences that violate the known temporal structure of MI events (e.g., selecting an Offset before an Onset). The third mechanism concerns Transformer-based history encoding. The decisions of window optimization require integrating historical con- text, while traditional RL methods often employing Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to capture temporal dependencies. When processing long sequences, RNNs are prone to gradient vanishing or explosion, resulting in training instability [26]. The main contributions of this paper include: • We propose a temporal window optimization method that embeds MI event temporal structure knowledge into a re- inforcement learning framework. The action space based on EEG time window evaluation is semantically aligned with neural event for interpretability and performance improvement. FSM-based action masking is designed to ensure physiological plausible decision sequence with constraint of neural activity progress in term of plan- ning/execution/recovery. • We design a Transformer-based history encoder to replace training-unstable recurrent networks. This encoder takes historical window sequences as input and leverages self- attention mechanisms to learn temporal dependencies across windows in parallel, improving both training sta- bility and computational efficiency. • TSE-RL is plug-and-play and can be integrated with various mainstream MI classifiers. Simulated online ex- periments on multiple public datasets demonstrate that it significantly improves decoding performance in both cross-session and cross-subject settings. II. METHODS We formulate time-window optimization for online MI- EEG decoding as an constrained sequential decision-making problem. In contrast to fixed-length window decoding and traditional window optimization, TSE-RL learns to assign each candidate window a semantic role (Discard, Onset, Sustain, or Offset). As illustrated in Fig. 1(a), TSE-RL encodes the MI event’s temporal structure as a Finite State Machine (FSM) that governs the valid action space of an RL agent via action masking. This mechanism ensures the learned policy adheres to physiological plausibility, elevating window optimization from isolated “good/bad” judgments to semantic alignment of complete neural events. Moreover, TSE-RL employs a Transformer-based history encoder to stably integrate histori- cal information in parallel, preventing the training instability and sequential overhead commonly observed in recurrent RL.
