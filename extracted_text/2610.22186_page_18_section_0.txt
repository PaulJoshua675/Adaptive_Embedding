TCA-Captioner emphasizes temporal and cross-modal alignment, binding auditory events to visual entities and capturing complex causal dynamics through data construction and SFT training. AVSCap achieves explicit cross-modal event binding through a designed reward, focusing on whether the caption captures temporally synchronized audiovisual events. While our EVA-Cap shares the same objectives as these studies, we emphasize comprehensive, fine-grained, event-centric caption optimization and offer a distinct solution. Although the data curation pipelines differ in detail, they all achieve high-fidelity caption annotation, providing diverse options for data scaling. Methodologically, the reward design in AVSCap is closely related to our approach, paralleling the mechanism employed in our Stage 3 annotation pipeline. A key distinction in reward design is that we decouple audio and visual events for calculation during the GRPO process, whereas AVSCap treats synchronized audiovisual events as a unified whole. We leave the experimental comparison of these design choices, as well as the exploration of data mixing and methodological integration, for future work. A.5 Failure Cases and Limitations As shown in Fig. 6, we summarize two common failure modes of EVA-Cap: 1) In videos with rapid scene transitions, omissions in visual descriptions and misalignment between audio and visual descriptions still tend to occur. 2) In multi-character scenes with off-screen speech, the speaker identity is also prone to being incorrectly bound. This issue likely arises from limitations in understanding instances and capturing key information in complex, dynamic scenes. We intend to mitigate this limitation in future work by introducing grounded audiovisual captioning, to further enhance the fidelity and trustworthiness of audiovisual captioning by binding audio and visual cues to specific instances in space and time. Although EVA-Data is constructed via a multi-stage pipeline (initialization–revision–filtering) that enables scalable annotation, it still relies on proprietary MLLMs for caption generation and quality control. This dependence may introduce biases from proprietary teachers and leave residual annotation errors after automatic verification.
