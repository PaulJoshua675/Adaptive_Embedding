Scene Reconstruction Using Ubiquitous Wireless Signals 4.3 PERFORMANCE ACROSS SCENE DENSITY Scene density governs the complexity of the propagation conditions and with it the difficulty of the inverse problem: in sparse environments most links propagate through free space, and each measure- ment admits a narrow family of consistent scenes, whereas blockage, reflection, and higher-order interaction widen that and force the posterior to reconcile many such families at once. Diffusion + FM and the marginal-probability variants improve with more measurements before saturating. Additional observations help in two ways: new links, particularly new reflected paths, reach cells no earlier observation touched and enlarge the observable set, while additional coverage of observed cells sharpens the posterior there, as described by Proposition 4. Sat- uration follows from the same analysis, since once the users of a single base station have illuminated most of the scene its links can reach, further observations revisit covered cells and the remaining errors concen- trate on structures no link reaches. Diffusion + Raw Meas. instead stays near chance throughout: without a representation exposing the scene-relevant structure of the observations, more raw data does not become usable evidence. The two foundation-model approaches scale differently. Marginal Prob. + FM is comparable to Dif- fusion + FM under few or moderately many observations, as both rely on the same representations under similarly incomplete evidence, but is increasingly surpassed as measurements accumulate. The value of modeling the joint posterior thus emerges once enough complementary observations are available to integrate, reflecting the complementary roles of our two components. 5
