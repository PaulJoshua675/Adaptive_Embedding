(B) RoleQ places the scales on the unit axis, per row for readers and per column for writers, never on the stream axis. (C) Qwen3-14B, ternary at equal bits: the placement alone lifts MMLU from 25.4 (chance) with per-output-channel scales to 38.5. Functional units also guide the allocation of extra bits. At a tight budget, selecting units by quantization-error magnitude can perform worse than random; per-unit Fisher saliency estimates how strongly each unit’s error affects the loss. RoleQ-T therefore optionally adds a sparse second ternary plane to Fisher-selected units, preserving multiplication-free matrix products. At 2.16 bpw, Qwen3-8B-Base reaches 9.30 perplexity, 65.9 zero-shot accuracy and 62.8 MMLU. On post-trained Qwen3-8B, 1.99 bpw surpasses CAT-Q on all three measures for 0.28 additional bits. We make three contributions. 1. Role-aligned scale placement (Section 3.1). In a rotated basis, RoleQ matches AdaDim’s (Heo et al., 2024) reconstruction quality at half the solver work of a two-way search, without cali- bration data or stored axis choices. Per-vector INT4 matches MXFP4 at 8% fewer bits; ternary perplexity improves over the best uniform placement by up to 2×, with gains at every tested size from 0.6B to 14B across two families. At 14B, RoleQ raises MMLU from 25.4 (chance) with per-output-channel scales to 38.5. 2. Efficient ternary conversion (Sections 3.2–3.4). RoleQ-T outperforms Qronos, PT2-LLM and QTEA on all three measures at matched or lower memory-resident rates, with the largest gains on knowledge. At 14B, it surpasses QTEA’s released checkpoint after scale distillation using 0.67 fewer bpw. Applying the same distillation to PT2-LLM leaves it over four perplexity points behind at a higher rate. 3. Fisher-guided secondary planes (Section 3.5). At 8B, +0.57 bpw recovers about half the MMLU lost on Base. On the post-trained model at 1.99 bpw, Fisher selection beats random by 6.2 points and Hessian×weight by 9.0. 2
