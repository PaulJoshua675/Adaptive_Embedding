Graph-free Retrieval with Graph-Modified Embeddings Preprint 3.2 Questions and Gold Evidence We generate questions and ground-truth (gold) answers from the structured facts underlying the dataset. Gold evidence includes every chunk required to establish an answer. The benchmark contains 11 question families, detailed in Appendix A. We group them into three categories: Single-fact questions: These ask for attributes directly associated with entities mentioned in the query and generally require a single evidence chunk or an immediate relation. For example, “What skills did a person use at a given employer?” can be answered using a job chunk that mentions the person, employer, and skills. Relational and collection questions: These ask for attributes or entities connected to a named entity through intermediate entities. For example, “Which universities were attended by the employees of an organisation?” requires evidence linking the organisation to its employees and those employees to their universities. The target chunks may lie two or three relations from the named entity and share little surface content with the query. Extremisation questions: These compare candidates to identify a maximum and may then request another attribute of the selected candidate. Establishing the maximum requires the relevant records of all competing candidates. For example, “What certifications does the most experienced worker at a given company hold?” requires experience records for the comparison and the winner’s certification evidence, which may be semantically unrelated to the comparison criterion. 3.3 Graph-Based Embedding Transformations We modify the original embeddings offline to incorporate information from related nodes in the graph.
