3) The Rosenbrock results reveal an opposite and more nuanced pattern, where the classical advantage of DE-based strategies proves conditional rather than intrinsic. Among the non-DRL baselines, JADE and LSHADE outperform LSHADE-SPACMA, reflecting DE’s advantage on Rosenbrock's continuously curving valley: lacking directional memory, difference-vector mutation can pivot immediately to track the valley’s rotation, whereas CMA-ES’s evolution-path-based covariance accumulation introduces a momentum-like lag that causes the covariance matrix to perpetually trail the valley’s curvature [47]. Among the DRL-based algorithms, this relationship is strikingly reversed: DRL-DE fails entirely, exhibiting prolonged stagnation with only sporadic, restart-driven improvement, while DRL-CMA-ES reaches the optimum through slow but steady progression. This reversal is attributable to the absence, in the DRL-DE implementation, of the sophisticated adaptive mechanisms present in LSHADE (success-history parameter memory, external archive, and linear population size reduction). Without these mechanisms providing systematic population management and parameter diversity, the structural limitation of DE, the coordinate-axis bias of binomial crossover, which prevents it from efficiently producing the arbitrarily oriented, anisotropic refinement steps demanded near the optimum of Rosenbrock, becomes the dominant factor. This confirms that the advantage of DE on Rosenbrock depends on the sophistication of its accompanying adaptive mechanisms, rather than being an unconditional property of the difference-vector mutation operator itself. 4) Tracking the elite and orchestration ratio histories of DRL-DCO in Figure 3 further illustrates how the learned policy evolves during training. The warm-up period provides the agent with an initial, unconstrained exploration space in which to accumulate first-hand experience and establish a preliminary search direction. Once training stabilizes, the agent settles into a narrower elite ratio, and adjusts its orchestration ratio according to the problem at hand. On the nonconvex Rastrigin and Rosenbrock functions specifically, the stabilized policy favors the DE component while retaining a partial CMA-ES contribution, preserving the directional flexibility needed to escape local minima without fully discarding the covariance-based momentum that supports faster convergence. On the comparatively easier Griewank function, by contrast, this adaptive orchestration proves largely unnecessary: both DRL-CMA-ES and DRL-DCO already reach the global optimum within the first warm-up episode (1000 generations), while the actor-critic policy is still in its early, largely untrained stage, indicating that the underlying evolutionary operators alone are sufficient for this landscape. 5) Overall, the DRL-DE and DRL-CMA-ES ablations, obtained by fixing β(t) to 1 and 0 respectively, show markedly inferior and less reliable convergence, particularly on Rastrigin, Rosenbrock, and Schwefel, where at least one of the two exhausts the maximum generation budget without reaching the convergence threshold. This demonstrates that the orchestration ratio is not a peripheral hyperparameter but a structurally essential mechanism, since committing to a single operator leaves the algorithm unable to compensate for that operator's inherent limitations on mismatched landscapes, regardless of how well its remaining hyperparameters are tuned. On Rastrigin, the generation-wise β(t) trajectory shows the agent learning to alternate between CMA-ES-dominant phases for rapid descent into candidate basins and DE-dominant phases for diversity restoration, effectively reproducing the role of population-restart strategies such as IPOP-CMA-ES [46] through continuous reweighting rather than discrete reinitialization. On Rosenbrock, the agent learns to engage CMA-ES for anisotropic local refinement while periodically invoking DE-driven population relocation to escape curvature-tracking stagnation. These emergent behaviors confirm that the learned orchestration policy captures complementary operator strengths in a problem-adaptive, trajectory-aware manner that neither fixed hybridization (LSHADE-SPACMA) nor single-operator DRL control (DRL-DE, DRL-CMA-ES) can replicate. Taken together, these results reflect a consistent, theoretically grounded pattern, echoing the NFL theorem’s prediction that no single operator or fixed hybridization strategy achieves uniformly strong performance across all landscape types. LSHADE-SPACMA, the strongest non-DRL baseline, excels on Rastrigin but lags on Rosenbrock, while JADE and LSHADE show the opposite tendency and perform less effectively on Ackley and Griewank at Nd = 50. Similarly, DRL-DE and DRL-CMA-ES each succeed where the other fails. DRL-DCO, through its learned generation-wise orchestration of both operators in response to the observed optimization state, achieves consistently competitive performance across all five functions, demonstrating that dynamic, state-aware operator coordination is not merely beneficial but necessary for robust high-dimensional optimization across diverse problem classes. 14
