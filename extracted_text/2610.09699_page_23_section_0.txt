Negative values indicate options that tend to improve performance, while positive values indicate options that tend to degrade performance, according to the surrogate model. Global importance. To assess overall importance, we aggregate SHAP values across all configu- rations by computing the median value per option, as shown in Figure 4. Several modules, such as Step-Size Adaptation (SSA), strongly influence performance, whereas others, such as repelling restart, have only a marginal effect. As these results are aggregated over all 24 BBOB functions, options that are highly beneficial for specific functions may appear less influential overall, and mechanisms that are only active under certain conditions (e.g., repelling restarts requiring a restart strategy) naturally show reduced impact (de Nobel et al., 2024c). The sign of the SHAP values indicates whether options tend to improve or degrade performance; for example, the binary modules Active and Orthogonal have slightly more positive than negative SHAP values overall, suggesting a modest negative effect on performance. However, these values reflect average effects within the explored configuration space and are influenced by interactions with other parameters, so options that are beneficial in isolation may appear detrimental on average. Finally, we observe substantial variation within modules: for instance, within SSA, CSA generally contributes positively, whereas MXNES tends to be negative, highlighting that both the specific option and the interplay with other modules de Nobel et al. (2021) play a key role in determining performance. Local analysis. To illustrate the insights provided by the SHAP analysis, we focus on the Matrix Adaptation module, as this is also examined in detail in Section 8. Similar results for the remaining modules are provided in Appendix B. For this module, we analyze the SHAP values on a per-function basis, revealing how individual options contribute to performance across the BBOB suite. The results are visualized as a heatmap in Figure 5, where each cell shows the median SHAP value for a given option and function. Immediately, the detrimental effect of disabling matrix adaptation on conditioned functions becomes apparent, particularly on the ellipsoid function f2 and its rotated counterpart f10. In contrast, separable adaptation yields a strong positive effect on f2, while performing similarly poorly to no adaptation on f10, consistent with expectations.
