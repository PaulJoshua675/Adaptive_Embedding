A qualifying animal study reports at least one specified parameter: R0, Rt, Re, r, growth rate, mutation rate, extrinsic incubation period, vector reproduction number, vector competence, or mosquito delays. Animal infection, disease, or serology results alone do not satisfy this animal-study condition. A risk factor qualifies when the study reports a statistical measure of its association with infection, severe disease, or death. A proposed, qualitative, or unmeasured risk does not qualify. Give systematic reviews and meta-analyses special consideration: retain them for full-text assessment when they may report pooled eligible estimates, but do not treat discussion of the topic alone as an eligible result. When no abstract is available, judge from the title and retain the record unless the title itself shows an excluded publication type, a different virus, or work clearly outside the eligible evidence. A record is relevant when its own work reports quantitative human outbreak characteristics, a mathematical or statistical transmission model, an eligible quantitative estimate, seroprevalence data, or risk factors with statistical measures, based on natural disease transmission; animal studies qualify only when they report specified transmission or vector parameters.
