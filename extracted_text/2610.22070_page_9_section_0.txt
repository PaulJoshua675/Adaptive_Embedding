We further stress-test our memory mechanism on the Historical Retrospection (HR) split of StreamArena (Zhang et al., 2026d); implementation details are provided in Appendix C. HR specifically targets long-horizon memory: the query is issued substantially later than the appearance of the relevant visual evidence, with a median query-evidence gap of 12.1 minutes. We compare MaLoW (Qwen3.5-9B) with strong streaming video models, including MOSS-VL-Realtime-11B (Wang et al., 2026), VideoChat3-4B (Li et al., 2026), AURA-8B (Lu et al., 2026), and VST-7B (Guan et al., 2026). These models either maintain a long context window (MOSS-VL-Realtime), or compress historical observations into text-based memory (VideoChat3, AURA, and VST). As reported in Table 3, MaLoW achieves an average score of 22.8, outperforming VST and MOSS-VL-Realtime by 2.2% and 4.5%, respectively. These results highlight the effectiveness of representing history in evolving model weights for retaining information over long temporal horizons. MaLoW generalizes across model scales and families. We further evaluate MaLoW across a broader range of model scales (0.8B–4B) and model families (Qwen3.5 and Gemma4); implementation details are provided in Appendix C. Following the official benchmark protocols, we evaluate all three splits of OVO-Bench (Backward, Real-Time, and Forward) and all four splits of StreamingBench (Real-Time, Omni, Proactive, and SQA). For each backbone, we compare MaLoW against two baselines: (1) the corresponding base model operating with full context and (2) the same model restricted to a local context window. Meanwhile, MaLoW substantially improves over the local-window baseline on memory-intensive splits (blue cells), highlighting the effectiveness of the internalized memory. 4.2 Additional Demonstration and Analysis Extension to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models. We further extend MaLoW to robotic planning, where it is used to internalize historical visual observations and action trajectory for action prediction. Following VLM4VLA (Zhang et al., 2026b), we adapt Qwen3.5-2B (Qwen Team, 2026) by attaching a lightweight MLP action decoder and fine-tuning the VLM backbone, resulting in our baseline model, Q3.5-2B-MLP. The model is trained on the Bridge and RT-1 subsets of the OXE dataset (O’Neill et al., 2024) to predict future actions based solely on the current visual observation. We then train MaLoW on top of Q3.5-2B-MLP using the Bridge subset of OXE. During this stage, the VLM backbone is frozen and receives only the current observation, while MaLoW and the MLP action decoder are optimized to internalize historical observations and action trajectories and produce more accurate action trajectories. We refer to the resulting model as MaLoW (Q3.5-2B-MLP). We evaluate both models on the Simpler-Bridge with WidowX robot, with results reported in Table 2.
