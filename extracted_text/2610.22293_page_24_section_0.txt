With projector Π (Eq. 3), Adam moments ˆm, ˆv, and preconditioned raw step ˆm/( √ ˆv + ϵ): • Project gradient: g ←Π(g); then ˆm, ˆv ←Adam(g); W ←W −lr · ˆm/( √ ˆv + ϵ). Here ˆv sees only free-subspace energy, and the applied step D Π( ˆm) with D = diag(1/( √ ˆv + ϵ)) re-enters the protected space because D and Π do not commute. • Project the applied update: ˆm, ˆv ←Adam(g) on the full gradient; Wpre ←W; W ←W −lr· ˆm/( √ ˆv+ϵ); W ←Wpre+Π(W −Wpre). The update is confined to the low singular value subspace because projection is last, and the free step keeps the scale induced by the full gradient. • Re-project cumulative change: g ←Π(g); take the Adam step; then set W ←Wref +Π(W −Wref). Note that W ←Wref + Π(W −Wref) is equivalent to Wt + Π(δt), with δt the step just applied, since every previously applied change already lies in the free subspace. The update lies in the correct subspace, but the step is based on projected-gradient moments and is therefore mis-scaled. The full procedure. Algorithm 1 details SST’s implementation, using the second of these three schemes. A superscript ℓindexes one constrained matrix, so W ℓ t is that matrix at step t and plays the role of Wpre above. The optimizer itself is never modified. It receives the full gradient, updates its moments, and proposes an ordinary step; SST then replaces the part of that step which lands on a constrained matrix with its free-to-free block. Parameters outside I, such as embeddings and layer norms, are left to the optimizer. B.9 Comparison with subspace-constrained baselines The closest baselines are Sculpting [34] and OPLoRA [54].
