F Verification The artifact [15] holds the Rocq development, the generators, the evaluators and the record of every run. Everything was run with Python 3.12.3, PyTorch 2.14.0 with CUDA 12.6, NumPy 2.5.3, safetensors 0.8.0 and GCC 13.3.0 on Ubuntu 24.04, on an Intel Core i9-12900H with 20 hardware threads and 31 GiB of memory and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070 Ti Laptop GPU with 8 GiB of memory. F.1 The Rocq development The Rocq development, rocq/ in the artifact, has 66 files and 24,230 lines. With Rocq 9.1.1, its standard library 9.2.0, Coquelicot 3.4.5, Interval 4.11.4 and MathComp 2.4.0 it builds in about two minutes. Table 6 gives the Rocq theorems behind each result. rocq/Assumptions.v names them and the lemmas they rest on, 144 theorems in all, and src/paper.py rocq rebuilds the development and prints the axioms of each. The theorems on netlists give every unit its value in terms of the state and show by induction over the units that each unit fires to it; those on programs follow a run step by step in SUBLEQA with the device of Definition 5.1; and those on stored networks relate each pass of the ring machine to one step of the machine stored in it, the organism’s decoder or the host with its device. Section 8 is proved on a probability space given by its properties. A function P on the events of the perturbations (eg) is monotone and subadditive, is 0 on the empty event and 1 on the sure one, multiplies on events that depend on disjoint sets of perturbations, and is additive and complementary on the events built from the threshold events {c ≤eg}; each perturbation has the tails P(eg ≥c) = Q(c/s) and P(eg ≤c) = Q(−c/s), and in Proposition 8.10 each output flips with probability ε.
