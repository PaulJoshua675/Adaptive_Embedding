Within this layer, capability-bearing Agent Card fields provide explicit structural signals for candidate genera- tion and ranking, while a maximum candidate budget controls the compact candidate context subsequently presented to the LLM orchestrator. To distinguish improvements arising from candidate pre- selection in general from properties specific to SSR, the end-to-end evaluation also instantiates the same candidate- selection architecture using the Best Matching 25 (BM25) ranking function [11]. SSR and BM25 operate under the same maximum candidate budget and the same LLM arbitration procedure. This controlled comparison allows the effects of maintaining a compact candidate context to be separated from differences attributable to the retrieval mechanism itself. The evaluation addresses three research questions: • RQ1: How does full-catalog LLM orchestration scale with registry size, and to what extent can SSR-based candidate pre-selection reduce its operational overhead? • RQ2: How effective and efficient is the SSR retrieval mechanism on naturally occurring text, and what are the contributions of IDF weighting and the ISI? • RQ3: How does retrieval-based candidate pre-selection affect end-to-end agent selection, candidate context, cost, latency, and operational reliability as registry size grows, and how does SSR compare with BM25 under the same maximum candidate budget? The main contributions of this work are: • We formulate scalable LLM-based agent discovery from a candidate-context scalability perspective, explicitly sep- arating registry-scale retrieval, retrieval computation, and the compact candidate context presented to the LLM orchestrator. • We propose SSR, a deterministic and training-free re- trieval method that integrates field-derived structural sig- nals, IDF-weighted matching, and selective inverted in- dexing into a dedicated pre-orchestration retrieval layer for Agent Card discovery. This layer produces a compact pool of top-ranked Agent Cards while preserving explicit score traceability. • We introduce SSR-AgentBench, a reproducible syn- thetic benchmark containing 1,000 procedurally gener- ated Agent Cards and 250 natural-language discovery queries with designated target Agent Cards for controlled registry-scaling experiments. • We provide a systems-level characterization of candidate- context scalability through controlled registry growth, public-corpus retrieval, and end-to-end LLM arbitration. The evaluation separates general effects of candidate pre-selection from SSR-specific retrieval behavior while measuring retrieval coverage, candidate context, prompt tokens, estimated cost, latency, reliability, and end-to-end selection effectiveness.
