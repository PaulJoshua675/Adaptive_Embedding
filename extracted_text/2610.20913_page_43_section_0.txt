End-to-end resource accounting. Disaggregation reduces online GPU consumption from 5.8 to 1.1 GPU-hours per million requests (−81.2%) without increasing storage or transfer on the online serving path. In colocated serving, scoring stores and transfers the full pre-tokenization behavioral history and repeatedly executes history-dependent computation; TransX instead materializes a compact behavior-memory KV snapshot for online reuse. As a result, persistent storage and median network traffic remain approximately flat, while the remaining history-state construction and refresh cost is moved to bulk and nearline paths outside the online serving path. The gain therefore comes from both avoiding repeated history computation and replacing the online history representation with compact reusable state. After converting online GPU, nearline CPU, daily bulk compute, storage, and network resources to a common normalized production-cost basis at matched traffic and SLO, total serving cost is reduced to 22% (colocated = 1.00, TransX 0.22). Operational robustness and independent scaling. TransX treats stale or unavailable behavioral state as a degradation condition rather than allowing recovery to enter the online serving path.
