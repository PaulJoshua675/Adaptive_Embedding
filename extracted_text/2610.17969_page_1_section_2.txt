Deep learning employs a broad family of mechanisms for controlling internal scale. Batch normalization, layer normalization (LayerNorm), and root-mean-square nor- malization (RMSNorm) normalize activations (Ioffe and Szegedy, 2015; Ba et al., 2016; Zhang and Sennrich, 2019). DeepNet, ReZero, LayerScale, and Fixup con- trol the scale of residual additions (Wang et al., 2024; Bachlechner et al., 2021; Touvron et al., 2021b; Zhang et al., 2019). Attention scaling and query–key normal- ization (QK-Norm) regulate softmax inputs (Vaswani et al., 2017; Henry et al., 2020; Dehghani et al., 2023; Team, 2024). Loss-balancing methods and adaptive optimizers manage scale imbalances in objectives and parameter updates (Kendall et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2021; Kingma and Ba, 2015; You et al., 2020). These scale-control mechanisms play a critical role in improving training stability and perfor- mance (Xiong et al., 2020; Zhai et al., 2023; Wortsman et al., 2024). All of these mechanisms manage how quantities of different scales interact within a neural network (Figure 1). We interpret these scale-control mechanisms through dimensional consistency. In physics, terms added to- gether must have compatible units, and the arguments of exp and log must be dimensionless. A physical unit, such as a centimeter or a meter, provides a reference scale for expressing a measured quantity. Inspired by physical units and dimensions, we define a neural unit as the rule governing how an internal tensor scales when the computation graph is rescaled. Neural units describe internal scaling behavior even when the data have no physical units. In terms of neural units, each scale-control mechanism in deep learning becomes a unit operation. Normalization removes units, residual branches restore stream units, attention temperatures convert logits to dimensionless quantities, loss weights convert loss units, and optimizers map gradient units back to parameter units.
