Table 1 compares gen- eration quality and efficiency on 5-second videos. Both verifiers maintain the overall VBench perfor- mance of the four-step generator while using fewer than 1.7 denoising steps per token on average. By accepting settled predictions early and continuing to refine the remaining tokens, our method pre- serves generation quality with less than half the de- noising token evaluations of the original schedule. Long video generation. Table 2 reports results on long video generation. The training-free verifier re- tains competitive quality with a small loss in con- sistency, while the learned verifier maintains both the quality and consistency of the four-step gener- ator. Both verifiers use approximately 1.7 denois- ing steps per token on average and achieve lower Drift than the four-step generator. Both also main- tain comparable dynamic degree, suggesting that the reduced quality variation is not simply a con- sequence of suppressing motion. World generation. Table 3 reports results on world generation, a more challenging setting in which camera control induces substantial changes across the visible scene. Accordingly, our methods tend to allocate more denoising steps than in standard video generation. Despite this increased difficulty, the training-free verifier remains competitive in quality while maintaining low Drift. The learned verifier preserves quality and consistency comparable to the four-step generator while achieving lower Drift. Camera-pose errors against the commanded trajectory also remain comparable, indicat- ing that acceleration preserves camera-following accuracy. Qualitative results. Figure 4 compares our generations with the corresponding four-step genera- tors. On both LongLive and Lingbot, our method preserves visual quality comparable to four-step generation without noticeable degradation over time. On LongLive, it maintains subject identity and image quality even in scenes with dynamic objects. On Lingbot, previously observed regions re- main coherent under large camera motion, while newly revealed regions are further refined, yielding consistent transitions between accepted and refined areas. 7
