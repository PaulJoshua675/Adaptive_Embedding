Public transport is the backbone of mobility in large cities, and its efficient management requires continuous information about which vehicles operate on each corridor, how often they pass, and whether they follow their assigned routes. Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) increasingly obtain this infor- mation from the surveillance cameras already installed along urban roads. These cameras generate thousands of hours of video, far more than can be inspected or annotated manually, so methods that extract useful information from such footage without human supervision are of great practical value. In this work, we address the problem of grouping public transport vehicles captured by a fixed surveillance camera into semantically meaningful categories, such as transit routes and operators, without using labeled data. The problem is challeng- ing for two complementary reasons. First, buses operating on different routes often share the same body structure and differ mainly in their paint schemes. Second, vehicles of different types, such as full-size buses, microbuses (small buses), and vans, may share similar colors while differing substantially in shape. A useful representation must therefore capture both morphology and color. Most vision-based vehicle classification systems are super- vised and rely on large, site-specific annotated datasets with fixed taxonomies in which “bus” is a single class [1]–[3]. Self- supervised learning has reduced the need for labels during representation learning [4], but in the vehicle domain it has mainly been used as pre-training for a supervised classifier [5]. Unsupervised clustering, on the other hand, has been applied mainly to vehicle re-identification, which groups images of the same physical vehicle rather than different vehicles that share a category [6]. Self-supervised Vision Transformers such as DINOv2 [7] now provide general-purpose descriptors that capture image structure well without fine-tuning. However, their features may under-represent pure chromatic information, which is crucial for distinguishing transit routes. To address this problem, we propose a hybrid unsupervised clustering pipeline for public transport vehicles. Buses are first detected and tracked in the video, and a single crop is stored per vehicle. Each crop is then described by two complementary descriptors: a morphological descriptor given by the class token of a frozen DINOv2 model, and a color descriptor given by hue and saturation histograms computed in the HSV color space. Both descriptors are normalized and combined through weighted early fusion, and the fused vectors are projected with UMAP and grouped with K-Means, as illustrated in Fig. 1. We apply the pipeline to images of buses operating on one of the main arterial roads of Mexico City, and evaluate whether it can isolate the buses of the Red de Transporte de Pasajeros (RTP), the city’s public bus network, as a coherent cluster.
