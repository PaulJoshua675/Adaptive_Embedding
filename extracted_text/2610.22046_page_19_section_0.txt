Residualization. For Section 3, we fit a logistic model of trace misses on failure type, trace length, answer position, difficulty, and best reachable value at step 0, V ∗ 0 . The difference between PITs and near misses after controls is the average marginal effect obtained by changing the failure type from near miss to PIT while holding each other variable at its observed value, then averaging over traces. For the detection AUROC after controls, we regress trace length, difficulty, problem source, answer length, and numeric-answer status out of the confidence probe’s log odds. We score the residual on held-out traces and average over five seeds. As a stratified check, we also compare the miss rates of PITs and near misses within cells of V ∗ 0 bin by trace-length tertile that hold at least 30 near misses. The PIT miss rate minus the near-miss miss rate is positive in all five such cells at 4B and in seven of eight at 8B, where the exception holds 33 near misses. The confidence probe is a causal transformer encoder over the steps of a trace, with a learned positional embedding and a two-layer sigmoid head. The two scales use the same architecture and training settings and differ only in input width and number of parameters (Table 4). For each step, the input concatenates the hidden states of the two read-site layers (Appendix D) at one token position. The objective broadcasts the trace’s final label Y to every valid step and minimizes per-step Bernoulli loss, averaged over the valid steps in the batch: Lconf = − 1 P i Ti X i Ti X t=1  Yi log ci,t + (1 −Yi) log(1 −ci,t)  , ci,t = σ(g(hi,1:t)). (3) Here hi,1:t is the sequence of hidden states read at the read site for steps 1 to t of trace i, g is the encoder’s scalar logit output, and σ is the logistic function. A Brier-loss robustness variant remains within the BCE variant’s seed spread on every detection and calibration measurement (Table 13). The experiment establishes no difference between the two objectives, which were not compared pairwise. Each model trains on all traces with the problem split, which holds out about 30% of the traces, so no problem contributes traces to both training and validation. We fit one temperature on held-out logits. Expected calibration error uses ten equal-width bins and is reported before temperature scaling, which reduces it (Table 13). As a leakage comparison, we also train with a 70/30 split by trace, stratified to preserve the base correctness rate, which we call the trace split. Under that split, 97.6% of validation traces share a problem with at least one training trace. Table 5 gives the trace-split results; the 4B difference between PITs and near misses after controls is +0.085 under the trace split and +0.037 under the problem split.
