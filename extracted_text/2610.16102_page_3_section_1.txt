Polanyi opened his account of “tacit knowing” with a face (Polanyi 1966, 4). We can pick out a familiar face among a thousand, even a million, yet we usually cannot say how we do it. He then described the police Identikit: a witness composes a likeness by choosing a nose, a mouth and a hairline from a stock of drawn features. The kit does communicate a face, but only because the witness can tacitly match each drawn feature against the remembered whole—which is why, in his phrase, “we can know more than we can tell” (Polanyi 1966, 4–5). The first commercial deployment of the VaCoAl architecture is also a face. Shuhari System’s CASRAM, a content-addressable memory that realizes VaCoAl on SRAM inside an FPGA, is offered in a webcam face-recognition system that identifies up to 30 people at once (Shuhari System n.d.; Shuhari System 2026). In that system a GPU-based feature extractor turns each face into an embedding whose dimensions no one can interpret, and CASRAM matches the embedding by block-wise majority vote, whose agreement can be read off as a confidence. The system knows more than it can tell. Unlike us, it can also show how strongly its parts agreed. This paper takes that coincidence seriously. The question has become urgent for a reason outside our programme. In July 2026 a coun- terexample to the Jacobian conjecture in dimension three, found with the Fable AI model, was announced; it can be verified by a brief calculation. Terence Tao observed that, presented this way, the construction looks like a “massive miracle”, and he wrote a “digestion” of it as an exercise to himself (Tao 2026b). In a Mathstodon thread Tao described the broader pattern (Tao 2026a). Responsible AI use could support all the follow-up activities that a solution nor- mally sets off, but often the opposite happens: results come from prompters who cannot answer questions about them or give talks, fewer seminars, workshops and collaborations follow, few newcomers join the field, and open directions are withheld for fear of being scooped. In a guest post on Tao’s blog, Grant Sanderson compressed the point into one line: “every AI-generated proof is born an unsolved exposition problem” (Sanderson 2026). We read these observations through Polanyi’s dictum that “all knowledge is either tacit or rooted in tacit knowledge” (Polanyi 1969, 144). An AI-produced result that no human holds tacitly is explicit knowledge without a root. It can be checked, but it is hard to teach, extend or value, because those activities draw on tacit knowing. We call such results rootless explicit knowledge, and we ask what kind of substrate could help re-root them. Our answer builds on the VaCoAl programme (Chuma, Otsuka & Sato 2026a–g). VaCoAl is a hyperdimensional memory whose every operation reduces to XOR-and-shift over GF(2). Its binding is exactly reversible, its retrieval is a majority vote with a readable confidence, and its multi-hop traversal of a mentor–student DAG of 25.5 million paths has been demonstrated in the software version of PyVaCoAl. Our companion paper on Minsky’s Emotion Machine observed that audit and valuation are one circuit at opposite settings of one parameter (2026f): in VaCoAl/PyVaCoAl, the same substrate yields either an exactly auditable trace or an emergent valuation of paths, not both at full strength. The step taken here is epistemological: we argue that this trade-off is the right formal model of the tacit–explicit relation. The paper makes five contributions. 1. An algebraic reading of Polanyi’s structure of tacit knowing (§3). The “from–to relation” (attending from individual clues to the whole they form; for example, when we recognize a face from its features) becomes non-commutative binding, “subsidiary aware- ness” (awareness of clues that we rely on but do not attend to; for example, the feeling in the palm that grips a hammer) becomes bundled-but-unqueried content, “focal aware- ness” (awareness of the object we attend to; for example, the nail being driven in) be- comes unbinding, the Identikit becomes role–filler composition, and recognition becomes majority-vote coincidence (Polanyi 1958, 55–56; 1966, 4–10). 2.
