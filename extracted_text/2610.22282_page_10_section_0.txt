Preprint 6 DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS RPO places its policy action inside attention, and this design choice also defines its current limits. First, the framework is confined to attention. Its action is a mask over key-value blocks, so it requires a backbone that computes attention. We have not yet generalized routing to other architectures, where no attention mass exists to propose routes. Second, the routing proposal and its likelihood are coarse. Each route logit averages the attention mass of a whole block over every routed layer and head, which hides token-level and layer-specific structure. Moreover, the velocity likelihood treats the routed deviation as Gaussian, although a discrete route produces it. A finer proposal and a likelihood that also credits the route choice could give the policy a more faithful view of which blocks matter. Finally, RPO currently adapts to an existing generator rather than shaping it. Co-designing the network architecture with RPO is a promising direction for achieving both higher efficiency and larger RL gains. 7
