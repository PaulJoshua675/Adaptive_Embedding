More structured approaches spread reveals across dilated position groups or compare short candidate decoding paths before committing tokens (Luxembourg et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2026). Adaptive-parallelism methods include Fast-dLLM, which combines confidence- thresholded decoding with approximate block-wise KV caching (Wu et al., 2026), and EB-Sampler, which dynamically reveals multiple positions subject to an entropy-based error tolerance (Ben-Hamu et al., 2025). Other methods refine the selection criterion: UNCODE/PC-Sampler calibrates uncertainty scores using position- and token-informativeness priors (Huang et al., 2026a); KLASS incorporates the temporal KL stability of token distributions (Kim et al., 2025b); and DAPD uses a self-attention-derived dependency graph to select weakly coupled positions for parallel decoding (Kim et al., 2026). Learned unmasking schedules. A closely related line of work learns a controller around a pretrained denoiser. Jazbec et al. (2026) formulate masked-diffusion sampling as a Markov decision process and use reinforcement learning to train a one-layer transformer that maps decoder confidences to monotonic unmasking decisions. Their transitions preserve already visible tokens, whereas TIPS extends the action semantics to reversible next-step visibility. Where-to-Unmask trains a supervised ranker to imitate a ground-truth probability-margin oracle (Asano et al., 2026). Adaptive Order Policies reweight masked-diffusion loss terms by policy probabilities to favor positions at which the denoiser is likely to be correct, considering both frozen-denoiser and joint-training settings (Mohamud et al., 2026). Self-Aware Scheduling (SAS) derives a dense trajectory reward from a bound on sequential decoding mismatch and trains an order policy with group-relative policy optimization while keeping the denoiser fixed (Xu et al., 2026). UPO similarly learns a stochastic position-selection policy for a frozen masked diffusion model, using a KL-regularized, GRPO-style objective around an explicit heuristic reference policy (Hong et al., 2026a). Its actions select only currently masked positions, preserving monotonicity. Re-masking and revision. Re-masking methods relax monotonicity by allowing earlier token commitments to be withdrawn. P2 and ReMDM allow visible positions to return to the mask state and be regenerated, with ReMDM deriving its sampler from a discrete diffusion process with a custom reverse transition (Peng et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025). Running Confidence Remasking (RCR), introduced alongside MDPO, revisits positions with poor running confidence (He et al., 2025). WINO uses a training-free parallel draft-and-verify procedure to reveal candidates and re-mask suspicious visible tokens (Hong et al., 2026b), while CoRe identifies revision targets by testing prediction stability under masked-context perturbations (Zhai et al., 2026). These approaches prescribe revision rules rather than learning a separate visibility controller. RemeDi instead modifies the language model to jointly predict tokens and confidence scores, then trains its re-masking behavior through supervised and reinforcement-learning stages (Huang et al., 2026b). TIPS also uses a supervised warmup phase followed by reinforcement learning. Reinforcement learning for diffusion language models. Policy optimization has also been applied directly to the dLLM. MDPO updates the masked token model along progressive denoising trajectories using intermediate rewards (He et al., 2025); mask-aware policy gradients account for both token and masking decisions (Raajesh et al., 2026); and SPG constructs upper- and lower-bound surrogates for the otherwise intractable sequence log-likelihood (Wang et al., 2026). TIPS instead applies policy gradients only to its auxiliary Bernoulli visibility policy, hence training requires neither sequence likelihood nor updates to decoder parameters. 6 Discussion We introduced TIPS, a small policy that learns unmasking and remasking around a frozen dLLM, separating visibility control from token prediction while a fixed schedule regulates expected progress. On GSM8K and MATH-500, TIPS improves low- and moderate-budget accuracy and remains competitive at higher budgets without retraining; full-canvas and blockwise variants offer complementary strengths. Ablations, corruption recovery, heuristic-prefix handoffs, remasking and editing statistics, and transfer experiments highlight the value of warmup and remasking, suggest that revision may help by withdrawing harmful context rather than only replacing tokens, and reveal transferable but domain-dependent behavior. 9
