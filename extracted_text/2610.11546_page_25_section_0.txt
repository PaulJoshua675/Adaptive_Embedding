This enriched state gives the DDPG-based actor a comprehensive, temporally coherent view of the search dynamics, enabling more informed and timely adjustment of the DE and CMA-ES hyperparameters across generations and episodes. 3) The incorporation of learnable population scaling and elite preservation mechanisms enables the DRL-DCO agent to dynamically modulate both the effective population size and the proportion of elite solutions inherited across generations, in direct response to the observed optimization state. This adaptive resource allocation allows the agent to deploy smaller, computationally efficient populations during exploitation-dominant phases and to expand search coverage during exploration-demanding stages, improving overall efficiency and stability on both high-dimensional nonconvex benchmark functions and the practical FEMU task. 4) DRL-DCO incorporates a learned restart policy governed by a learnable restart probability, estimated from stall count and diversity statistics, which triggers population reinitialization to escape deceptive local minima on highly multimodal landscapes such as Rastrigin and Schwefel. This is complemented by two auxiliary, independently learnable CMA-ES control actions, a step-size parameter and a covariance-scaling action, which continuously regulate the search radius and distribution orientation without discarding accumulated covariance information. 5) DRL-DCO achieves reliable optimization performance through its two operating phases. During the agent-training phase, the actor–critic agent learns the orchestration policy through online interaction with the optimization environment and critic-mediated reward feedback. After training, the agent-inference phase uses the trained actor alone to generate generation-wise control actions through forward inference, without further policy updates or online adaptation. The stable control trajectories observed during inference indicate that the trained policy has captured effective search behavior rather than relying on continued learning during optimization. Notably, a single training run of 20–30 episodes produces a policy that generalizes reliably to independently initialized instances across all tested problem classes, demonstrating the practical reusability and self-contained intelligence of the proposed method. Although DRL-DCO and related RL-assisted evolutionary approaches demonstrate strong adaptability, they inevitably inherit the stochastic uncertainty of neural network training, coupling the optimization of the target problem with the stochastic training of the policy itself. Future work will therefore investigate more physically informed, model-based reinforcement learning strategies, incorporating explicit mathematical mappings between structural parameters and target modal characteristics. Replacing or augmenting the data-driven critic with mechanistically transparent physics-based models and gradient- informed feedback may further reduce training uncertainty, enhance interpretability, and improve the reliability of RL-assisted optimization for structural model updating and other engineering inverse problems.
