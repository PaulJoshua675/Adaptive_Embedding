3 Experiments We ask the central question: When a model is fine-tuned on a new task, does confining the update to the non-principal subspace let it learn the task while retaining what it already knew? Models and tasks. We fine-tune Qwen3-8B [56] on data containing explicit <think>. . .</think> traces in four domains: Science, Finance, Pathology, and Medicine. Science uses DeepSeek-R1-distilled traces from the open-r1/Mixture-of-Thoughts release [17]. Finance uses the reasoning-annotated FinQA/ConvFinQA release [12, 13]. Pathology and Medicine use MedMCQA questions [37] with traces generated by Claude Sonnet 4.6 [3]. Evaluation. In-distribution accuracy (ID) measures how well the new task was learned. Prior capability measures forgetting: the average over held-out general-purpose benchmarks not used for fine-tuning. We evaluate on a broad set of benchmarks before and after training, namely GPQA-Diamond [39], MMLU-Pro [53], GSM8K [14], HumanEval [11], and IFEval [59]. Baselines. We compare against standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and self-distillation fine-tuning [SDFT, 44], an on-policy method designed to reduce forgetting. We report SFT and SDFT alone, and each combined with SST at rank fraction r=0.99 unless specified otherwise (Appendix B.11 sweeps r and finds that stronger protection improves both retention and in-domain accuracy), optionally with basis refresh every 100 (R100) or 10 (R10) steps. We also include a Muon-optimizer variant [5] and the post-hoc KL-repair baseline ReInvoke [44] where relevant. Full training and evaluation details are in Appendix B.14. Refresh mechanism improves the in-domain accuracy matching SFT performance. 3.1 SST learns without forgetting We begin by fine-tuning a thinking model on external chain-of-thought data from a new domain. Unconstrained recipes collapse. Figure 6 shows broad losses in prior capability after both SFT and SDFT across the four domains. For example, as the model learns the new task, it often stops producing compilable code or respecting requested output formats. Post-hoc KL repair recovers only part of this capability and reduces in-domain gains, leaving a learning–retention trade-off rather than improving both simultaneously. SST retains prior capability. Across domains, SST remains close to the base model while matching the in- domain performance of the corresponding unconstrained recipe. The benefit composes with SDFT and with the Muon optimizer, indicating it generalizes across training recipes. Figure 1 summarizes the improved learning–retention trade-off with R10 refresh.
