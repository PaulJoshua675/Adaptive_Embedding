Document parsing and reinforcement post-training. End-to-end parsers such as Donut and Nougat generate structured documents directly from images (Kim et al., 2022; Blecher et al., 2024). Recent systems extend this setting to diverse layouts and capture conditions (Cai et al., 2026; Lu et al., 2026). Reinforcement post-training introduces rewards for transcription, layout, reading order, output validity, and structural fidelity (Wang et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2025; Poznanski et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026a; Hunyuan Vision Team et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026). Table- and element-specific approaches further tailor the prediction unit and reward to local content (Zhang et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026a; Wang et al., 2026c). These systems motivate heterogeneous quality measures, while P2TPO studies how feedback for individual reference elements reaches tokens within a full-page rollout. Appendix A.1 compares reward units and optimization supports. Group-relative optimization and reward normalization. GRPO and RLOO construct response advantages from sampled groups without a learned critic (Shao et al., 2024; Ahmadian et al., 2024). GSPO uses sequence- level importance weighting and clipping, while DAPO and Dr. GRPO revise sampling, clipping, reduction, or normalization (Zheng et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2025a; Liu et al., 2025). GDPO, RDPO, and GD2PO address the normalization and interaction of multiple reward dimensions (Liu et al., 2026b; Bai et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026a). NGRPO combines virtual-maximum calibration with asymmetric clipping to learn from homogeneous failures (Nan et al., 2025). UAP shares ceiling-informed feedback, while unifying missing outcomes and imperfect reward consensus within reference-element comparisons and routing their feedback to generated tokens. Appendix C.1.4 details the evaluated baselines; Appendix A.1 compares reward interfaces. Structured and local credit assignment. Process supervision evaluates reasoning steps (Lightman et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024); GiGPO compares repeated environment states while retaining trajectory feedback (Feng et al., 2025). CSPO extends GRPO with table-wide functional-attribute rewards whose normalized advantages are routed to corresponding tokens alongside global feedback (Yang et al., 2026). P2TPO performs local comparisons for individual reference-block instances in full-page rollouts, preserving quality differences between elements of the same type and supporting omission feedback and intra-block redistribution. Functional-attribute assessment and reference-instance comparison are complementary; Appendix A.2 discusses possible adaptations. 4. Experiments Unless otherwise specified, “seeds” refers to distinct RL training runs rather than repeated evaluations of a fixed checkpoint. The reported means and standard deviations summarize benchmark scores across these training runs. The experiments test whether reference-element feedback improves full-page parsing, whether UAP and SCR contribute to those gains, and whether alignment-informed placement matters beyond nonuniform weighting. Additional settings assess applicability across initializations, model scales, and backbones; parameter sweeps and a 500-update study examine sensitivity to credit parameters and training horizon. 4.1.
