Pairwise Selection aims to counteract this by using only the best point (based on f(x)) of each mirrored pair in the parameter update. 3.4 Orthogonal Sampling Orthogonal sampling extends the idea of mirrored sampling by ensuring that all (randomly) sampled mutation vectors are orthogonal using a Gram-Schmidt procedure (Bj¨orck, 1994). This concept was introduced in (Wang et al., 2014), combining orthogonalization with mirrored sampling and pairwise selection. This extension results in more evenly distributed offspring, providing better local coverage of the search space. This was demonstrated in Wang et al. (2014), where orthogonal sampling improved local convergence and performance on the BBOB benchmark compared with the mirrored sampling method. 3.5 Threshold Convergence Threshold Convergence aims to improve exploration in ES, particularly effective for complex multi- modal optimization problems (Piad-Morffis et al., 2015). The method imposes a minimum distance between parent and offspring solutions to prevent premature convergence. It introduces a threshold T (g) that decays based on the number of generations g and enforces a minimal distance a sampled point xi must have from the population mean m. If ∥xi −m∥< T (g) the sampled xi is ‘pushed’ farther from m, by reflecting it across the threshold radius. 3.6 Boundary Correction If candidate solutions are sampled outside the search domain, they can be transformed back into it by applying a boundary-correction operation. Specifically, if a point x is sampled outside the (box-constrained) search space bounded by [lb, ub], where lb ∈Rd and ub ∈Rddenote the lower and upper bounds of the domain, then an operation can be applied to ‘repair’ the solutions’ violating coordinates. Seven options for performing boundary correction have been implemented in ModCMA, based on the methods described in Caraffini et al. (2019): 1. None: No correction is applied to infeasible coordinates of solutions. This may lead to undesirable behavior if f(x) is undefined outside the bounds. 2. Resample: If a solution xi is out of bounds (oob), resample zi until xi is within the bounds. This may lead to stagnation, particularly when m is close to the boundary. 3. Mirror Correction Strategy: Mirrors all infeasible coordinates of a solution with respect to its closest boundary. For example, if xi,j > ubi,j then xi,j = ubi,j −(xi,j −ubi,j). 4. Uniform Resample: Replaces all infeasible coordinates of a solution with new coordinates sampled uniformly at random within the search space. 5. Complete One-tailed Normal Correction Strategy: All infeasible coordinates are replaced with new coordinates inside the search space according to a rescaled one-sided normal distribution centered on the boundary. The standard deviation of this distribution equals σ(g) 3 where σ(g) is the global step-size. 6. Saturation Correction Strategy: All infeasible coordinates are set to the closest corresponding bound, e.g., when xi,j > ubi,j then xi,j = ubi,j. 7
