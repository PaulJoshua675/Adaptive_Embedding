The full-surface training objective and sampling procedure are given in Sec. 3.5. 3.5 Training and Inference Full-surface training objective. The generation stream X synthesizes material latents over the surface S. Training observations are constructed from ground-truth materials of public 3D assets using visible-surface observation encoding, with complete material latents as targets. With Zgt the normalized ground-truth latents and ϵ ∼N(0, I), Zt = (1 −t)Zgt + [σmin + (1 −σmin)t] ϵ, v∗= (1 −σmin)ϵ −Zgt, (9) where σmin is a small residual noise scale, t = 1 corresponds to pure noise, and t = 0 to material latents with residual noise σminϵ. The loss is LFM = E[MSE(vθ(Zt, t; O, G), v∗)] , (10) averaged over all valid tokens and channels, including observed coordinates. The observation representation remains fixed and is not noised along the flow path; it serves as conditioning input to the generator. The generation stream is supervised against complete-material targets over the entire surface, learning to reconstruct visible appearance from the fixed observations and to complete unobserved regions. Inference. Given dataset-provided or predicted geometry and cameras, we encode the mesh into G and S and the filtered observations into anchors O (Sec. 3.3). Starting from Z1 ∼N(0, I) over S, we integrate the learned velocity field with Euler steps, then de-normalize and decode the final latents with Dmat into full-surface PBR attributes for export. Optional KV caching reuses the observation stream’s per-layer keys and values across sampling steps; Table 6 reports its runtime and memory effects.
