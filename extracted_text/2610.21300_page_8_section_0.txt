For auditors, whether the preliminary assessment behind a recorded judgment can be produced again is a concrete question, to be scaled to risk as Computer Software Assurance recommends [14]. Determinism holds within a hardware class, so records outlive the hardware that produced them only as stored outputs. Technical documentation must be kept for at least 10 years, and 15 for implantable devices [1, 41], longer than a GPU model may be available and far longer than a hosted model ver- sion, which providers retire on months’ notice [42–44]. Container images, model weights and drivers should therefore be archived with the records, and reference outputs re-established on new hardware by listing the changed assessments. This favours models an organ- isation serves itself, which today means open-weight models whose architecture the serving software sup- ports deterministically; batch invariance cost 18% in wall time in our test, and newer engines narrow the gap in some regimes [21, 22]. Regulations and regulators’ guidance do not require byte-level reproducibility, but they repeat- edly ask that a recorded result can be checked, and industry guidance frames validation by risk [45, 46]. Part 11 requires audit trails and reliable, consistent performance of electronic-record systems, applies to records that predicate rules require, here the expert’s recorded judgment, and has been enforced with dis- cretion since 2003 [15, 47]; the EU device regulations require the technical documentation to locate the evi- dence of conformity [1, 41]; the FDA–EMA principles for AI in drug development ask for “detailed, trace- able, and verifiable” documentation [48, Principle 6]; EU GMP Annex 11 requires life-cycle validation [49]; and the EU AI Act requires high-risk systems, from 2028 for devices under notified-body assessment, to log events and to perform “consistently in those respects throughout their lifecycle” [50, 51], which we cite by analogy. Only a consultation draft of EU GMP Annex 22 names identical outputs, limiting its scope to “models with a deterministic output” and excluding LLMs from critical use [52], and its drafting group has since discussed admitting generative mod- els under a risk-based strategy [53, 54]. FDA’s 2019 discussion paper defines a “locked” algorithm as one that gives the same result for the same input [55], and FDA’s and PMDA’s 2026 papers on generative- AI devices list reproducibility across repeated runs among the properties to evaluate [56, 57]. Previous work has made individual stages deter- ministic or measured variation, but not tested a complete assessment pipeline. Batch-invariant and tree-based kernels, verified replay and fixed execution shapes make inference independent of batching [16, 20–23, 58]; ReproRAG finds embeddings and FAISS retrieval reproducible run to run under fixed seeds but displaced by insertions [17], and deterministic-first retrieval, retrieval-aware ingestion and a finance- oriented harness address other stages without extrac- tion or OCR [59–62]. Variation of hosted LLMs at temperature 0 has been measured in research set- tings [19, 63, 64], the same model has been found to differ between routes [36, 40, 65], and providers describe seeds as “best effort” and temperature 0 as not “fully deterministic” [66, 67]. LLMs have been validated for extraction from FDA device documents without repeated runs [4] and proposed for source- linked safety analysis [7]. To our knowledge, no prior work tests byte-level reproducibility of a complete assessment pipeline from text extraction to quoted evidence, relates it to medical-device quality work, or measures, field by field on an assessment record, how commercial deployments vary across repeated requests, routes and days. The study has limitations. It concerns repro- ducibility only; the assessment items are guidance sentences chosen by rule, and public 510(k) docu- ments for two device types stand in for manufactur- ers’ records. The pipeline used one judgment model family on a single GPU, relies on the batch-invariant mode of one inference engine, and its six proper- ties were sufficient under the operations tried, which is an empirical finding, not a proof; no one outside the author’s company has yet reproduced it. The commercial measurements cover two days per deploy- ment, one client region and pay-as-you-go tiers, with eight deployments sampled at provider defaults; the prompts were whole documents, and the 70 prompts come from 49 device–item pairs resampled as inde- pendent units; the positive control, the concurrency test, the between-day count and the two addenda were added during the study (Supplementary Note 2). Usefulness is a property of assessments in the hands of experts, and this study establishes reproducibility, not usefulness: how often the assessments are cor- rect, whether the quoted evidence is what an expert would cite, and whether review helps or biases the expert [9, 68] are questions we cannot answer alone, and we publish this preprint in part to find the reg- ulatory and quality-management experts with whom to answer them. With a deterministic pipeline, what the experts evaluate is fixed, and any later difference can be attributed to a change. Methods Definitions. An assessment item is a requirement to be assessed, defined by the assessment’s administrator, who also defines the label set. A preliminary assessment is, for one item and a set of documents, a label from the label set, a comment giving the reason, and quoted evidence—passages copied verbatim from the docu- ments, each with its cited file; the model’s internal reasoning is not part of it.
