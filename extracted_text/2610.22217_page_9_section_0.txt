(c) Layer-wise attention hit rate on task-relevant visual regions with and without skill conditioning. 5.4 EFFECTIVENESS OF SKILL-AUGMENTED DISTILLATION IN SCOPED In this section, we further investigate how skill augmentation regularizes expert supervision during the distillation process of SCOPED. As illustrated in Figure 3, we examine its effects from three complementary perspectives: the preservation of expert-induced behavior in the unified student, the consistency of expert trajectories across instances within each capability, and the utilization of task-relevant visual evidence. We first characterize functional capability preservation using token-level log-probability shifts rel- ative to the base model on fixed trajectories (Klabunde et al., 2025). For each trajectory, we con- struct a shift vector from these log-probability changes and compare the unified student with the corresponding expert in terms of amplitude retention and expert alignment. Amplitude retention quantifies how much of the expert-induced shift is preserved by the student, while expert alignment measures the cosine similarity between their shift vectors. We evaluate models distilled from differ- ent capability-specific RL experts across visual understanding capabilities. As shown in Figure 3a, SCOPED achieves higher amplitude retention and expert alignment than Mix-RL, demonstrating better preservation of expert-induced functional behavior across capabilities. Notably, SCOPED also outperforms SCOPED w/o Skill Augmentation, indicating that the extracted skills help the student better acquire and reconcile complementary expert capabilities. To further uncover the role of skill augmentation in SCOPED, we evaluate the consistency of expert generation by encoding reasoning trajectories produced by each capability expert with and without its corresponding skill using Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B (Li et al., 2026). Within each capability, we compute pairwise cosine similarities between trajectory embeddings generated from different inputs to obtain the distributions shown in Figure 3b. Higher similarity indicates more consistent expert behavior across instances. With skill conditioning, SCOPED shifts the trajectory similarity distribution toward higher values, indicating that explicit skill guidance regularizes expert genera- tion and promotes more consistent trajectory representations. Finally, we investigate whether this trajectory-level regularization extends to visual evidence utilization. Building on findings that atten- tion patterns in VLMs can effectively capture task-relevant visual grounding (Kang et al., 2025), we compare each capability expert with and without its corresponding skill under identical inputs. At each decoder layer, we identify the most-attended visual token for each attention head and record a hit if it falls within the task-relevant region. We then average the resulting hit rates across attention heads and evaluation samples. As shown in Figure 3c, skill-augmented experts consistently achieve higher hit rates across most of the upper decoder layers, with the largest improvement observed in the final layer. This finding indicates that explicit skills steer capability experts toward more accurate allocation of attention to task-relevant visual evidence. Taken together, these multi-level analyses suggest that skill conditioning promotes more consistent expert trajectories and more effective visual grounding, while enabling the distilled student to better preserve expert-induced functional behavior. 9
