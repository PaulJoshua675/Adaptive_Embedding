3.2 Determining the gradients of the path function To evaluate Equation 3 via a Riemann sum over K steps, we require the following extra gradients at each point α(p) = p/K, p = 1, . . . , K, along the path: ∂γi ∂α (α(p)), ∂γj ∂γi (γ(p) i ), (4) with γ(p) i = bi + α(p)(xi −bi), i.e. a straight-line path as in regular integrated gradients. To compute these, we need an explicit formulation of the path functions γi. To this end, we interpret the model and the feature dependencies as a structural causal model (SCM) (as previously in Janzing et al., 2020, Heskes et al., 2020). If the arrow directions in the chain rule diagram of the left panel of Figure 2 are switched, the graph effectively represents a causal graph, and the path functions can represent the structural mechanism between parent and child nodes of the graph. We will interpret α as residual information, which explains the part of the variation in the children that is not explained by their parents. As is common in causal inference, we assume this residual enters additively. Concretely, for a fully connected DAG with features ordered topologically, we model each feature as: xi = γi(x1, . . . , xi−1, α) = ci(x1, . . . , xi−1) + hi(α), i = 1, . . . , n, (5) where ci is the causal mechanism mapping the parents of xi to its expected value, and hi(α) is an additive residual term. We approximate the causal mechanism ci with a surrogate model si trained to predict xi from its causal parents, so that the residual h(p) i ≈x(p) i −si(x(p) 1 , . . . , x(p) i−1) captures the variation in xi not directly explained by its parents. The required gradients are then: ∂γj ∂γi (γ(p) i ) = ∂sj ∂xi (x(p) i ), ∂γi ∂α (α(p)) ≈h(p) i −h(p−1) i ∆α , (6) where the last one is approximated via a backward finite difference, consistent with related work [Zhuo and Ge, 2024]. The quality of these gradients depends on the choice of surrogate model si, which we discuss in the next section. 3.3 Modeling feature dependencies through a surrogate model Any differentiable surrogate model can be employed to model the feature dependencies under a known causal structure. Here, we introduce a surrogate model that is flexible enough to model complex dependencies while being efficient and scalable to high-dimensional datasets. We propose a sparse masked surrogate s that parameterizes parent-to-child relationships as a sum of edge-wise contributions, operating on a directed graph defined by an adjacency matrix M ∈Rn×n, where Mij = 1 indicates that feature i is a parent of feature j. Each parent feature xi is first transformed using a per-feature basis expansion ϕ(xi) ∈RQ of size Q, to give the surrogate model more flexibility in learning nonlinear effects. The basis consists of three components applied to standardized inputs: 1) polynomial terms, 2) truncated power functions with learnable knots, and 3) Fourier features (see Appendix B for a detailed explanation of the basis expansion). For every active edge (i →j) such that Mij = 1, the prediction for child node j is obtained by summing contributions from all parent nodes: sj = ˆxj = X i:Mij=1 ⟨wij, ϕ(xi)⟩+ µj, (7) where µj and wij ∈RQ are learnable per-node intercept and edge-specific coefficients, respectively. Rather than materializing all possible n2 parent-child interactions, the model stores only active graph edges. This sparse formulation reduces both memory usage and computation, making the model scalable to large sparse graphs. The surrogate is trained to reconstruct the set of predictable child nodes from their parent nodes by minimizing the mean squared reconstruction error, with early stopping on validation loss (full hyperparameters in Tables G.3 and H.1).
