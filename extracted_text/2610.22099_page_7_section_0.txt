Overall, this demonstrates that both methods are effective at many budget levels without the need to re-fit, and show the value of learning a lightweight controller for the decoding trajectory. 4.3 Supplementary Experiments We investigate whether TIPS can transfer beyond its decoder backbone during training, different decoding geometries, and whether it can effectively continue decoding from states generated by another sampler. These experiments use one seed and, whenever applicable, are evaluated with decoder NFE budgets T ∈{16, 32, 64}. Unless explicitly mentioned, the mathematical reasoning results are micro-averaged over the test set of GSM8K and MATH-500 as before. Additional experiments and results appear in appendix B. Zero-shot transfer to Dream-7B. We apply TIPSfull policy network to Dream-7B-Instruct without retraining and compare the performance to Fast-dLLM on a single 256-token canvas (Ye et al., 2025). As seen in figure 2 and table 2, these gains show that non-trivial visibility decisions can transfer to a different decoder backbone without the need for re-training. Zero-shot transfer across block sizes. We reuse TIPSfull for 2 × 128, 4 × 64, and 8 × 32 decoding, keeping the total response length and decoder budget fixed, in order to examine TIPS’ ability to generalize to different block sizes which, in turn, also shed light on its performance on different response lengths. Also seen in figure 2 and table 2, TIPSfull can preserve accuracy at slightly smaller block sizes before eventual degradation at 8 × 32. Handoff from a heuristic sampler. We evaluate whether TIPS can effectively continue decoding from intermediate states generated by another sampler. To do so, we run Fast-dLLM’s confidence-thresholding sampler with default threshold of 0.9 for a fraction q ∈[0, 1] of the total forward calls then switch to TIPS.
