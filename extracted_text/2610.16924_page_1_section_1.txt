Although spiking neural networks (SNNs) show promise in energy-efficient computing through event-driven computation and stateful temporal processing, their recurrent membrane dynamics render their training inherently sequential and in- efficient for long sequences. Existing methods achieve full- sequence parallelism in SNNs through two main strategies: redesigning or approximating spiking dynamics to avoid reset- coupled temporal dependencies, or relying on iterative re- laxed fixed-point solvers that suffer from excessive memory costs and iteration-dependent backward overhead. To address these limitations, we present FidLIF, a novel framework that reconciles the incremental update and hard-reset behavior of standard spiking LIF neurons with parallel forward propa- gation and BPTT-equivalent backward propagation. FidLIF iteratively recovers the full-sequence membrane-potential tra- jectory in parallel. By exploitingthe lowerbidiagonal structure induced by the local recurrence of LIF neurons, FidLIF formu- lates each forward trajectory correction and backward adjoint propagation as associative scans with an O(log T) temporal depth. This formulation also supports input-dependent neu- ronal parameters and allows the same neuron to perform full- sequence parallel training and incremental step-by-step infer- ence at deployment. In our numerical convergence evaluation, FidLIF achieves a mean absolute error below 10−7 relative to the matched serial relaxed LIF dynamics, while yielding gradients numerically equivalent to exact BPTT. Our Tri- ton implementation further delivers favorable long-sequence latency and memory scaling, while achieving competitive performance on four sequential classification benchmarks. By reconciling parallel training with step-by-step execution, FidLIF enables high-fidelity spiking dynamics to benefit from modern parallel hardware without compromising incremental inference at deployment.
