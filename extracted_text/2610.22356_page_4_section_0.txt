DK-VLM: Geometry as Position Geometry-Augmented Vision-Language Models. One line of work introduces 3D informa- tion by encoding coordinates into visual tokens, including Video-3D LLM, LLaVA-3D, and HiSpatial (Zheng et al., 2024, Zhu et al., 2024, Liang et al., 2026). Video-3D LLM adds coordinate embeddings to visual features. Another line introduces features from 3D reconstruction mod- els: VG-LLM adds projected VGGT features to visual tokens, VLM-3R queries reconstruction and camera tokens with cross-attention, and GeoThinker retrieves geometric features according to the current semantic state (Zheng et al., 2025, Fan et al., 2026, Li et al., 2026b). These fusion mechanisms let a VLM use geometric representations from reconstruction models, but require aligning features learned for reconstruction with pretrained semantic representations. In contrast, DK-VLM uses estimated 3D points and camera poses to directly modulate attention among visual tokens, rather than incorporating coordinate embeddings or reconstruction features into their content. Geometric Attention and Positional Encoding. Positional mechanisms provide another interface for introducing 3D information into Transformers. Geometric Transform Attention (GTA) incorporates group actions induced by camera transformations into attention, while PRoPE, RayRoPE, and ViewRoPE use 3D points, rays, or viewpoints to parameterize positional relations between tokens (Miyato et al., 2024, Li et al., 2025c, Wu et al., 2026, Xiang et al., 2026). These studies show that geometry can directly shape relation computation. How to introduce these mechanisms into a pretrained VLM while retaining its native positional encoding remains less explored. 3 Method Given a sequence of RGB images and a spatial query, DK-VLM uses a geometry frontend to estimate aligned patch-level 3D points and frame-level camera poses from the images (Wang et al., 2025a, 2026a,b, Lin et al., 2025, Murai et al., 2025). DK-VLM uses these geometric states rather than latent features specific to a frontend, allowing compatible geometry frontends to be replaced. It models input position and scene geometry through parallel relation kernels. The semantic kernel retains the backbone’s native positional computation, while the geometric kernel is parameterized by the estimated states. Dual-Kernel Attention computes and fuses the outputs of the two kernels (Section 3.1). To supervise the composition of geometric relations across observations, Reference-Frame Composition links verified single-step annotations into training chains with stepwise targets (Section 3.2). Training jointly optimizes the instruction and pairwise geometric objectives in a single stage (Section 3.3).
