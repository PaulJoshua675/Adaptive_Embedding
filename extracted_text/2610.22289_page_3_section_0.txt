To investigate the remaining specialization gap, we use the textual agent from our earlier ARC-AGI-3 study as a reference, updated to twma_v1.61. Its level-and-attempt organization, reset management, and textual-model instructions prescribe ways of using information already available to the general agent, rather than supplying additional game rules or solutions. We choose it instead of the verification variant to avoid the additional predefined interfaces and programs supplied by that variant. Because twma_v1.61 still differs in several respects, we also perform a targeted comparison: adding scripted RESET management to Stop-and-report recovers approximately 34–60% of the observed gap. Codex is instructed not to call RESET, and the controller issues it at GAME_OVER. This tests whether a convention already available in the environment description becomes more useful when implemented explicitly in the controller. Section 6 describes the two ARC-AGI-3-specific agents. The AGI Maze tutorial experiment provides complementary evidence about the value of prior experience. Tutorials reduce RHAE70 for both GPT-5.5-based variants at both effort settings. With GPT-5.6-sol they help both variants at xhigh, while their effects are smaller and mixed at max. Together, the prompt comparison, ARC-AGI-3 diagnostics, and tutorial experiment highlight the distinction between having useful information and tools available and using them effectively. The performance gains may reflect more effective information use, more reliable control, or additional or differently allocated inference. These effects may act together; the experiments do not separate them. We introduce EnvTraverse, an experimental framework for running and evaluating agents across interactive environments, together with the Baseline agent evaluated in this study. These are intended to support further research on improving environment-agnostic agents through better procedures or additional general capabilities. New agent variants can be compared with Baseline across environments to assess performance gains, possible regressions, and inference cost. Code and data availability.
