Latent recursion. Latent recursion applies the same network repeatedly to an internal state. The Tiny Recursive Model (TRM) keeps two states: a solution state y and a latent reasoning state z. One cycle refines z over repeated passes conditioned on the input and on y, then updates y from the refined z; a supervision step chains several such cycles, all but the last without gradients. Deep supervision gives every step its own loss, and the states are detached between steps, so the model learns successive corrections without backpropagating across them. TRM achieves strong results on Sudoku, maze solving, and ARC-AGI benchmarks [5]. Edge heatmaps. An edge heatmap records the model’s confidence that each transition belongs to a high-quality solution. DIFUSCO produces such heatmaps for the travelling salesman problem by iteratively denoising edge variables [6]. However, DIFUSCO’s learned edge representation is separate from the procedure that converts it into a discrete solution. Feasible decoding. A heatmap is not itself a route. For CVRP, each decision must account for the visited customers and remaining vehicle capacity. The Attention Model, a neural routing model, constructs a route autoregressively, selecting one node at a time while masking infeasible actions [2]. DeepACO instead learns a heuristic measure for an ant colony optimization system: a population of simulated ants builds routes in parallel, each choosing only feasible transitions [7]. Learning from sampled routes. REINFORCE allows a routing policy to learn without optimal labels: complete routes are sampled, scored by their cost, and used to improve the policy [2, 8]. The signal is sparse, however, because one route-level reward must guide every edge decision made during construction. Self-imitation adds denser feedback: the lowest-cost sampled route serves as a temporary teacher, and a cross-entropy loss trains the heatmap to favour its transitions [9]. 3 RELAGRAM RELAGRAM brings latent recursion to routing.
