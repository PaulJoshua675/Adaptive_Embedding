2.1 LLM SEARCH AND OUTCOME-BASED POST-TRAINING LLMs increasingly learn retrieval actions from downstream outcomes rather than surface similar- ity. Search-R1 (Jin et al., 2025) trains multi-turn search with reinforcement learning, while RAG- R1 (Tan et al., 2026) and parallel-search methods (Zhao et al., 2025) extend this paradigm to multiple queries or parallel decomposition. Outcome-based post-training typically assigns supervision at the response or trajectory level: GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) derives a scalar group-relative advantage for each sampled response, whereas process supervision provides finer feedback along a reason- ing trajectory (Lightman et al., 2024). These methods refine where supervision is assigned across responses or trajectories. Our setting concerns a different axis: several retrieval actions are gener- ated jointly within one response and share one downstream outcome, raising the question of what counterfactual information is needed for context-dependent attribution within the realized action set. 2.2 CREDIT ASSIGNMENT UNDER AGGREGATE FEEDBACK Shared-reward credit assignment is well studied in cooperative reinforcement learning. COMA constructs an agent-specific counterfactual baseline by marginalizing one agent’s action while hold- ing others fixed (Foerster et al., 2018). Standard difference rewards instead remove or replace one agent (Wolpert & Tumer, 2001). Value-decomposition methods impose structure on joint value, ad- ditively in VDN (Sunehag et al., 2018) and monotonically in QMIX (Rashid et al., 2018). Shapley- based methods aggregate marginal contributions across coalitions and have also been used for policy learning (Li et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2020).
