We use padv = 0.5 on Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct and padv = 0.25 on Qwen3.6-27B, where in this latter case an epoch is one pass over Dadv accompanied by three times as many utility data. Loss weights are λt = λa = 0.5 and λu = 1.0, with cut-offs tcut = 0.5 and acut = −5. The inner attack uses K signed-gradient steps (K = 10 on Qwen3.6-27B, 5 on Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) with step size α = 10−4 and radius ϵ = 0.05, initialized at the instruction embeddings. We train at a learning rate of 5 × 10−5 for up to 30 epochs, with early stopping on the held-out training loss and a guard against utility-loss degradation. CAPO runs use no utility data, per Eq. 4, with β between 0.5 and 1.1 depending on the model and the loss. Appendix A.3 gives the full configurations. Baselines We compare CATCH with (1) the base model, evaluated with no honesty interven- tion; (2) prompting methods, which include an honest system prompt, an honest-persona system prompt, and an honest-persona system prompt with prefilling (Wang et al., 2025) (Appendix A.2); (3) activation steering, specifically, Steer-to-Target-Projection (StTP) (Herbster et al., 2026), which uses contrastive steering directions fit by logistic regression on honest/dishonest hidden states with selective intervention on misaligned tokens, and is the state-of-the-art activation steering method optimized for honesty; (4) SFT on the toward targets alone (SFT-t), SFT on the toward targets with utility data (SFT-tu); and SFT on the toward and away targets and utility data (SFT-tau), which removes the adversarial perturbation in CATCH, isolating the contribution of the attack in Eq. 3.
