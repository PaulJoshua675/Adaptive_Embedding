Recent work develops MLE capabilities by training models on interaction experience. ML-Agent [6] combines multi-turn demonstrations with single-step RL, while RL for MLE Agents [32], MLE-RL [33], and AceGRPO [34] study online MLE training with execution feedback, asynchronous sampling, or intermediate trajectory states. Learning to Ideate [35] trains models to generate algorithm improvement suggestions, whereas Frontis-MA1 [7] applies SFT and RL to program evolution operators and combines them through an external evolutionary framework. These approaches primarily train individual interaction steps or local components of externally organized optimization workflows. SandMLE [36] applies trajectory-wise on-policy RL in micro-scale synthetic MLE environments. We train model-directed long-horizon interaction policies on real-world MLE tasks, with WIRL using execution windows to learn multi-turn decisions from delayed execution feedback under asynchronous updates. 6.2. Post-Training Long-Horizon Agentic Models Recent research has increasingly focused on post-training agentic models for sustained interaction, complex tool use, and long-horizon decision making. SWE-Gym [37] trains coding agents using trajectories collected from executable environments, while Search-R1 [38] and WebRL [39] learn multi-turn tool use from environment feedback and outcome rewards. AgentFlow [40] directly optimizes planning policies in general multi-turn agentic interaction and uses trajectory-level outcomes to train intermediate decisions. Recent work has also explored generalist agentic models for long- horizon interaction. Agents-A1 [41] uses long-horizon knowledge-action trajectories and multi-stage training, together with multi-teacher on-policy distillation (OPD), to combine capabilities from multiple domain teachers into a unified model. Ornith-1.5 [14] extends self-scaffolding by jointly optimizing task generation, scaffold construction, and solution rollouts through RL.
