Existing approaches often fail to account for scene-specific variance: (a) semantic misalign- ment in simple cases, (b) loss of fine-grained prompt details in complex scenarios, and (c) inability to adapt to diverse viewpoints and scales. These issues often lead to repetitive subject structures and weakened alignment with scene-specific prompts. In contrast, our method achieves better scene-level fidelity by adaptively harmonizing identity consistency with the unique context of each scene. underrepresent scene-specific variance in both text and vision spaces—the di- verse semantic and visual elements that vary across narrative contexts (Fig. 1). First, at the semantic level, current methods [17, 30, 40] often treat narrative prompts as monolithic inputs, where various semantics (e.g., appearance and scene descriptions) are encoded into a unified embedding. This can lead to in- formation dilution, making it difficult for the model to uniformly attend to all details [14]. As a result, even when a prompt contains rich details (e.g., specific actions or environmental changes), the model often fails to selectively extract scene-specific cues from the prompt. This directly undermines narrative fidelity, producing scenes that ignore their intended semantics (Fig. 1a,b). Second, at the visual level, existing approaches typically rely on spatially blind feature injec- tion, where identity features are transferred without accounting for the spatial constraints or framing of each scene. This oversight of structural scene vari- ance (e.g., camera angles or subject scales) can lead to subject inflexibility, such as contextually mismatched poses or repetitive structural patterns across scenes (Fig. 1b,c). Consequently, subjects are rendered with rigid structural pat- terns that ignore each scene’s intended layout, further weakening alignment with scene-specific prompts. Therefore, achieving coherent storytelling requires scene- specific adaptation, where identity consistency is harmonized with the semantic and structural variance of each scene.
