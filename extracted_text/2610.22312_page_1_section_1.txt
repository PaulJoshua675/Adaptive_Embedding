Spatial reasoning supports understanding scene descriptions and interpreting instructions about objects and their surroundings (Shi et al., 2022; Long et al., 2016). Such instructions appear in tasks ranging from object manipulation to geometric design, where language specifies actions or construction operations that change a spatial configuration (Shridhar et al., 2021; Khan et al., 2024). When instructions describe a sequence of operations, earlier changes can alter the spatial conditions on which later operations depend. Moving an object, for example, can free one position while blocking another, changing the outcome of a subsequent move. Predicting the resulting configuration from text therefore involves relating the described operations to the spatial states they successively produce. We investigate how accurately large language models predict these outcomes across varied operations, event histories, and spatial structures. Existing benchmarks evaluate selected spatial relations and entity states (Mirzaee et al., 2021; Shi et al., 2022; Kim and Schuster, 2023), while recent studies also examine complete-grid and chess-state prediction from event sequences (Chapra et al., 2026; Walker and Lyons, 2026). We introduce Grid-Bench to study complete- state prediction in discrete spatial worlds, across operations that draw on different spatial information. A reflection rearranges values within a specified geometric region; region growth acts on a connected component whose boundary changes with the state.
