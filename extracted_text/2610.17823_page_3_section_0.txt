An important property of softmax is its full support: every item receives strictly positive probability regardless of its relevance to the current context. This induces dense competition over the entire item catalog. Every negative item participates in the loss computation and receives a non-zero gradient update on every training example, so gradient mass is spread across a very large number of irrelevant negatives. This observation motivates sparse probability mappings, which replace dense global competition with context-dependent sparse competition among a subset of candidate items. 3.2 Sparse Probability Mapping Recently, entmax was introduced as a sparse generalization of soft- max [29]. Since entmax as- signs zero probability to some items, the logarithm in the CE ob- jective becomes undefined. Moreover, naive substitution does not preserve the desirable gradient structure. The main difference lies in sparsity. Only negative items with non-zero probability receive non-zero gradients. Thus, entmax re- stricts competition to a context-dependent subset of items. Items with sufficiently high logits remain inside the active support and compete with the target item, while low-scoring irrelevant items are excluded from optimization. In this sense, entmax can be interpreted as a differentiable adaptive hard negative selection mechanism: it focuses learning on difficult negatives determined by the current context rather than uniformly competing against the entire catalog. The sparsity property of entmax improves training efficiency and ranking quality by restricting competition to contextually rel- evant alternatives, but does not directly address popularity bias. The active support is determined entirely by current logits, with no mechanism to account for global item popularity. Consequently, entmax can be expected to improve ranking accuracy on its own, while its effect on popularity-related metrics remains limited and dataset-dependent. We confirm this empirically in Section 5.3. Ad- dressing popularity bias requires augmenting sparse mapping with explicit prior-awareness, which we develop next. 4 Sparse Prior-Regularized Objective Our goal is to make sparse competition explicitly popularity-aware: popular items should be more likely to compete with the target when they are contextually plausible, while irrelevant items should still be filtered out by the sparse mapping. To achieve this, we incor- porate prior information directly into the variational principle that defines the probability mapping.
