Unfragmenting Nawatl NMT: Single-Variant Specialization for Masewalkopa LoRA Adaptation, Morphosyntactic Conditioning, and Decolonial Evaluation Luis Armando Mercado-Campos⋆and Alexis Palmer Department of Linguistics, University of Colorado Boulder Luis.MercadoCampos@colorado.edu, alexis.palmer@colorado.edu Abstract. In minoritized (low-resource) language technologies, a com- mon response to data sparsity is to aggregate text across multiple varieties and treat the combined data as a single corpus. However, mixed corpora of closely related indigenous variants can produce an artificial register that is accountable to no single speaker community, a structural failure we call variant fragmentation. This issue is acute for minoritized languages native to Mexico, such as Nawatl. Building on a 70,000-pair multi-variant foun- dation [18], we specialize an mT5-large checkpoint to Masewalkopa (nhn), the Nawatl variety spoken in Santa Ana Tlacotenco. We curate 2,665 par- allel pairs, reserve all sentence-level data for evaluation, and incorporate 192 elder-transcribed target sentences via asymmetric backtranslation. A custom finite-state analyzer reduces unanalyzed surface forms from 82.0% to 19.9% and supplies functional syntax tags as plain-text prefixes, allowing the model to reuse existing subword embeddings rather than relying on frozen special tokens. The resulting lora_v9c_b model achieves 58.46 dev chrF++ and 58.20/57.90 directional test chrF++. Finally, CTranslate2 int8 quantization reduces CPU latency from 137.5 to 1.8–2.6 seconds per request, enabling cost-bounded Cloud Run deployment.
