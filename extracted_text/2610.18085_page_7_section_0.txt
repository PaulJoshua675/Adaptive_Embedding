The experiments below measure how much an imbalanced position de- grades language-model training and how much of this degradation rebalancing recovers. We create such a position with an orbit edit, a deliberate rescaling of exact pairs, at initialization (Sec. 5.2) and in a check- point before training resumes (Sec. 5.3), and we also train without an edit to check whether rebalancing costs anything when no imbalance is imposed. The primary 101M model uses ReLU. Hard SAGGS rebalances all exact pairs every 100 steps. At λ = 0.2, SAGGS reduces PPL by 0.374 ± 0.026 across seven paired seeds, with p+ sf = 0.0078 (Table 2). Without an edit, Vanilla ends with a Q–K log-norm ratio of only about 0.15, compared with about 3 after the edit, and removing this small imbalance barely changes PPL. 5.2.2 Architecture and Tokenizer Replication To test whether these results depend on the GPT-2 architecture or the GPT-2 tokenizer, we repeat the comparison on a 31M model with the modern architec- ture and T5 SentencePiece tokenization. Over seven paired seeds, Soft SAGGS reduces PPL by 0.780±0.040 at λ = 0.2, with p+ sf = 0.0078, while the difference with- out an edit is +0.019 ± 0.024 PPL (Table 7).
