Proposition 3.2 suggests one reason. For Fisher-type estimates the sampling noise is multiplicative, so the heteroscedastic rule assigns its largest weights to the largest estimates. It pulls the most important parameters toward the mean and leaves the rest almost untouched, which removes protection from the parameters with the largest estimated importance. A log-scale rule would shrink every estimate by the same factor in log space, a power transform of the importance whose exponent approaches one as 𝑛grows. We expect such a rule to keep the adaptivity of EB to 𝑛without concentrating shrinkage on the largest estimates, and testing it is the obvious next experiment. For MAS, where much of the benefit at large 𝑛does not come from noise, a variance-driven rule cannot recover it, and the EB weights of Study 4 were indeed near zero for 𝑛≥50. At 𝑛= 10, where EB shrank as much on average as the fixed weight and still lost accuracy, the allocation again appears to be the problem. 6.3 Shrinkage trades plasticity for stability Because every task has a fixed protection budget, shrinkage moves protection from high-importance to low-importance parameters, and the latter are the ones new tasks would otherwise use freely. Shrinkage lowered learning accuracy in most cells of Studies 3 and 4, a form of the intransigence that consolidation penalties are known to cause [8]. It therefore pays when forgetting is the bottleneck, as on P-MNIST with a shared head, where BWT without shrinkage ranged from −8 to −13 points in Study 3, and it costs accuracy when forgetting is not, as on the multi-head Split CIFAR-100, where BWT ranged from −0.9 to −1.6 points. The CIFAR-100 superclass benchmark has plenty of forgetting but showed no detectable effect for MAS, possibly because the selected penalty was weak there. 6.4 What remains of the nanotube idea Our results give no support to the hypothesis that activity-dependent exchange of plasticity signals improves consolidation. The biologically motivated rules never beat random links, and random links appear to help because, in expectation, they implement a statistical correction that needs no graph at all. The results do establish a weaker fact that may still be useful. Any mass-conserving exchange of plasticity signals between randomly paired units acts as shrinkage of noisy importance, with a strength set by how many links are alive and how long they last. Whether biological nanotubes form at random with respect to plasticity signals is an empirical question that these experiments cannot answer. The same logic matters for applied continual learning with noisy, subject-specific data. In EEG- based emotion recognition, importance heuristics degrade under noise and covariate shift [33], and fixed or log-scale shrinkage of importance is a cheap intervention to test in that setting. 6.5 Limitations • The networks and benchmarks are small, a two-layer MLP and a four-layer CNN on MNIST and CIFAR-100. We have not tested larger models, longer task sequences, or other domains.
