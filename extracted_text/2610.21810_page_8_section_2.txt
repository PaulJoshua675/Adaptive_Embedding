CP compresses lookup tables with row codes, lo- cal scales and a shared decoder while preserving independent access to requested rows. Across three hashed-memory models, two-bit codes reduce ta- ble storage by roughly four- to eightfold, with most reported task scores close to their original values despite some task- and model-dependent losses. Under GPU memory pressure, smaller ta- bles allow larger workloads and reduce prefill time. When memory is sufficient, complete inference steps show little speed improvement in our imple- mentation. Limitations Coverage. Our conclusions concern post- training compression of three models with hashed lookup tables; they need not extend to other mem- ory architectures, including token-indexed tables (Appendix H). Most downstream comparisons use the two primary code rates, with less task coverage at higher rates than in the KL sweep. Small task-score differences do not establish equivalence. Analysis. The fitted-codebook and structured- residual comparisons evaluate output KL only on Qwen, limiting their generality across models. Some equal-budget comparisons use interpolated trellis values without uncertainty estimates. The Gaussian source approximation does not establish how training produces the observed row statistics. Deployment. Serving measurements use a single node and eager inference; optimized engines may spend a different fraction of time on lookup. Some configurations have only one measurement run, and tested workload limits are not exact capacity thresh- olds. Access counts come from English web text, so frequency-based allocation and caching results may change with the deployment distribution. 8
