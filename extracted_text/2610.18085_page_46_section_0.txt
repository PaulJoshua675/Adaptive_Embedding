We next test the same paired design at 296.7M parameters, increasing both the width and depth of the RMSNorm, RoPE, SwiGLU, and GQA architecture. Design. The model uses the same T5 SentencePiece tokenization and causal RMSNorm, RoPE, SwiGLU, and GQA block, with d = 896, 14 query heads, two KV heads, SwiGLU hidden width 2432, 32 layers, tied embeddings, sequence length 512, and 296.7M trainable parameters. We train the paired grid of Vanilla and Soft SAGGS with α = 0.5, crossed with λ = 0 and λ = 0.2, over three paired seeds for 12K AdamW steps on WikiText-103 with learning rate 2.0 × 10−4, weight decay 0.1, 10% warmup, cosine decay, gradient clipping at 1.0, BF16 XPU autocast, batch size 8, and rebalancing every 100 steps. As in the 123.8M scale replication and checkpoint-surgery resume, the intervention acts on Q–K, V–O, and SwiGLU value–output pairs. The run uses all 12 Intel Data Center GPU Max 1550 XPU tiles. Table 26 reports the three-seed summaries. At λ = 0, Soft SAGGS achieves slightly lower PPL in all three paired seeds, with ¯∆Vanilla−Soft = +0.088 ± 0.052. Under the same exact λ = 0.2 all-pairs reparameterization, Vanilla ends at 111.45 ± 4.83 PPL, while Soft SAGGS ends at 25.64 ± 0.10 PPL; the paired protection is +85.813 ± 4.730 PPL with all three seeds favoring Soft SAGGS. The final pair-norm statistics show balance control: Vanilla runs at λ = 0.2 retain large maximum absolute log-ratios, Q–K about 7.5, V–O about 5.4, and MLP about 6.4, whereas Soft SAGGS keeps all three below 2.5 × 10−4. Absolute PPL is tokenizer- and budget-dependent; the contrast is the within-tokenizer, seed-paired PPL recovery under deliberate rescaling at a 300M-class modern-architecture scale. H FURTHER DISCUSSION The results distinguish invariance of the current function from equivariance of an optimizer trajectory. Standard adaptive and sign-based rules can cancel gradient scales while retaining a dependence on parameter scales. Transforming moments alone does not correct the update-scaling mismatch under shared hyperparameters. Full-state canonicalization addresses this dependence at restart by bringing orbit-related checkpoints to the same numerical state. Periodic rebalancing serves a different practical role: controlling pair-scale imbalance as training proceeds. Its strongest measured benefits occur after deliberately imposed rescaling, including the checkpoint-resume workflows. Imbalance also arises naturally, in both trained-from-scratch and public models, and rebalancing can be kept on throughout training to control this imbalance: A rebalance rescales each pair only as much as the pair is imbalanced, correcting a large imbalance and leaving pairs that are already close to balance almost untouched. Soft SAGGS brings the same control to Lion, whose update depends on a momentum buffer of past gradients: Soft SAGGS leaves the optimizer state unchanged and applies the correction in several smaller steps, so the update changes gradually, and training recovers from the degradation caused by an edit. The normalization layers of a Transformer, either layer normalization or RMSNorm, do not remove the pair symmetries, because these layers act on the residual stream rather than on the paired matrices.
