A Extended Related Work Classical continual-learning approaches often restrict the update direction: regularization penalizes move- ment along Fisher-important directions [23], while projected-gradient methods enforce constraints by re- placing gradient g with a projection Π(g) built from stored data, past-task gradients [9, 18, 27], activation subspaces [40], the feature-covariance null space [51], or earlier tasks’ adapters [52]. Continual learning for LLMs differs from these settings in several ways. These methods estimate subspaces from data, but reconstructing a distribution representative of an LLM’s pretraining corpus is infeasible at scale. Truly continual deployment may also lack explicit task boundaries. SST instead preserves a subspace derived from the weight spectrum (Eq. 3), requiring neither stored examples nor task boundaries. Several parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods also construct adapters from singular directions. PiSSA [31] initializes and trains LoRA factors from the principal singular components, prioritizing high-energy directions rather than protecting them. MiLoRA [50] is closer in motivation: it retains the principal components and adapts minor singular components, but represents the update through a fixed low-rank factorization. CorDA [57] first forms a context-oriented decomposition using input-activation covariance and initializes low-rank adapters from task- or knowledge-oriented components. In all three methods, the spectral choice is encoded in a parameter-efficient factorization derived at initialization. SST instead projects the dense update actually proposed by the optimizer into the two-sided non-principal complement. It therefore constrains the realized weight change after stateful optimizer preconditioning, does not rely on a trainable adapter factorization or task-specific covariance estimate, and can refresh its protected basis during training. A complementary line leaves the optimizer alone and reduces the distribution shift driving forgetting: self- distillation rewrites the supervision into the model’s own output distribution [44], and online RL forgets less because its updates stay KL-close to the base policy [45, 48], both consistent with forgetting tracking distributional movement [24, 29]. B.1 Model Merging If a constrained update stays in the low-energy complement of the weight spectrum, then two updates trained independently should interfere less when added back together. We test this with model merging experiments. In particular, we fine-tune a separate single-domain expert for Science, Medicine, and FinQA, then form Wmerged = Wbase + P d(Wd −Wbase) over all the 252 weight matrices. We copy every other parameter from the base model. No merge coefficients are tuned and no data is used to fit the combination. Each merged model is then scored in-domain against its own single-domain expert under identical decode settings. We report the interference caused by merging rather than the raw accuracy gap, which already favors SST. SST experts compose, SFT experts collide. Figure 11 shows that independently trained SFT experts interfere strongly when their task vectors are combined, and the interference compounds as more experts are merged. SST experts compose much more cleanly: pairwise merges remain close to their corresponding single-domain experts, and the three-expert merge degrades substantially more slowly. Each SST expert modifies only the low-energy subspace, so adding their updates leaves the protected principal directions unchanged.
