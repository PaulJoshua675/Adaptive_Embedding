We study context-dependent within-set credit for jointly generated retrieval actions under set-level feedback. CASI constructs comparable coalition evaluations through snapshot-grounded set inter- vention, aggregates contextual marginals with Shapley weighting, and incorporates their relative credit through bounded zero-sum advantage shaping. We show that endpoint observations do not in general determine the Shapley allocation for m ≥4, while additional coalition evaluations are redundant under additive set values. In a five-action sponsored-search setting, CASI achieves the largest median cosine similarity to the replay-defined Shapley reference under the deployed training evaluator, improves held-out auction-replay set value across independent training runs, and yields an observed 3.51% advertiser-value lift over production control in a randomized A/B test.
