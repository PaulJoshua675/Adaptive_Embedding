With a tolerance, Proposition 2 binds only softly: LaSER-LR reaches order 5 on a target of order 8 and still attains R2 ≥0.99 in all thirty runs, because a smooth target can be approximated below its exact order. Every condition succeeds here and the effect survives only in the magnitude of the error. At ε = 0 on the cube the constraint binds exactly, which is why the separation in Section 4.2 is visible at all. 4.4 Innate and learned behaviour across generations The learner is refitted every generation and never inherited, so this is lifetime learning, and the innate behaviour recorded at every generation lets us ask whether evolution comes to do the job alone. Two hypotheses are separable from the trajectories: under assimilation the innate behaviour rises and the gap to the learned behaviour closes; under what we will call evolving learnability the learned behaviour rises while the innate behaviour stays flat or falls, so the gap widens. Across the sixty-three problem and learner combinations of both domains, assimilation occurs in four, three of which are canonical GP, for which success and innateness are by definition the same event. In forty-four the gap widens instead. On PAR4 under a random forest the innate accuracy of the best individual falls from 0.713 at the first generation to 0.394 at the last while its learned accuracy rises from 0.713 to 1.000: evolution moves away from representations that predict the target on their own, because the learner has made that property unnecessary.
