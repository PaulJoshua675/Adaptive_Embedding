We presented XViewRank, a lightweight conditional fusion method for LLM- augmented sparse retrieval. The method preserves normalized BM25 scores from the original, hypothesis, and step-back views, and uses result-list overlap to condition how those scores contribute to document ranking. Under matched inputs, XViewRank improves nDCG@10 and MAP numerically over fixed fusion on both TREC DL test years without jointly encoding query and passage text. The full-split statistical evidence is limited to DL19 MAP, and the smaller DL20 gains remain inconclusive. Our analyses further indicate that overlap is most useful as context when the views retrieve heterogeneous evidence, not as direct relevance evidence or as a reason to force query diversity. These results show that multi-view query expansion depends not only on generating useful views, but also on preserving and conditionally combining their retrieval evidence. A Reproducibility Details A.1 Query-view generation prompts For every topic, the original query is used unchanged as the original view. The fol- lowing instructions are supplied to GPT-4o mini, with the original query placed in the user message. Exactly one output is generated for each additional view and then cached. Hypothesis view. Write a concise passage that could answer the question and use terms likely to appear in relevant passages. Step-back view. Rewrite the question into a higher-level retrieval query focusing on the under- lying concept, mechanism, or broader information need. Generation uses a maximum of 500 output tokens, temperature 0.6, and top- p 0.9. Cached outputs are shared by XViewRank and all matched-view baselines so that fusion methods are compared on identical query text.
