A Appendix A.1 Extended Related Works As large language models are increasingly deployed, a broad literature has emerged around adaptive inference to balance response quality and computational cost. This design space generally splits into two paradigms: prompt-based routing, which selects a model before any text is generated [8, 35], and sequential cascades, which evaluate a cheaper model’s output before deciding whether to escalate [5, 19, 51]. While recent works such as [7] develop a unified formulation bridging both approaches, our work is specifically grounded in the cascade paradigm. Motivated by the growing need to deploy LLMs locally on resource-constrained edge devices [28, 39, 50], we study a two-model cascade with a significant capability gap. In this setting, a weak local model first generates candidate responses, and the system subsequently decides whether to invoke a more capable, expensive strong model based entirely on the realized generation history. To contextualize our contributions, the remainder of this section categorizes prior work along three critical axes: when the routing decision occurs, the required test-time inference compute, and the reliance on ground-truth supervision. Prompt-Based Routing and Learning to Defer. Prompt-based routers learn which model should answer an input without first observing its generated response. Building on classical literature regarding optimal deferral rules [19, 33], methods like Hybrid LLM [8] and Route LLM [35] allocate queries between models using learned predictions of quality. Other approaches focus on selecting a suitable expert from a larger pool of LLMs by predicting model performance through meta-models [2], distilling reward assessments to identify expertise [29], using input-dependent routing [44], estimating translation improvements using prompt representations [31], or decomposing input uncertainty using labeled calibration data to avoid routing inherently ambiguous queries [37]. These methods fundamentally differ from our approach in two ways: they make routing decisions before observing the generated weak response, and they heavily rely on ground-truth correctness or reward signals for training. In contrast, our cascade evaluates the realized weak response before routing, strictly in an unannotated two-model setting. Supervised Confidence and Deferral (N = 1).
