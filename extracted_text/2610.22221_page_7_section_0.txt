We therefore treat the temporal contribution as modest, while noting that it costs no acoustic supervision whatsoever. 6.3 Distillation Results Distillation raises vision-only Q prediction to 0.440, from 0.298 with the same four-frame input and no teacher. This is marginally above the 0.430 obtainable from perfect material-category knowledge and still far below the audio teacher. Because the pooled measure is dominated in practice by category information, exceeding that reference by 0.01, which lies inside the three-seed range, is not conclusive (Figure 2), so we turn to a measure under which category identity earns nothing. 6.4 Leave-One-Material-Out, with the Head Held Out LOMO is the discriminating test, because correlations are computed within a held-out material: a model that outputs a category’s median Q predicts a constant there and is scored zero, since Spearman ρ is undefined for constant predictions. Two conditions must both hold for the test to mean anything. The material must be excluded from the probe, and it must also be excluded from the distillation head—otherwise the head has already learned that material’s appearance-to-audio mapping and the probe merely reads it back. We refer to this stricter protocol as true LOMO. Table 4 reports the outcome. Distillation improves LOMO by +0.104 over the single-frame base- line, with 15 of 17 materials improving (Wilcoxon p = 0.002), and by +0.053 over the four-frame baseline, with 14 of 17 improving (p = 0.001). The gains are uneven: the five largest (single-frame baseline →distilled) are on granular and natural surfaces, namely gravel 0.364 →0.708, grass 0.377 →0.610, dirt 0.285 →0.605, leaf 0.290 →0.541, and rock 0.247 →0.469, which together account for about three-quarters of the total gain. 6.5 Controls Table 5 collects the three controls, which together bound what this result can mean. 7
