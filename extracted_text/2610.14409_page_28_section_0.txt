The contrastive term raises the flow residual |g|, which enlarges the nudge displacement βγ|g|/λ2 of equation 26 and tightens equa- tion 12; the phase-2 settings reduce its prefactor βγ/λ2 by four to compensate. γ falls by 33× and β rises by the same factor, keeping βγ fixed, and the tether stiffens to λ = 2, paired with dtx = 0.5 so that λ dtx = 1 remains the deadbeat choice of Section 3.2. What matters is the reduced prefactor, not how it is achieved. In short contrastive runs from the same checkpoint, every setting at the phase- 1 prefactor lost relaxation convergence within about 100 steps, whatever its λ, including the stiffer tether with β raised to restore the phase-1 prefactor. Every setting with the prefactor reduced by a factor of four trained cleanly, among them the simplest choice, β reduced by a factor of four with γ, λ and dtx at their phase-1 values, which ran 200 contrastive steps with the relaxation converged in every phase. K SCOPE OF THE CLAIMS This appendix states, in one place, what the paper does and does not claim, on four points where a reader might reasonably read more into the results than the evidence supports. The solver is not the substrate. Every equilibrium in this paper was found by gradient descent in error coordinates (App. E), and a single deadbeat sweep of that solver is algebraically a backward pass (Goemaere et al., 2026, Thm. C.9). That is a choice of simulation method: the claim of Sec- tion 3.1 is a property of the equilibrium, not of the route to it. The local state dynamics equation 36 share that equilibrium exactly and converge towards it (App. E), but too slowly under explicit dis- cretisation to run at this scale. What the paper establishes is that a state whose local errors carry −∇xV exists, that the parameter updates EP reads from it match backpropagation’s, and that train- ing on those updates works at CIFAR-10 scale. What it does not establish is that a physical system settles there in useful time; that is the open question a substrate would answer. 28
