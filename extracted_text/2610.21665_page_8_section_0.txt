In these systems, hidden representations develop through local activity-dependent learning, while perturbation-based supervision acts at the readout. The present study addresses WP across all weight matrices. Together, these approaches motivate examining how the allocation of learning between local representation mechanisms and scalar-feedback updates affects accuracy, stability, and computational cost. The empirical scope of the present results is fully connected MNIST classification. Extending the comparison to more complex tasks will determine how the required training budget changes with problem difficulty and network size. Relating the large exposure budgets to biologically feasible learning times is also an important question. The immediate methodological priority is to reduce the cost of reaching the high-accuracy regime demonstrated here, through more informative perturbation estimates and a better balance between learning rate, batch size, and population averaging. 4.1
