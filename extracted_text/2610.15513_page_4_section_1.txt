We review related work in four areas: software-engineering approaches to security testing of LLM- based systems, the landscape of jailbreak attacks on aligned LLMs, automated prompt optimization as the intellectual ancestor of REPRO’s optimization machinery, and a consolidated framework comparison. For a comprehensive taxonomy of red-teaming methodologies, we refer readers to Jabbar et al. [15]. 2.1 Security Testing of LLM-Based Software Systems A growing body of software-engineering research treats LLM security as a testing and quality- assurance problem. Wang et al. [37] survey the landscape of software testing with LLMs, identifying adversarial robustness evaluation as a core open challenge. Chen et al. [10] provide a systematic literature review of the security of language models for code, cataloging attack and defense tech- niques and motivating principled, reproducible evaluation methodologies. On the defensive side, Zhang et al. [43] propose JailGuard, a universal detection framework for prompt-based attacks that uses TAP [28] as one of its primary baseline attacks, the same baseline against which RE- PRO is evaluated. Methodologically, Jiang et al. [16] articulate the challenges and opportunities of LLM-assisted fuzzing, while Sun et al. [35] demonstrate LLM-guided test generation for SMT- solver fuzzing, establishing the precedent for LLM-driven test synthesis as a software-engineering technique. Our prior work on red-teaming frameworks for specific evaluation targets such as faithfulness [3] established the methodological pattern of treating red-teaming as an automated, reproducible test-generation activity rather than a manual exercise, and motivated the need for the general-purpose framework developed in this paper. REPRO contributes to this line of work by providing an offensive test generation framework that complements defensive systems such as JailGuard: discovered attacks form a continuously growing adversarial test corpus against which defenses can be regression-tested, and the framework itself transfers established software-fuzzing principles (mutation operators, seed corpora, coverage- guided exploration) to the LLM security domain. 2.2 Jailbreak Attacks on Aligned LLMs We organize the jailbreak literature by access mode (white-box, gray-box, black-box) and search strategy, which together determine applicability to production LLM APIs and compatibility with continuous-integration testing workflows. 2.2.1 White-Box and Gradient-Based Methods. Zou et al. [46] developed the Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG) attack, which optimizes adversarial suffixes through token-level gradient descent and achieves 80–95% ASR on open-weight targets. Liu et al. [24] proposed AutoDAN, a white-box genetic algorithm that generates stealthier jailbreaks through hierarchical mutations at the word and sentence levels (72–98% ASR). Both methods require gradient access to the target model’s parameters and are therefore inapplicable to proprietary production APIs, which is the deployment setting REPRO targets. 2.2.2 Gray-Box Methods. Andriushchenko et al. [4] represent the current state of the art in raw effectiveness.
