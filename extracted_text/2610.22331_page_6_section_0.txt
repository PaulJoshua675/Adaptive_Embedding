On BrowseComp-ZH, KnowGPO reaches 38.8%, sur- passing the same-scale WebExplorer-8B by 6.8% and the 32B WebSailor-32B and DeepDive-32B by 13.3% and 9.1% respectively, and trailing the strongest open-source system DeepMiner-32B-RL by only 1.3%. On Xbench-2505 it reaches 48.0%, outperforming the 8B MiroThinker-v1.0 by 12.6%. These results show that knowledge-grouped credit assignment narrows the gap to substantially larger open-source systems without proprietary data or larger backbones. A gap to proprietary systems remains, for example 23.5% to Kimi-K2.5 on BrowseComp-ZH and 28.1% to Claude-4-Sonnet on GAIA, which we attribute to training data scale, model capacity, and inference-time optimization beyond our scope. Training dynamics across baselines. Figure 3 tracks validation accuracy on Xbench-2505 in RL training. All four methods start from the same SFT checkpoint and follow comparable trajectories during the first 16 steps, after which they diverge. KnowGPO opens a clear gap by around step 20 and plateaus near step 32 at roughly 50%, while GRPO, GiGPO, and IGPO settle below 46%. The gap is consistent across the second half of training, sug- gesting that knowledge-grouped credit assignment provides a denser signal throughout rather than a small final-stage advantage. 4.3 Ablation Study We next ablate the components of KnowGPO. The first ablation studies the mixing coefficient ω; the second isolates the contributions of incremental knowledge gain and knowledge-state grouping; the third compares incremental gain against discounted future returns as a process reward.
