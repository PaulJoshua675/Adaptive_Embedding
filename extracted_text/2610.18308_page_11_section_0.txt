A T2 Degeneracy Diagnostics The main text reports only the pooled T2 popular- ity correlation (§4.3). This appendix documents why per-user and persona-anchored variants are not reportable. A.1 Per-User Popularity Correlation For each user, the per-user popularity Pearson r is the correlation between the simulator’s rating and the MovieLens-25M mean rating across the movies that user mentioned. This requires a minimum sample of ∼30 (user, movie) pairs per user for a reliable estimate. Only 6/300 FULL users reach this threshold (me- dian 7 pairs per user, maximum 47); on the matched subset, only 2 users in each of FULL and STATIS- TICALONLY reach this threshold. A paired test across users is therefore not feasible. The pooled estimate in Table 2 aggregates all (user, movie) pairs across the cohort, treating each pair as an in- dependent observation, a weaker statistical claim than per-user testing would provide, but the only one available at our sample size. A.2 Persona-Anchored T2 (Liked vs. Disliked) A second variant of T2 correlates the simula- tor’s in-conversation rating with the user’s ReDial liked/disliked label (binary ground truth). Table 4 reports the diagnostics. In every condition, 94– 97% of high-confidence ground-truth labels are liked, giving varianceredial < 0.1, a near-constant variable. Pearson r against a near-constant variable is driven by a handful of disliked pairs and is not a reliable discriminative metric, so the apparent ordering across conditions is not interpreted. A.3 Balanced Accuracy as an Alternative A natural alternative is balanced accuracy on liked/disliked classification. Computing this re- quires users with both classes in ground truth. As shown above, only 2 users per condition satisfy this, too few to estimate a meaningful per-user bal- anced accuracy distribution. Pooled simple accu- racy (94–96%) equals the majority-class rate and is uninformative. Recommendation for future work.
