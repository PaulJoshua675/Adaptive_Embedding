The decoder ablation (Section 4.4.2) tells a complementary story: UNet-128 and ConvNeXt- 512 land at statistically indistinguishable downstream accuracy (p=0.17), so whatever the orig- inal SparK recipe’s hierarchical decoder buys on natural images, it is not required to transfer well to S1GRD at a matched learning rate. That makes ConvNeXt-512 the better default when- ever VRAM is tight, since it recovers 7.5–8.5% per GPU (hardware-dependent) for no accuracy cost. LLRD, meanwhile, is a smaller but broadly positive lever for the UNet-128-pretrained encoder: mean performance improves and variance shrinks across all four tasks, reaching signif- icance on two of them after correction (Section 4.4.3), enough that we recommend it as a default for pretrained encoders rather than an optional extra; it did not help a non-pretrained encoder (Section 3.4). The data scaling analysis reveals that SAR MIM pretraining is remarkably data-efficient: classification tasks and change detection saturate with as few as 10–25% of the pretraining corpus (24,396 tiles), while dense semantic segmentation (DFC2020-S1) keeps improving significantly up to 25% (60,992 tiles) and continues to gain, more gradually, through 50% (121,984 tiles). This saturation behavior suggests that the limited visual diversity of SAR amplitude imagery, which is typically dominated by a relatively small number of backscatter regimes corresponding to major land-cover types, can be effectively characterized by a surprisingly small pretraining corpus.
