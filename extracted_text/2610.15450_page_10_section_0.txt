Preprint 5 Discussion CoFlu-Brain shows that relationships among FC estimates supported by different frame com- positions provide useful self-supervision. Its representations transferred across demographic, developmental, and clinical tasks, while random partitioning improved aggregate transfer. The complete objective combines retained-view consistency, dropped-view prediction, and distributional regularization as complementary signals. Limitations and future directions. CoFlu-Brain uses the framewise cofluctuation de- composition of Pearson connectivity to construct multiple FC estimates with explicitly con- trolled frame support. Because each view aggregates the selected framewise products into a subset-specific FC matrix, the current model does not preserve the timing, order, or am- plitude of individual cofluctuation events. Its scope is instead to learn from structured relationships among finite-sample FC estimates: reproducibility across retained-frame com- positions and prediction of complementary held-out estimates. Future work could com- bine this estimate-level objective with order-aware or edge-time-series encoders to capture transient dynamics. Biological validation, causal analysis, and integration with voxel-level features remain important directions beyond the predictive evaluations considered here.
