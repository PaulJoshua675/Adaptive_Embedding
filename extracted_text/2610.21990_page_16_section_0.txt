These monocular depth maps provide addi- tional structural information which might be missing from the RGB and thermal maps by leverag- ing the pre-trained representations. Surface normals are then obtained by normalising these maps through gradient extraction. The main innovation introduced by the core component of the VesperMUSE, its semantic segmenta- tion module, lies in the attention mechanism employed during the encoding stage. The underlying concept is that each modality is analyzed both globally and at the level of fine local features, thereby providing a comprehensive representation. To capture the contribution of each modality, hierarchical feature maps are fused and treated as inputs to the main attention components. Given that the atten- tion mechanism is conceptually designed for one primary modality and k ∈N auxiliary modalities, it can be readily configured to support an arbitrary number of modalities with varying channel con- figurations (e.g., RGB with three channels or single-channel thermal maps). This flexibility makes the model highly adaptable to diverse scenarios and imaging conditions where the availability of different modalities is different than the current analysed scenario, thereby addressing the second question proposed before. Data availability and adaptation. One important aspect to take into account is the limited avail- ability of data for conducting such a study. While multi-modal semantic segmentation datasets pro- vide a high degree of reliability, and a wide range of datasets serve as effective benchmarks for LLIE, no available dataset integrates well-lit and low-light counterparts RGB, thermal imagery, structural information, and annotated semantic maps simultaneously. Given that semantic segmen- tation annotations are expensive to obtain, the most appropriate approach is to utilize datasets that include well-lit RGB images, thermal/structural data, and semantic maps. Consequently, a novel synthetic low-light adaptation pipeline is proposed as part of the Vesper- MUSE framework. The algorithm leverages local image structure and global lighting information, generating degradations that capture natural exposure variations, noise characteristics, and colour distortions. A dedicated analysis provides a quantitative evaluation of the physical and perceptual quality of the synthesized images to show the generated images’ conformity with real-world data. This integration of both local and real-world illumination information ensures that the generated images faithfully reproduce the deficiencies of natural low-light environments. 16
