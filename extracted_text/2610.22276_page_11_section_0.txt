Finally, we test whether the same point-classification hierarchy can recover multi- ple targets without additional training. Replacing repeated continue/stop decisions with a spatial probability tree raises Point-Bench counting success from 19.39% to 38.78% (Table 21). Root pruning retains the same success rate while reducing the mean number of decision nodes from 91 to 51.10 and inference time from 4.716 to 2.638 seconds per question, a 1.79× speedup. The default 10% peak threshold achieves 38.78% suc- cess, compared with 30.10% and 37.24% at thresholds of 5% and 20%, respectively (Table 22). The shared classifier thus extends to multi-point prediction through its decision tree, with pruning reducing the cost of this extension. 4.4 Training Speed The scheduling study tests whether similar-length microbatches and cross-GPU workload balancing reduce the cost of training heterogeneous spatial questions. Four fresh runs use the same historical 72K corpus, global batch 256, microbatch eight, four accumulation steps, and eight L20D GPUs. Each runs 100 updates; after eight warmup updates, the measurement window contains the same 23,552 QA. The comparison holds the model and training computation fixed apart from scheduling. Local length grouping raises throughput from 99.06 to 116.91 QA/s and reduces padding from 35.17% to 12.36%, but increases mean gradient-ready spread from 115.0 to 306.6 ms (Table 6). Global grouping with cross-GPU balancing reduces padding to 1.77% and ready-time spread to 145.7 ms, reaching 129.54 QA/s: 30.78% higher throughput, or 23.53% less measured time for the same QA volume, than the original strategy. Since ready-time spread remains above the original value, the gain reflects the combined scheduling change. A 128-QA cross-update buffer lowers padding to 1.03% but does not improve throughput and increases step- time variability. We retain the unbuffered layout to preserve optimizer-batch membership. Distributed checks verify per-question loss normalization with unequal workloads and incomplete tails, without implying bitwise equivalence across BF16 batch shapes. These short runs measure throughput, not convergence or full-run duration; they include no downstream quality evaluation. Full timing and batch-size feasibility profiles appear in Section H.
