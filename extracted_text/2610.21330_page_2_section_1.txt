Political bias in LLMs. Existing work studies political bias in LLM outputs across models and political contexts. Studies report a left- progressive lean in U.S. political content but more mixed patterns in South Korea [15], stronger alignment of GPT-4o with left-leaning German parties [6], and overrepresentation of radical-right actors in European election queries [9]. Other work finds that such patterns do not always persist for concrete voting decisions, such as Swiss referenda [5]. These studies examine political bias in model outputs, whereas we study how modifying external web content changes candidate or party exposure in generated responses. Voter persuasion with LLMs. Prior work identifies several fac- tors relevant to political persuasion with LLMs, including factual and evidence-rich information, personalization, and refutational counterargumentation. Factual and evidence-rich information re- peatedly emerges as an important driver of persuasion [3, 11, 16], while the additional benefit of personalization is mixed across stud- ies [11, 16, 17, 27]. Refutational messages can also improve persua- sion or reduce issue polarization [13, 21]. These studies focus on how LLM responses influence voters. In contrast, we study how external content influences the information produced by LLMs. Generative engine optimization. Existing GEO studies develop strategies for improving source visibility or item ranking in gener- ative engine responses.
