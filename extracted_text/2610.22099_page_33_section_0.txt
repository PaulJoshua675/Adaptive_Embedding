B.4.3 Training TIPS for coding We separately train full-canvas and blockwise policies for coding while keeping the decoder frozen, using supervised warmup followed by an RLVR continuation to the fixed 935-update endpoint. Table 25 compares these policies with the same five baselines, so changes relative to the zero-shot table reflect the policy’s domain adaptation. Coding training improves the full-canvas policy in every measured dataset–budget condition. Its HumanEval pass@1 increases to 19.51%, 25.61%, and 27.44%, while its MBPP score increases to 24.00%, 31.80%, and 37.80%. The largest gains over the corresponding math-trained full-canvas policy occur on MBPP, reaching 13.40 percentage points at T = 16. The coding-trained full policy exceeds every retained baseline on HumanEval at T = 16 and on MBPP at T = 16 and 32. Nevertheless, Non-adjusted Fast-dLLM with 8 × 32 decoding remains stronger on HumanEval at T = 32 and 64, and slightly stronger on MBPP at T = 64. The block policy responds less consistently to coding training: it improves at T = 16 on both tasks but falls below its zero-shot counterpart at T = 64. These results support domain-specific policy training, especially for full-canvas decoding, while leaving room for a coding-specific recipe and hyperparameter study. We do not infer that the present coding configuration is optimal. Table 26 additionally reports actual mean calls, since adaptive baselines may finish below their requested caps.
