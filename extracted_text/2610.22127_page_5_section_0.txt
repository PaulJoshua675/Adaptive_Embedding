Polysemanticity. To investigate the polysemantic- ity behavior of neurons in MoE experts compared to dense LLMs, we use k-sparse probing (Gurnee et al., 2023). Following Herbst et al. (2026), we es- timate the mean difference of activations between positive and negative tokens per concept and com- pute the binary classification of a probe considering only the top-k neurons with the highest activation difference. For our personalization task, we con- sider the top 5 most frequent sub-dimensions from MULTIFACETED COLLECTION as concepts for each dimension, resulting in 20 independent con- cepts. For each concept, we take the response to- kens (up to 1024 per response, 100 queries) of samples whose system message specifies that sub- dimension as positives, and those of samples spec- ifying a different sub-dimension of the same di- mension as negatives (e.g., formal vs. casual under style), giving about 50,000 tokens per class. Fol- lowing Herbst et al. (2026), we only consider to- kens routed to the target expert for MoE LLMs and identify the best layer containing the expert achiev- ing the highest classification accuracy on the test split (25% split). For dense LLMs, the best layer is that with the highest-performing MLP.
