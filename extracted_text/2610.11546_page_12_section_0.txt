These functions are standard test cases in the optimization literature because they collectively span a diverse and complementary range of challenging landscape properties, including multimodality, non-convexity, inter-variable coupling, ill-conditioning, and deceptive optima, enabling a rigorous assessment of global search capability, local refinement precision, and robustness against premature convergence. Briefly, the Ackley function combines a flat outer region with a sharply structured, multi-optima central basin; the Griewank function exhibits widespread low-amplitude oscillations with strong inter-variable coupling that intensifies with dimensionality; the Rastrigin function presents a highly multimodal landscape with densely and regularly distributed local minima; the Rosenbrock function forms a narrow, ill-conditioned curved valley that is comparatively easy to locate but difficult to traverse toward its unique global optimum; and the Schwefel function is strongly deceptive, with its global optimum located far from the majority of local optima. Together, these properties test complementary aspects of exploration, exploitation, and convergence robustness, consistent with the practical implications of the No Free Lunch (NFL) theorem [42], which states that no single algorithm can universally dominate across all problem classes. This motivates the adaptive hyperparameter regulation and dynamic operator orchestration central to the proposed DRL-DCO algorithm, whose embedded DDPG-based actor-critic agent continuously modulates search behavior according to the observed optimization state, aiming to enhance robustness and flexibility across this diverse benchmark suite. All five benchmark functions were evaluated at three problem dimensionalities, Nd = 20, 30, and 50. As Nd = 50 represents the most challenging and practically informative setting, results for this dimensionality are reported in the main text; results at Nd = 20 and 30 follow consistent trends and are omitted for brevity. Collectively, these benchmark functions present complementary and mutually reinforcing optimization challenges, including multimodality, non-convexity, inter-variable coupling, ill-conditioning, deceptive local optima, and exploration–exploitation imbalance. Consequently, achieving consistently strong performance across all benchmark functions at increasing dimensionality is inherently difficult, and reflects the practical implications of the NFL theorem, which establishes that no single optimization algorithm can universally outperform all others across all problem classes. Motivated by this challenge, the proposed DRL-DCO algorithm incorporates adaptive hyperparameter regulation and dynamic operator orchestration through an embedded DDPG-based actor–critic agent, which outputs continuous generation-wise control actions to continuously modulate search behavior in response to the observed optimization state. This adaptive mechanism is designed to enhance robustness and flexibility across the diverse optimization landscapes represented by the benchmark suite. For comparative evaluation, five baseline algorithms were considered: 1) JADE, an adaptive DE variant employing the current-to-pbest mutation strategy with adaptive parameter sampling and an optional external archive [38]; 2) LSHADE, the CEC 2014 competition-winning algorithm extending JADE’s success-history-based parameter adaptation with a linear population size reduction mechanism [39]; 3) LSHADE-SPACMA, a well-established self-adaptive hybrid algorithm integrating LSHADE and CMA-ES mechanisms [44]; 4) DRL-DE; and 5) DRL-CMA-ES. Here, the DRL-DE and DRL-CMA-ES serve as 2 ablation variants of the proposed DRL-DCO framework, obtained by fixing the orchestration ratio β(t) to 1 and 0, respectively, such that the DDPG-based agent regulates only the DE or CMA-ES component while the overall RL control architecture remains identical to that of DRL-DCO. This design enables rigorous assessment of the contribution of the dynamic DE–CMA-ES orchestration mechanism beyond single-operator DRL-governed optimization. Together, the five baselines form a progressive algorithmic hierarchy, from modern adaptive DE (JADE), to competition-level adaptive DE with population reduction (LSHADE), to a state-of-the-art non-DRL hybrid (LSHADE-SPACMA), to the two DRL-augmented single-operator ablations, enabling systematic attribution of performance gains to each successive design advancement. The initial population size was uniformly set to 100 for all algorithms. Hyperparameters for the JADE, LSHADE, and LSHADE-SPACMA followed the recommended configurations in their respective original references [38, 39, 44], while the hyperparameters of the DRL-DCO follow the specifications in Table 1. The primary performance metric is the total number of generations required to reach full convergence to the global optimum, declared when the absolute deviation between the obtained and true global optimum falls below ε = 1 × 10−8.
