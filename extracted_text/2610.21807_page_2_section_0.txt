This paper is an exploratory empirical study that works across both levels, using Yahoo! Chiebukuro discourse on logistics as a case. Because the analysis is restricted to nouns extracted from a machine translation, it characterizes the topi­ cal lexical content of posts — which referential nouns and semantic clusters they con­ tain — rather than wording in the broader linguistic sense (style, politeness, hedging, sentiment). Logistics is a useful case for this kind of content-outcome analysis. It is a professional, operational domain where questions range from narrow transactional queries (tracking a specific parcel, comparing shipping services) to broad, debatable civic and infrastruc­ ture topics (road congestion, transportation policy, retail supply chains), giving the cor­ pus real variation in both the concreteness of its content and the structural fate of its posts. We ask three questions. First, which nouns are associated with an answer being selected as “best” rather than passed over on the same question? Second, which nouns are associated with a question attracting many answers and replies rather than few or none? Third, do these word-level patterns aggregate into coherent semantic clusters, and are those clusters still associated with the two outcomes once the length of the post — and, for the question-level models, its posting year — is statistically controlled? Throughout, the study is observational: it reports conditional associations between con­ tent and outcomes, not causal effects, and the answer-level and question-level analyses are associations measured on different units of text. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews related work on platform affordances, online knowledge sharing, answer selection, and social media discourse in logistics contexts. Section 3 describes the data collection, translation, and preprocessing pipeline, and the keyness and cluster-regression methods used to ana­ lyze it. Section 4 presents the results: the noun vocabulary associated with best-selected answers and high-engagement questions, the semantic cluster structure recovered from the corpus, and which clusters are associated with each outcome. Section 5 discusses these results together with the study’s limitations and avenues for future research, and Sect. 6 concludes.
