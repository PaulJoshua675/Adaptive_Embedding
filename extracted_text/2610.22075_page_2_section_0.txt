In contrast to these two regimes, we pursue a third option of retrofitting a pretrained VLM with recurrence, following a prune-then-loop strategy. Namely, we remove some intermediate blocks of the language backbone of the VLM and loop over a subset of the remaining layers. This strategy is supported by two complementary research findings: first, one set of recent works shows that a fraction of a VLM’s layers can be removed at a limited cost in accuracy (Huang et al., 2026; Madinei et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2025), and, second, another set of works shows that the layers of a pretrained model can be skipped or repeated at inference (Li et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026). We thus hypothesize that iteratively executing the subset of layers of a pruned VLM can recover the capacity lost due to pruning. We test this hypothesis by retrofitting, i.e., by converting a pretrained non-recurrent VLM into a recurrent one while reusing its existing weights rather than pretraining a recurrent model from scratch. We refer to the resulting model as retrofitted recurrent VLM (RR- VLM). We prune a block of intermediate layers from the language backbone of a pretrained VLM and designate a contiguous subset of the remaining layers as a recurrent block (McLeish et al., 2025). To feed the block’s output back into its input for successive iterations, we concatenate the output with the tokens from the layers preceding the recurrent block and train a linear layer to compress this concatenated representation to its original dimensionality (McLeish et al., 2025). This surgery, however, degrades the pretrained language backbone, and although text-only training repairs it, the repair leaves the language model misaligned with the original projector. We resolve this with a three-stage training curriculum: first, we apply a text-only training to repair the backbone, second, we apply a projector-only training to restore the image-text alignment, and third, we fine-tune the entire model on visual instruction data. In the text-only and the instruction-tuning stage, we add a knowledge distillation (KD) term, with the pretrained model as teacher, to avoid the language backbone from drifting too far from the original representation that the projector was aligned with. We evaluate the proposed method on the TinyLLaVA-1.4B backbone (Zhou et al., 2024), training it on 60B tokens of text as well as 493k image–caption pairs and 660k instruction tuning samples from the base model’s training data, without introducing any new vision-language data. We compare the resulting model against the pretrained TinyLLaVA-1.4B model and four baselines trained on the same data: (1) the unpruned model subjected to the identical recovery training, (2) the unpruned model without the text-only stage, (3) the pruned model without recurrence, and (4) a recurrent lan- guage model that has been extended to a VLM. Our experiments show that our proposed retrofitted recurrent model outperforms the unpruned baseline while having 23.3% fewer language-model pa- rameters and show that this gain is due to the recurrence rather than the recovery training. The gain concentrates on reasoning-oriented benchmarks, whereas performance on basic visual under- standing benchmarks remains on par with the baseline. Compared to a recurrent language model extended to a VLM, our retrofitted model achieves a higher average accuracy. We further ablate each component of the retrofit: the training stages, the layer allocation, the knowledge distillation term, KV-cache sharing at inference and the adapter.
