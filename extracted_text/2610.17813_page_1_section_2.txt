Modern voice agents [1] increasingly rely on external knowl- edge to answer spoken requests, making speech-driven re- trieval a key component of natural spoken interaction [2]. Conventional systems first transcribe a spoken query using Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) and then pass the tran- script to a text retriever [3]. Although this cascade benefits from powerful text retrieval models, sequential ASR decod- ing adds latency, and transcription errors propagate directly to retrieval [4, 5]. End-to-end speech-to-text retrieval instead encodes speech directly as a retrieval query, avoiding explicit transcription [6, 7]. At inference, the speech query is embedded in the same manner for retrieval. which are costly to collect and difficult to scale across do- mains and languages. SpeechRAG demonstrated that such retrieval-specific supervision can be avoided by aligning speech with its transcript embedding, transferring the re- trieval capability of a pretrained text embedding model to speech through speech-transcript pairs [4]. While this re- sult establishes the potential of speech-transcript alignment, its broader applicability as a training paradigm for speech retrieval has remained largely unexplored. We extend this idea by using standard ASR corpora as the sole source of supervision, turning widely available speech-transcript data into a scalable resource for training speech retrievers without retrieval-specific annotation. We then systematically exam- ine its scalability and generalizability across data and model scales, languages, retrieval directions, and training configura- tions. We introduce SSEmb (Simple Speech Embedding), a modular end-to-end speech retriever trained using only stan- dard ASR corpora. SSEmb connects a frozen speech encoder to a frozen text embedding model through a lightweight adapter.
