Explicitly representing visitation distributions and importance weights requires quantities indexed by state–action pairs. Such a representation can be costly when |X × A| is large, and is unsuitable when these spaces are infinite. We therefore use the known feature map to formulate the sampling design problem in terms of the feature dimension d. In a finite context–action space, the usual importance weight for evaluating a target policy π using samples from a behavior policy is the target-to-behavior ratio Pπ(X = x, A = a)/Pµ(X = x, A = a), provided that the behavior distribution covers the target distribution. In the linear setting, we can instead construct weights using feature means and second moments. We write ϕπ := Eπ[ϕ(X, A)] for the mean feature vector of policy π.
