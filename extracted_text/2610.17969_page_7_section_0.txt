We test whether GCT’s normalized residual stream (Sec. 4) limits this additional error and preserves validation per- formance when states are stored at low precision. NDA predicts that reducing token-to-token scale variation lowers the error associated with a shared quantiza- tion scale. To test this prediction, we apply PTQ to the residual stream at every block boundary, as in hidden-state caching, offloading, or transmission. In a pre-norm network, the skip path carries the stored tensor, including the rounding errors, into subsequent residual additions. Prop. 5 and Thm. 7 show that, under the stated as- sumptions and high-resolution regime, the worst-case downstream output error grows as Θ(κscale/q), where κscale is the ratio of the largest token RMS to the small- est and q = 2b−1 −1. This dependence remains even when subsequent normalization cancels pure changes in magnitude exactly. In effective-resolution terms, cross- token scale variation consumes log2 κscale bits. The zero-floor GCT stream fixes every token’s RMS at one, setting κscale = 1 and removing this source of resolu- tion loss. We compare one scale for the entire tensor with one scale per token to test whether adapting the quantization spacing to each token’s magnitude reduces the baselines’ error and narrows the error gap to GCT. We use weight-only, linear-input activation, and key– value (KV) cache quantization as controls to identify where scale causes the largest errors. The strongest sep- aration between normalized streams and the other base- lines occurs when the residual stream uses a shared ten- sor scale (Tables 2 and 10).
