Given a set of documents, the policy generates a set of ideas U = {u1, . . . , uk}; for reasoning models, the whole set is generated after a single shared reasoning prefix. Each idea ui is scored by its quality qi and its distance di to the closest earlier idea (d1 = 1), giving a per-item reward ri = qi · di. The set reward R(U) = P i ri is used as the RL signal to update the policy, encouraging sets that are both high-quality and diverse. previous attempts at training an ideation model against execution signal has been shown to collapse onto a single dominant idea rather than a spread of them (Si et al., 2026). In our work, we close this gap by treating idea generation as a creative search problem. Given a cor- pus of prior documents, an agent should propose a set of K ideas, each grounded in specific source documents, that are high-quality and differ from each other by design. We study this for document- grounded ideation (Wang et al., 2024). We combine quality and diversity into one set-level training signal by adapting the Creative Utility formulation (Wadhwa et al., 2026) where each idea earns credit for its own quality and for its distance from the ideas emitted before it, so a redundant idea earns little no matter how well-written it is (as illustrated in Figure 1). To achieve this, we train an ideation model (Qwen3-8B (Team, 2025)) in two steps. We first do a su- pervised fine-tuning step to initialize the policy on well-formed idea sets, followed by GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) against a set-level objective (adapted from the creative utility formulation). We then report this utility measure on a contamination-free test set that we put together. We also look at two existing benchmarks, GiantsBench (He-Yueya et al., 2026) and IdeaSeed (Chen et al., 2026). Our tested baselines include the untrained base model and its SFT checkpoint, strong closed-source agents, GIANTS (He-Yueya et al., 2026), and DARLING (Li et al., 2025), an alternative quality- diversity objective that we retrain in our setting. We show that our 8B trained model achieves the highest creative utility on our test set, outperform- ing strong closed-source agents and competing quality-diversity objectives such as DARLING. A small pilot human study with four expert annotators finds that our sets are comparable to Claude Opus 4.8’s in the number of promising ideas, while containing more mutually distinct ones. Finally, when we execute each set of ideas in a fixed sandbox with a coding agent, our model is compa- rable to Claude Opus 4.8 on success@k, showing that we are able to increase diversity without compromising important aspects of ideation like executability. Contributions Our contributions are (1) a set-level RL objective for training that balances quality and diversity; (2) an instantiation of this objective for document-grounded ideation; and (3) an 8B trained ideation model that outperforms strong closed-source agents and models trained on compet- ing objectives on creative utility, and whose ideas are comparably promising in a small pilot human and execution study. 2 TRAINING WITH A SET-LEVEL REWARD 2.1
