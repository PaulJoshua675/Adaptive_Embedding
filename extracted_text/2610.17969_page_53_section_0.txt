The optimizer rule in Sec. 2.2 requires the full displacement to transform from dual to primal units. Muon supplies a competitive shape-aware orthogonalized direction with a fixed scalar step. The experiment tests compatibility rather than the covariance law. All three architectures improve at matched tokens, with similar gains for the typed and untyped blocks. Relative to the shared-recipe AdamW point at matched tokens, the changes are −0.309 nats for GCT, −0.329 for Pre-LN, and −0.242 for QK-Norm. The result shows that the typed architecture and this shape-aware optimizer compose without changing the architecture. Width transfer and µP composition. Two experiments probe the parameterization axis in Table 14. The learning-rate robustness protocol of Table 5 is repeated at d ∈{256, 1024} with dh = 64 and the rest of the recipe unchanged. Raising the rate from 6 × 10−4 to 4.8 × 10−3 improves GCT at every width, with changes of −0.592, −0.369, and −0.245 nats for d ∈{256, 512, 1024}. At the higher rate, Pre-LN is +0.141, +0.131, and +2.039 nats above Pre-LN’s best sampled value at the respective width. We then compose both architectures with the learning-rate-only µP rule at the respective d = 512-tuned learning rates. This rule scales hidden-matrix learning rates by 512/d and leaves initialization and multipliers unchanged. The rule changes GCT loss by at most 0.006 nats at either width, namely −0.005 at d = 256 and +0.006 at d = 1024, while the Pre-LN changes are +0.020 at d = 256 and −0.068 at d = 1024. These results support combining the tested width-dependent learning-rate rule with canonical activation scaling.
