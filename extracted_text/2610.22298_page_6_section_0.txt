Accordingly, the contribution of the present study is not a presumption that multidisciplinary deliberation is intrinsically superior, but an empirical decomposition of the stages and design choices that produce a clinical second opinion. 3 Methods 3.1 Study Design We conducted a benchmark-development and evaluation study using published, diagnostically resolved clinical cases. The generation pipeline comprised three stages: (i) case-specific assignment of clinical roles, (ii) independent role- specific analysis before consilium (the pre-consilium stage), and (iii) multidisciplinary synthesis (the consilium stage). The dataset’s separate Answer field was not supplied to the generation models and was reserved for evaluation. Physician evaluation comprised three independently scored components (Figure 1). First, physicians evaluated the clinical reasoning within the relevant role-specific pre-consilium output, excluding its clinical opinion. Second, they evaluated the clinical opinion of the same role-specific output. Third, they evaluated the final case-level consilium conclusion. The same eight quality criteria were applied independently to each component using a 0-3 scale, a score assigned to one component was not carried over to another. Three additional ratings-panel adequacy, case complexity, and overall quality across all stages - were assigned once per case assessment. The reference pipeline supported two pre-consilium prompt conditions—free-form narrative responses and structured, schema-constrained responses with expanded clinical-reasoning guidance—and two inference topologies (single and N +1). All multidisciplinary synthesis used the interactive consilium protocol described below. The conditions included in each analysis were specified prospectively.
