Recent work on retrieval-augmented generation has increasingly moved beyond flat passage retrieval toward structured knowledge, multi-hop reasoning, and autonomous search. Three lines of research are particularly relevant to our work: knowledge-graph retrieval for question answering, causal retrieval and reasoning, and agentic retrieval. 2.1 Knowledge Graph Retrieval for LLM Question Answering Knowledge-graph retrieval represents entities and their relations explicitly, enabling LLMs to access evidence that is distributed across multiple con- nected pieces of information. Representative works include GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2024), KG2RAG (Zhu et al., 2025), GRAG (Hu et al., 2025), G- Retriever (He et al., 2024), SubgraphRAG (Li et al., 2025a), KAR (Xia et al., 2025), GraphSearch (Yang et al., 2025), HybGRAG (Lee et al., 2025), CoRAG (Zheng et al., 2025), and MoR (Lei et al., 2025). These approaches demonstrate the value of com- bining search with graph structure, but they primar- ily determine relevance through textual, relational, or structural compatibility. They do not explicitly ask whether a retrieved piece of evidence is conse- quential or essential to answering the query, which motivates our causal relevance formulation. 2.2 Causal Reasoning for LLM Question Answering A growing body of work incorporates causal rela- tions into retrieval and reasoning. Representative works include CDF-RAG (Khatibi et al., 2025), HCG-RAG (Saouda et al., 2026), CausalAgent (Wang et al., 2026), and CausalRAG (Wang et al., 2025). Although these approaches differ in implementa- tion, they generally associate causality with cause- effect relations, dependency structures, or causal mechanisms represented in the underlying knowl- edge. Our notion is different. CARA does not require a retrieved edge or node to represent a lit- eral cause-effect relation. Instead, we define causal relevance at retrieval time in terms of whether evi- dence is consequential and essential for answering the query. Thus, our focus is not causal structure in the world being described, but the causal con- tribution of retrieved evidence to the answering process. 2.3 Agentic Retrieval for LLM Question Answering Agentic retrieval turns retrieval from a fixed pipeline into an adaptive decision process. Rep- resented works include ReAct (Yao et al., 2023), AvaTaR (Wu et al., 2024a), MA-RAG (Kumar and Jain, 2025), SEMA-RAG (Huang et al., 2026), and OmniSearch (Li et al., 2025b). Several re- cent methods extend agentic retrieval to knowledge graphs, including Tree-of-Traversals (Markowitz et al., 2024), Think-on-Graph (Sun et al., 2024), Think-on-Graph 2.0 (Ma et al., 2025), RoG (Luo et al., 2024), Search-on-Graph (Sun et al., 2026), and Explore-on-Graph (Yan et al., 2026). Together, these methods establish graph retrieval as an adap- tive search process in which an agent can decide where and how far to traverse. However, existing agentic retrieval approaches are primarily based on textual similarity instead of causal retrieval we advocate for. Our work ad- dresses this gap by introducing causal retrieval to agents in the broader sense of identifying evidence whose presence and absence materially contributes to resolving the information need. 2
