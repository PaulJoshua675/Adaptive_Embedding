Figure 2: Continuous 72-hour multi-day simulation across the five clinical cohorts. (A) Homeostatic sleep pressure S(t) and circadian pacemaker C(t) trajectories against environmental photoperiods (shaded regions denote nocturnal intervals, 23:00 to 07:00). Homeostatic trajectories S(t) for paradoxical insomnia (dashed purple) identically coincide with normal controls (solid blue) due to intact subcortical homeostatic kinetics. Note the +4.0 h phase delay in CRSWD and daytime somnogenic clearances in narcolepsy. (B) Concurrent PSG hypnograms scored via AASM criteria (W=Wake, N1–N3=NREM stages, R=REM), displaying physiological 90-minute ultradian cycling in normal sleep, prolonged nocturnal awakenings in chronic insomnia, and daytime sleep attacks in narcolepsy. 6.2 Inference 2: Circadian Desynchrony and the Phase-Shifted Clearance Deficit In the CRSWD cohort, a +4.0 h phase shift in the endogenous pacemaker delayed the circadian nadir until 10:00 (see Figure 2A). Although the model accumulated 16.63 h of sleep, its Well-Rested Index was restricted to 47.9%. The delay forced sleep episodes past the 06:30 dawn threshold into ambient sunlight (10,000 lux). The photic stimulation provoked premature LHA activation, causing elevated nocturnal latency (SOL = 99 min), fragmented sleep continuity (WASO = 454 min), and incomplete homeostatic clearance (Snadir ≈0.38 versus 0.18 in controls) [31, 38]. 11
