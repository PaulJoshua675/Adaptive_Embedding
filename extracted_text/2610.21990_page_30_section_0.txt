(3.16) 3.2.2 Auxiliary modalities VesperMUSE’s segmentation module works with three modalities: the main modality (well-lit RGB or LLIE map obtained with the algorithm presented in Section 3.2.1 and the thermal-guided en- hancement model described in Section 3.3), and two auxiliary modalities: thermal and surface nor- mal derived from the monocular depth map obtained from the main RGB modality. Thermal. As previously discussed, the thermal modality provides enhanced robustness under vary- ing illumination conditions. Furthermore, its effectiveness is largely attributed to the intrinsic phys- ical properties it captures, namely the thermal inertia and emissivity of different materials and sur- faces. These characteristics offer complementary information to the Fusion Vision Transformer, beyond what is available in the visible spectrum. In the PST900 dataset [8], which depicts a subterranean environment with minimal heat sources, thermal imaging acts as a reliable structural cue, enabling the detection of humans even in the pres- ence of obscurants. Conversely, in the CART dataset [25], the thermal modality facilitates the dis- crimination of diverse textures commonly encountered in natural outdoor environments. Surface Normal. To better extract the context of the scene, a monocular depth map is extracted to highlight the structural and material differences between objects or surfaces. The noise and colour distortions, attenuated but still present in the enhanced RGB images and the thermal consistency of nearby objects in thermal maps might misguide the network in distinguishing edges and sepa- ration of entities. The first order gradients of the depth map are taken and turned into unit normal vectors to isolate and emphasize the geometric discontinuities where objects might protrude from their backgrounds. Furthermore, the scale-invariant representation of the geometric features pro- vides the robustness needed in terms of the relative position of the objects to the camera, enabling a informative features extraction for close objects, as found in the PST900 dataset [8], and distant objects as in the CART dataset [25]. 30
