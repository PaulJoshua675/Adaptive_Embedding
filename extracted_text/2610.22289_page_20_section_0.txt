The results concern the tested model–effort configurations and three environment families. They compare performance under the reported execution policies, not at matched inference cost. The small, mixed differences in mean Terminal-Bench scores do not establish that Stop-and-report is at least as effective as Baseline. 9 Discussion The first research question concerns improving an environment-agnostic agent across environ- ments. Stop-and-report improves mean ARC-AGI-3 RHAE and AGI Maze RHAE70, with small, mixed changes on Terminal-Bench. These results show that a small change can improve a general agent beyond the environment that motivated it and illustrate how to test its effects elsewhere. The second question concerns why agents using the same underlying LLM, with access to the same task information and general-purpose tools, can nevertheless perform differently. One hypothesis is that a sufficiently capable LLM could construct useful procedures for itself, leaving little advantage to developer-supplied task-specific scaffolding. This expectation is related to Sutton’s The Bitter Lesson, which emphasizes the long-term value of general methods that exploit increasing computation [10]. Exgentic finds that backbone choice explains more aggregate performance variation than architecture in its closed-source comparison, although architecture remains consequential within a model [1]. Our previous ARC-AGI-3 study likewise found that gains from model capability and reasoning effort often exceeded differences between the tested agent variants [9]. These observations motivate the hypothesis; they do not establish that scaffolding will become irrelevant. The present results show that supplied procedures still matter with GPT-5.6-sol. Even at max reasoning effort, Stop-and-report, Controlled_RESET, and twma_v1.61 achieve different mean ARC-AGI-3 scores. The specialization advantage therefore remains in the evaluated setting. We consider two possible, complementary explanations: how effectively the agent uses available information and procedures, and how much inference it performs and where it allocates that inference. 9.1 Using available information and procedures One possible explanation is that scaffolding helps the agent reconsider relevant information or follow a useful procedure more reliably. When Stop-and-report returns, the controller reminds Codex of the goal and suggests rereading the environment description (Appendix B.1.2). This may help it reconsider an unsuccessful approach or revisit neglected information. The same reminder is available to Baseline; the modification encourages Codex to return when it encounters difficulty. It creates an opportunity for reconsideration rather than enforcing a reflection procedure. twma_v1.61 illustrates the distinction between available information and prescribed orga- nization. The general agent receives information about levels and attempts and is explicitly permitted to reorganize observations or create workspace files (Appendices A.1 and B.1.1). It could therefore build level-and-attempt indexes or maintain a textual account of the game. twma_v1.61 supplies the organization and requires that account. Its advantage need not come from additional game facts; it may come from prescribing useful procedures instead of leaving the agent to devise them.
