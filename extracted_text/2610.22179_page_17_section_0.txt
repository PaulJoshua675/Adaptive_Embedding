B.2 Overflow allocation and memory accounting Kernels count overflowing streams and total tail length, compact their metadata with local prefix sums and atomic reservations, and gather raw exponent tails. B.4 Comparison with SplitZip SplitZip (Guo and Joshi, 2026) is a GPU-native framework for lossless KV-cache compression. Like LCT, it compresses BF16 exponents using a codebook prepared before use. SplitZip assigns 4-bit codes to the 16 most common exponent values. Less common exponents are stored verbatim with a 16-bit integer marking their position. Its average storage per value, excluding other metadata and padding, is therefore bSplitZip = 8 + 4 + 24ϵ = 12 + 24ϵ bits/value, (7) where the terms account for the sign–fraction byte, the 4-bit code, and overflow with probability ϵ. With no overflow, the compression ratio is 16/12 = 4/3 ≈1.33. LCT achieves ratios above 1.4 by assigning variable-length codes and optimizing which symbols use escapes. SplitZip stores a 16-bit position for each overflow value; LCT instead uses an escape prefix of at most eight bits followed by an eight-bit literal. LCT also adapts its codebook through runtime exponent centering. SplitZip does not perform this adjustment, so changes in tensor scale can increase overflow under a fixed codebook. Adaptation is particularly useful during training, when tensor magnitudes evolve. In the evaluated SplitZip implementation, encoding synchronizes with the host to determine the overflow-buffer size, adding to encoding latency.
