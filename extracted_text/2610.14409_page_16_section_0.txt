The gradient correspondence of Section 3.2, on which training rests, needs more: that the gap’s θ- derivative is O(γ) too, which does not follow from a pointwise bound alone. It follows instead from smoothness, in three steps. First, the equilibrium is as smooth as the layers. Applying the implicit function theorem to the spring-clamped system equation 9 in the joint variables (x, e), whose Hessian at γ = 0 is λI ⊕I and hence invertible, the free equilibrium (x∗, h∗) is a Ck function of (θ, γ) whenever the layers are Ck+1. Second, the velocity is finite at γ = 0. Every error vanishes there, so ∇xEPCN(x∗, h∗) vanishes and x∗= xt; a Ck function that vanishes at γ = 0, divided by γ, is Ck−1, so v∗= −γ−1∇xEPCN is Ck−1 in (θ, γ) including at γ = 0, where by Step 4 its value is −∇xVθ(xt). Hence LPCN(θ, 0) = L(θ). Third, the mean value theorem in γ: if ∇θLPCN has a bounded γ-derivative, its change from γ = 0 is at most that bound times γ. That derivative is a mixed second derivative of LPCN, so LPCN — and hence v∗, of which it is a smooth function — must be jointly C2. Tracing back through the two derivatives lost above, one to the implicit function theorem and one to the division by γ, this holds for layers with four continuous derivatives, which smooth activations such as SiLU and tanh have. Then ∇θLPCN(θ, γ) = ∇θL(θ) + O(γ), which is the correspondence. B THE TRAINING CONSTRUCTION IN DETAIL This appendix fills in the construction of Section 3.2. LPCN differs from L in two ways, both O(γ). First, the correspondence is inexact at finite γ: vPCN = −∇xVθ+O(γ) by Proposition 1. Second, v∗= vPCN(x∗) is the velocity at the relaxed state x∗rather than at xt, an evaluation point displaced by x∗−xt = γv∗/λ. Keeping that displacement small is the tether λ’s job, and it has less work to do here than in FlowEqProp (Gower, 2026), where GradEP was originally introduced: there the velocity converted to an extension with a factor O(1/λ), so a close evaluation point required λ ≫1, whereas equation 10 converts with γ/λ so the requirement is only γ/λ ≪1. The smallness of γ that the correspondence already demands therefore supplies this constraint too, leaving λ free to be chosen on other grounds (see below). γ and β. The injection strength γ plays for V the role β plays for the cost C in Equilibrium Propagation: a small linear term on one node whose stationary response, divided by that term’s strength, is a gradient. As in EP, the division is a constant gain on the readout, which costs nothing on an analog substrate. Sweep budgets. The free phase relaxes for Tfree sweeps and each nudged phase for Tnudge sweeps from the free equilibrium, with h equilibrated by Kh inner sweeps per step of x in both. Parameters. Five quantities appear in the training construction besides γ. The spring stiffness λ is not tuned: with the visible state updated as x ←x −dtx ∇xEfree, the spring extension contracts by (1−dtx λ) per sweep, up to the O(γ) curvature of EPCN in x (App. C), so λ dtx = 1 settles it in a single step, exactly as the error parameterisation settles h in one sweep; larger λ dtx overshoots. Phase 1 uses λ = 1, dtx = 1 and phase 2 uses λ = 2, dtx = 0.5 (App. J). The nudge strength β is the one parameter that must be chosen: it sets the displacement between the two nudged equi- libria, which must be large enough to resolve in finite-precision arithmetic and small enough for equation 11 to hold, a window Section 3.2.1 derives and Section 4.2 measures. The budgets Tfree, Tnudge and Kh are the sweep counts of the three relaxations; the last two are satisfied by a single sweep for the same deadbeat reason as λ, so the cost of an EP update is the free-phase equilibration Tfree alone. 16
