For applications such as product digitization and embodied AI, generating relightable 3D assets from a single reference image requires faithful preservation of the reference appearance. We refer to this requirement as super-alignment: accurately reproducing fine-grained text, logos, and patterns at their corresponding visible surface locations, while completing unobserved regions with materials consistent with the visible appearance. Two prominent approaches to appearance generation are multi-view synthesis and native 3D generation. Multi-view pipelines leverage geometry-conditioned 2D generative models to synthesize appearance across surrounding viewpoints, then project and fuse the resulting images onto the mesh (Tencent Hunyuan3D Team, 2025a). However, cross-view discrepancies can introduce seams and ghosting, while blending conflicting predictions blurs sharp reference details (Lai et al., 2026; Zeng et al., 2026). Native 3D generation circumvents view-fusion artifacts by synthesizing appearance directly on 3D representations (Lai et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026; Zeng et al., 2026; Xiang et al., 2026), yet preserving fine reference details remains challenging. For example, TRELLIS.2 employs a 3D material VAE to compress surface PBR properties into material latents, which are generated under geometry and image conditioning before being decoded into surface materials. Its image cross-attention provides no explicit correspondence between image details and 3D surface locations, leaving the generator to learn where those details belong. To improve spatial alignment, recent work lifts high-resolution image features into 3D through camera projection (Li et al., 2026a). Yet even with explicit spatial correspondence, these conditions remain visual features rather than material observations. Consequently, the 3D generator must simultaneously shoulder the responsibilities of inferring PBR properties from visual features, reconstructing fine reference details at their corresponding surface locations, and synthesizing materials for unobserved regions. We argue that these responsibilities should not fall on a single 3D generator. Given the inherent scarcity of 3D training data, requiring the 3D model to learn these heterogeneous tasks jointly increases its learning burden. Instead, we offload visible-material estimation to 2D generative priors and establish explicit surface alignment through camera projection, separating both from the 3D generative task. Our key insight is to translate visible reference evidence into native material observations before 3D generation, then organize full-surface completion around this grounded evidence. Rather than forcing the 3D model to infer physical materials from abstract visual features, this formulation enables the generator to anchor directly on established material evidence, preserving reference details while focusing more of its capacity on completing unobserved surfaces. Based on this principle, we introduce SUPRA-Tex, a framework for generating relightable 3D assets with super-aligned textures. First, we leverage strong 2D priors by training an MLLM-based image editing model to jointly predict lighting-separated PBR maps in the reference view in a single forward pass, while preserving fine visual details. Next, we project these maps onto valid visible surface locations and encode the resulting partial materials with the pretrained material VAE encoder into native observation latents. Sharing the target latent space and directly decodable by the pretrained material decoder, these latents provide spatially aligned material content rather than abstract visual features that the 3D backbone must translate into PBR properties. We also encode the spatial extent of these observations to guide completion. To guide full-surface material generation, we design an interleaved flow transformer that reads the native observation latents as fixed, read-only reference memory at every block. Its blocks alternate between updating unobserved latent cells and the full surface, allocating more updates to cells without observations while retaining full-surface interactions for coherent completion. Since observations are indexed by 3D surface coordinates, the same interface incorporates additional views, including 2D-generated back views, and interactive 2D edits without retraining the 3D backbone. Through this division of labor, SUPRA-Tex delivers What You See Is What You Get fidelity in the reference view while completing the asset with globally coherent, relightable materials, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Using only public datasets for training, SUPRA-Tex achieves SOTA results in both geometry-conditioned texturing and end-to-end asset generation. With synthetic back-view conditioning, SUPRA-Tex achieves reference fidelity and visual quality on real photographs comparable to or better than those of the leading commercial systems in our comparisons. Code and checkpoints are released on our project page to facilitate future research: supra-3d.github.io/supratex. Our main contributions are as follows: • Our key insight is to establish surface-aligned material observations before 3D generation, allowing the generator to focus on preserving reference details and completing unobserved surfaces. Built on this insight, SUPRA-Tex is trained solely on public datasets and, with synthetic back-view conditioning, achieves reference fidelity and visual quality comparable to or better than those of leading commercial systems in our comparisons. • We present native observation encoding, which projects reference-view PBR estimates onto valid surface locations and encodes partial materials in the target latent space, simplifying learning to preserve reference appearance. • We propose an interleaved flow transformer that uses fixed, read-only observation anchors and alternates unobserved- cell and full-surface updates for reference-faithful, coherent material completion. 2
