These measurements connect an upstream engineering change to its downstream consequences. Two complementary experiments establish the importance of this distinction. Six-backbone evaluation shows that candidate- dependent and direct strategies reach different populations, often excluding different proportions of the two Gold classes. Paired inference-precision experiments then hold the backbone and input IDs fixed. Even unchanged aggregate coverage can hide an exchange of scored items, and a precision change can make a development target feasible while evaluation coverage declines. Shared-subset comparisons, threshold sensitivity, and extraction recovery explain how these changes affect the interpretation of system performance. Our contributions are: (1) A complete-stream evaluation framework for event admission. We separate item eligibility from target avail- ability and connect the scored population to calibration feasibility, shared-subset ranking, and upstream versus de- cision loss. (2) Evidence that score availability is an operational sys- tem property. Six-backbone comparisons and paired pre- cision perturbations show how processing choices change the evaluated population and attainable operating points. Recovery and fixed-score calibration analyses isolate their consequences for complete-stream decisions. (3) A layered resource supporting reproducible evalua- tion. The 3,290-record resource separates human evalu- ation and calibration from AI-assisted observation.
