Two distinct strands of work are particularly related to our problem. Robotic world models have made rapid progress in predicting action-conditioned futures (Zhou et al., 2025; 2024; Zhu et al., 2024; Zhen et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2026; Gao et al., 2026). More recent work explores test-time adaptation to improve long-horizon memory or incorporate new behavioral supervision (Wan et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026; Feng et al., 2026; Yoo et al., 2025; Jang et al., 2026). These approaches predict future outcomes from visual observations alone, without accounting for underlying environmental variation that is not directly observable. (fig. 2 (a)). In contrast, T2-Z disentangles unobserved environmental variation from the world model’s observable scene representation, through environment latent conditioning. Environment representations and system identification have demonstrated the value of environment-specific representations for system identification and rapid behavioral adaptation (Yu et al., 2017; Nagabandi et al., 2019; Rakelly et al., 2019; Lee et al., 2020; Kumar et al., 2021; Miki et al., 2022; Nahrendra et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023; He et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2024). The Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) family and related methods typically rely on predefined privileged param- eters (Kumar et al., 2021; Miki et al., 2022; Nahrendra et al., 2023; He et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2024). For example, RMA restricts adaptation to 5 factors and uses their ground-truth values to learn an em- bedding that an adaptation encoder later recovers from interaction history (Kumar et al., 2021). This prespecifies the environment representation and ties inference to a learned history-to-embedding mapping, limiting flexibility under unknown, compounded, or out-of-distribution variation (fig. 2 (b)). These methods also generally rely on a learned history encoder (Chen et al., 2024; Xing et al., 2026), limiting generalization when the amount of test-time interaction differs from that seen during training. In contrast, T2-Z learns representations of environmental factors implicitly through world prediction error, without predefined ground-truth physical parameters or a learned history encoder. Our method is also related to the literature on Test-Time Adaptation (TTA), which has enabled robots to improve after deployment by adapting policy, prompts, or memory rather than optimizing a dedicated environment representation. Existing approaches include test-time adaptation of diffusion and flow policies (Li et al., 2025; 2026), online policy adaptation (Liu et al., 2026; Jin & Zhang, 2026; Zhu et al., 2026), and adaptation through latent prompts, fast weights, memory, or retrieval (Zhang et al., 2026; Jiang et al., 2026; Feng et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2026; Park et al., 2026). TTA has also been applied directly to predictive models through video replanning, world-model training, and closed-loop latent adaptation (Ko et al., 2025; Wan et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026). 3
