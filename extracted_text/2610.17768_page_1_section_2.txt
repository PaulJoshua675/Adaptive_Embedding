Social highlighting platforms let readers see which passages other readers marked. On a page with few readers there is little to show, and a model of where readers converge (crowd salience) can fill the gap. Our earlier work found that such a model is learnable from the page alone [9], that language models agree with one another far more than with readers [11], and that fusing several frontier models with reading position recovers more of the predictable structure than any single model [12]. The best model we had in August 2026 was a 3-billion-parameter language model distilled from such a fusion and then trained on readers’ highlights: it ranked sentences well above every model it learned from. Two months later the frontier had moved. Seven new models were available, and the natural plan is to ask whether they close the gap zero-shot, and if not, whether they make a better teacher for the trained model. A third option needs no new model: more reader data. This paper compares the three on the highlights readers leave in ordinary use, with the comparisons fixed in advance. This paper makes one claim: for predicting where readers converge, reader data still beats frontier models.
