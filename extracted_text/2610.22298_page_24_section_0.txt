Structured prompting had its clearest advantage when the response required explicit action planning. Its management difference was the largest format-associated criterion contrast and had the same direction across seven of eight models. This comparatively large gain explains why structured reports had higher overall means in seven pipelines even though several smaller criterion differences favored free-form reports. Smaller advantages in prioritization and grounding further indicate that the implemented structured workflow produced a more organized and actionable plan. Structured prompting therefore added value through management quality and organization rather than through uniform improvement of every criterion. 5.2 Value of the Benchmark The benchmark was designed to evaluate a clinical second opinion as a complete decision-support artifact rather than as a single diagnostic answer. Its eight response-quality criteria separately measure whether a model uses the supplied facts correctly, identifies and prioritizes the clinical problems, integrates relevant data, constructs coherent reasoning, develops an appropriate differential diagnosis, reaches a calibrated conclusion, proposes an actionable management plan, and addresses safety. This profile makes it possible to distinguish models that produce fluent diagnostic reasoning from those that can also convert that reasoning into a grounded, usable, and safe clinical recommendation. The two evaluation checkpoints add a second level of resolution. Retaining the primary-role opinion before consilium and evaluating the final multidisciplinary synthesis on the same cases makes it possible to determine whether the complete synthesis pipeline adds value and which clinical dimensions change. Likewise, applying free-form and structured conditions to the same case–model panels separates a broad response-format effect from the substantially larger effect of model identity. Table 6 demonstrates this capability directly: it recovers a stable model hierarchy while Table 8 shows that the apparent advantage of structured reports is specifically concentrated in management rather than distributed uniformly across the rubric. Finally, the benchmark records the process that produces the final answer. First-round dissent, consensus frequency, review rounds, the distinct finalization stage, and token use are reported alongside clinical-quality scores. This makes it possible to distinguish procedural agreement from output quality and to identify configurations, such as GPT-5.6-Sol, that combine strong clinical scores with rapid convergence. Together, these features provide a comprehensive and extensible framework for comparing clinically relevant model capabilities, prompting strategies, and synthesis pipelines without reducing performance to diagnostic accuracy alone. 6
