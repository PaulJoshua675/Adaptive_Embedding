They as- signed one of three labels: EXCLUDED (a seri- ous, evidence-supported issue warrants removing the turn), FLAGGED–RETAINED (a meaningful is- sue exists but does not warrant removal), or UN- FLAGGED (no meaningful issue is identified). The annotators agreed unanimously on 70/105 turns (66.7%; Fleiss’ κ = 0.664). Using majority human judgments as the refer- ence, the auditor achieved 86.7% three-class accu- racy (91/105), with agreement on 31/35 excluded, 31/35 flagged-but-retained, and 29/35 unflagged turns. Collapsing both retained classes yielded 95.2% binary filtering accuracy (100/105): four auditor-excluded turns were judged retainable and one auditor-retained turn was judged excludable. These results suggest that Stage 4 can distinguish concerns that warrant removal from those that need not discard an otherwise usable response. After audit filtering, the paired samples contained 111 KBRD and 80 UniCRS users. The aspect- entropy improvement persisted for KBRD (∆= +0.265 and +0.280) and UniCRS (∆= +0.330 and +0.369) under GPT-4o-mini and DeepSeek- chat, respectively (all p < 0.001). Including all technically completed conversations also yielded positive effects (+0.275 to +0.560; all p < 0.001).
