A.1 FRAME-SLICED GILBERT ORDER A generalized Hilbert curve, or Gilbert curve, visits every cell of a rectangle of arbitrary size exactly once and moves to an adjacent cell at every step. Jenga (Zhang et al., 2025c) partitions video tokens along such a curve for block-sparse attention, and we adopt the frame-sliced variant of its released implementation. Let the visual latent contain F frames of H × W tokens, so it holds M = FHW tokens. Token p = (f, m, n) lies in frame f ∈{0, . . . , F −1}, row m, and column n. The order assigns each token a rank: γ(f, m, n) = fHW + gH,W (ϕf(m, n)) . (9) Here gH,W assigns the ranks 1, . . . , HW along the two-dimensional Gilbert curve of an H × W grid, which starts and ends at the two corners of one longer side. The map ϕf reflects the rows, the columns, or both, so that frame f starts at the corner nearest to the end of frame f −1. The first map ϕ0 is the identity, and the offset fHW keeps every frame contiguous. Intuitively, the curve fills one frame, steps to the same spatial position in the next frame, and fills that frame in a reflected direction. Every step therefore moves to a neighboring token, including the step across a frame boundary. Figure 6 contrasts this order with the raster order. Contiguous runs of this order are compact patches.
