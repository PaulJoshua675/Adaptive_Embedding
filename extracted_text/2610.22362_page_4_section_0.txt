CoDA: Co-Evolution of Data Engines and Action Policies Episodic tier: construction and rollout evidence. The append-only trace store B records each scene pro- gram g, its verifier ν, and summaries of the genera- tion trace, including violated constraints and verification outcomes. For deployed environments, it also records the success rate bp(s) over N rollouts and the learn- ing progress LV(s) obtained from training [49]. The vision–language critic C uses scene and rollout evidence to produce a failure diagnosis ψ = (δ, p, c, e), where δ∈{TASK, SPATIAL, ATTRIBUTE, EXECUTION} identifies the attributed factor, p the trajectory phase, c a natural- language description of the cause, and e the supporting evidence. These diagnoses are stored alongside the corre- sponding traces. The additional label EXECUTION covers motor-execution failures. Semantic tier: reusable difficulty modes. Episodic records are clustered online into difficulty modes Kj using the factor–phase pair (δ, p) and similarity between diagnosed causes. Each mode captures a recurring failure condition that can arise across tasks and policy backbones, such as “an occluder adjacent to the target on the approach side”. We characterize each mode by two quantities, hardness and importance. Hardness is measured by the policy’s success rate bpj, with lower rates indicating greater difficulty. Importance wj is a running score updated from mode-level learning progress, mea- sured as the change in policy success rate on that mode before and after training [49]. These two quantities reflect different properties of a mode. A mode can re- main hard even when training on it yields little learning progress, or become easy after substantial improvement. Each mode also carries a validation status determined by control batches (§3.3), and only validated modes are compiled into procedural memory. Procedural tier: generation and training programs. The two consumers of memory improve differently. The data engine G adapts in context by retrieving and exe- cuting programs between rounds, while the action policy πθ learns through gradient updates and acts from obser- vations. We therefore compile the same validated mode into two complementary programs: • Generation programs, stored in Ω, encode how to reproduce and vary the diagnosed scene conditions. They retain the parent scene program, validated vari- ants along one factor, compilation-check outcomes, and the required relational structure expressed over symbolic object roles. • Training programs, stored in Φ, specify how to address the policy failure through subgoal decompositions, po- tentials, and learning tools. They retain the diagnosed phase and evidence of which training programs im- proved policy performance. The generation compiler Rgrind produces the former, while Text2potential implements the training compiler Rhone (§3.4). During training, these programs evaluate subgoal completion and compute potentials that shape value targets. At deployment, the trained policy pre- dicts actions from observations and the task instruction without executing these programs. 3.3. Memory-Guided Scene Generation At each round, memory guides which difficulty modes to revisit and how to instantiate them in new environments. We allocate the generation budget across difficulty modes using an upper-confidence rule based on their hardness and observed learning progress. An ε-fraction of the budget is reserved for novel environments, while a separate replay fraction revisits previously encountered modes to detect performance regressions. A validated generation program is added to the pool Ωwhen the policy’s success rate on its variants falls within the designated success band and is removed when success rate is outside that band. For each selected mode, the engine retrieves a generation program specifying the scene conditions to reproduce and the factor to vary. Generation programs express scene conditions in terms of symbolic object roles, allowing the engine to reuse them across task families by assigning those roles to objects in a new scene (Appendix A). For each selected candidate difficulty mode linked to a scene factor, the engine generates a targeted batch that varies the identified factor and a control batch that varies a different factor. After verification checks, we evaluate the current policy on both batches. If the targeted variants yield a lower policy success rate than the control variants, the proposed difficulty mode is com- piled into reusable generation and training programs. The policy learns from its rollouts using the success predicate ν and the potential-based feedback supplied by Text2potential (§3.4). A scripted planner also attempts each valid variant, and trajectories that satisfy ν are retained as demonstrations Dt, providing additional imitation supervision when available. 3.4. Text2potential The success predicate ν determines whether a gener- ated task is complete, but provides little feedback on intermediate progress. This makes value learning diffi- cult when success requires a long sequence of actions. Text2potential converts a validated failure diagnosis into subgoals and potentials over simulator states. These po- tentials provide dense feedback for value learning (§3.5). Prior language-based reward synthesis provides dense training feedback, but its iterative refinement relies on humans to describe policy failures observed in rollouts, and can produce rewards that favor behavior other than task completion [42]. Text2potential instead uses a critic to diagnose failures from the policy’s own rollouts and compiles these diagnoses into a potential function that shapes the value critic’s training targets, providing dense feedback without replacing the original task reward.
