Modern agents select tools from registries, but exposing entire registries incurs high token costs and can impair selection as they grow. We present TRACER, a retrieval front-end that indexes observed tool-call sequences in a trie and ranks continuations using a learned blend of empirical and contextual priors. On a mixed retail-and-telecom τ 2 environment, TRACER improves GPT-5.4’s tool-selection accuracy by 4.7 points while reducing input tokens per step by 80%, exposing just 5 of 128 tools. In au- tonomous full-turn execution, it matches GPT- 5.4’s full-registry task success with 61% fewer input tokens per step. Across six environ- ments and four models, TRACER improves tool- selection accuracy over full-registry selection in 21 of 24 model-environment pairs, with aver- age gains of 6 to 10 points; the three exceptions trail by at most 2.2. We analyze failures in both regimes to identify directions for future work. 1
