A per-token MLP achieves verification accuracy comparable to OursH, suggesting that the back- bone’s contextualized token features already provide useful convergence cues. Training on-policy, where the head sees the truncated history during training as well, is no better even though these measurements are taken on that distribution. The latent-based signals support our training-free design. Redundancy is available at the first step but becomes less useful as a token is refined, while stability is stronger afterwards yet undefined at the first step. Our training-free verifier therefore uses redundancy at the first step and stability after, at the cost of two thresholds. Motion-based scheduling stays below redundancy at every step and tolerance. The head leads every latent signal throughout. C.2 ADDITIONAL ABLATION ON OUR HEAD We evaluate head variants under the same Lingbot-World 2.0 benchmark setting, with thresholds cal- ibrated to obtain comparable mean denoising steps. As shown in Table B, training on commitment rollouts or replacing the convolutional head with a per-token MLP yields similar generation quality and throughput. Spatiotemporal smoothing encourages coherent acceptance decisions across neigh- boring tokens and achieves lower Drift and higher imaging quality, supporting more stable rollouts. C.3 ADDITIONAL QUANTITATIVE RESULTS We report all quality and semantic dimensions for LongLive in Tables C and D for 5-second videos using VBench, and Tables E and F for 30-second videos using VBench-Long. We compare uniform sampling with one, two, three, and four denoising steps against Speculative Forcing at ϵ = 0.24 and ϵ = 0.28. We additionally implement the step allocation policy of Surprise Forcing (Shi et al., 2026) on LongLive, retaining LongLive’s original memory management. For each chunk, the policy ranks the maximum cosine distance between adjacent latent frames of the first clean estimate against the preceding 20 chunks. Chunks below the rank threshold of 0.4 use only the first and fourth denoising steps, while the others follow the full schedule. We retain the published settings, including full-step generation for the first five chunks, which cover five of the seven chunks in 5-second videos.
