State-covariant hard rebalancing provides a complementary diagnostic when momentum-buffer transformations are available. F.6 Branched Checkpoint-Surgery Resume Motivation. The 123.8M scale replication in Appendix G.15 measures sensitivity to orbit imbalance at initialization. We now test a checkpoint-resume setting in which weights are edited and training continues with a fresh optimizer. For each seed, one shared phase-1 checkpoint is cloned into four continuation branches, isolating the effect of the edit and subsequent rebalancing. Design. For each of the seven paired seeds, we train the same RMSNorm, RoPE, SwiGLU, and GQA T5 SentencePiece model used in the 123.8M scale replication for 10K normal WikiText-103 AdamW steps. We then reset the optimizer and branch into four 5K-step resumes: control, surgery without SAGGS, surgery with hard SAGGS, and surgery with Soft SAGGS. The non-control branches receive an exact all-pairs λ = 0.2 orbit surgery at the handoff; the SAGGS branches then rebalance Q–K, V–O, and SwiGLU value–output pairs every 50 steps. Hard SAGGS applies an initial exact rebalance at the handoff; Soft SAGGS applies α = 0.5 at the handoff and during the resume. The optimizer state is deliberately not carried across the handoff. Each phase uses a fresh cosine schedule with 10% warmup: 1K steps in phase 1 and 500 steps in phase 2. The base learning rate, weight decay, batch size, context length, and precision match the 123.8M scale replication. The handoff uses an exact symmetry edit: Across all edited branches, the absolute validation-loss change after surgery and any initial rebalance is below 7.4 × 10−5. These changes are floating-point residuals of the full handoff operation. The optimizer-state-reset resume exposes a large coordinate effect. Surgery with no SAGGS reaches 24.62 ± 0.58 PPL, while the control resume reaches 20.84 ± 0.25 PPL, a paired degradation of +3.775 ± 0.416 PPL with all seven seeds in the same direction, with p+ sf = 0.0078. Hard SAGGS and Soft SAGGS recover the final validation PPL closely: Their final PPLs are 20.85 ± 0.25 and 20.85 ± 0.25, only 0.010 and 0.009 PPL above the control resume, respectively. The final Q–K log-ratio makes the mechanism visible: No-SAGGS surgery ends near 6.14, whereas both SAGGS variants end below 1.7 × 10−4. The experiment demonstrates protection in a controlled checkpoint-resume workflow with a deliberately introduced symmetry-orbit displacement and an optimizer-state reset. The comparison measures recovery of the final PPL; identical resumed trajectories require canonicalizing both checkpoints (Corollary 4).
