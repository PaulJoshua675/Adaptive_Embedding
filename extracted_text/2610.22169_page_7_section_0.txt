The lenses diverge by several nats, although LEXFRAME[0] uses no more of the context than they do; transport through the model’s own layers hence matters more than a fitted map. The gap is widest in SmolLM3, whose output frame is also its input frame: at the embedding its logit lens names the input to- ken itself for 0.83 of the positions, against 0.00 in OLMo3. Conversely, a layer skip, which feeds a layer’s states to the last four layers with attention but without transport, remains at 7.00 and 2.59 nats: a reading needs both transport and attention. In Table 1, LEXFRAME[1] names the model’s top token for 0.86 and 0.86 of the states, and for nine tenths of them from layer 18 and 17 on, whereas the lenses reach one half only one or two layers before the output. LEXFRAME[1] does not merely reproduce the output: at the embedding, where most of the computation remains, it agrees on only 0.42 and 0.39. Its calibration error against the ac- tual next token, 0.027 and 0.018, is close to that of the model itself, 0.018 and 0.023, so its proba- bilities can be interpreted as a prediction in their own right, unlike those of the lenses. Counted back from the last layer, LEXFRAME[1] keeps the model’s top token over 0.82 and 0.81 of the layers, a lens over at most 0.11. An early exit at the first layer whose reading is confident, with the threshold set on held-out sequences for 0.95 agreement with the model, would therefore skip 0.45 and 0.42 of the layers with LEXFRAME[1] but at most 0.04 with a lens, whose confidence does not track whether it is right.
