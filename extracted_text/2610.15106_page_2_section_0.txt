Figure 1: Overview of RELAGRAM. A CVRP instance is encoded as edge-token input x; the answer state y and workspace z start from fixed vectors. The shared transformer first updates z from x+y+z (red), then updates y from y +z (blue). Both states (y, z) are carried forward (magenta) in a recursive loop. A linear head maps yt to edge logits, which are decoded into a solution. We introduce RELAGRAM, a novel application of the latent recursion idea to graph combinatorial optimization, and demonstrate it on CVRP. After brief training, a 0.53M model reaches a 13.4% greedy gap on CVRP-100, reducing it to 8.3% under a 256-candidate search. On CVRP-1000, RELAGRAM achieves gaps of 29.9% and 21.7% under greedy and 256-candidate decoding, respectively. Notably, it continues to improve beyond its trained depth: on CVRP-1000 it achieves its best greedy gap of 28.45% at depth ten — six stages deeper than seen during training. 2
