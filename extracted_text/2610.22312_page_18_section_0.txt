A.2 Instruction execution and entity state Controlled language tasks have long connected narrated events to changing worlds. bAbI generates questions about locations, possessions, and other facts after sequences of events (Weston et al., 2015). SCONE pairs an initial world with context-dependent instructions in three executable domains; predicted logical forms are evaluated by the world they produce (Long et al., 2016). Both use simulation to make consequences checkable, while placing different demands on the predicted answer. Procedural-text research studies changes that may be implicit in natural descriptions. Neural Process Net- works model actions as operators that transform learned entity states (Bosselut et al., 2018). ProPara anno- tates entity existence and location as a process unfolds (Dalvi et al., 2018), and OpenPI extends procedural state changes to open-vocabulary entities, attributes, and before/after values (Tandon et al., 2020). TRIP associates multi-sentence physical stories with state annotations that support tiered evaluation of plausibility and its underlying evidence (Storks et al., 2021). This line asks models to infer consequences from ordinary language; Grid-Bench makes the transition rules explicit and varies the spatial structures on which they act. Entity-tracking studies isolate update and retention more directly. Box-transfer tasks query container con- tents after PUT, REMOVE, and MOVE operations and distinguish changed from unchanged states (Kim and Schuster, 2023). Subsequent work uses behavioral tests, probes, and interventions to study how models process these changes (Tang et al., 2026). Related probing work recovers and manipulates entity-state infor- mation in models trained on Alchemy instructions and TextWorld transcripts (Li et al., 2021). These studies motivate examining both final values that differ from the initial world and those that match it when a model returns an entire state. The use of evolving histories also appears in social and personalized interaction. SocialMaze evaluates reasoning across social settings with changing interactions and uncertain information (Xu et al., 2025d); PersonaConvBench evaluates personalized predictions and generation from conversation histories (Li et al., 2025b). Memory Retrieval for Changing Preferences selects historical turns as evidence of a user’s current preferences (Qin et al., 2026), while RaMem preserves temporal and participant context when retrieving past experiences (Yang et al., 2026b). The scope of retained information matters as well: shared-state agents can misapply information from one user’s interaction to another (Yang et al., 2026a). For procedural memory, HyperSkill links reusable skills and subtask steps through the trajectories in which they occurred (Xu et al., 2026a). This literature examines which past information remains useful and how it should be represented; spatial-state prediction provides explicit operations whose consequences can be checked at the cell level. A.3 Complete-state prediction Board games provide deterministic state transitions over long sequences. Language modeling of chess move records has been evaluated with piece-location and legal-move probes (Toshniwal et al., 2022); Othello-GPT work uses probes and interventions to study board representations learned from move transcripts (Li et al., 2023). These studies connect sequence modeling to an underlying board state through targeted queries or internal analyses. Two recent benchmarks make complete state recovery the evaluated output. Chess-World-Model predicts 75 categorical labels for board occupancy and auxiliary chess state at successive move prefixes, reporting both exact and labelwise accuracy (Walker and Lyons, 2026). The Alien Grid domain of the Complexity Ceiling Benchmark requests intermediate and final 3 × 3 grids after rotations, corner swaps, and shifts at increasing sequential depths (Chapra et al., 2026). SnorkelSpatial likewise applies translations and rotations to tex- tual boards and particles, then asks targeted questions about the transformed result (Vishwakarma, 2025). Grid-Bench evaluates complete-state prediction across eight operation families and two-, three-, and four- dimensional grids.
