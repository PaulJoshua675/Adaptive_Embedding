Clustering is a method used to divide a dataset into meaningful groups called clusters (Caraka et al., 2020). Objects within the same cluster ex- hibit homogeneity while differing significantly from objects in other clusters. Clustering methods are frequently used for automatic classification in di- verse fields, including medical sciences, engineering, and socio-economic anal- ysis (Han et al., 2011). One notable socio-economic application of clustering is geo-demographic Analysis (GDA), a specialised area within Geographic Information Systems (GIS) that utilises spatial analysis techniques to iden- tify and categorise demographic characteristics of specific regions (Paez et al., 2011). Such an analysis provides essential support for the development of tar- geted socio-economic policies (Wijayanto and Purwarianti, 2014a; Wijayanto et al., 2016). The application of clustering techniques within GDA is particu- larly critical for policy making, as it allows efficient and precise assessments of regional characteristics, thus helping decision-makers who otherwise would spend considerable effort evaluating each region individually (Spiezia and Weiler, 2007). A widely used clustering method in GDA is fuzzy clustering, notably the Fuzzy C-Means (FCM) algorithm (Kwon, 1998). FCM is valued for its sim- plicity and improved performance over traditional hard clustering methods such as K-Means and Hard C-Means (Singh et al., 2013). Based on FCM, Mason and Jacobson (2007) introduced fuzzy geographic weighted clustering (FGWC), a spatially aware variant of FCM that incorporates geographi- cal information into the clustering process, enabling geographically coherent clusters (Wijayanto et al., 2016). However, despite its advantages, both FCM and FGWC are sensitive to the initial selection of cluster centroids, leading to suboptimal or unstable so- lutions (Izakian and Abraham, 2011; Wijayanto et al., 2016). This drawback requires enhancements to achieve globally optimal clustering results that bet- ter represent the underlying spatial-demographic structure of the data (Wi- jayanto et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2008; Nayak et al., 2014).
