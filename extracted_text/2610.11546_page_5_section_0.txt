This study addresses the FEMU problem within a two-stage, modal-property-based updating framework: rather than directly matching time or frequency domain structural responses, the updating process minimizes discrepancies in modal properties obtained through eigenvalue analysis, substantially reducing the computational burden of repeated finite element evaluations while retaining the dynamic information essential for reliable parameter identification. The present study focuses on this second-stage optimization problem and the proposed DRL-DCO solver, without addressing the preceding system identification procedure. 2.2 Evolutionary Optimization as a Controlled Stochastic Process Let χ(t) = {x(t) 1 , x(t) 2 , . . . , x(t) Ns} denote a population of Ns candidate solutions at generation t, where each solution x(t) k ∈RNd is an Nd-dimensional vector (k = 1, . . . , Ns). In a general evolutionary optimization framework, the population evolves according to a transformation: χ(t+1) = Te  χ(t); θ  where the operator Te(·) represents the evolutionary operations and effects, including mutation, re- combination, selection, and adaptation mechanisms, and θ denotes the hyperparameters governing them. In conventional EAs, Te(·) and θ are fully specified before the optimization begins. That is, all hyperparameters governing mutation strength, recombination rates, step sizes, and population size remain fixed or are adjusted through predefined heuristic rules. As a result, the search dynamics are largely decoupled from the actual landscape encountered during optimization. In this study, the DRL-DCO algorithm instead reformulates this evolutionary process as a controlled stochastic system: χ(t+1) = Ts  χ(t) a(t) , a(t) = π  s(t) where a(t) denotes an action (control) vector applied at generation t, and s(t) is the state observed by the agent, constructed from χ(t). The action vector explicitly parameterizes the evolution operator, allowing its behavior to change dynamically as optimization progresses. Under this formulation, the operator Ts(·) plays the role of the stochastic system dynamics; the population χ(t) represents the underlying system state; and the action vector a(t) regulates exploration intensity, exploitation strength, and structural adaptation of the search. This controlled process view provides a natural bridge between evolutionary optimization and optimal control theory, and forms the basis for the DRL core mechanism introduced in Section 2.3, which learns the control policy π generating a(t). 2.3 Markov Decision Process Formulation for the Optimization To enable adaptive, state-dependent control of the evolutionary search process, the optimization process introduced in Section 2.2 is formalized as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) [37]. This formulation provides a mathematically principled mechanism for learning an optimal control policy that governs the evolution operator based on the observed search state. The MDP is defined by a state space, an action space, transition dynamics (inherited from the population evolution operator Ts(·) in Section 2.2), and a reward function, each specified for the DRL-DCO algorithm in the following subsections. The optimization proceeds over successive generations t, with the FEMU (or general optimization objective function) f (θ) providing the basis for evaluating population fitness and constructing the reward signal at each generation. 2.3.1 State Space The state at generation t, denoted s(t), is constructed to characterize both the current search quality and the evolutionary dynamics of the population.
