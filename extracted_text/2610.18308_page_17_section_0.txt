Grounding (3-class): • Evidence-grounded: the claim is anchored to a specific quotation or behavioral pattern in the interview. • Generic: the claim is consistent with the in- terview but could apply to many users; the evidence is weak or non-specific. • Cliche/stereotype: the claim performs a rec- ognizable persona archetype not specifically attested in the user’s data. Numeric ratings (1–5): • Consistency: how consistent the claim is with the user’s behavioral profile (1 = inconsistent, 5 = highly consistent). • Usefulness: how useful the claim would be for conditioning a simulator (1 = not useful, 5 = highly useful). • Overreach: how much the claim overshoots the available evidence (1 = no overreach, 5 = severe overreach). • Confidence: the annotator’s confidence in their judgment (1 = uncertain, 5 = highly con- fident). Mean ratings across all 315 annotations are 3.77 (consistency), 3.61 (usefulness), 1.70 (overreach), and 3.00 (confidence). F.3 Annotator Pool and Procedure Three annotators (graduate students with prior NLP annotation experience) independently rated claims sampled at random from the 582 enriched-attribute claims of the FULL profiles. We report the 105 claims, drawn from 45 users and covering all six attribute types, that were rated by all three anno- tators (315 annotations). Annotators worked in- dependently without discussion of disagreements; no calibration round was performed prior to anno- tation, a methodological choice we identify as a limitation (see Limitations).
