Recommender systems often need to balance multiple conflicting objectives. Controllable Multi-Objective Recommendation (CMOR) enables dynamic adjustment of objective trade-offs at inference time without retraining, adapting to shifting user or system re- quirements. Existing CMOR approaches typically rely on output- level reranking or parameter-generating modules, which either limit the scope of feasible trade-offs or incur substantial training and inference cost. Model merging provides a promising alterna- tive by reusing multiple objective-specific models via parameter combination. However, its direct application to recommender sys- tems is challenging due to the lack of a shared linear regime and cross-objective interference. To address these issues, we propose RecSoup, a lightweight two-stage approach for effective CMOR via specialized model merging. RecSoup first mitigates parame- ter interference across objectives through a pruning strategy that jointly considers objective-specific importance and cross-objective relevance. It then performs adaptive parameter merging via a learn- able preference-to-coefficient mapping, allowing dynamic trade-off control at inference time without joint retraining or additional gen- erative modules. Theoretically, we prove that RecSoup outperforms the parameter generation paradigm in effectiveness and efficiency, as it can fully navigate the Pareto front without smoothness con- straints. Extensive experiments on multiple benchmarks demon- strate that RecSoup achieves improved Pareto front quality and ef- fective controllability while delivers 165–228× faster inference-time latency than parameter generation approaches.
