Researchers often remember an idea before they remember a title: a neighborhood with uncertain boundaries, a circuit transformation with a particular constraint, or an assistant that recognizes when a complex task changes. Recovering relevant prior work from such descriptions is difficult because vocabulary, application setting, and the level of technical detail can differ substantially between a query and a paper. A useful assistant must retrieve candidates, establish their bibliographic identities, and distinguish a close conceptual match from work that merely shares a topic. LLM-based research systems provide useful ways to explore and synthesize sources. STORM organizes research into multi-perspective information gathering and article outlining [3], Co-STORM supports interactive exploration [4], and Agent Laboratory connects literature review with experimentation and reporting [5]. Our focus is narrower: given an idea, which five identifiable papers are most likely to match it, and what evidence explains their selection? This objective is not equivalent to the quality of a general research report. Preprint. arXiv:2610.20857v1 [cs.IR] 14 Oct 2026
