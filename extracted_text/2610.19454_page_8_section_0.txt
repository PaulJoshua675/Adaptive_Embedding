5.1 Experimental Setup Experimental environment: Experiments use the AuctionNet simulator [14] with its official period length and traffic volume. The environment contains 48 ad units that can purchase traffic. We control 12 units organized into 3 accounts, each containing 4 units, all using Abid as the fixed underlying bidder. We use two environments, AuctionNet (Sparse) and AuctionNet (General). The former uses the public simulation environment, where each controlled account’s initial budget is 1/12 of the total budget of all units. For AuctionNet (General), we calibrate the pValue generation scale, ad-unit budgets, and CPA constraints using the public General dataset, while keeping the other auction mechanisms unchanged. Detailed configurations are provided in Appendix D. Evaluation metrics: Delivery performance is measured using ADV, CPA, and a composite score. For ADV, which uses tCPA-weighted predicted conversion values [8], we report the per-period average of total ADV across all controlled accounts within the evaluation scope. CPA is total spend divided by total conversions over the same evaluation scope [9]. The invalid output rate IR is the fraction of responses that violate the required format or action specifications, such as parsing failures, incorrect ad-unit decision counts, or tCPA multipliers outside the allowed set. Each invalid response is counted once. With No Operation under the same environment and budget as the reference, method m’s composite score is Scorem = ADVm ADV0 · CPA0 CPAm −IRm. (13) Here, subscript 0 denotes No Operation, whose score is 1. Invalid output rates are reported as percentages in tables and used as decimal fractions in the formula. Comparison methods: We use No Operation, Rule, and AdPilot* as advertising delivery baselines to compare delivery performance across different approaches to operational decision-making. No Operation makes no operational adjustments, while Rule adjusts budgets and tCPA using hand-crafted CPA-based rules. AdPilot* follows AdPilot’s Reflector–Actor workflow [2], where a single agent generates actions informed by delivery diagnosis. AdCoEvo (Fixed) retains all roles and the diagnostic workflow, with model parameters fixed at the offline GSPO checkpoint during evaluation. We also evaluate representative closed- and open-source foundation models [38–41]2 that directly perform advertising delivery tasks, comparing delivery performance, output validity, and operational overhead. Implementation and evaluation: AdCoEvo uses Qwen3-8B [42] with separate LoRA parameters [43] for each role. AdPilot* uses the same backbone and follows AdPilot’s post-training procedure [2], with SFT followed by GRPO. Its model parameters remain fixed during formal evaluation. We set the observation length to W = 12, with at most K = 8 tCPA adjustments per ad unit per period. Each experimental group uses five independent market sequences of 50 consecutive delivery periods, matched across methods. These sequences were not used for SFT, offline reinforcement learning, or selecting the default parameters. AdCoEvo independently starts each sequence from the same offline GSPO checkpoint. AuctionNet regenerates market conditions, including traffic and predicted conversion probabilities, each period, while role policies and diagnostic guidance carry over within the sequence. 5.2 Overall Delivery Performance We first examine whether AdCoEvo can achieve better delivery outcomes than fixed operational rules derived from human experience.
