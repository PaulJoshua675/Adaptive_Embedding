A widely held hypothesis is that the brain’s efficiency and learning capabilities arise from its ability to represent temporal information (Maass, 1997b; Thorpe et al., 2001; Olshausen and Field, 2006; Eshraghian et al., 2023; Habashy et al., 2024). This hypothesis has motivated the development of Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) (Neftci et al., 2019), which emulate spike-based neural activity. However, spike-based dynamics do not by themselves specify how time is represented: a network may process signals over time while still failing to represent time as a computational variable. This raises a central question: which mechanisms in neural networks explicitly represent time, and how does this affect performance and computational/energetic efficiency? In this work, the degree of temporal representation of a neural network is determined solely by the temporal or non-temporal nature of its functions. A function is said to be temporal if and only if it explicitly manipulates time as a computational variable; otherwise, it is non-temporal. We consider four function types: (i) encoding functions, mapping raw data to network input variables; (ii) activation functions, defining forward computations; (iii) decoding functions, mapping outputs to task targets via the loss; and (iv) learning functions, defining gradient-based parameter updates. Any of the network’s functions may be temporal or non-temporal, depending on whether time explicitly appears as a variable in their computation. For instance, a spike is a binary, non-temporal variable indicating event occurrence. Accordingly, SNN activation functions that operate only on binary spikes (or spike counts) are non-temporal under our definition, even though the network evolves over time. Temporal representations arise only when functions explicitly manipulate temporal variables such as time stamps, delays, or temporal intervals, e.g., in spike-time activations or time-based decoding. The degree of temporal representation ranges from non-temporal networks, where none of the functions manipulate time, to fully temporal networks, where all functions explicitly operate on temporal variables.
