Large Language Models (LLMs) are now core components of production software systems, powering applications from customer-service automation to healthcare diagnostics, code generation, and educational platforms [7, 30]. Despite extensive alignment through reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) [31] and constitutional AI [6], contemporary LLMs remain vulnerable to adversarial prompts that bypass safety guardrails and elicit harmful content. As these models are integrated into safety- and privacy-sensitive software pipelines, software engineers face an urgent practical question: how can the security of an LLM-based system be evaluated rigorously, automatically, and reproducibly before deployment?
