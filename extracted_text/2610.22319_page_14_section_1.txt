Minecraft has long been a testbed for open-ended agents. MineRL released human demonstrations and ran a competition on sample-efficient learning of the diamond task (Guss et al., 2019b; Guss et al., 2019a). Video PreTraining learned keyboard and mouse control from online videos and crafted diamond tools (Baker et al., 2022), DreamerV3 collected diamonds from scratch with reinforcement learning (Hafner et al., 2025), and STEVE-1 followed short-horizon open-ended text instructions (Lifshitz et al., 2023). MineDojo called defeating the Ender Dragon, starting barehanded in a fresh world, one of its moonshot goals (Fan et al., 2022). SIMA and SIMA 2 act from pixels with a keyboard and mouse across many 3D games, and SIMA 2 was also tested on held-out Minecraft tasks (SIMA Team, 2024; SIMA Team, 2025). These systems learn their policies from large amounts of gameplay data or experience. Another line of work uses language models as planners or programmers, building on interleaved reasoning and tool use (Yao et al., 2023) and code as policy (Liang et al., 2023). Voyager wrote Mineflayer programs into a growing skill library under an automatic curriculum (Wang et al., 2024). DEPS, GITM, JARVIS-1, Plan4MC, and Optimus-1 added Minecraft-specific planners, knowledge, memory, or learned skills (Wang et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024). Multi-agent studies examined coordination and social behavior in games, including Minecraft (Gong et al., 2024; Altera.AL et al., 2024; White et al., 2025). Reported long-horizon results in this line center on the Overworld technology tree, such as obtaining diamond tools. Two community projects have recently reported end-to-end dragon kills: a single Claude Opus 5 agent calling 37 high-level Minecraft tools on Normal difficulty (AI Bengineering, 2026), and a planner paired with an action-selection model on a fixed speedrun seed in Peaceful difficulty (Malde, 2026). Our setting differs mainly in what the agents were given. Four general-purpose coding agents received the standard Mineflayer library, as published, with its pathfinding plugin, a one-paragraph objective, and their general instructions. We added nothing on top: no instructions about the game, no curriculum, no skill library, no human-written tools, and no route. Every program they ran, they wrote themselves. They played a standard survival server on Easy from a random seed and coordinated through chat and shared notes. 14
