We train for 25,000 optimization steps using four H200 GPUs. The per-device batch size is four and gradients are accumulated for four steps, giving a total batch size of 64. We use AdamW with \b e ta _1=0. 9, \b e ta _2=0. 95, \ e psilon =10^{-8}, and weight decay 10^{-8} and learning rate of 1 \ times 10^{-5} in this experiment. We use a cosine schedule with 1,500 warm-up steps and a final learning rate of 5 \ times 10^{-7}. Training is performed in bfloat16 with FlashAttention-2 and ordinary distributed data parallelism. Evaluation Details. We evaluate MaLoW (Q3.5-2B-MLP) on the four WidowX Bridge tasks in SimplerEnv: Stack Green Block on Yellow Block, Put Carrot on Plate, Put Spoon on Towel, and Put Eggplant in Yellow Basket. Each task contains 24 evaluation episodes, for a total of 96 episodes. Evaluation is conducted in a fully interactive streaming setting. At environment step zero, no historical context is available, and the model exactly reproduces the Q3.5-2B-MLP policy. At each subsequent step, completed two-frame blocks and the corresponding actions are permanently incorporated into the cached DeltaNet state mt and updated into the global sematic st. If the history ends with an unpaired frame, we duplicate it to form a provisional block and generate the current adapter using a temporary copy of the recurrent state. At the next step, we discard this temporary update and incorporate the completed two-frame block into the persistent state. The ht state is reset at each episode boundary. The policy predicts four-action chunks and applies action ensembling over a horizon of four steps. Because ensembling may cause the action executed by the environment to differ from the most recent raw model prediction, we feed the normalized executed action back to the policy server. This executed action, rather than the unensembled model prediction, is paired with the corresponding observation when constructing subsequent history blocks. Evaluation Details. For the evaluation on the OVO-bench, we follow the example used in lmms-eval (Zhang et al., 2025a), and for the evaluation on the StreamingBench we follow the example used in the official repository. For a question associated with video prefix [0, T], the interval [0, T −2] is treated as historical context and sampled at 1 fps for the MaLoW. The base VLM receives the question together with two frames sampled from the current observation [T −2, T]. For OVO-Bench, we evaluate all backward, real-time, and forward splits. Backward and real-time questions are scored by accuracy. For the forward split, repetitive-event counting is scored by exact count matching, while sequential-step recognition and contextual-readiness recognition are scored using exact accuracy. We follow the benchmark’s macro-averaging protocol across tasks for each split, and we further present the macro-averaging number over 3 splits. For StreamingBench, we evaluate the Real, Omni, Sequential-QA, and Proactive subsets base on how the benchmark annotations are organized. Real, Omni, and Sequential-QA are scored by accuracy. Following the original protocol, Sequential-QA includes the gold question–answer history from earlier timestamps in the same video. For Proactive evaluation, the model is queried once per second until four seconds after the annotated trigger time.
