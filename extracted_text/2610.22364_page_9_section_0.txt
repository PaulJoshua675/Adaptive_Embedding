Bold markers denote our chosen trajectory count, after which performance largely saturates. 4.4 ABLATIVE & QUALITATIVE STUDIES We ablate the key design choices of T2-Z in fig. 5 and table 3, including latent optimization, interac- tion evidence, latent parameterization, and alternating training. In fig. 5(a), we vary the learning-rate strategy. Our schedule performs best, validating both its coarse-to-fine form and learning-rate scale. Figure 5(b) varies test-time optimization from 10–60 steps; performance improves up to 40 steps and then saturates, motivating our default. Figure 5(c,d) ablate the number of test-time interaction tra- jectories. Performance largely saturates beyond the selected number of trajectories, indicating that additional interaction provides limited benefit. Recall that T2-Z uses only one test-time trajectory for 7 tasks, and two for 4 tasks, demonstrating that T2-Z can infer useful environment information with as few as one or two interactions. Additional ablations in table 3 study latent parameterization and training. All the ablated models underperform our model, supporting our alternating schedule and latent mechanism.
