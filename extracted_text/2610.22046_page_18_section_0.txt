The 8B comparison uses the five seed means and reports sample standard deviations over seeds in place of a bootstrap interval on the difference. Label corrections. The stored final labels mark some correct answers as incorrect. The mislabelled answers are yes/no answers given as their true/false synonyms, and answers to true-or-false questions whose reference answer is coded as a letter. The reported numbers use the stored labels, and the probes are trained on them. Correcting these labels changes the 8B capability-gap miss rate under the problem split, from 0.797 to 0.779, which is the largest change among the reported quantities. The 8B PIT miss rate moves from 0.815 to 0.800. The shares of the failure types move by at most 0.006, detection AUROC by at most +0.004, and the difference between PITs and near misses after controls by at most +0.005. The stored labels also mark as incorrect the answers that the grader could not parse. There are 2,853 such capability-gap and PIT traces at 4B and 2,994 at 8B. Removing these unparsed traces leaves capability gaps and PITs missed more often than near misses at both scales. Removing instead every problem whose reference answer is a yes/no answer or a letter removes 47% of the incorrect 4B traces. On the remaining traces, the interval of the 4B difference between PITs and near misses after controls on the problem split is [+0.009, +0.085], which contains the minimum effect. On all traces the interval is [+0.012, +0.064], which lies below the minimum effect. Seeds and intervals. The confidence probe, combined probe, value probe, and pointer probe each use five seeds, with new weights for each seed and a new problem split for each seed, except the pointer probe, whose problem split is the same for every seed. The final-label probe uses the same five seeds; its problem split is not recorded. The value probe, the final-label probe, and the pointer probe use the common set {42, 137, 271, 314, 577}. The combined probe also uses the common set; the confidence probe uses seeds 0 to 4. Read-site selection uses bootstrap intervals without a seed axis. The comparison of the confidence probe’s predicted correctness at the PIT step with other steps uses, for each trace, only the seeds that held it out. The failure-type classifier uses six repetitions of five-fold cross-validation grouped by problem, with the same folds for the classifier and every baseline; the margin is applied to the mean difference over the six repetitions, and each repetition has its own bootstrap interval (Appendix B.1).
