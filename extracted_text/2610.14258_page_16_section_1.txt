The question in our title has a short answer. Links that follow activity, the feature we borrowed from dendritic nanotubes, did not help our networks, while random links did, because in expectation they shrink noisy importance estimates. A biologically inspired mechanism for sharing plasticity signals between neurons thus turned out, in its only working form, to be a shrinkage estimator in disguise. Taking that seriously led to a simple intervention for regularization-based continual learning, shrinking importance estimates toward their mean, and to a test that separates two reasons it helps. On Permuted MNIST, for EWC with few samples it reduces variance, and its benefit vanishes as samples grow. For MAS it also corrects something that more samples do not remove, which we read as bias. The heteroscedastic empirical Bayes rule we tried shrinks the largest Fisher-type estimates most, and a log-scale rule is the natural next step. Importance estimates are estimates, and treating them as such adds almost no computation.
