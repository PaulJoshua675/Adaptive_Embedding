Thin bars in (b) show pointwise 95% task-bootstrap intervals (Section B.3). 5.3 ACCURACY, MODEL SIZE, AND INFERENCE COST PathFM combines the highest FPS skill among the compared models with a compact 126M-parameter network (Figure 4). With one evaluation per output block, it generates 32 paths in 51.6 ms on an NVIDIA L4, placing it on the observed accuracy–latency frontier for path forecasts. We compare the number of function evaluations in Section B.3 and sampling controls in Section C.3. Ensemble size and distillation. Figure 5 examines two ways to reduce the sampling budget in separate controlled studies. On all 100 fev-bench tasks, stratification improves marginal accuracy most at small ensemble sizes: 32-path stratified sampling achieves almost the same FEV skill as 256 iid paths. With one evaluation, the student approaches the ten-evaluation teacher; two evaluations slightly exceed its FEV skill. This shows the power of distillation for time series flow matching models, and the effectiveness of the parallel decoding distillation. Scaling to 300M parameters. We investigate scalability by comparing PathFM-126M with PathFM- 300M. Figure 6 tracks flow pretraining; the larger model reaches lower training and corpus-evaluation losses at the same number of updates. The larger model improves both marginal accuracy and trajectory quality (Figure 3 and section B.5). These gains come at roughly twice the inference latency for generating 32 paths on an NVIDIA L4 (Figure 10). The scaling curves suggest that larger models may continue to improve accuracy.
