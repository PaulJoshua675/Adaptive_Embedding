However, this subset contains only a small portion of 2,011 labels. 7 Conclusion and future works We present TAGHN, a heterogeneous graph reranker for auto- mated subject indexing that treats the controlled vocabulary as a structured graph rather than a flat label list. TAGHN scores each candidate over a per-document subgraph of Stage-1 candidates augmented with non-assignable BT/NT/RT context nodes, condi- tioning every part of message passing on the document through a per-relation edge-type gate and a three-way attention over target, neighbor, and document. Cross-encoder representations enter the same attention behind a gate initialized at zero. A decomposed scor- ing head combines graph-derived features with Stage-1 retrieval signals. On STW, an economics thesaurus of 6,198 descriptors under a low-resource regime, TAGHN reaches 0.380 macro F1, ahead of a fine-tuned cross-encoder (0.346), the XRTransformer (0.278), and Omikuji-Bonsai (0.220), while remaining competitive on micro F1 (0.487 vs. 0.517). The gain over the cross-encoder comes mainly from precision. TAGHN emits 5.65 descriptors per document against the cross-encoder’s 6.46, yet recovers more gold descriptors (4,645 vs. 4,039 true positives), with true and false positives at a near 1:1 ratio (4,645 to 4,759) against the cross-encoder’s 4,039 to 6,692. Predicting fewer labels while recovering more of them indicates that gold descriptors moved up the ranking. TAGHN wins per-document F1 in 66.3% of documents and reduces the indexer-visible failure to return nothing correct from 4.8% to 3.6%. The per-frequency view shows that TAGHN and the XRTransformer are near mirror images. The XRTransformer is strongest above 100 occurrences and scores zero on unseen labels by construction, while TAGHN concentrates its advantage in the 6 to 100 range, which contains 1, 391 of the 2, 011 test-gold descriptors. For future work, we would like to focus our efforts on raising the Stage-1 ceiling. It gives us the highest leverage, since 0.783 caps every downstream gain. We also plan to validate TAGHN on other vocabularies, at label scales of 20,000+ descriptors, and in the cross- lingual setting that our bilingual thesaurus supports but our English- only evaluation does not exercise. In addition, a more detailed ablation study, isolating the edge-type gate, neighbor attention, context nodes, and structural loss terms, remains to be carried out; the present evaluation removes only the cross-encoder channel. Finally, we will explore enriching the neighborhood through cross- vocabulary mappings. The relation edges among the thesaurus structure are unevenly distributed. Of the 6,198 active descriptors, from the table 1, only 28.3% have a narrower term. Tail descriptors are specific by nature and therefore sit at the leaves of the hierarchy with no children at all. This suggests that importing neighborhood structure could be useful for tail labels. Because the thesaurus is published as linked open data in SKOS, its descriptors can be aligned to concepts in other vocabularies through skos:exactMatch and skos:closeMatch links, and the neighborhoods those vocabularies provide can be transplanted onto the local graph. Ethics and privacy statement This work uses bibliographic metadata describing published works, not people, and involves no human subjects. The system is intended to propose descriptors for a professional indexer to accept or re- ject, not to index unattended: its accuracy is uneven across the vocabulary, weakest on the unseen descriptors, so unsupervised use would systematically under-apply the specific terms that make a collection navigable.
