Without rebalancing, the edit more than doubles the mean PPL of Lion, from 24.15 to 58.32, whereas the edited runs with Soft SAGGS reach 23.86. 5.3 Checkpoint Recovery and Diagnostics Training often resumes from a checkpoint with a fresh optimizer, for example when the checkpoint is imported without its optimizer state. To measure how much an imbalanced checkpoint degrades training in this setting, we train the 123.8M model for 10K steps with each of seven seeds, reset its optimizer, and clone the check- point into four 5K-step continuations: control, edited, edited with SAGGS, and edited with Soft SAGGS. The three edited branches receive the same exact all-pairs edit at λ = 0.2, and SAGGS and Soft SAGGS rebalance at the restart and every 50 steps thereafter. At restart, the edited checkpoints are indistinguishable from the original by their loss: The edit and any first rebalance change the validation loss by less than 7.4 × 10−5. Nevertheless, the edited run without rebalancing finishes 3.775±0.416 PPL worse than the control, with p+ sf = 0.0078 (Table 11). Hard and Soft SAGGS finish within 0.02 PPL of the control (Table 3), so rebalancing recovers almost all of the degradation caused by the edit. Instead of being reset, the AdamW moments can be kept and transformed along with the weights. On the 31M model, keeping and transforming the moments leaves a nonzero gap of 0.058 ± 0.017 PPL relative to a control that also keeps its optimizer state (Table 12), consistent with Theorem 2: Under a shared learning rate, transformed moments alone do not keep the two runs equivalent. Case studies on public checkpoints (Appendix I) show that pair imbalance also arises in ordinary training: In Pythia 410M, the query and key norms drift apart during pre-training by a factor of up to about 6 in deep layers. The case studies also test canonical restart on GPT-2 774M, fine-tuned from the released check- point and from an exact Q–K edit of this checkpoint: Canonicalizing both checkpoints at restart makes the two runs identical, whereas without canonicalization the edited run predicts a different top-1 token than the canonicalized runs at 7.3% of held-out positions after 200 updates.
