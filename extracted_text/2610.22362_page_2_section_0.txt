CoDA: Co-Evolution of Data Engines and Action Policies CoDA closes this loop with a heterogeneous memory shared by the data generator and the action policy. It records repairs needed to make scenes valid, together with policy failure diagnoses grounded in rollout evi- dence and localized to one generative factor and one trajectory phase. The memory tracks learning progress for recurring difficulty modes to guide subsequent gener- ation. This evidence is compiled into separate programs for scene generation and policy training, with the latter specifying subgoals and learning tools [38, 42]. As tasks involve longer sequences of dependent subtasks, sparse success signals can slow value learning, while de- signing effective dense rewards can require manual effort. We therefore introduce Text2potential, which compiles a failure diagnosis into a potential over the simulator state and uses it to densify value targets for action chunks [7, 15]. The resulting value estimates guide policy updates through weighted regression on the policy’s own rollouts, favoring action chunks with higher estimated value. Across four manipulation tasks, CoDA achieves 27.6% out-of-distribution success, compared with 5.3% for the supervised fine-tuning baseline. With the same budget for generating environments, a loop guided by per-task success rates achieves 18.1%, while open-loop variants yield 8.5%. Extending our loop to more rounds in- creases out-of-distribution success to 42.1%. Moreover, on LIBERO-Pro, training on source-task variants gener- ated by CoDA increases success from 25% to 84% on po- sition generalization and from 1% to 76% on task gener- alization. Finally, we observe that reusing memory across policy backbones transfers accumulated experience, en- abling a second backbone to increase out-of-distribution success from 3.8% to 21.1%. In summary, the contributions of our work are: • Closed-loop co-evolution. We introduce CoDA, a framework that co-evolves a simulation data engine with a trainable action policy. Failure diagnoses grounded in policy rollouts guide environment gener- ation and training, while measured learning progress determines which difficulties to revisit. • Shared heterogeneous memory. We introduce a memory shared by the generator and policy that combines episodic evidence, reusable difficulty modes, and procedural programs. The same diagnosis com- piles into a generation program that recreates the difficulty and a training program that helps the policy overcome it. Both programs are indexed by difficulty mode, making them reusable across tasks. • Dense supervision through Text2potential. Text2potential compiles failure diagnoses into po- tentials over simulator states that provide dense targets for learning the value of action chunks [15]. Reward shaping requires potential evaluations only at chunk endpoints and preserves the policies that maximize the original task return. • Improved out-of-distribution generalization. Across four manipulation task families, CoDA achieves 27.6% out-of-distribution success, compared with 5.3% after supervised fine-tuning and 18.1% for success- rate-guided generation using the same accepted- environment budget. On LIBERO-Pro, fifteen rounds of CoDA training increase π0.5’s success from 25% to 84% under position perturbations and from 1% to 76% under instruction perturbations. Reusing memory across policy backbones also improves OpenVLA- OFT’s out-of-distribution success from 3.8% to 21.1% without additional environment generation.
