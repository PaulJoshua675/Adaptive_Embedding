Multimodal generative models have made remarkable progress in recent years (Cao et al., 2025; Seedream et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026), producing visually compelling images conditioned on textual descriptions. Beyond visual fidelity (Wiedemer et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024b; Yang et al., 2025b), a central challenge lies in whether generated images faithfully follow complex instructions and reflect coherent world knowledge (Zhou et al., 2025; Jin et al., 2026; Deng et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025a; Huang et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026b). Motivated by this challenge, visual verifiers have emerged as a crucial component for assessing the correctness of generated results. By providing feedback on the alignment between images and textual descriptions, verifiers not only serve as evaluation tools, but also act as general critic models for inference- time scaling and reinforcement learning-based training. As generative models are increasingly applied to open-ended environments and reasoning-intensive tasks, the reliability and expressiveness of visual verifiers become a key bottleneck for advancing multimodal intelligence. Despite their importance, most existing visual verifiers (Zhang et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2025a; Xiong et al., 2025) adopt a binary judgment paradigm that produces only True/False decisions about whether an image satisfies a given prompt. Although simple and efficient, this design has several inherent limitations. First, binary supervision provides coarse feedback that fails to capture the partial correctness of generated samples. By assigning the same negative label to imperfect samples of varying quality, binary rewards obscure meaningful quality differences and weaken the within-group learning signals required by group-relative reinforcement learning. Moreover, the limited information conveyed by binary labels may encourage shortcut learning and increase susceptibility to reward hacking (Shao et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026c), as models may exploit superficial patterns to predict the expected labels without adequately reasoning about the e subtl compositional relationships among objects, attributes, and spatial constraints. To address these limitations, we propose the Decomposed Graded Verifier (OmniVerifier-C1), which automatically decomposes complex prompts or environmental rules into fine-grained sub-questions and performs structured reasoning over each component. It assigns continuous scores based on the proportion of satisfied sub-questions, capturing partial correctness and providing informative critique signals. Our ex- periments demonstrate that decomposition-based structured reasoning significantly improves the verifier’s out-of-domain multimodal critic capabilities. To train this verifier, we introduce a Decoupled RL training strategy that jointly optimizes sub-question-level binary judgment and global alignment scoring, using precise 0/1 feedback on individual sub-questions to develop strong fine-grained verification capabilities that support reliable global scoring. Moreover, our theoretical analysis shows that the decomposition- and-scoring paradigm better preserves the underlying true quality ordering among generated samples, providing finer-grained learning signals for group-relative reinforcement learning. Building upon this verifier, we further explore its role as a generative reward model for reinforcement learning across two types of tasks. First, we apply the verifier to traditional text-to-image alignment, where it provides fine-grained feedback that encourages generative models to satisfy detailed compositional relationship. Second, we extend our study to the more reasoning-intensive and challenging setting of structured generative world modeling, where generative models are used to simulate environments and reason over sequential states. In these rule-governed environments, we find that pairwise verification, which compares generated states against ground-truth states, provides more accurate and reliable critiques than pointwise verification. This observation suggests that the effectiveness of visual verification in rule- governed world-modeling scenarios critically depends on the availability of reference states for comparison. Extensive experiments across diverse tasks, such as Maze and Sudoku, demonstrate that the proposed verifier provides more reliable reward signals and substantially improves reinforcement learning performance. Our contributions can be summarized as follows: • We propose the Decomposed Graded Verifier, which automatically decomposes complex prompts or environmental rules into fine-grained sub-questions, performs structured reasoning over each component, and assigns continuous scores based on the proportion of satisfied sub-questions. Our experiments demonstrate that its structured outputs significantly improve out-of-domain multimodal critic capabilities.
