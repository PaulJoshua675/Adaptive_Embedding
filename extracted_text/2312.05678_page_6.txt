We offer a utility-based method for developing sampling plans that builds on this inference framework while integrating the SFP-risk assessments of Nkansah et al. (2017). This method adds to a literature of settings using Bayesian experimental design. A design is set of input parameter choices for an experiment; see a review in Chaloner and Verdinelli (1995). Bayesian experimental design suits settings involving binary, count, or categorical data where data collection is expensive, such as PMS. The loss determines the cost of a decision made from an experiment with respect to the latent truth. Chaloner and Verdinelli stressed the importance of constructing a loss matching experimental aims. In Section 4, we propose such a loss for PMS. Bayesian experimental design has been applied to a variety of problems using different losses or utilities, where a utility is an integration of the loss over a distribution of key parameters. Lookman et al. (2019), for example, used an expected improvement utility for materials discovery. This utility fits settings where the goal is to maximize a response: the best designs balance uncertainty with the prospect for response maximization. Lookman et al. aimed to identify materials maximizing the level of different physical attributes. This contrasts with PMS, where the objective is an effective deployment of corrective actions, which may not correspond with heightened SFP detection. Phillips et al. (2021) used a measure of the distance between the prior and posterior, called the Shannon information, for key parameters in nuclear physics experiments.
