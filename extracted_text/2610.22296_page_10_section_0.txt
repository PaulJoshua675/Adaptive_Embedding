In production, our detectors must not accept third-party watermarks, and our watermarked content must not be accepted by third-party detectors. We tested both directions against the publicly released zero-bit AudioSeal model [5], using all eight pair of models trained with the full method: (i) the audio watermarked by each of our embedders is passed to the AudioSeal detector, and (ii) the audio watermarked by the AudioSeal embedder is passed to each of our detectors. Because AudioSeal operates at 16 kHz, audio is resampled to 16 kHz at its input. We use AudioSeal’s default decision threshold of 0.5. For every one of the eight versions, and in both directions, not a single file is detected, so TNR is 100% for every pair. This agrees with the negligible interference that Petrov et al. [9] report for independently trained watermarks. 7. Robustness Analysis Maximizing robustness to signal-processing and codec attacks is out of scope, and none of our models are trained with attack augmentation. We ask a narrower question: does backward compatibility itself survive distortion? A deployed watermark is transmitted and edited before detection, so a framework that held only on clean audio would be of little practical use. We therefore test, rather than optimize for, robustness, re-running the evaluation under the DeepMark benchmark [29].
