We first review softmax cross-entropy, the standard training objective in sequential recommendation, and then describe entmax and the Fenchel–Young framework for sparse probability mappings. 3.1 Softmax and Cross-entropy Sequential recommendation models such as SASRec [16], BERT4Rec [38], and GRU4Rec [13] are typically trained using the cross-entropy (CE) loss, either over the full item catalog or with sampled negative items [18, 39]. Given a user interaction sequence, the model produces logits z ∈R|I|, where I is a set of items and each logit 𝑧𝑖represents the relevance score of item 𝑖. The objective is to distinguish the ground-truth next item from all other items in the catalog. Prior work has shown that softmax-based CE training substantially outperforms binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss in se- quential recommendation settings [18, 30, 42], and CE training has become the standard optimization objective for modern sequential recommenders.
