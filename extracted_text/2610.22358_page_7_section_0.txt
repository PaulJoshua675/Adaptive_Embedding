FPS selects each quantile model’s better independent or comonotone completion. 5.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP Unless stated otherwise, PathFM refers to the 126M model; we use PathFM-126M and PathFM-300M when comparing sizes. We evaluate marginal accuracy on fev-bench (Shchur et al., 2025) and TIME (Qiao et al., 2026). Baselines include TimesFM-3 (Jain & Sen, 2026), Chronos-2 (Ansari et al., 2025), Toto-1.0/2.0 (Cohen et al., 2025; Khwaja et al., 2026), and TiRex/TiRex-2 (Auer et al., 2025; Podest et al., 2026). Quantile models use explicit independent or comonotone completions, whereas Toto-1.0 and Sundial (Liu et al., 2025) retain their native sampled paths. GIFT-Eval results appear in Section B.5.1, and the full component comparisons in Section E.1. PathFM and all fps-bench forecasts use 32 paths per forecast. For PathFM, we use stratified Gaussian sources and one model evaluation per output block. For each quantile model, we evaluate independent and comonotone completions and report the higher aggregate skill. 5.2 RESULTS PathFM-300M and PathFM-126M achieve the two highest FPS scores among the compared models while remaining competitive on marginal accuracy (Figure 3). The 300M model ranks second on fev-bench and third on TIME; both sizes have lower TIME CRPS than Chronos-2. On fps-bench, both outperform TimesFM-3 and Chronos-2 even when each quantile baseline uses its better independent or comonotone completion. Both also improve on Sundial’s native sampled forecasts across all three benchmarks. TimesFM-3 leads both marginal benchmarks, but its lower FPS ranking illustrates why marginal accuracy alone is insufficient to assess forecast paths. Path-dependent scores. Figure 13 compares the six FPS components for PathFM and the three strongest external baselines. The clearest gains over TimesFM-3 (the strongest baseline) occur in scores for temporal changes, horizon totals, and maxima. These gains highlight the value of complete paths for downstream tasks that depend on temporal structure beyond marginal forecasts. Ablation studies.
