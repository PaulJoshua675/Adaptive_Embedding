B.6 ENERGY SPECTRA We compute the power spectrum of the global sea-level pressure and 10m wind fields as a function of spherical harmonic degree l, at five lead times, and average it over storms (Figures 18 and 19). This checks whether a model keeps the distribution of energy across spatial scales during the rollout rather than smoothing out small scales. At large scales all models follow the reference; they separate only at the smallest scales, shown from l = 150 in panels (b). In 2021–2023, FCN3 follows the ERA5 spectrum at all degrees and lead times, and GenCast has slightly less small-scale energy than ERA5. IFS-ENS has markedly more, at every lead time including 12 hours, consistent with its native grid being finer than the 0.25◦evaluation grid (Table 3). In 2025, HRES-fc0 itself has much more small-scale energy than ERA5, and the operational models follow HRES-fc0 rather than ERA5 at these scales. GenCast stays close to HRES-fc0 for both variables, slightly below it for wind. IFS-ENS matches HRES-fc0 for pressure and has more energy for wind. 24
