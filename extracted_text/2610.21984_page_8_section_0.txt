Removing the rule term substantially degrades lane deviation (from 0.0105 to 0.0150) and lowers the overall score, showing that explicit rule- aware shaping is important for route-consistent behavior. Remov- ing the comfort term leaves safety-related metrics relatively stable but worsens jerk (0.1538 vs. 0.1166) and increases latency, indicating that the comfort term is important for motion smoothness. The largest degradation occurs when removing the safety term: the score drops markedly to 74.85, while collision rate and lane devia- tion worsen to 0.0708 and 0.0287, respectively, demonstrating that safety remains the dominant constraint in closed-loop planning. The single-term variants further support this decomposition: safety only preserves relatively better collision performance, rule only better preserves lane consistency, and comfort only achieves the smoothest trajectories with the lowest jerk but performs poorly on overall planning quality and safety. Overall, these results match the intended design of RRS: the safety term anchors collision avoidance, the rule term enforces scene-consistent behavioral constraints, and the comfort term regularizes motion smoothness; only their combi- nation provides a dense and well-balanced planning signal aligned with the full closed-loop objective. Ablating the graph-reward integrated objective. Table 6 and Fig. 7 show that the full on-policy objective is essential for achieving the best overall closed-loop performance. Removing the critic baseline yields only marginal gain over the offline baseline (80.03 vs. 79.90), indicating that value estimation is important for producing a usable low-variance im- provement signal. Removing advantage reweighting or the OPSD term also degrades performance, with score dropping to 80.76 and 80.29, respectively, together with worse safety and tracking metrics, showing that reweighting helps focus learning on better rollouts while OPSD helps consolidate these improvements into the policy. The OPSD-specific ablations further clarify this effect: removing the advantage gate preserves relatively competitive collision, lane, jerk, and latency metrics, but still lowers the score to 80.89, suggest- ing that ungated distillation weakens overall policy improvement; using a random teacher causes the largest degradation (79.57 score, 0.0894 collision rate, 0.1753 jerk), demonstrating that teacher quality is crucial for successful consolidation. This trend is also reflected in Fig. 7, where the full objective exhibits faster conver- gence and consistently lower collision rate during training. Overall, the results support the intended role of each component: the critic baseline stabilizes advantage estimation, advantage reweighting emphasizes better trajectories, and OPSD transfers these gains into a stronger closed-loop planner. 5
