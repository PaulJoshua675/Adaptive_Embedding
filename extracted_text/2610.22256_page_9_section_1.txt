We presented BitSparse, a GPU-resident tile-wise sparse representation for ReLU and ReLU2 feed-forward activations that is lossless and can be used as a drop-in replacement to reduce memory usage. It is fast, using optimized fused Triton kernels, with overhead typically less than 1% for large sequence lengths while reducing memory by up to 30%. Because this sparsity arises naturally, BitSparse converts existing exact zeros into memory savings without approximation. BitSparse supports both BF16 and FP8 formats and can operate without host synchronization. BitSparse depends on the continued success of ReLU-family activations, since its gains rest on exact structural zeros. The scope is limited to feed-forward activations; attention and Mamba layers are not affected. Future work should extend the representation to gated activations and co-design it with checkpointing, offloading, and distributed training. Reproducibility statement The supplementary materials include the BitSparse implementation (lib_sparse), benchmark scripts (experiments), and language-model experiments (nanogpt and nemotron). Section 3 describes the storage format, GPU kernels, and shared buffer; Algorithm 1 gives the forward and backward passes. Appendix B contains the proofs of the entropy and compression results. Appendix D specifies hardware, software versions, random seeds, and baselines. Appendices D.3 and D.4 detail the MLP and language-model workloads, precision settings, and timing and memory measurements. Appendix E.1 describes the data and aggregation used to measure activation sparsity, and Appendix E provides the full-training comparison, additional benchmark results, and ablations. Ethics statement BitSparse compresses intermediate activations during model training and does not itself collect data or introduce a new data source. Reducing GPU memory requirements may make larger training workloads more accessible. Beyond this, any privacy, bias, and misuse risks depend on the use case, rather than BitSparse. Its environmental impact depends on the memory savings and runtime overhead of the complete training workload. 9
