Shortcut auditing connects annotation artifacts and shortcut learning [Gururangan et al., 2018, Geirhos et al., 2020] to group-shift tests, contrast sets, behavioral evaluation, and benchmark sensitivity [Koh et al., 2021, Gardner et al., 2020, Ribeiro et al., 2020, Dehghani et al., 2021, Alzahrani et al., 2024]. Related work tests surface heuristics, AgentHarm cues, benign triggers, construct validity, and broken specifications [Li et al., 2026, Meng et al., 2025, Tasawong et al., 2025, Li et al., 2025, Bean et al., 2025, Wang et al., 2026, Tu et al., 2026]. Paired cue tests are established; we contribute a three-level inference ladder across released agent-safety artifacts. AgentDojo, AgentHarm, ToolSafe, LinuxArena, and StepGuard span safety guards and monitoring [Debenedetti et al., 2024, Andriushchenko et al., 2025, Mou et al., 2026, Tracy et al., 2026, Zheng et al., 2026]. Other work audits AgentDojo end to end or develops broader guards [Bhagwatkar et al., 2025, Chen et al., 2025, Huang et al., 2025, Greenblatt et al., 2024]; we audit released step records, evaluation units, and black-box decision sensitivity. 8 Limitations and broader impact The Luna replication does not reproduce the Haiku-sized effects, indicating that the observed sensitivity is configuration-specific. The 64-step cohort is not a probability sample, and intervals are exploratory and unadjusted. The marker rewrite changes provenance wording, aliases are not natural global renamings, and neither has blinded human validation. Q8 is quantized, GPT-5.5 lacks repeats, and StepGuard stops at a non-equivalent Mac feasibility check. LinuxArena uses completed trajectories, and AgentHarm subset identity is not proven guard-visible. We study evaluation validity, not deployment safety; execute no tool calls; and use no human-subject data. 9
