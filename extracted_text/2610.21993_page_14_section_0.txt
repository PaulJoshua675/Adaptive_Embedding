4.4. Ablation Studies 4.4.1. Output Resolution: 7 × 7 vs 14 × 14 Because Table 7 pretrains the standard 7×7 ConvNeXt encoder and our 14×14 ConvNeXt-RS encoder under otherwise identical conditions (UNet-128 decoder, pretraining lr=10−3), it dou- bles as a resolution ablation. The higher-resolution encoder’s edge is concentrated in the dense prediction tasks (+1.4 pp DFC2020-S1, +0.8 pp Flood-S1) and much smaller for classification (+0.3 pp EuroSAT-S1), which is what we would expect if the extra spatial tokens mainly help pixel-level rather than scene-level prediction. The matched learning rate control largely rules out the pretraining learning rate as a con- founding factor: the original 7×7 experiments used a pretraining lr=10−4 (required to stabilize training), while our 14×14 ConvNeXt-RS uses a pretraining lr=10−3 (all models are finetuned at lr=10−3). Table 7 shows that pretraining the 7×7 encoder at lr=10−3 with UNet-128 still under- performs 14×14 at the same pretraining learning rate, confirming that the resolution modification is the primary contributor. At a pretraining lr=10−4, UNet-128 outperforms UNet-768 despite its lower decoder capacity, indicating that the larger decoder’s deficit is not explained by its reduced learning rate alone. Decoder Design and Efficiency UNet-128 and ConvNeXt-512 decoders achieve comparable downstream accuracy across all four tasks (Table 5), despite ConvNeXt-512 operating on only the final encoder stage rather than aggregating multi-scale features. This result is consistent with the hierarchical decoder not being necessary for strong downstream accuracy: the lightweight ConvNeXt-512 decoder achieves statistically indistinguishable performance (paired t-test, p=0.17) while reducing VRAM by 7.5– 8.5% per GPU, depending on hardware (Table 1). We note that decoder capacity is not perfectly disentangled from learning rate in this comparison, since the higher-capacity UNet-768 decoder (Table 7) required a lower learning rate for FP16 stability; the finding above is therefore best read as evidence against a decoder-capacity effect at matched learning rate and precision (UNet- 128 vs. ConvNeXt-512, both at lr=10−3), rather than a fully controlled ablation across the entire decoder-capacity range. 4.4.3.
