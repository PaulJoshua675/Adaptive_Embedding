Conversational Recommender Systems (CRSs) require reliable user simulators for cost- effective evaluation. LLM-based simulators have made realistic simulation possible but typ- ically condition on profiles derived from ex- plicit ratings, demographics, and static sources capturing surface preferences while omitting implicit behavioral characteristics such as decision-making style, aspect-level preferences, and domain expertise. We propose SimInter, a pipeline that elicits these attributes via syn- thetic interviews between two LLM agents, grounded in the user’s statistical profile and his- torical conversational evidence. Before profile enrichment, an audit agent explicitly flags po- tential fabrication and stereotyping at the turn level. We instantiate and evaluate SimInter on movie recommendation. We simulate 300 Re- Dial users and, on a matched subset of 100 users, compare SimInter with a statistics-only baseline: SimInter significantly increases the diversity of expressed preference aspects (fil- tered aspect entropy: 1.619 vs. 0.859; paired d = 0.96, Holm-adjusted p < 0.001). This gain persists when the simulator converses with two trained recommenders, KBRD and Uni- CRS, under two independent evaluation judges (+0.28 to +0.56 nats; all p < 0.001). Human evaluation finds 94.3% of enriched-attribute claims supported or partially supported, while human judgments agree with 95.2% of the au- ditor’s binary filtering decisions on a stratified sample. 1
