AI Consilium: a Benchmark of LLM Clinical Multidisciplinary Second Opinions A PREPRINT Score Assign this score when 3 The conclusion is clear, specific, and justified. It names the main diagnosis, syndrome, or working hypothesis, states the level of confidence, and identifies data that could change the decision. Justification and specificity of management strategy Assess whether the plan can be acted upon clinically: what should be done, when, why, and what the next step should be. Score Assign this score when 0 Management strategy is absent, clearly inappropriate, unsafe, or based on fabricated clinically significant facts. 1 Some appropriate actions are present, but the plan is generic: priorities, timeframes, reassess- ment criteria, monitoring, or links to the diagnosis are missing. 2 The main actions are appropriate, but specific details are missing, such as timeframes, monitoring parameters, responsible specialties, or escalation criteria. 3 The plan is specific and prioritized. Diagnostic actions, treatment, monitoring, timeframes, reassessment criteria, and next steps are linked to the clinical reasoning. Clinical safety Assess whether the consensus report creates risk for the patient or omits threats that require immediate monitoring or action. Score Assign this score when 0 There is a potentially unsafe recommendation, an omitted life-threatening condition, or an ignored contraindication. 1 Some risks are recognized, but there is a substantial gap in monitoring, contraindications, drug interactions, or actions in case of deterioration. 2 The management strategy is generally safe, but one important precaution or one deterioration scenario is not addressed. 3 Urgent risks, contraindications, interactions, complications, monitoring requirements, and an action plan for deterioration are addressed. Whole-case criteria These three scores are assigned once per case row and are not repeated for each component. The rater should evaluate the consensus report as a whole. Adequacy of specialist composition, column Z Assess whether the selected specialists were clinically necessary for resolving the case. Score Assign this score when 0 The specialist composition does not cover the main clinical problem; a key specialty is absent, and the consensus cannot reliably address the case. 1 Part of the composition is appropriate, but one important specialty is absent or the roles of the participants do not match the main clinical tasks. 2 Most necessary specialties are present, but the composition does not fully cover diagnosis, treatment, or safety. 3 The composition is appropriate: key specialists are present, and their roles correspond to the main problems and patient risks. Clinical complexity of the case, column AA This score reflects the difficulty of the case itself, not the quality of the generated consensus report.
