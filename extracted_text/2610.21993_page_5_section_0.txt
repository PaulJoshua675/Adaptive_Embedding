Among the contrastive methods, SeCo (Mañas et al., 2021) builds positive pairs from sea- sonal multi-temporal imagery, and GASSL (Ayush et al., 2021) instead treats geographic co- location as a proxy for scene similarity. Both, however, rely on augmentation pipelines built around electro-optical statistics, which do not transfer cleanly to SAR backscatter. MIM has also been adopted for electro-optical remote sensing. SatMAE (Cong et al., 2022) adapted MAE for multi-spectral and multi-temporal satellite data. Scale-MAE (Reed et al., 2023) introduced scale-aware positional encodings for images at varying ground sample distances. FG- MAE (Wang et al., 2025a) proposed feature-guided reconstruction targets better aligned with semantic downstream tasks. A separate line of work pairs Sentinel-1 SAR with electro-optical data rather than treat- ing SAR in isolation. SoftCon (Wang et al., 2024) pretrains contrastively across multispectral and SAR imagery using multi-label land-cover supervision as guidance. DOFA (Xiong et al., 2024) takes a different route, using a dynamic spectral adapter, so that a single ViT can ingest whatever sensor modality it is given. CROMA (Fuller et al., 2023) instead unifies contrastive and masked autoencoding objectives within one radar-optical model, and Copernicus-FM (Wang et al., 2025b) pushes this further still, training a multi-modal ViT across four Sentinel missions on 18.7M aligned images at a reported cost of roughly 512 GPU hours on NVIDIA A100 GPUs. These models perform well, but their multi-modal inputs and near-exclusive reliance on frozen- encoder evaluation make them a poor match for the SAR-only, constrained-compute setting this work targets. 2.3. SAR-Specific Self-Supervised Learning Compared to optical imagery, SSL research targeting SAR data exclusively is still com- paratively unexplored. Allen et al. (2023) demonstrated that large-scale MAE pretraining on Sentinel-1 S1GRD data can substantially reduce label requirements across two downstream tasks, vegetation-cover prediction and land-cover classification, with the pretraining benefit gen- eralizing geographically and increasing, rather than merely holding up, on regions outside the pretraining set. SAR-JEPA (Li et al., 2024) introduced a joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA)- based approach for SAR automatic target recognition (ATR) by predicting multi-scale gradi- ent features rather than raw pixels. SARATR-X (Li et al., 2025) subsequently incorporated multi-scale gradient features into a masked image modeling framework, using a two-step self- supervised procedure that first performed MIM pretraining on ImageNet to obtain a favorable weight initialization, then continued MIM pretraining on a curated corpus of unlabeled SAR target samples using the multi-scale gradient features to suppress speckle noise, rather than the joint-embedding predictive approach SAR-JEPA uses. However, both models are trained and evaluated exclusively on ATR tasks, limiting the generalizability to land-cover and change de- tection problems. Applying DINO (Caron et al., 2021)-style self-distillation to SAR imagery, Gallego-Mejia et al. (2023) and Martínez-Ferrer et al. (2023) found only modest gains over training from scratch, with the resulting self-distilled embeddings generalizing poorly to geographically unfamiliar re- gions without further domain-specific adaptation. To the best of our knowledge, SparK-SAR is among the first methods to adapt sparse con- volutional masked pretraining specifically for S1GRD data and demonstrate its transferability across diverse downstream SAR tasks under constrained compute. 5
