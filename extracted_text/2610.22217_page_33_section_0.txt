Preprint. Capability Skill: Multi-Image Reasoning Objective Answer the current benchmark item accurately from the provided image(s) and question. Evidence Protocol • Apply ONLY the task section matching the current question type; ignore the others. • When the task involves sequential operations, update the working state after each step before proceeding. • Do not use dataset names, filenames, memorized examples, or external information. Task-Specific Checks Apply when answering questions that reference multiple images. • When comparing images for thematic or semantic similarity, verify that at least two distinct, non-redundant visual anchors are consistent across candidate images. • When evaluating whether a collection of images satisfies a condition, confirm the relevant quantifier: a universal claim fails if any required image contradicts it, whereas an existential claim requires only one valid instance. • When identifying a paired comparison, verify that the images share a meaningful semantic or structural relationship rather than superficial visual resemblance. • When no candidate image matches the reference in key structural or spatial anchors, do not force a comparison. • When judging whether images share the same scene, building, or identity, prioritize stable structural anchors over decorative or transient elements. Final verification: • Confirm that the selected answer is uniquely supported by the visual evidence. • Do not infer absence solely because an object or motion is not clearly visible. • For directional or quantifier reasoning, verify that the correct reference frame and logical in- terpretation have been used. Apply for multi-image spatial and relational reasoning questions. • When evaluating directional terms such as “turned to the right” or “facing left,” confirm the intended reference frame rather than relying on image tilt or an object’s internal orientation. • When a statement specifies a spatial relationship, interpret it literally unless the question ex- plicitly introduces another reference frame. • When a “cannot be determined” option is available and a precise answer requires information that is not visually recoverable, prefer the insufficient-information option rather than inferring unsupported details. Final verification: • Confirm that the selected answer is uniquely supported by the visual evidence. • Avoid reasoning based on inferred absence when the relevant evidence is not visible. • Verify the interpretation of directional and quantifier relations before producing the final an- swer. Output Follow the output format stated in the question exactly, including any answer tags it specifies. Do not shorten, skip, or reorder the reasoning the question asks for, and never state the final answer before the checks above are done.
