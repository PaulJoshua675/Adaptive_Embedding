When finetuning on RoboTwin, RoboCasa365 and RoboCasa-GR1, we add a mirroring augmentation, which extends the horizontal flip common in image classification to robot data. With probability 0.5, a sample is replaced by its left-right mirror image. The images are flipped, the state and the actions are reflected through the same plane, the arms of a two-arm robot are swapped, and the prompt marks the sample as mirrored (Appendix C.2). The augmentations and other training settings, such as the state dropout rate, have a large effect on robustness: on LIBERO-Plus, disabling camera dropout or the image augmentations lowers the success under camera-viewpoint changes by more than 20 points, and disabling state dropout lowers the success under changes of the initial robot state by 8.1 points (Appendix D.2). 6 EXPERIMENTS 6.1 RESULTS Benchmarks. LIBERO (Liu et al., 2023a) has four suites of ten single-arm tabletop tasks, and LIBERO-Plus (Fei et al., 2025) perturbs its 40 tasks along seven dimensions, from the camera viewpoint and the initial robot state to lighting and object layout. The models are finetuned on the four suites and evaluated on both. RoboTwin 2.0 (Chen et al., 2025b) has 50 tasks on the dual-arm AgileX ALOHA, in clean scenes and in scenes with a randomised background, lighting, clutter and table height. For the out-of-distribution evaluation (Clean2Rand; Yuan et al., 2026), the models are finetuned on clean scenes only and evaluated under six conditions: on clean scenes (Easy), under each randomisation alone and under all four together (Hard). The Clean2Rand score is the mean over these six conditions. RoboCasa365 (Nasiriany et al., 2026) has kitchen tasks for a Franka arm on a mobile base. The models are trained on the human demonstrations of its 300 pretraining tasks and evaluated on 50 target tasks: 18 atomic tasks, 16 composite tasks seen in training and 16 composite tasks unseen in training. RoboCasa-GR1 (Bjorck et al., 2025) has 24 tabletop tasks for the Fourier GR-1 humanoid, with 1000 demonstrations per task. We compare mainly with VLAs of similar complexity, which pair a VLM with an action expert, such as π0.5, GR00T, ABot-M0 and InternVLA-A1.5. As a gauge of the current upper bound on these benchmarks, we also report larger models pretrained on in-house data, Qwen-RobotManip, RLDX-1 and Xiaomi-Robotics-1, and the world-action model ABot-M0.5, built on a video generation model. They are trained under different conditions, with more or proprietary data or a video model, so they are a reference rather than a like-for-like comparison. Figure 4 summarises the results across benchmarks, and Appendix Tables 4 to 6 list them in full. Out of distribution. REKI stands out most when the test conditions differ from training. On LIBERO-Plus and Clean2Rand, it ranks first among the VLAs pretrained on public data, despite having the fewest parameters (2.4B, Figs. 4a and 4c). It leads the next of these models by 5.6 points on LIBERO-Plus (InternVLA-A1.5, 84.8) and by 10.1 points on Clean2Rand (ABot-M0, 55.7).
