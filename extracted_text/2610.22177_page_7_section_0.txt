The resulting 5-dimensional vector is passed through a three-layer MLP, referred to as the patch encoder, to produce a residual-flow embedding with the same dimensionality as the visual patch tokens extracted by the DINO backbone. The encoded residual-flow feature is then scaled by a learnable weight Wflow and added to the corresponding patch token: ˜tpatch = tpatch + Wflow · fflow. Gravity Encoding. In addition to motion cues, we incorporate scene orientation information through gravity direction. The gravity vector is first normalized and then encoded using a two-layer MLP, referred to as the gravity encoder, producing a representation aligned with the dimensionality of camera tokens. The encoded gravity feature is scaled by a learnable parameter Wgrav and fused with the camera token: ˜tcam = tcam + Wgrav · fgrav. (3) The specific implementation of this fusion varies depending on the model architecture. For π3, since there are no specialized camera tokens, we define the camera tokens as the aggregator encoder’s output embeddings that feed into the camera head. The gravity feature corresponding to a specific image is fused with all constituent patch tokens for the image. For VGGT, we modify the gravity input to align with its reference- frame design. The first camera token in the sequence receives no gravity information, as it serves as a fixed reference for camera parameters. All subsequent tokens are fused with a concatenated gravity vector containing both their own view’s orientation and that of the first view. This ensures the gravity features remain aware of the reference frame with minimal learning overhead. This design allows the model to incorporate global orientation priors that help stabilize camera estimation and improve geometric consistency across views. 7
