Unlike the video branch, which generates frames through iterative denoising, the action branch predicts the full hand and camera trajectory in one forward pass. We therefore represent missing actions with learned query embeddings rather than noisy action states. Each query corresponds to a frame and one of three action types (camera, left hand, or right hand). Through attention to the video and observed action tokens, the model predicts the states at these query positions. 3.4 OPTIMIZATION We train video generation with flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023), restricting the loss to unobserved video latents: Lvideo = Eτ,ϵ,m " w(τ) max(1, |Uv|) X k∈Uv ∥ˆvθ,k −(ϵk −zv,k)∥2 2 # , (1) where Uv contains the unobserved video-latent elements and w(τ) is the diffusion training weight. In PREDICT and PLAN, we iteratively denoise the unobserved RGB latents while keeping observed images fixed. For PLAN, we keep the generated video and the action trajectory predicted at the final denoising step. The VAE decoder reconstructs the video frames. The MANO decoder and fixed MANO layer recover the hand motion, and the camera decoder recovers the camera motion. Long tasks require several manipulation steps, each with its own target arrangement. We use an image generator to create visual subgoals for these steps. Each planning window takes its starting observation, instruction, and goal image as input and generates the video and action trajectory between the observed images. Figure 4(a) illustrates the spatial control provided by a visual target: the goal image specifies the separation between the red blocks, which the text instruction leaves ambiguous. In the longer task in Figure 4(b), successive visual subgoals specify the block arrangement at each stage. The generated sequence follows these arrangements across planning windows, while the example without goal images accumulates placement errors and departs from the intended structure. 4 EXPERIMENTS We first examine whether unified training benefits each mode (Sec. 4.1), then compare PERCEIVE, PREDICT, and PLAN with specialized baselines on their respective tasks.
