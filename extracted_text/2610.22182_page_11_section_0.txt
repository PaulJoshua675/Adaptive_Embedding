Ten datasets have distinct validation and test lists. For ImageNet, support images come from the training partition, while the same official 50,000-image validation parti- tion is used for both parameter selection and benchmark scoring, following the released Tip-Adapter and ProKeR implementations. The eleven-dataset aggregate therefore com- bines these evaluation protocols. Published comparisons retain their different selection procedures: CLIP-LoRA shares hyperparameters across tasks, whereas LAMP uses dataset-specific validation. Paired analyses of the three-seed benchmark and its controls use 5,000 paired crossed bootstrap replicates, with shared support-seed and class-stratified query-identity draws. Selected models remain fixed during resampling. The benchmark tasks were used during method development; these intervals do not cover repeated-selection or development uncertainty. 4.2. Comparison with state-of-the-art methods We compare LAMP and LAMP-F with representative published methods for prompt learning, feature adaptation, and support matching across five support budgets. Tables 1 and 2 report mean accuracy over eleven datasets for ViT-B/16 and RN50. The methods included in each comparison depend on their backbone requirements and the availability of published results. For example, MaPLe [15] inserts deep visual prompts into Trans- former blocks and is evaluated with ViT-B/16. SSP [7] reports eleven-dataset mean accuracy at all five support budgets for RN50, but does not report the corresponding means for ViT-B/16. We therefore use the ViT-B/16 results reported by CLIP-LoRA and CAPEL [17, 19] to compare with methods that require training, and the RN50 results reported by ProKeR and SSP [6, 7] to compare with training-free methods. LAMP achieves state-of-the-art mean accuracy among the training-free RN50 meth- ods compared at 4, 8, and 16 shots. At 16 shots, it reaches 78.73%, exceeding the best published training-free result by 1.98 percentage points without gradient optimization. The ViT-B/16 comparison includes published methods that require training. LAMP exceeds CLIP-LoRA, the strongest published baseline at 8 and 16 shots, by 0.35 and 0.30 points, respectively, without gradient-based training. LAMP-F further raises mean accuracy to 80.94% and 83.57%. CLIP-LoRA updates encoder weights, while LAMP keeps both the encoders and class prototypes fixed; LAMP-F optimizes only the class prototypes. These results show that combining residual corrections from multiple depths can achieve competitive accuracy with a frozen encoder and fixed text prototypes. On RN50, training-free LAMP outperforms the strongest published method by 0.77, 1.18, and 1.98 points at 4, 8, and 16 shots, respectively. APE+SSP [7] achieves the highest training-free mean accuracy at 1 and 2 shots, while LAMP’s advantage grows with the number of support images. LAMP-F further improves the means to 65.91, 68.83, 72.65, 75.50, and 79.10%. Across the two backbones, native-layer residual integration is competitive with both training-free support matching and methods that require training, and prototype optimization provides further gains.
