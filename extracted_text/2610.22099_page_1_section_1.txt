Masked diffusion language models (dLLMs) generate responses by predicting unresolved positions in parallel and exposing selected predictions as context (Sahoo et al., 2024; Nie et al., 2025). This flexibility creates a decoding problem: under a limited number of denoising passes, a sampler must decide which predictions to commit and whether earlier commitments should be reconsidered. Revealing more positions makes faster progress toward a complete response, but those predictions also condition subsequent generations. An early commitment can therefore influence later predictions even after the surrounding context changes. The resulting visibility trajectory—which tokens become context, when they appear, and whether they can be revised—is an important component of the accuracy–compute trade-off (Kim et al., 2025a). We study how to learn this trajectory while leaving the pretrained token model unchanged. Existing methods address different parts of this problem. Confidence-based and uncertainty-aware samplers select positions using hand-designed rules (Wu et al., 2026; Ben-Hamu et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2026a). Learned unmasking policies instead optimize position selection around a frozen denoiser, but typically preserve monotonic visibility: once a token is exposed, the policy cannot withdraw it (Jazbec et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026; Hong et al., 2026a). Conversely, remasking methods allow revision through prescribed transitions or heuristic criteria (Wang et al., 2025; Hong et al., 2026b; Zhai et al., 2026), while other approaches learn remasking behavior by updating the language model itself (Huang et al., 2026b). These developments motivate a complementary question: can a small auxiliary policy learn both commitment and revision without modifying the dLLM? We introduce Tiny Inference-time Policy for Scaling (TIPS), an approximately 600K-parameter controller assigning to each response position a binary next-step visibility action. At masked positions, the action reveals the decoder’s proposal or leaves the position masked; at visible positions, it retains the current token or remasks it for subsequent regeneration.
