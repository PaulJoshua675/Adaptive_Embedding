Semantic segmentation is not the only high-level CV task that has been benefiting from adding multi-modality in learning, with object detection, tracking, autonomous driving perception systems [59] also improving performance by leveraging multiple representations of the same scenes. In the case of RGB-Depth semantic segmentation, early methods focused on fusing modalities at the input or feature level with some approaches directly concatenating RGB and depth maps or fusing them by summation [60, 61]. In time, models have evolved to a two-stream architecture [4], where independent networks extract features separately before integrating them in the decoding process. The recent focus has been placed on feature fusion approach which separates the fusion logic from the backbone and employing modules like Separation-and-Aggregation Gate [62] or Attention Com- plementary Modules [63] to provide parallel modality streams at each hierarchical level. A significant focal point of recent work is discriminative treatment of modalities [64]. This repre- sents a change from the initial models which treated all network layers equally. More recent frame- works like LASNet [65] argue for level-specific treatment. This involves designing distinct models for different hierarchies: enhancing shallow spatial details (thermal-focused) and deep semantic features (RGB-focused) separately. Methods frequently integrate channel weighted selection and spatial-attention mechanisms to mitigate the inherent discrepancies (originating from distinct under- lying data distributions) between modalities [44]. The inherent limitations of CNNs, stemming from their constrained receptive fields, are also evident in modality fusion models. The Transformer architecture [27] has emerged as a compelling solution to this issue [66]. Driven by the capacity of the self-attention mechanism to model complex rela- tionships across different contexts, current research is shifting from dedicated dual-modality models toward unified architectures. While earlier methods perform well on specific pairings like RGB- Depth or RGB-Thermal [67], unified frameworks capable of integrating multiple concurrent data streams offer a much richer understanding of scene context, semantics, and structure. Consequently, these architectures yield superior performance on advanced scene understanding tasks. 22
