Its train- ing data includes audio-video samples from different generation paradigms and models, with anno- tations from multiple expert MLLMs and human annotators, while its two-stage training procedure uses supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to cold-start the structured evaluation process and group relative policy optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) to align the scoring with human preferences. Mean- while, to measure the agreement between audio-video consistency metrics and human perception across generation paradigms and relation complexities, we construct an audio-video consistency benchmark, AVC-Benchmark. To our knowledge, it is the first audio-video consistency benchmark primarily built from diverse samples generated by various types of audio-video generation models. We evaluate the reward model from two complementary perspectives. For evaluation effectiveness, it achieves state-of-the-art alignment with human judgment on AVC-Benchmark, and unlike a black-box scalar, it provides interpretable, structured natural-language rationales that localize where a generation fails (e.g., a temporally misaligned event or a hallucinated sound). For usability as a reward, applying it as a training signal for the online reinforcement-learning fine-tuning (Zhang et al., 2026a) of a joint generation model LTX-2 (HaCohen et al., 2026) measurably improves the audio-video consistency of generated outputs, demonstrating that it closes the optimization loop. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We formalize audio-video consistency across generation paradigms with an event-centric, three-level metric designed to handle complex generated scenes. • We instantiate this metric as a trainable audio-video reward model built on the Qwen3- Omni-Thinking (Xu et al., 2025) model with rigorous event-level verification. • We construct AVC-Benchmark for reproducible human-alignment evaluation, and validate the reward model both as an interpretable automatic evaluator and as an effective training signal that improves the audio-video consistency of a joint generation model. 2
