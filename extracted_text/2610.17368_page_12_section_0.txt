The deduplication token therefore provides a deterministic mechanism for distinguishing items that share the same three-level semantic code. 9.1.5 Training-Data Construction. Hierarchical-loss training. The hierarchical loss is a training ob- jective rather than a separate data-generation pipeline. It is applied to the same semantic-ID conversational training corpus used by the flat causal CE baseline, reweighting the per-level losses without changing the underlying examples. For Sports, Beauty, and Toys, conversational SFT data are gener- ated from item metadata, semantic IDs, and user sequences using fixed instruction templates. Sports and Beauty use 26 conversation types, while Toys uses 22 because several types requiring lower- coverage fields are omitted. Repeated instructions mapping to the same SID are intentionally retained to reinforce the SID-item map- ping and provide surface-form diversity. Examples are shuffled with a fixed seed and split 92/5/3 into training and validation. The conversation types fall into five groups: • SID→text decoding: recover title, description, context, cat- egory, or brand from a semantic ID. • Text→SID encoding: predict a semantic ID from title, de- scription, or brand information. • Sequential next-item prediction: predict the next item from histories of length 2, 3, 5 and all. • Co-engagement: predict co-purchased, frequently bought, or similar products. • Category/prefix structure understanding: reason over shared semantic-ID prefixes and their associated categories or examples. This produces 2,383,517, 1,634,627, and 1,320,505 training con- versations for Sports, Beauty, and Toys, respectively. Attribute-loss training. The attribute-conditioned loss is evalu- ated on Fashion-140k and Internal-fashion, where structured at- tributes are sufficiently dense. The loss is applied as an auxiliary objective over the training corpus rather than being restricted ex- clusively to attribute-conditioned conversations. Fashion-140k. The 1,336,658-example training corpus is orga- nized into five families: • AF1 – SID↔text/attribute mappings: approximately 700K examples covering SID-to-text, text-to-SID, and attribute-to- SID mappings. • AF2 – attribute-based retrieval: approximately 27K exam- ples using gender, product type, color, style, occasion, brand, and material, including single-, two-, and three-attribute queries. • AF3 – sequential prediction: approximately 200K next- item and co-purchase examples. • AF4 – outfit color coordination: approximately 1K exam- ples derived from Fashionpedia and Polyvore color-compatibility rules. • AF5 – semantic hierarchy understanding: approximately 117K examples based on SID-prefix clusters and similar-item relationships. AF2 and AF4 provide the most direct attribute supervision be- cause their targets are explicitly organized by shared attributes such as color, material, gender, and style. Internal-fashion. The Internal-fashion SFT corpus contains 368,482 training and 19,482 validation conversations using the same 57-type schema as the underlying pipeline. In addition to SID↔text tasks, it contains exhaustive attribute-conjunction retrieval exam- ples over combinations of brand, color, material, gender, and cate- gory. Examples include queries for all products matching a particu- lar color-category or brand-color combination. Rather than restricting the auxiliary objective to these explic- itly color-named examples, the color loss is applied as a soft per- example reweighting over the entire corpus. Examples whose con- versation type contains “color” receive the full auxiliary weight (𝛼= 0.1), while all other examples receive a reduced scale of 0.2𝛼. Thus, even items that do not occur in explicitly color-conditioned conversations receive a weaker color-anchoring gradient. The color objective uses soft-label cross-entropy over color- similar candidates, with similarity determined by exp(−Δ𝐸/𝑇) in CIE-LAB [4] color space and an additional frequency-based weight- ing term that upweights rare items. This formulation allows at- tribute supervision to provide stronger fine-grained signals while retaining the broad conversational training distribution. 9.2 Implementation Details 9.2.1 Hierarchical Loss.
