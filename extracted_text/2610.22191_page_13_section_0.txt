(iii) The dataset entropy gener- alizes to an entropy of the weighted vote: pairwise and exponential networks are the two ends of one formula, with Meff = M and extensive crosstalk on one side, Meff = MΦM(x) and exponentially small crosstalk on the other. The labels are not needed: inside the win- dow the examples of the correct class receive the weight by themselves (W →1), and the attention layer, which never sees a label, performs the same majority vote as the pairwise network built on class averages at zero load, Eq. With keys equal to examples the fixed points of the dynamics are the examples, and the archetype is an attractor only for small ρ; the retrieval of an unseen archetype is a one-step operation, not a convergence. For attention layers. (i) An attention head over a set of noisy examples has a computable phase diagram in the parameters (ρ, β, N, K, M), and the softmax tem- perature is the axis along which it moves from crosstalk to prototype to memorization; the two edges of the gen- eralization window scale as 1/N and 1/ √ N, so that a larger model generalizes from the same context over a wider range of temperatures. (ii) Memorization is a measurable property of a head whose keys are training examples: the participation ratio of its weights, which drops to order one at the REM threshold, and whose departure from the REM curve measures the structure of the data (Sec. 5). Extending the diagnostic to heads with learned projections, where the logits are no longer the overlaps of Lemma 1, requires identifying the ana- logue of x and is left open. (iii) In our experiments the first steps of gradient descent on the cross-entropy are Hebbian, the later ones fit the noise of the examples, and weight decay returns the keys close to the Hebbian prototypes; this suggests a reading of early stopping and weight decay in attention layers as a distance from the Hebbian solution, to be tested beyond the present setup. (iv) In the present setting the single application of a layer generalizes over a wider range than its fixed- point iteration, which converges to a training example unless ρ is small; this is consistent with the feed-forward use of attention, although we do not claim it explains it. (v) On structured data the memorization regime is not a failure but the operating point: the classes of MNIST and F-MNIST are made of sub-clusters, the class proto- type is a poor target, and the softmax at a few times the condensation edge reads out the local sub-prototype, do- ing with the temperature what the hidden layer of [13] did by construction; the departure of the condensation curve from the REM measures how far the data are from the single-archetype picture. These implications are structural rather than numer- ical: the data are unstructured, the layer is single, the dynamics is noiseless and the signal-to-noise theory is Gaussian, hence quantitative only while the softmax pools many examples (within 0.03) and off by up to 0.1 once it has condensed. Several directions follow. The natural tool to close the latter gap is the REM- like treatment of exponential models of [8], or the large- deviation evaluation of the noise of [10], with examples in place of archetypes; either would also give the selec- tion bias of the closest example. The latter work, at β →∞, finds generalization to unseen cues well be- low memorization: in the present picture that is the memorization regime, and the window of Corollary 1 is where the two capabilities meet. The iterated dy- namics, in which the archetype emerges as a fixed point of the softmax map for small ρ (Fig. 4b), deserves an analytic condition. The finite-temperature equilibrium statistics, which in the pairwise model gives the first- order retrieval transition of [13], is the obvious next step, as is the theory of structured datasets, where Sec. 5 in- dicates the route: a hierarchical random energy model [28] for the within-class overlaps, whose levels of conden- sation should correspond to the levels of the ultrametric organization uncovered in [13]. Finally, the diagnostic of point (ii) above and the Hebbian reading of regular- ization in point (iii) are the two statements that could be confronted with trained transformers, once the map- ping from learned projections to the variables of Lemma 1 is understood.
