Limitations Evidence grounding and audit reliability. Sim- Inter grounds enriched attributes in historical user evidence, while independent validation against real user behavior remains an important direction for future work. Human evaluation assesses evidence support rather than direct behavioral fidelity. More- over, audit decisions involve uncertainty, particu- larly when distinguishing unsupported rationaliza- tions from legitimate preference elaboration. Auto- matically retrieved evidence and differences in an- notator judgments further limit conclusions about audit reliability. Evaluation scope and generalizability. Our ablations do not include direct LLM-based at- tribute inference without interviewing, leaving the interview’s independent contribution unresolved. Sparse and imbalanced preference evidence limits individual-level alignment evaluation. Enrichment also involves trade-offs between aspect richness, item diversity, and lexical variety. Although cross- CRS experiments support the robustness of aspect- level findings, they rely on automated evaluation and do not establish agreement with real-user as- sessments of recommenders. Generalization be- yond English movie recommendation remains to be investigated.
