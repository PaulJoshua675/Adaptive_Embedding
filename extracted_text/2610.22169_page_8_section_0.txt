Together they compose a state from labels; we test whether a frame carries both to intermediate layers. In steering, the task is to make the runner-up token u, the tar- get that needs the smallest move, overtake the top token v by moving a single state. A frame offers two directions for this: the lexical readout of the logit difference, bℓ(u) −bℓ(v), which is its gradi- ent, and the difference of the lexical basis vectors, sℓ(u) −sℓ(v). We move each state along each di- rection in steps from 0.01 to 1 times its norm and record the first step that flips the prediction and the change in the other tokens at that step. Along the lexical readout, LEXFRAME[1] flips 0.92 and 0.94 of the predictions, nearly as many as LEXFRAME[∞] with 0.95 and 0.97, and the lenses at most 0.81. The lexical basis flips nearly as many, but it disturbs the other tokens far less, 0.009 and 0.004 nats at the flip against 0.033 and 0.025, and therefore makes the most clean flips, 0.73 and 0.85 of the predictions. As in the lexical-basis test, the readout is the steepest ascent of the logit difference and spreads the change over every token moving with u or v, while the basis is the move whose first- order effect on the output comes closest to raising u against v alone.
