Stage two re-runs the survivors with no perturbations and normal gravity, and adds pose gates, requiring the object to drift less than 0.10 m and rotate less than 45◦over the hold. The configuration and object pose recorded at the end of stage two are the settled values stored in the dataset and used at WBD-EX reset, so training starts from poses settled under contact rather than raw optimizer solutions. We refer throughout to a grasp’s mode, meaning which arms must contact the object at the goal, as specified by its optimization contact group. Mode L requires left-arm contact, R right-arm contact, and B both; single-arm goals also require the opposite arm to release (Appendix III-C). APPENDIX II VOXELIZATION AND THE WBD GRASP DATASET A. Dataset Contents Each object’s accepted grasps are stored as a single dataset that WBD-EX loads at startup. Per grasp, we keep the settled arm configuration qsim (14 joints), the pressed post-grasp target qpost used as the servo setpoint at reset, the settled object pose T torso obj as a homogeneous transform in the torso frame, and the grasp mode m ∈{L, R, B}. B. Position Voxelization We voxelize by the settled object center expressed in the torso frame, taking the translation of T torso obj and binning it on a 2 cm lattice anchored at the torso origin. A voxel’s representative position is the mean of its member grasp positions. Within a voxel, grasps are grouped by mode, so a node of the goal graph is an occupied (voxel, mode) pair. Across the object set, this yields 1.0–5.5 × 103 occupied voxels and 1.7–9.1×103 nodes; spheres sit at the low end and the elongated prisms at the high end, because an elongated object reaches a wider spread of settled centers for the same body region. The occupied region spans roughly ±0.21 m laterally and 0.03–0.45 m vertically. C. Goal Graph Mode compatibility and goal sampling follow Section III- B, with B ↔B transitions also permitted. A mode change within a single voxel is a legal zero-distance transition. Intermediate goals exclude the current (voxel, mode) node. Far goals exclude the current voxel. If no legal-mode node lies within the sampled distance, the sampler selects the nearest legal-mode node toward the far goal. If no legal- mode node exists, the episode terminates as a dead end. A curriculum ramps dmax linearly from 0.02 m to 0.08 m over the first 5000 iterations. Holds earn no transition credit. D. Dataset Construction Candidate grasps enter the dataset only after the two-stage simulation filter of Appendix I. We then apply a safety filter to the settled pose with two components. The settled object center must lie at least 9 cm above the torso-frame origin, or 3 cm for objects whose mesh bounding-box height exceeds 15 cm. An exact on-body clearance test then drops any grasp whose object surface sits farther than 5 mm from the torso. Coverage measures sampled object-center positions around the torso. For each object, we offset exterior torso- face centroids along their normals at six evenly spaced distances between the object’s minimum and maximum bounding-box half-extents. We deduplicate positions on a 2 cm grid and keep those at torso-frame heights z ∈ [0.09, 0.29] m. A reference position is covered if a grasp center lies within the object’s minimum half-extent. The per- object score is the fraction covered, averaged equally across objects. For pooled coverage, we combine all grasp centers and reference positions, deduplicate the latter on a 1 cm grid, and use the 7.6 cm mean minimum half-extent as tolerance.
