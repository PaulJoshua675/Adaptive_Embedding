Per-phenotype agreement (Table 3) shows nine phenotypes reached 6/6, six reached 5/6, and two (dysautonomia, refusing medica- tion) reached 4/6. Adverse-effect presence. Documented rates vary across the adverse effects (Figure 3A). Other side effects are documented in 11% of patients at baseline and rise sharply to 58% at 6 months, settling at 38–49% thereafter. Medication refusal rests on a single patient at baseline, so that cell is suppressed, and it peaks at 38% at 18 months before falling to 15–16% at the later visits. Weight-loss documentation is already high before treatment starts (63%) and stays in a 62–71% band across follow-up, so it is unlikely to be treatment-emergent and more consistent with a pre-existing feeding or growth concern. Vomiting fluctuates modestly, peaking at 38% at 12 months and falling to 22% by 30 months. Diarrhea, the only validated adverse effect, is characterized separately (Figure 2). Rett clinical-feature presence. These 13 features are manifestations of Rett syndrome itself and not adverse effects of trofinetide, so their documented presence describes the disorder under treatment rather than harm caused by it. They span a wide range of documented prevalence (Figure 3A; full rates in Supplementary Table S8). Gross- and fine-motor impairment, impaired language/communication, and hand stereotypies are the core neurodevelopmental features of the disorder, and all are documented as present in nearly all patients at every visit (92–100%). Dysautonomia, breathing, bruxism, sleep, and seizure form a middle band at intermediate rates, while attention/alertness, anxiety, and the affective and behavioral findings of mood and aggression are documented least often.
