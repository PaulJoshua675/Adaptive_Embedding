As shown in Table 6, the default setting of W = 12 and K = 8 achieves the highest ADV and lowest CPA among the configurations compared. Extending W from 6 to 12 improves both metrics, whereas extending it further to 18 reduces performance. A longer observation period provides more information before decisions are made but shortens the time during which planning and adjustment can affect subsequent delivery, requiring a balance between observation and execution. Reducing K from 8 to 4 decreases ADV by 1.28% and raises CPA by 0.31%, while performance remains close to the default configuration under the tighter adjustment constraint. Relaxing the limit to 12 lowers ADV and raises CPA, so more adjustment opportunities do not necessarily improve performance. 6 Discussion and Conclusion We present AdCoEvo, a multi-agent system for advertising delivery that organizes delivery decisions through role specialization across different timescales and operational granularities, and improves delivery policies through offline development of operational capabilities and co-evolution. Experiments show that operational- level decisions, role collaboration, and post-training all help improve delivery performance, while co-evolution provides further gains beyond offline reinforcement learning. However, gains vary across environments, and the effectiveness of co-evolution still needs evaluation in specific delivery scenarios. How operational roles’ decision frequencies and scopes of influence affect co-evolution, and how advantage estimation and update mechanisms can be improved accordingly, warrant further study to help multi-agent systems use actual delivery feedback more effectively.
