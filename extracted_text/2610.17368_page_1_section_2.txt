Large language models (LLMs) have triggered a paradigm shift across modalities, replacing task-specific pipelines with a single model that is pre-trained once and then adapted through instruc- tions or lightweight fine-tuning [3, 33]. Recommender systems are undergoing an analogous shift. Industrial recommenders are con- ventionally built as cascades, commonly described as matching, pre-ranking, ranking, and re-ranking [23, 27], whose stages are developed and trained as separate models with their own objec- tives: YouTube’s two-stage system, for instance, trains candidate generation as an extreme multiclass classification problem and ranking under a watch-time-weighted logistic objective [5], while pre-ranking [37] and re-ranking [25] each introduce objectives of their own. Generative recommendation instead casts the entire retrieval problem as a single sequence-to-sequence task: given a user’s interaction history, the model generates the identifier of the next item directly. [10] TIGER [26] established the form this now takes, replacing arbitrary item IDs with residual-quantized seman- tic IDs. Subsequent work has shown that ranking and retrieval can be unified in a single generative backbone [7, 40], whose quality scales as a power law of training compute [40]. A parallel line of work marries generative recommendation with LLM backbones in order to exploit the world knowledge and seman- tic priors that recommendation data alone cannot supply [11, 41].
