3.3 Temporal gradient of the DeNN Because of the temporal encoding-decoding process involved with the spike-time delayed activations, the backward pass directly depends on the arrival times of spikes to the post synaptic neuron: ∂L ∂ds ij ∝ T X t=1 e−(zi[t]+dij) (15) These short-time dynamics cannot be captured by weighted activations of the ANN described in section 2.2.1 because learning happens entirely in the space dimension. This causes the DeNN to be more efficient in learning temporal dependencies in datasets. For each timestep, the direct contribution of parameter ds ij is thus a function of time. In a temporal network equipped with long-term memory, we compute the contribution of signed-delay ds ij: ∂ezj[t] ∂ds ij = ∂zj[t] ∂ds ij − t−1 X h=t−ν αh j e−|δh j [t]| ∂δh j [t] ∂ds ij (16) In equation 16, the first term corresponds to direct, short time dynamics. The second term is the contribution of the past dynamics to the delay variable. Our analysis of the gradient descent shows that learning for synaptic delays is dominated by short-term dynamics, even when equipped with long-term memory. See Appendix C for more details on the gradient. Finally, we provide an analysis of the energy consumption on different hardwares in section 4.3. 4.1 DeNN and ANN trajectories To compare the trajectories, we trained 10 networks with different seeds, using the PyTorch library (Paszke et al., 2017). DeNNs’ parameters were initialized with a uniform distribution U(−b, b) with b = 1 √ nL−1 . Backpropagation was performed with Stochastic Gradient Descent for a shallow fully connected network (no hidden layer), on the N- MNIST dataset (Orchard et al., 2015). Both networks were trained in parallel to ensure that the gradient was computed on the same batch of samples for each backpropagation step. We show on figure 1a the trajectory of the average absolute difference ∆(lemma 3.1), which represents the dif- ference between the outputs of the ANN and the DeNN. We note that, while the difference between the two trajectories decreases with each backpropagation step, it tends to stabilize at a non-zero value. Furthermore, we show on figure 1b the evolution of the Frobenius norm of the δ-matrix containing the differences between the weights of the ANN and the synaptic function of the DeNN, and we observe that it increases and tends to stabilize at a non-zero value, which means that the two trajectories are increasingly different with each backpropagation step. Moreover, we show on figures 2a and 2b the evolution of the bounds of theorem 3.2 (see lemmas A.1 and A.2 in appendix for more details on the computation of the bounds). The lower bound Υ is shown on figure 2a and appears to decrease and go toward 1.0092 ± 0.0082. The upper bound Ψ (figure 2b) has more variance but also decreases towards a value of 0.7428 ± 0.4292. Finally, we evaluated the cosine similarity of the exact gradient with the approximated gradient computed in equation 15. Figure 3 shows that the cosine similarity of the proxy gradient (equation 15) of the DeNN parameter ds ij with its true gradient is high, indicating that short-term temporal dynamics are the most important ones.
