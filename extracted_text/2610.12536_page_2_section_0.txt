The retained table supplies context for sub- sequent proposals and preserves previously discovered high-scoring programs. 2.1 Generation and repair EliteTable uses three operators: new, edit, and remix. Generation one contains only fresh proposals; the top K evaluated solutions enter the elite table. Subsequent generations use the allocation in Table 2. Fresh proposals receive elite descriptions and scores, edits receive one parent’s source, and remixes combine information from up to three parents. Parents are sampled uniformly from the table frozen at the start of the generation. Before evaluation, proposed solutions are checked for schema compliance, valid source edits, and parseable Python syntax using the abstract syntax tree (AST). Solutions must satisfy the required policy interface and pass duplicate checks. Duplicate detection compares AST structures rather than source text. Solutions are then passed to the evaluator. If the revised solution still fails, it returns to the repair loop, up to five repairs per candidate slot. Candidate timeouts are treated as failures, not zero- reward episodes or successful truncations. Infrastruc- ture failures interrupt execution rather than enter the candidate-repair loop. 2.2 Elite selection As briefly mentioned, EliteTable retains the highest- scoring programs across completed generations. For elite capacity K, the update rule is Et = TopK (Et−1 ∪Ct) . (1) Here Et is the retained elite set after generation t, Ct contains valid new candidates from that generation, and TopK selects up to K programs. Candidates are ranked by descending mean search reward. For equal scores, edits take priority over remixes, fol- lowed by incumbent elites and then fresh proposals. Any remaining ties are resolved by ascending organism identi- fier. This persistent elitist mechanism ensures that the high- est recorded search-panel reward cannot decrease between completed generations, assuming previously recorded eval- uations remain unchanged.
