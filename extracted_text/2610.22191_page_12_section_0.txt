To characterize what the slots have learnt we measure, for each slot, the largest cosine between the slot and an archetype and the largest cosine between the slot and a training example, and we average these over the slots with weights given by the mean attention each slot receives on the test queries (usage-weighted cosines): a slot aligned with an archetype is a prototype, a slot aligned with an exam- ple is a memory of that example. Figure 9 collects the results. The trained layer always fits the training set, but its test accuracy on fresh examples lies systemati- cally below that of the Hebbian attention at its best β, which on these data coincides with the nearest-centroid readout (Remark 6) and serves as the reference (it is not claimed to be optimal). The gap is not a function of ρ alone: at fixed ρ it grows with M, i.e. it is governed by the noise r of the individual examples; we interpret this as the keys fitting the noise directions of the ex- amples they attend to, which are the more numerous the smaller r. The training curves (panel b) show the classical peak-and-decay: the test accuracy reaches the Hebbian value after a few tens of steps, when the keys are proportional to the class sums of the examples (the gradient of the cross-entropy at initialization is Heb- bian), and then decays while the keys fit the noise. A weight decay of order 10−2 on the keys stops the decay, brings the test accuracy back close to the Hebbian ref- erence (within 0.01–0.03 at r = 0.3, within 0.03–0.08 at r = 0.2), and turns the learned keys into prototypes, their cosine with the archetypes rising from 0.4 to 0.7 (panel c). These observations refer to this setup, full- batch Adam with a single learning-rate schedule and two datasets per point, and are not a statement about optimally tuned training. In the language of [13], gra- dient descent discovers by itself the averaged, “replica- symmetric” representation of each class, and overfits by moving away from it. 7 Conclusions and outlook We have transferred Hebbian learning from examples [12, 13] from the pairwise Hopfield network to the expo- nential one, whose zero-temperature update is a layer of softmax attention with the training examples as keys and values. The signal-to-noise analysis closes in this setting, and yields a one-step magnetization m(1) = erf(1/√2ρeff) that has the same majority-rule form as in the pairwise network but with the dataset en- tropy replaced by an attention entropy, Eq. (15), built from two analytically computable statistics of the soft- max: the weight of the correct class and the number of examples it pools. The implications are of two kinds. For Hopfield networks.
