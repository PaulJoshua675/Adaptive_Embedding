The paper builds on known ingredients: that a PCN’s equilibrium errors implement the backward pass (Whittington & Bogacz, 2017; Millidge et al., 2022), the spring-clamped free phase of GradEP (Gower, 2026), and the linearised nudge of Dual Propagation (Høier et al., 2023). Its contributions are the injection-term construction that turns a scalar-output network into one whose visible layer carries −∇xV (Proposition 1); the joint γ–β window and its derivation (App. C, Ta- ble 3); and the demonstration that these compose into competitive generation on CIFAR-10, where prior backpropagation-free generative models remain at MNIST scale. Where a backward pass remains in training. The EP arm’s parameter updates in both phases come from measurements at equilibrium. Its phase-2 negatives, however, were sampled by a feedfor- ward twin of the network using autograd velocities, since relaxing 200 Langevin steps per negative was beyond our budget. No parameter gradient passes through the twin, and the two velocity fields agree to a cosine similarity of 1 to nine decimal places (Section 4.1), so we expect replacing it with relaxation changes the negatives by an amount below the sampler’s own noise, though we did not test this. “Without backpropagation” in Section 5 refers to how the weights were updated, not to every computation the training loop performed. The hardware-plausible architecture is an existence proof. The norm- and attention-free net- work of Section 5.1 is wider than the reference, used a different schedule, and was trained and sampled feedforward only. Its FID of 3.51 shows that neither component is needed for competitive generation; it does not show that they can be removed at matched cost, nor that the resulting network trains under EP. Both are left to future work, as is the run-to-run variance of every arm in Table 2, each of which is a single run.
