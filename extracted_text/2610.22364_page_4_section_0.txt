During training, T2-Z alternates between two stages: (1) world-model optimiza- tion, which learns shared action-conditioned dynamics with environment latents fixed, and (2) environment- latent optimization, which optimizes the latents through prediction error with the world model frozen. (3) At test time, the world model remains frozen while a new environment latent is directly optimized from interaction and then reused to predict the outcomes of different actions. We introduce T2-Z (fig. 2 & algorithm 1), a framework for adapting robotic world models to hidden environmental variation through direct optimization of an environment latent. Our key idea is to sep- arate the general visual predictive capability of a world model from unobserved environment-specific information. During training, we learn these two components through alternating optimization un- der a shared flow-matching objective. At test time, we freeze the world model and optimize only a newly initialized environment latent from observed test-time interaction trajectories. The optimized latent subsequently conditions the frozen world model to predict the outcomes of diverse actions. 3.1 PROBLEM SETUP We consider a robotic world model that predicts future visual observations y from the observation x and action a.
