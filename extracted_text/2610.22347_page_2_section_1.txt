Diffusion models have enabled high-quality image and video generation, with recent video models achieving realistic appearance, natural motion, and faithful prompt following (Team Wan et al., 2025; Kong et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025b; NVIDIA et al., 2026; Team Seedance et al., 2026). Autoregressive (AR) video generators extend these capabilities to streaming generation (Yin et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025a; Liu et al., 2025b; Zhu et al., 2026; Lu et al., 2025) and interactive world models (Team et al., 2026; Gao et al., 2026; He et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026; Seo et al., 2026; AlayaWorld Team et al., 2026). Within a video, however, different regions may require different amounts of refinement. Some may readily reproduce familiar content from the history, while others must form newly appearing structures or resolve fine details. This raises a fundamental question: Does every token need to be denoised until the final step? We study this question in distilled few-step AR video generators, whose intermediate predictions can already serve as plausible video outputs. Self Forcing (Huang et al., 2025) uses intermediate clean estimates as outputs for distribution-matching training (Yin et al., 2024b;a), making them natural candidates for early commitment. We observe that many tokens already approximate the generator’s own full-step output in the initial steps, while others continue changing until the last step (Figure 2). These differences often follow intuitive patterns. Regions that reproduce previously observed con- tent can settle early, while nearby regions continue refining appearance or motion. Similar variation arises in world models as actions change the observed content. These observations motivate a simple principle: stop denoising tokens once they have converged, and spend the remaining computation only where refinement is still needed. We introduce Speculative Forcing, a framework that predicts when each token’s estimate has suf- ficiently converged, allowing individual tokens to be accepted at different steps (Figure 1). Each intermediate clean estimate serves as a draft of the generator’s full-step output. A verifier deter- mines whether to accept the draft or continue refining it. Once accepted, the estimate is fixed as the token’s output and receives no further denoising updates, while retaining its cached keys and values as context for the remaining tokens. This requires predicting whether a token’s current draft is already close to its full-step output without running the remaining denoising steps. We address this challenge with two verifiers. Because drafts are clean latent estimates, similarity to previously generated content and changes between consecutive estimates of the same token provide evidence of convergence without additional training. Our training-free verifier uses similarity to clean history or reference content at the first step and stability between consecutive drafts at later steps. We also introduce a learned verifier for demanding settings such as world models, leveraging contextual cues encoded in the pretrained generator’s intermediate features. A shallow head trained with the generator frozen predicts each token’s gap to the full-step output, showing that the interme- diate features contain cues about how much its predictions will change with further denoising. Both verifiers support token-wise acceptance without modifying the generator’s weights. We evaluate Speculative Forcing on LongLive (Yang et al., 2025a) and the world model Lingbot- World 2.0 (Gao et al., 2026). On both short and long videos, our method maintains the quality of the four-step generator with approximately 1.7 denoising steps per token, running over 1.5× faster at 41.7 and 37.8 fps. On controllable world model rollouts, a harder setting where camera motion continually changes the visible content, it maintains quality with approximately 2.5 denoising steps per token, running 1.35× faster at 24.5 fps. 2
