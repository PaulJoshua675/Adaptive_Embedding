In the Core Chunks + Full Context representation, each core chunk is enriched with the same contextual information used in Hierarchical Chunks: the document title, summary, and titles of all enclosing structural units. In our example, Chunk 2 is enriched with the metadata, whereas Chunks 4 and 8 are also enriched with the title of the chapter I. The Core Chunks + Metadata strategy limits the enrichment to the document metadata. Chunks 2, 4, and 8 are all enriched only with [Bill No. x/xxxx | Summary of the Law.], and information describing the hierarchical position of the article, such as chapter, section, or subsection titles, is omitted. Finally, the Core Chunks + No Context representation resembles traditional passage seg- mentation, as each chunk is indexed using only its original text, without any additional context. We selected these models based on previous evaluations on Brazilian legal test collections. BM25 achieved the highest nDCG@10 on both NormasTCU and Ulysses-RFCorpus in the Juá Benchmark [8]1. Among the dense retrievers evaluated in the same benchmark, Qwen3-Embedding-4B achieved the highest MRR on these collections [8]. We also include jua-4B-mixed, a domain-adapted embed- ding model developed for Brazilian legal information retrieval that has achieved state-of-the-art results on some Brazilian legal test collections [25]. Finally, we include text-embedding-3-small because it has also shown competitive performance on some Brazilian legal retrieval tasks [21]. Each document Di is represented by a set of chunks based on the representation strategies described in Section 3.3: Di = {ci,1, ci,2, . . . , ci,ni}, where ci,j denotes the j-th chunk of document Di and ni is the number of chunks in the document. During indexing, every chunk is treated as an independent retrieval unit. Given a query q, the retrieval model scores each chunk: score(q, ci,j). To map the retrieved chunks to their source document, we define the document score as the highest score among its retrieved chunks: score(q, Di) = max{score(q, cij)} [11]. Documents are then ranked according to their scores.
