To test whether the intrinsic–extrinsic divergence is an artifact of a particular system implementation, corpus version, or backbone model, we evaluate four configurations spanning two factors: sys- tem configuration (simpler vs. main) and corpus (pre-release vs. the publicly released set), plus the main configuration with an upgraded backbone model. The simpler system on the pre-release corpus gives MAD = 0.164 ± 0.027, Di- vRate = 43.3%, signed divergence +0.088 ± 0.034, and κ = 0.104. Holding the corpus fixed (pre-release) but upgrading to the main system configuration shifts intrin- sic accuracy up most sharply among all four configura- tions (57.6% →61.1%) and raises DivRate to its highest value (49.4%, MAD = 0.181 ± 0.022, signed divergence +0.126 ± 0.025). Switching to the publicly released corpus on top of the main system configuration (used for all other results in this paper) pulls DivRate back down to 43.6% (MAD = 0.168±0.025, signed divergence +0.118±0.029, κ = 0.118), close to the simpler-system starting point; the system upgrade and the corpus release thus move DivRate in opposite directions and partially cancel. Finally, re-scoring the main configuration with an upgraded backbone model gives MAD = 0.158 ± 0.035, DivRate = 39.7%, signed divergence +0.121 ± 0.036, and κ = 0.035. Across all four configurations, intrinsic accuracy remains in a narrow 57.6–61.4% band, extrinsic accuracy is essentially flat (48.5– 49.2%), and signed divergence stays positive throughout; pass/fail agreement stays near chance in every configuration, falling further under the upgraded backbone, consistent with weak-to-absent agreement rather than a single stable value. The configuration used for the main results in this paper adds retrieval relevance filtering, concurrent fact-update han- dling, and improved large-document extraction; the simpler- system ablation lacks these refinements, which accounts for its modestly lower intrinsic accuracy. All configurations im- plement the same extract-embed-classify-synthesize pattern (Figure 1). The divergence therefore persists across all four, with full statistics in Appendix H. 6.3 Diagnostic Decomposition A key advantage of intrinsic evaluation is diagnostic decom- position: when a system fails, we can identify which pipeline stage is responsible: • Extraction failures: Claims present in artifacts but not captured by the system’s observation layer. • Synthesis failures: Claims extracted but incorrectly merged, duplicated, or lost during fact aggregation. • Update failures: State transitions missed or false updates applied. • Conflict failures: Contradictions not detected or resolved incorrectly. Table 4 reveals three co-occurring failure patterns that in- tensify with difficulty. First, the claim miss rate rises from 58% at L1 to 88% at L3, indicating that subtle or embedded claims are increasingly lost during extraction. Second, fact count inflates from 9.1 to 20.7, showing the system fragments knowledge into many small, partially redundant facts rather than synthesizing them into coherent evolving beliefs. Third, classification accuracy degrades from 37% to 10%, mean- ing the system rarely identifies the correct action (create vs. update vs. reinforce) at higher difficulty. These three signals are mutually reinforcing: poor classification leads to creating new facts instead of updating existing ones (inflating count), which in turn fragments claims across facts (increasing miss rate on claim-level matching). None of these diagnostics are available from the extrinsic QA score, which remains flat at ∼50% regardless of the underlying failure pattern. 7 Discussion and Limitations Complementary, Not Replacement. Intrinsic evaluation does not replace extrinsic evaluation, it complements it. Ex- trinsic captures end-to-end utility as intrinsic enables diag- nosis. A system scoring high on both is genuinely effective; high extrinsic but low intrinsic signals fragility (dependent on LLM compensation); high intrinsic but low extrinsic in- dicates a retrieval or generation bottleneck. Synthetic Document Validity. Generated documents may not capture all properties of real multi-author text. We miti- gate this through style grounding, multi-model rotation, and hybrid injection at L3. Future work should validate transfer via rank correlation with human-annotated real document sequences. Scope and Limitations. FEB-30 targets factual evolution across multi-author artifacts. It does not evaluate episodic memory, procedural memory, or single-turn conversational recall, capabilities addressed by existing benchmarks. The benchmark contains 30 trajectories, sufficient for diagnostic evaluation but not for fine-grained statistical comparisons. Scoring relies on embedding similarity thresholds and LLM judges, introducing potential measurement noise. 8
