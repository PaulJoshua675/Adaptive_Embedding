These symptoms arise from the selective loss of hypothalamic neurons producing hypocretin and orexin, which destabilizes transitions between wakefulness, NREM, and REM sleep [26, 11, 34]. Addressing these clinical discrepancies requires computational models capable of bridging micro- scopic synaptic and neuromodulatory mechanisms with macroscopic sleep architecture. Traditional computational models of sleep have largely existed in isolation: phenomenological differential equations modeling macroscopic circadian and homeostatic sleep propensity [5, 10, 1], lumped-rate models of hypothalamic switching [32, 33, 27], or biophysical spiking neural networks (SNNs) composed of leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neurons focused on local cortical oscillations during brief sleep spindles or slow waves [12, 17]. Prior modeling efforts have not united these biophysical scales into a continuous, multi-day simulation capable of reproducing distinct human dyssomnias within an identical environmental photoperiod (the structured daily cycle of ambient light and dark exposure, here configured as a 16-hour light/8-hour dark schedule). In this work, we present DyssomniaNN, an end-to-end multi-scale biophysical computational framework implemented in PyTorch. Our contributions are summarized as follows: 1. We formulate a hierarchical biophysical model coupling Borbély’s two-process regulation with Saper’s reciprocal hypothalamic flip-flop switch and a corticothalamic LIF network across continuous multi-day photoperiods (72.0 h). 2. We parameterize five distinct clinical phenotypes: Healthy Normal Sleep, Circa- dian Rhythm Sleep-Wake Disorder (CRSWD) of the delayed-phase type, Chronic Psychophysiological Insomnia (abbreviated in this paper as simply “Chronic Insomnia”), Paradoxical Insomnia (Sleep State Misperception), and Narcolepsy Type 1. 3. We validate emergent corticothalamic electroencephalography (EEG) spectra against em- pirical human PSG recordings from the PhysioNet Sleep-EDF database [18, 19], achieving quantitative spectral convergence across Delta, Theta, Alpha, Sigma, and Beta bands. 4. We formulate the Well-Rested Index (R ∈[0, 100%]), demonstrating that perceived sleep restoration is dissociated from TST and is governed by the preservation of slow-wave sleep against cortical hyperarousal and circadian phase misalignment. 2
