A. Medication Recommendation EHR-based MR aims to recommend personalized, clinically safe medication combinations. Existing approaches include longitudinal modeling with RETAIN and DMNC [10], [11], time-aware modeling with MR-DTR [12], and DDI-aware recommendation with GAMENet, SafeDrug, and MoleRec [1], [2], [13]. DKINet, TCMPR, and SECGI [14]–[16] enhance patient representations with external knowledge or semantics. However, most methods target single-center settings and may suffer from negative transfer across hospitals. B. Cross-Domain Transfer and Multi-Center Learning CDR and multi-center transfer alleviate data sparsity through knowledge sharing. EMCDR [5] and DisenCDR [17] align domain representations, while TEMPT [7] adapts to hospitals through pre-training and prompt-tuning. However, general CDR overlooks combination therapy and DDI risks, while medical transfer lacks explicit consensus–preference disentanglement, risking negative transfer. C. LLM Agents and Knowledge-Augmented Healthcare LLMs support clinical decision-making through semantic reasoning [18]. Knowledge-enhanced methods include multi- agent collaboration (MedAgents, MDTeamGPT [19], [20]) and retrieval- or tool-augmented reasoning (ClinicalGPT, TxAgent, JointCoder, SOAPTriage [21]–[24]). However, their prompt- based knowledge integration often lacks explicit separation of general medical safety principles from hospital-specific execution rules for multi-center MR. III. PRELIMINARIES A. Multi-Center Electronic Health Records Data Multi-center EHR data comprise independent ICU records from multiple hospitals. Let H = {h1, . . . , h|H|} denote the hospital set, and D, P, O, and M the diagnosis, procedure, abnormal laboratory signal, and standardized medication vo- cabularies, respectively. The i-th record is represented as: ri = (hi, Di, Pi, Oi, Mi), (1) where hi ∈H is the hospital identifier, Di ⊆D, Pi ⊆P, Oi ⊆ O, and Mi ⊆M denote the observed diagnoses, procedures, abnormal laboratory signals, and medications, respectively. B. Clinical Anchors The clinical anchor set for EHR instance i comprises ob- served diagnoses, procedures, and abnormal laboratory signals: Ai ={(diag, d) | d ∈Di} ∪{(proc, p) | p ∈Pi} ∪{(lab, o) | o ∈Oi}. (2) Each anchor a ∈Ai represents a typed clinical signal: a diagnosis, procedure, or abnormal laboratory state. C. Drug-Drug Interaction Knowledge We denote the drug-drug interaction knowledge as a graph: ΩDDI = (M, EDDI), (3) where M is the standardized medication vocabulary and EDDI ⊆M × M contains potentially interacting medication pairs. Each node represents a medication, and each edge (u, v) represents a potential interaction with severity score sev(u, v).
