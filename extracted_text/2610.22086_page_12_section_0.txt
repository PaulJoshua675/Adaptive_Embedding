C.2 Multi-PersonaIF: Incomplete Instructions and Constraint Sensitivity Multi-PersonaIF exhibits one of the lowest agree- ment values (0.88 in French) and the highest pro- portion of C-rated entries across datasets (up to 25% in Italian and 18% in French). Analysis of annotator comments reveals two main sources of error. Constraint Violations. PersonaIF instructions include explicit formal constraints (e.g., “write in lowercase,” “include the e vowel in every word”). Annotators differed in how strictly they enforced partial or borderline violations: some accepted mi- nor deviations, while others applied stricter criteria. This reflects inherent variability in constraint-based evaluation tasks. Incomplete Instructions. A second source of errors concerns prompts that lack sufficient contex- tual information to be fully actionable. For exam- ple, an instruction may ask “How many awards did the actress win?” without specifying the actress, or refer to an unspecified entity. Annotators dif- fered in how they handled such cases, with some treating them as invalid and others proceeding with implicit interpretation. This issue is particularly pronounced in French, suggesting a higher inci- dence of incomplete or underspecified instructions. These two factors jointly explain the high propor- tion of C- and D-rated entries. Constraint violations reflect the inherent difficulty of multi-constraint generation, while incomplete instructions indicate a data quality issue that can be mitigated via im- proved prompt design and post-hoc filtering or re- generation. C.3 Multi-TableGPT: Language-Specific Translation Challenges The Spanish subset of Multi-TableGPT exhibits a substantially higher C/D-rate (24%) compared to other languages within the same dataset (German 2%, Italian 2%, French 3%). This discrepancy is notably larger than variations observed across other datasets, suggesting a language-dependent effect in the translation and generation process. 12
