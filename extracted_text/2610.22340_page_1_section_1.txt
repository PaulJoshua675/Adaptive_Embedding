Humans, especially infants and children, develop dexterity from playing [1]. Play offers a task-agnostic source of interaction. Similarly, for robots, learning via playing holds tremendous potential for learning a rich physical understand- ing of the world required for dexterous manipulation without human supervision. In practice, however, play is a difﬁcult behavior to engineer. Prior works model play by manually deﬁning explicit heuristics as guidance [2], [3], [4] and by engineering restrictive safety boundaries to prevent hardware damage [5]. However, play is inherently intuitive and difﬁcult to formal- ize with hand-crafted rules, while manual safety constraints require excessive tuning that limits scalability. In this work, our core hypothesis is that play is a behavior that can be modeled as a ‘goal-agnostic’ state-conditioned probabilistic model in a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and therefore be learned through a standard behavior cloning pipeline. In other words, even though a safe play behavior is hard to be deﬁned explicitly with heuristics, it can easily be learned implicitly through demonstration data. Therefore, we propose DSP: Dexterity from Supervised Play, a framework that turns minutes of human play into a policy that can continue collecting robot interaction au- tonomously. An action-conditioned world model learns from these data, while the same play policy provides candidate action sequences for model-predictive control (MPC). Our primary contribution is a framework for learning dex- terous manipulation from data collected by an autonomous play policy. We evaluate how autonomous play supports world-model learning and goal-directed planning through four technical contributions: • Supervised play. We formulate supervised play as a mechanism for turning a limited human interaction prior into autonomous contact-rich data collection. • Scaling autonomous play data. We show that held-out world-model prediction improves monotonically as the real-robot autonomous-play dataset grows from 0.5 to 5 hours. • Play policy as an action prior. We show that using play policy as an action-proposal model for MPC sig- niﬁcantly improves manipulation performance. • Self-play policy distillation.
