Strictly speaking, for a fixed prompt x, the estimator in (5) is only unbiased for the inner expectation over y ∼ˆπ(· | x) in (4). It is unbiased for ∇J(θ) when the prompt x is drawn from D. This is what happens in practice, where a batch of prompts is sampled and the per-prompt estimators are averaged. For brevity, we will assume that x is drawn from D in all subsequent discussions unless mentioned otherwise. 2.1.2 Policy gradient with a baseline To reduce the variance of sample-average approximation, one important technique is introducing a baseline (Greensmith et al., 2004) to the reward in ∇J(θ). Let b be a baseline, which may be random and may depend on the prompt x. It turns out that ∇J(θ) remains unchanged after b is introduced to R(x, y), i.e., ∇J(θ) = Ex∼D,y∼ˆπ(·|x) πθ(y | x) ˆπ(y | x) (R(x, y) −b) ∇θ log πθ(y | x)  provided that b is conditionally independent of y given x.
