Transition-specific us- age memory: each edge stores the conversations that transition was taken in, telling apart candi- dates whose descriptions do not. State-dependent weighting: history and semantics are not equally informative, so a learned per-step blend sets how much each should count at any step. Where a reg- istry’s calls concentrate in generic utility tools that no single state predicts, TRACER+G unions a small global set into every candidate set. On a mixed τ 2 environment spanning retail and telecom domains, TRACER paired with GPT-5.4 outperforms the standalone frontier GPT-5.4 model by 4.7 points in tool-selection accuracy, 57.2% to 61.9%, all while cutting prompt tokens per step by 80% (Figure 1). Running a full turn on its own selections, TRACER matches full-registry task suc- cess, 58.3% against 58.2%, despite presenting only 23% of the tools at any time and cutting per-step tokens by 61%. Similar results largely hold across five other environments, four models, and budgets k from 3 to 30 (§5). Contributions. • A comprehensive state-conditioned retrieval front-end.
