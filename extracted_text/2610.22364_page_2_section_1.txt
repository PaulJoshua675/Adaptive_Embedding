What a robot observes is insufficient to determine how the world will respond. Beneath visually similar scene configurations reside hidden environmental factors, such as friction, mass distribution, and unobserved human-defined interaction rules, that can fundamentally alter the consequences of the same action (fig. 1 (a)). Such factors are often revealed only through interaction with the target environments, as their influence becomes observable through the outcomes they induce. Explicitly representing these factors (Kumar et al., 2021), however, requires specifying in advance what factors may vary, their exact values, and how they should be parameterized, which is difficult to scale to unknown or compounded environmental variation. On the contrary, humans reason through “analy- sis by synthesis” (Yuille & Kersten, 2006), using internal generative models not only to anticipate future outcomes, but also in reverse to infer latent structure that best accounts for what they observe. This raises a natural question: can hidden environmental factors be similarly captured in a latent representation that is directly optimized from test-time interactions? We introduce T2-Z for test-time optimization of environmental latents, which augments an action- conditioned robotic world model with a learnable environment latent ze. ze is a randomly-initialized compact representation that is expected to encode the unobserved factors governing how an envi- ronment responds to interaction, with no supervision about what environmental factors it should represent. The world model predicts future visual observations conditioned jointly on the action and ze. We iteratively optimize the world model and ze in an alternating two-stage procedure: 1) world-model optimization: with ze fixed, we train the world model across all trajectories to learn shared action-conditioned dynamics across environments; 2) environment-latent optimization: with the world model frozen, we optimize the conditioned latent ze through prediction error, so that it captures underlying environment-specific variation not explained by the general world model alone. Both stages are optimized with the same flow-matching loss and alternated for multiple rounds until convergence, allowing the environment representation to emerge directly through prediction without predefined physical-factor supervision or a separate environment encoder. At test time, the world model is frozen and T2-Z turns environment inference into a direct opti- mization problem. For a previously unseen environmental configuration, we initialize a new ze and optimize ze to explain the outcomes of one or more observed test-time interactions. Specifically, we update ze for multiple optimization steps through the same flow-matching objective used dur- ing stage-two training, minimizing prediction error on the observed interactions. Because inference is optimization-based rather than amortized through a learned history encoder, T2-Z is not tied to the trajectory count used for training and can flexibly incorporate any number of test-time trajecto- ries. Once optimized, the latent turns the frozen world model into an environment-adapted test-time simulator, enabling prediction and comparison of candidate action outcomes before execution. We evaluate T2-Z across 7 simulated settings and 4 real-world robotic tasks. Quantitatively and qualitatively, T2-Z improves world-model prediction across visual quality, physical prediction met- rics (e.g., relevant object locations), and downstream action selection. T2-Z reduces physical pre- diction error from 12.86 to 4.80 in simulation and tracked-state error from 19.30 to 10.85 pixels in the real world, while improving action success from 37.9% to 74.2% and from 26.5% to 70.8%, respectively. PCA of the learned latents reveals structured representations that reflect the underly- ing environmental factors, including a multi-dimensional organization when more than one factors vary jointly, despite the model never knowing how many factors are present or what they represent (fig. 1(b)).
