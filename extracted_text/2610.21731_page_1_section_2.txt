Deployed LLM assistants such as ChatGPT (Ope- nAI, 2024) often use web search to access up-to- date information. However, retrieving relevant search results does not guarantee that models use the information correctly. Even when updated evi- dence is available, search-augmented LLMs may still produce outdated answers or cite sources that contradict their responses (Figure 1). This mat- ters because citations lend answers provenance. This raises an important question: Why do search- augmented LLMs fail to use available evidence? However, how search- augmented LLMs use web search results has re- ceived less attention, and it is unclear why these models fail even when relevant evidence is avail- able. To this end, we construct a dataset of post-cutoff temporal facts, whose values changed after the de- ployed model’s training cutoff. We build a deter- ministic and regenerable pipeline that generates these facts from primary sources, and draw 225 facts from its output, across six domains including sports, finance and medicine.1 Each fact has an outdated value and a current value. This lets us identify outdated answers, test whether models use updated evidence correctly and check whether the cited pages support the models’ claims. We first study a proprietary LLM under its na- tive web search to identify failures in a deployed setting. We then test whether these failures recur under controlled retrieval, giving the same propri- etary LLM and six open-weight model configura- tions an identical menu of frozen search results. Our experiments reveal two main findings.
