Online Learning and LLM-Based Approaches: To narrow the simulation-to-reality gap in network control, recent work has explored online learning and Large Language Model (LLM) integration. Liu et al. propose EdgeSlice [17], a de- centralized deep RL framework for jointly orchestrating radio and computing resources under slice-level SLAs. For network automation, ALLSTAR [18] generates and validates RAN schedulers from high-level operator intents using LLMs. Other LLM-based efforts include TSpec [19], TelecomGPT [20], slice-aware resource allocation [21], and intent-based deploy- ment [22]. However, existing online-learning methods still rely on fixed neural architectures, while current LLM-based solutions often operate as static inference engines without continuous adaptation, closed-loop validation, or latency guar- antees suitable for sub-1s RIC control. BC Approaches: Behavioral Cloning (BC) has emerged as a promising alternative to address RL’s sample efficiency by learning from expert demonstrations [8], with applications in automated driving [23] and UAV detection [24]. However, ex- isting BC approaches require extensive manual demonstrations that are hard to collect at scale and lack reasoning integration. Our Approach. We address these limitations through three key aspects: (i) automated demonstration collection using dual- filtering BC to generate high-quality training data without manual intervention, (ii) integration of BC with transformer- based reasoning modules that provide natural-language ratio- nales for explainable control, and (iii) joint optimization of BC policies with agentic reasoning components for continuous adaptation in dynamic O-RAN environments. To the best of our knowledge, our work is the first to integrate automated BC within the O-RAN architecture for agentic RAN control. Our Contributions. The contributions of this paper are as follows: i) We design a novel eXplainable RAN intelligent controller, X-RIC, that integrates agentic AI with RL in a hybrid control architecture to enable sub-second decision- making based on natural-language intents. ii) We introduce a domain-specific evaluation framework, BARX, for assessing the suitability of agentic reasoning models in closed-loop telecom control. iii) We propose a dual-filtering BC mecha- nism with automated demonstration harvesting, using episode- level policy-impact gating and action-level critic validation to improve sample efficiency while ensuring deployment-grade reliability. iv) As a case study, we formulate a network slicing resource allocation problem minimizing operational cost while meeting SLA requirements, achieving 89% cost reduction versus baselines under Near-RT RIC timing constraints. X-RIC FRAMEWORK DESIGN We present the X-RIC framework integrating agentic reasoning into Near-RT RIC operations, introduce BARX for agentic RAN benchmarking, and describe dual-filtering demonstration collection for sample-efficient BC training. A. TLM Module and Benchmarking Our X-RIC framework, shown in Fig. 1(a), is integrated within the O-RAN near-RT RIC; see [25] for an architectural overview. To avoid the computational overhead of full LLM fine-tuning, X-RIC uses a Telecom Language Model (TLM) architecture that augments a frozen pre-trained LLM with: (i) domain-specific prompts encoding O-RAN operational knowledge, and (ii) a trainable RAN-state encoder that maps structured telemetry, including KPIs, slice states, and resource metrics, into 256-dimensional embeddings. The encoder is trained via contrastive alignment on state-text pairs to project RAN observations into the LLM representation space, en- abling RAN-aware reasoning without full model fine-tuning. Integrating agentic reasoning modules into O-RAN work- flows introduces system-level challenges due to the mismatch between probabilistic language model outputs and determin- istic telecom control requirements. Four critical challenges emerge: (i) Missed scheduling deadlines when TLM inference time exceeds the 10 ms to 1s latency budget for Near-RT RIC operations [25], (ii) Malformed control messages due to syntactically invalid JSON/YAML configurations, (iii) Non- deterministic behavior when identical network states produce different TLM outputs due to sampling randomness, and (iv) Unintended SLA degradation when semantically plausi- ble but operationally suboptimal TLM decisions reduce net- work performance. To address these challenges, we introduce a novel domain-specific framework, Benchmarking Agentic Reasoning (BARX), based on O-RAN specifications.
