Collective advantages, such as the dyadic advantage reported by Bahrami et al. (2010), suggest the possibility that the benefit of interaction dynamics can allow collectives of relatively simple agents to outperform a more computationally costly system. If so, collective interactions may be an evolutionarily favorable strategy for collective intelligence. The challenge of evolving ever more complex organisms may be overcome by instead evolving more complex interactions among relatively simple agents. Our model demonstrates how more efficient decision-making can emerge from larger ensembles of small networks rather than smaller ensembles of larger networks. Our finding of collective efficiency is partly due to the lack of scaling in reservoir networks like ESNs. Their recurrent dynamics are nonetheless useful for modeling biological and behavioral dynamics (Schubert & Gros, 2021), as well as predicting chaotic time series (Jaeger & Haas, 2004; Pathak et al., 2018). For instance, the synchronization of chaotic time series has been modeled using an ensemble reservoir approach similar to ours (Hu et al., 2022). However, the limited scalability of reservoirs has so far made them particularly favorable in machine learning applications where computational resources are limited, like edge computing (Kokalj-Filipovic et al., 2021). Some previous studies have shown that ensembles of ESNs can perform better on some machine learning benchmarks (Casanova et al., 2023), and our findings on ensemble scaling suggest future directions for research into ensemble reservoir computing. Most notably, our approach differs from previous efforts that use more complex topologies and means of collective processing (Qiao et al., 2016; Viehweg et al., 2025). Our model is less costly in terms of computation and yet yields substantial performance improvements. The model also lends itself to further improvement through ESN pre-training to create so-called "mixtures of experts" (Ghahramani & Hinton, 2000; Jacobs et al., 1991). This well-known approach to machine learning is highly effective across a wide range of applications, including large language models (Fedus et al., 2022; Shazeer et al., 2017) and convolutional neural networks that aggregate network outputs with adaptive soft mixture-of-experts (Hoang & Phan, 2026). It is also highly efficient because only small handfuls of experts may be needed to process input. In our model, further improvements may be gained by training low-cost ensembles of specialized ESNs. For example, negative correlation learning (Liu & Yao, 1999) is a technique in which neural networks are trained to produce negatively correlated outputs and it has been shown to yield better performance in ensemble reservoirs (Rodan & Tino, 2011). Along with mixtures of experts, this finding is consistent with evidence that crowd wisdom improves in maximally divergent crowds (Barrera-Lemarchand et al., 2024). Taken together, these studies along with ours motivate further research into collective performance and efficiency using interactions in reservoir ensembles. 5.4
