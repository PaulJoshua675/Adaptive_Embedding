Because the world model is fixed, prediction errors over the observed interactions can only be reduced by updating ztest. The latent is therefore optimized toward a representation under which the frozen world model reproduces the interaction outcomes observed in the current environment. Once optimized, z∗ test is reused to condition the world model for actions that were not observed during latent optimization. Given a different action a′, we predict its outcome as ˆy′ ∼pθ∗(y | x, a′, z∗ test) . (8) The resulting conditioned world model serves as an environment-adapted test-time simulator, allow- ing it to predict the consequences of different actions under the inferred environment. Unlike amortized history-encoder approaches, T2-Z uses direct optimization. This avoids commit- ting to a fixed history mapping and searches for the representation that best explains the current envi- ronment. The formulation can naturally incorporate any number of test-time trajectories that differs from training, by optimizing a latent against all available evidence. Moreover, T2-Z is not restricted to the range of factor values during training, providing greater flexibility under out-of-distribution configurations. Ablation in fig. 5 shows this optimization remains stable across varying test-time trajectory numbers, and is interaction-efficient with a single trajectory sufficient in most settings. 4 EXPERIMENTS 4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP Tasks We evaluate T2-Z across 7 simulated settings and 4 real-world robotic tasks. In simulation, Friction requires predicting how far an object moves after a push under different surface friction; Gravity varies gravitational strength and requires predicting the object motion; Mass Collision varies colliding object mass and predicts the resulting collision motion. Light Switch assigns dif- ferent human-defined hidden interaction rules that determine whether a robot action turns the light on or off; Mass Balance predicts the tilt of a dumbbell under different mass distribution; Mass × Friction jointly varies object mass and surface friction at the same time; Diverse Visual Back- grounds evaluates the same friction variations across 30 diverse scene setups, testing whether the learned environment representation remains consistent across visual changes.. In the real world, Door Close varies the spring-lock stiffness, which changes how the door responds to closing ac- tions; Ball Friction requires predicting the motion of a pushed ball of unknown mass; Stick Bal- ance requires predicting the motion of a stick with objects of different masses attached to its ends; and Soft Pull requires predicting the deformation a soft object under different rotating actions. Implementation Details. We adapt BWM Team (2026), an action-conditioned world model built on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B. The environment latent has a dimension of 32. Training starts with 300 world-model updates, followed by 200 latent updates and alternating 200-200 updates. Stage I/II use LR 10−5 / 0.03 respectively. At test time, we freeze the world model and use a coarse-to-fine LR schedule of 3.0/1.5/0.5/0.15, 10 steps each, for 40 steps. T2-Z uses only one test-time trajectory on randomly-sampled action for Friction, Gravity, Mass Collision, Mass × Friction, Ball Friction, and Soft Pull, and two for Light Switch, Mass Balance, Door Close, and Stick Balance. Evaluation Metrics. (1) Physical Error measures task-relevant state prediction: object-location error in pixels for Friction, Gravity, Mass Collision, and Diverse Backgrounds; lamp activation error for Light Switch; and bar-tilt angle error in degrees for Mass Balance. For real-world tasks, we measure tracked-state error in pixels. We report Physical Error (Mean), averaged over predicted frames, and Physical Error (Final), measured at the final predicted frame. (2) Video Generation Quality is measured by LPIPS and PSNR between predicted and ground-truth videos. LPIPS mea- sures perceptual discrepancy in a learned feature space, capturing differences in visual structure and appearance, and serves as our primary measure of generation quality. In contrast, PSNR primarily reflects pixel-level reconstruction fidelity and is less informative about semantically meaningful dif- ferences or whether object motion is physically correct. (3) Action Success treats the world model as a simulator: given candidate actions, we predict their outcomes and select the action most likely to achieve the task goal, such as pushing a ball to a target, balancing a stick, or closing a door.
