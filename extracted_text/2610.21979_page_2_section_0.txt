P2TPO: Structure-Aligned Page-to-Token Credit Assignment for Full-Page Document Parsing We analyze this phenomenon at three levels. First, reference identity maintains contrasts between individual elements, including instances of the same type. Second, element-wise comparisons must account for missing outcomes and retain feedback when all outcomes are equally imperfect: an omitted element lacks a generated span, and centering tied scores diminishes its relative signal. Third, feedback must differentiate erroneous positions from correct content within a matched block, rather than assigning uniform credit across the block. Together, the four failure modes illustrated in Figure 1(b) highlight three key requirements: preserve element identity, complete the comparison outcomes, and control where credit is assigned. We introduce Page-to-Token Policy Optimization (P2TPO), which assigns local credit while retaining full- page generation and page-level feedback (Figure 1(c)). It first aligns each output to reference elements so that corresponding instances can be compared across rollouts. Unified Anchor Pricing (UAP) completes these comparisons with missing outcomes and uses a quality ceiling to retain feedback at imperfect consensus. Matched coefficients act on generated blocks; whole-element omissions act through existing boundary tokens. Span Credit Redistribution (SCR) then reallocates an eligible matched block’s coefficient toward aligned errors when negative and correct positions when positive, preserving its sign and total credit mass. The final update combines this local feedback with the shared page term. Our contributions are fourfold: • Reference-element credit assignment. We retain individual element identities across full-page rollouts and characterize the local quality contrasts that aggregation can erase under a fixed-weight additive page utility. • Complete outcome feedback. We propose the UAP, which outlines a unified set of rules for handling matches, managing missing values, and addressing uniformly imperfect outcomes. This includes a feedback mechanism for instances where an entire element is absent. • Budget-preserving intra-block redistribution. We developed the SCR mechanism, which reallocates block credits while preserving both the sign and the total amount of matched block credits. We proved the conservation of credits and specified the conditions under which informative masks can increase the allocation to the target position. • Empirical effectiveness and generality. Using a shared supervised checkpoint, matched update budgets, and a five-seed run, P2TPO outperforms four response-level baselines on all 13 primary splits across six benchmarks that include digital documents, scanned pages, and real-world photographs. In addition, across three different model and initialization settings, it achieves the highest mean in 37 out of 39 comparisons of splits and settings. 2
