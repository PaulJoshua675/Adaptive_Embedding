The Speech-Transcript Alignment Paradigm We compare four training configurations to evaluate speech– transcript alignment against explicit speech–document super- vision. Our default model uses LibriTTS speech-transcript pairs and the combined loss (Ours in Table 4). To separate the effects of supervision and data domain, we additionally train on synthesized HotpotQA spoken queries [12] using either speech-transcript alignment (w/ ASR) or speech-document pairs with the combined loss (w/ Q-D) and InfoNCE alone (w/ Q-D & I.). The results show that speech-transcript align- ment achieves the best retrieval performance, outperforming direct speech-document supervision. The frozen text embed- ding model already captures query–document relationships, allowing SSEmb to inherit this retrieval capability by directly matching speech to its transcript embedding. In contrast, spo- ken query–document training must bridge the modality gap indirectly through document-level relevance signals, making the learning objective more complex and difficult to optimize. 4.3. Dataset Scale Language-specific adapters trained on ASR corpora of vary- ing sizes achieve reasonable retrieval quality. To examine this relationship systematically, we vary the amount of En- glish ASR training data and measure its effect on retrieval quality. As shown in Table 4, performance degrades grace- fully as training data is reduced: down to approximately 10 hours, retrieval quality remains within a reasonable margin of the full setup, demonstrating that the alignment framework is data-efficient. Additionally, incorporating over 46k hours of diverse data from the Emilia dataset [20] further improves re- trieval performance. This indicates that the framework can ef- fectively leverage large-scale ASR resources when available. 4.4.
