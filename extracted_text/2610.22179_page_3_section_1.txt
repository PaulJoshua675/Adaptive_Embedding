GPU-accelerated lossless compression. Several methods have investigated using lossless compression on the GPU. The nvCOMP library (NVIDIA Corporation, 2026) is a GPU-optimized library that implements several widely used compression methods, including LZ4 (Collet, 2011), as well as proprietary methods for floating-point data such as Bitcomp and Cascaded. Fast GPU-optimized ANS coding (Duda, 2013) was explored by Weißenberger and Schmidt (2019). Huffman encoding has emerged as an efficient GPU compression and decompression method. Yamamoto et al. (2020) explored using gap arrays for efficient compression and decoding. Tian et al. (2021) explored optimizing the full Huffman encoding and decoding process on GPUs. Lossless compression for inference. Lossless weight compression techniques have been explored, such as DFloat11 (Zhang et al., 2025), which utilizes Huffman encoding with gap arrays for fast GPU decoding. ZipNN (Hershcovitch et al., 2025) uses similar principles. Both methods encode the exponent, with the encoding process done on the CPU. This allows for fast decoding, but encoding is comparatively slow. ZipServ (Fan et al., 2026) combines a tensor-core-aware bitmap format with ZipGEMM, which fuses decompression with matrix multiplication. Agrawal et al. (2026) and Lin et al. (2026) use GPU-native Huffman codes for network communication. Recently, SplitZip (Guo and Joshi, 2026) proposed lossless KV-cache compression by replacing exponents with 4-bit symbols and using escape values for rarer cases. While simple, its encoding achieves suboptimal compression. Appendix B.4 provides a comparison between LCT and SplitZip. 3 Compressibility in tensors 3.1 Exponent compressibility in floating point data The structure of floating-point tensors provides an opportunity for lossless compression. The BF16 data type consists of 1 sign bit, 8 exponent bits, and 7 mantissa bits (Burgess et al., 2019). Typical data is well described as the quantization of a concentrated continuous distribution, the sign and mantissa have high entropy, while the exponent has lower entropy. Compressing the exponent only is sufficient, for example, a standard Gaussian rounded to BF16 has exponent entropy 2.545 bits/symbol, while the full distribution’s entropy is 10.464 bits/symbol. Encoding the full BF16 value saves only 0.081 bits/value compared with compressing only the exponent. Appendix D.5 gives the calculation. Appendix D.3 explains why this is the case. 3.2 Structure in weights and activations The distributions encountered in neural networks are compressible. Figure 5 shows the exponent distributions of the six largest parameter groups in the Nemotron-H-8B LLM (Blakeman et al., 2025). All distributions are highly concentrated around their peaks. In each group, just 9–11 of the 256 exponent symbols account for at least 99% of the weights, with marginal entropy of 2.58–2.70 bits. The compressibility of the weights has been noted in several works (Hershcovitch et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025; Fan et al., 2026). A similar trend emerges in the activations. Figure 6 shows the exponent distributions of five Nemotron-H-8B activation groups, aggregated across layers and four 1024-token sequences. Again, the distributions are highly concentrated, though there is more variation. In particular, the squared ReLU activation has high sparsity, with 93.23% being exactly zero.
