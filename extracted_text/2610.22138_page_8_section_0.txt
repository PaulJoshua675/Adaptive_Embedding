Showing that conceding causes the switch would need a controlled intervention; in practice, however, a public concession signals an increased likelihood of switching. 4.6 Infection dynamics over rounds Figure 6 traces per-round private infection. Onset is fast: five of six models reach half their final in- fection within one round, and about 80% of the eventual infection is in place by round 4. Curves then plateau rather than climb, and models differ mainly in how high they plateau, from 31% (HY3) to 76% (GPT-OSS-120B); from round 3 on, HY3 and QWEN are the least infected. GPT-OSS-120B is the exception in speed: it is the least infected model after the first round (23%) but keeps climb- ing and ends the highest, so resisting longer delays the collapse without preventing it. With a single at- tacker, infection starts more slowly and ends lower in every model. The window for detection or inter- vention is therefore the first few rounds. 5 Implications for Discussion-Based Multi-Agent Systems Our results challenge a premise underlying multi- agent deployments such as debate, ensembling, and agentic RAG: that social redundancy provides ro- bustness. The failure is hard to manage in practice. Transcript monitoring reveals only about a third of infected turns. Most infection emerges within four rounds and rarely reverses, so reviewing a run after it ends comes too late. Choosing a more knowl- edgeable model does not help either, since baseline accuracy does not tell how a model abandons what it knows. We therefore recommend treating agent messages as untrusted input, adding an adversarial peer-pressure test to model selection, monitoring agents’ private answers rather than only their public messages, and intervening early. 6
