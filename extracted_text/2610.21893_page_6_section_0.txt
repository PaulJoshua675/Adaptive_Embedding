4 Experiments In this section, we evaluate goal-adaptive con- text compression on GACC-Bench, a multi-source benchmark spanning seven heterogeneous collec- tions. The complete release contains 500 goals, 281 unique source contexts, and 2,571 source-grounded questions. The complete benchmark data and eval- uation scripts are publicly available in the accom- panying anonymous repository. Detailed source-, goal-, and question-level distributions are provided in Appendix A.1 and Appendix A.2. We evalu- ate compression extrinsically through downstream source-grounded answerability. For each goal, its associated questions probe complementary infor- mation required to fulfill that goal. This proto- col measures whether compression preserves goal- relevant evidence under a fixed token budget rather than evaluating standalone summary quality. 4.1 Experimental Setup Benchmark. GACC-Bench is constructed from seven established long-context and domain-specific benchmarks: LongBench-v2 (LBv2) (Bai et al., 2025), HELMET (Yen et al., 2025), L-Eval (An et al., 2024), Loong (Wang et al., 2024), Meet- ingBank (MB) (Hu et al., 2023), LongCodeBench (LCB) (Rando et al., 2025), and SWE-Bench Ver- ified (SWE) (Jimenez et al., 2024). For the SWE- Bench source, we use agent trajectories collected from SWE-Bench Verified tasks with the Open- Hands harness (Wang et al., 2025). We use these abbreviations consistently throughout the paper. Models and comparison conditions. All exper- iments use a fixed DeepSeek-V4-Pro reader and judge. The reader answers each question from the provided context, while the judge evaluates the an- swer against the original source context, the ques- tion, and the reference answer. Keeping the reader and judge fixed provides a common evaluation set- ting for comparing compressors. Evaluation settings and metrics. We measure the token length of each source context using the cl100k_base tokenizer. Given a budget ratio r, the target budget is set to L = ⌊rN⌋, where N is the original context length, and L is provided explic- itly in the compression prompt. We report Micro accuracy, defined as the fraction of all questions answered correctly, and Macro accuracy, defined as the fraction of goals for which all associated questions are answered correctly. 4.2 Main Results Table 3 reports the performance of the fixed DeepSeek-V4-Pro reader under no-context and full- context conditions. Near-ceiling full-context ac- curacy, contrasted with low no-context accuracy, indicates that answering the benchmark questions generally requires source information. Table 2 re- ports compression results using a fixed DeepSeek- V4-Pro reader and judge. Larger retention budgets generally improve accuracy, but compressed con- texts remain substantially below the full-context reference, particularly in Macro accuracy. The tested compressors therefore recover answers to individual questions more reliably than they sup- port correct answers to every question associated with a goal. This persistent gap highlights the chal- lenge of retaining information across the different aspects of a shared goal, even when more tokens are available. Compression difficulty also varies across sources. LongBench-v2 and SWE-agent generally retain stronger answerability, whereas MeetingBank and L-Eval are more challenging un- der tight budgets. Model rankings can also differ between Micro and Macro accuracy, showing that strong aggregate question-level performance does not necessarily imply reliable goal-level coverage. 4.3 Goal Ablation Studies To isolate the effect of goal information during compression, we conduct a controlled ablation on GACC-Bench-testmini, which contains 124 goals, 62 source contexts, and 651 source-grounded ques- tions. Each context is associated with exactly two goals. In the Own goal condition, the compressor receives the target goal. In No goal, it receives no goal information. In Cross goal, it receives the alternative goal from the same context, while the reader is evaluated on the target-goal questions in all conditions. Table 4 reports overall Micro accu- racy under 5% and 10% retention budgets. Own goal consistently outperforms No goal and Cross goal across all models and budgets, with larger gains at the higher budget. Cross goal performs worst throughout, indicating that an unrelated goal can misdirect evidence selection rather than serve as a useful substitute. These results show that goal conditioning directs compression toward task- relevant evidence, although it does not by itself guarantee complete goal-level retention. 6
