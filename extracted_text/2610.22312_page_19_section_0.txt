A.4 Sequential computation and structured reasoning Formal and synthetic tasks study how neural models realize computations over sequences. Computation-graph analyses cover multiplication, logic-grid tasks, and dynamic programming (Dziri et al., 2023). Automata experiments show that the number of state transitions in a specification need not determine the computation a Transformer learns (Liu et al., 2023a); evaluations across the Chomsky hierarchy relate length generalization to task and architecture classes (Delétang et al., 2023). Permutation-composition studies combine behavioral evaluation with probes and activation patching to examine learned algorithms (Li et al., 2025a). Scratchpads instead expose intermediate computations as generated text for tasks including program execution (Nye et al., 2021). In multi-hop question answering, Wan et al. (2026) analyze single-pass reasoning through an information-theoretic capacity model and use it to motivate decomposition of interdependent evidence. Program analysis provides a setting in which intermediate claims have formal semantics. Mitchell et al. (2025) prompt LLMs to reason as abstract interpreters, evaluating the soundness of generated invariants and recurring reasoning errors. For natural-language queries, Qin et al. (2025) instead convert premises into logical representations and check them against retrieved sources before generating a response.
