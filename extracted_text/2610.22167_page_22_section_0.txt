Let these observations earn their place in the explanation. If the original overlooks a relationship cue, an intervening turn, a relevant activity, or a change from one emotional tone to another, add the missing connection where the interpretation needs it. Do not append a generic context summary. Timing markers are transcript metadata: refer to a relevant gap accurately in ordinary prose, outside conversational evidence tags, and explain what it changes. Do not recite every pause or introduce fixed time cutoffs. ## 2. Preserve the original text and its reasoning progression Keep supported original sentences verbatim and in their existing order. Preserve language, paragraph structure, contextual analysis, useful decomposition, valid quotes, and existing comparisons. Do not shorten, translate, reorganize, or polish sound material for stylistic preference. Do not remove valid background simply because you would have written a more concise explanation. The request for natural wording applies to newly inserted reasoning, not to polishing supported original sentences. Add missing relationship, timing, emotional-flow and label analysis beside the passage that needs it; do not replace the original analysis with a newly organized account. Treat improved fluency or extra coverage obtained by unnecessary rewriting as a failure. **Preservation also applies to a meaningful mistaken inference that can be followed by a useful correction .** Keep the original passage through its relevant initial conclusion, then insert the correction after that passage. Do not retroactively replace its label, change its commitment into a tentative possibility, or rewrite its supporting argument merely to make the entire explanation agree with the eventual answer. Before editing a meaningful mistaken branch, locate the shortest coherent original span containing the inference and its actual conclusion. Keep that original commitment; do not turn “”is into “initially ”seems or add “on that ”reading retrospectively. Repair invented quotations or factual transcription errors locally without using the repair to erase a still-intelligible mistaken conclusion. The subsequent check, not a rewrite of the initial commitment, must do the retracting. An earlier statement can remain incorrect as an explicitly reconsidered and retracted intermediate conclusion. Its subsequent correction must be unmistakable. This is different from leaving an error uncorrected or continuing to use it as a premise. Preserve a compact, intelligible mistaken branch, not a long accumulation of false claims. Insert the check after the relevant complete inference, before further reasoning continues from the updated understanding. Keep unrelated sound passages. If an old later sentence reasserts the rejected conclusion, revise that later sentence locally so it no longer undoes the correction. ## 3. Revise reasoning after that correction and the final answer as needed. | | A consequential misreading of speaker, referent, scope, time, or conversational function drives the conclusion | Make the rereading visible: identify the earlier reading, check the actual source, and explain the consequence. For every substantive deletion or replacement, identify the concrete defect and why insertion or local revision is insufficient. Brevity and stylistic preference are not sufficient reasons. Do not put this editing assessment into the training completion. ## 4. Build a substantive correction after the actual mistaken inference When the original contains a meaningful reasoning error, the inserted continuation must do all of the following: 1. **Identify the precise inference being reconsidered .** Point to what the preceding reasoning assumed or treated as sufficient evidence. Do not merely ask whether the label is correct. 2. **Check that inference.** Use the source and the exact task definition to identify the missing support, overlooked detail, contradiction, or distinction between alternatives. An already quoted passage may suffice; a new quotation is not mandatory. 3. **Explain the update.** State what remains supported, what must be withdrawn or qualified, and why. An unchanged answer can still require correction of its justification. 4. **Justify the intended conclusion independently.** Rejecting the first label does not establish the supplied ground-truth label. Identify the source evidence and defining conditions that support each label in `labels`. If those conditions cannot be supported, use the conflict procedure rather than selecting a different answer or fabricating support. 5. **Carry the update forward.** All reasoning after the correction and the final answer must use the revised understanding. Do not rewrite the earlier mistaken branch out of existence to achieve global agreement. Make the newly inserted correction sound like a person noticing something while reading. Pause naturally to reread the specific point rather than starting with an editorial diagnosis. For example, a brief “Wait, let me read that exchange ”again may fit an English explanation; use an equally natural move in the original language when useful. This illustrates a rereading move, not a required phrase or a phrase bank. A short rereading turn can make the change natural, but the important move is to return to the actual words: who said this, what had just happened, or what the target is replying to. Use the existing language and voice. Do not announce an editing operation with phrases such as “I need to correct the reading”, describe an inference as being formally “withdrawn”, or turn the passage into a grading report. Do not insert a stock transition in every row. For a real error, preserve the original commitment, naturally reopen the point, check the specific source detail, and let the change in understanding follow. For missing support without an error, simply supply the connection; no artificial reversal is needed. Do not introduce the revised label first and then backfill a convenient justification.
