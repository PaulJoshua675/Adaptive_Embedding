The score features in- dicate whether a candidate is strongly supported by each view, while the overlap features provide the query-level context needed to interpret that support. The overlap values are constant for all candidates of the same query, but they can still affect within-query ranking through the nonlinear scorer: the same score pattern can be mapped differently when the views are highly redundant than when they retrieve mostly complementary result sets. More formally, let s(q, d) contain the three document-level score features and let o(q) contain the three query-level overlap features. The scorer has the conditional form sθ(q, d) = fθ(s(q, d), o(q)). (5) Although o(q) is constant within a query, the nonlinear scorer allows it to change the local sensitivity ∂sθ(q, d)/∂ˆsv(q, d) to each view-specific score. Thus, overlap is not treated as direct document relevance evidence. It conditions how the model interprets candidate-level retrieval evidence. Section 4.5 verifies this behavior through linear controls, overlap perturbations, and input-gradient analysis. 3.3 Fusion Scorer XViewRank uses a small multilayer perceptron to map the score-overlap feature vector to a relevance score: sθ(q, d) = fθ(x(q, d)). (6) The model has two hidden layers with 128 and 64 hidden units, ReLU activa- tions, and dropout rate 0.2. It is intentionally much smaller than a text-based reranker. The goal is not to model full semantic matching from passage text, but to combine retrieval evidence across views. 3.4 Training Objective The fusion scorer is trained with weighted query-balanced binary cross-entropy. During training, Dq is the union of the top-1,000 BM25 results from the three views. A candidate is positive if it has a positive MS MARCO relevance label for the query, and unlabeled candidates in the pool are treated as negative. Let yq,d ∈{0, 1} denote this label. Since sparse retrieval candidate pools contain far more negatives than positives, we use a capped positive-class weight. Let P and N be the total numbers of positive and negative training candidates in the training pool. The positive-class weight is γ = min(N/P, 200). For a positive candidate, we multiply γ by 1/ log2(rmin + 2), where rmin is the best rank of the candidate among the three view-specific ranked lists; negative candidates receive weight 1. Let wq,d denote the resulting example weight. The loss is: L = 1 |Q| X q∈Q 1 |Dq| X d∈Dq wq,d BCE(yq,d, sθ(q, d)). (7)
