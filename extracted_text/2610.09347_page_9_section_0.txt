Table 4 is the measurement the paper turns on. It reports the order of nonlinearity of the representation evolution produced, over the successful runs only: an unsuccessful run says nothing about the order that would have been required, so an unconditional median would understate the constraint at high order by averaging in representations that never worked. This is Proposition 2 realised: an affine map cannot raise the order of its input, so a run succeeds only if evolution has supplied the target’s order in full. The tree based learners succeed with order two or three throughout. The difference between the two is the order of nonlinearity that has moved into the learner, and panel (b) of Figure 5 is the price of not being able to move it. Three further readings support the same conclusion. Taken unconditionally rather than over successful runs, LaSER-LR and LaSER-Tree both give median order 3 on PAR6, yet their mean accuracies are 0.512 and 0.925: the representations are of equal order, so the entire difference is what the learner does with them.
