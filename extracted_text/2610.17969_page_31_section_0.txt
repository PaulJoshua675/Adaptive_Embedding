Expanding the invariance identity and comparing the coefficient of each ℓi gives wi(D(t)S) = e−a⊤ i twi(S). We now solve this covariance equation completely. Define βi(S) := Siwi(S). Because the ith coordinate of D(t)S is ea⊤ i tSi, the covariance equation is equivalent to βi(D(t)S) = βi(S) for all S ∈Rm + and t ∈RK. Pass to logarithmic coordinates y := log S ∈Rm and put bi(y) := βi(ey), with the exponential also componentwise. Since log(D(t)S) = y + A⊤t, this identity says precisely that bi is constant on every affine orbit y + im A⊤. We now identify those orbits without any regularity assumption on w. The columns of N span ker A, so ker N ⊤= (im N)⊥= (ker A)⊥= im A⊤, where the last equality is the finite-dimensional fundamental theorem of linear algebra. Therefore N ⊤y = N ⊤y′ ⇐⇒ y′ −y ∈im A⊤. Thus the fibers of y 7→N ⊤y are exactly the G-orbits. Since N has full column rank, N ⊤: Rm →Rm−s is surjective. We may define, for each z ∈Rm−s, ψi(z) := bi(y) for any y satisfying N ⊤y = z. The orbit characterization makes this definition independent of the chosen y, and the definition yields βi(S) = ψi(N ⊤log S), wi(S) = 1 Si ψi(N ⊤log S), which proves necessity of Eq. E.4. Conversely, let the weights have the form in Eq. E.4. Since AN = 0, we have N ⊤log(D(t)S) = N ⊤log S + N ⊤A⊤t = N ⊤log S. The argument of every ψi is invariant, while the prefactor transforms as (D(t)S)−1 i = e−a⊤ i tS−1 i . Hence the coefficient covariance holds, and expanding the scalarization proves the invariance identity. This direction establishes sufficiency and completes the characterization. Finally, for any n ∈ker A, m Y j=1 (D(t)S)nj j = exp(t⊤An) m Y j=1 Snj j = m Y j=1 Snj j . Taking logarithms gives n⊤log S. Conversely, every invariant linear combination of the log-scales has coefficient vector in ker A, so the coordinates supplied by a basis N exhaust the dimensionless monomial groups.
