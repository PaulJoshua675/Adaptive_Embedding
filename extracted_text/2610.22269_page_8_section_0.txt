Over-the-Air Testbed Implementation To validate simulation results through OTA deployment, we implement a testbed with three main components (Fig. 9). i) RAN and control: OpenAirInterface gNB using USRP B210 SDR interfaces with FlexRIC near-RT RIC via E2 for closed-loop slice control. KPIs stream to a Kinetica real-time accelerated database; TLM is based on L3-8B with LangChain tools.ii) 5G core: OAI 5G core (AMF, SMF, UPF) configured for network slicing and per-slice policy enforcement. iii) 5G UEs: Sierra with programmable SIMs. Over-the-Air Experimental Setup. We instantiate X-RIC with L3-8B as the TLM foundation and BC-trained DDPG as the RL Control Agent, implementing a two-slice subset: URLLC (Slice 1, UE1, 12.1.1.0/24) with strict QoS require- ments (zero packet loss, low latency) and eMBB (Slice 2, UE2, 12.1.1.128/24) with high-throughput requirements. Following the objective in Section III, the operator intent is: “Minimize total operating cost while maintaining SLA-defined QoS re- quirements: the URLLC slice must satisfy strict latency and reliability constraints; the eMBB slice must meet throughput targets, with resource allocation prioritizing URLLC during congestion.” X-RIC autonomously translates this intent into concrete controls: PRB allocations, compute workload distri- bution, and per-slice throughput guarantees. Initially, the network operates under balanced resource allocation with equal PRB distribution (50:50 ratio). To create realistic congestion scenarios, we use iperf3 in UDP mode to enable controlled congestion testing. Unlike TCP’s automatic throttling, UDP transmits at configured bitrates regardless of capacity, allowing traffic demands to exceed available bandwidth and exposing packet loss for QoS evaluation. We deploy two iperf3 servers running on ports 5201 (Slice 1) and 5202 (Slice 2) to generate traffic demands exceeding the system capacity (∼296 Mbps in our setup). The servers generate per-UE metrics (throughput, jitter, packet loss) at 1- second intervals, which are ingested into a Kinetica accelerated time-series database. When aggregate traffic demand exceeds available radio capacity, X-RIC detects congestion through KPI monitoring and evaluates SLA compliance across both slices. The critic-guided mechanism dynamically reallocates PRBs to restore SLA requirements with minimal resource consumption. Performance Baselines. We evaluate the SLA compliance and resource efficiency of X-RIC against three representa- tive baselines using Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) analysis across four key metrics: eMBB throughput, URLLC latency, vCW utilization, and vRB utilization. We compare X-RIC against the following approaches: (i) Equal Allocation, which distributes vRB and vCW resources uniformly across all slices without adaptive control, representing static provisioning strategies; (ii) TLM-RL, which integrates TLM guidance with the RL agent but without the behavioral cloning mechanism, isolating the contribution of automated demonstration harvest- ing; and (iii) X-RICn, our framework initialized with n dual- filtered demonstrations to assess the impact of demonstration budget on performance. SLA Compliance Analysis. Fig. 10(a)–(b) show eMBB throughput and URLLC latency vs. SLA requirements of 12.0 Mbps and 10.0 ms, respectively. Equal Allocation op- erates close to limits: eMBB at 12–13 Mbps and URLLC at 9–10 ms, risking violations during congestion. X-RIC500 provides larger safety margins: eMBB at 17 Mbps (42% above SLA) and URLLC at 5 ms (50% below) with min- imal variance.
