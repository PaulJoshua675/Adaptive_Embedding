The search reads the rollout rates that define the reference PIT steps, so its agreement with them is a different quantity from a probe’s Hit@±2 and is reported separately. 5 Discussion and limitations The confidence probe misses capability gaps and PITs more often than near misses at both scales (Figure 3). At detection it exceeds the strongest baseline we tested that uses no hidden states, but it does not meet the margin (Section 3). The text baseline’s miss rates by failure type were not measured, so the pattern is established for the confidence probe only. Either ordering of PITs and near misses can be read from the hidden states that the confidence probe reads. The combined probe reads the same hidden states and gives PITs lower final-step scores than near misses at both scales, the opposite of the confidence probe (Figure 3C). We think the training target itself produces the combined probe’s order: by Definition 1 a near miss keeps a rollout rate above θ at its last evaluated prefix and a PIT does not, so a probe fitted to these rates is trained toward ranking near misses above PITs. The confidence probe and the combined probe differ in more than their labels, and we did not isolate the contribution of the labels (Section 3). At 4B, no single probe exceeds the length rule at locating the PIT step on held-out PITs, and at 8B the one tested probe, the value probe, is below the length rule in every seed (Section 4). The hidden states may still carry the PIT step; the tested probes do not recover it. Two limits bear directly on the localization result. First, each step’s hidden states were read at a position at or before the step’s last token, and for about half of the traces the position read for the final step lies in an earlier step (Section 3). No result was re-scored with corrected positions, so the localization result holds for these stored hidden states. Second, the PIT steps are assigned from a small number of rollouts at each evaluated step, and the two collections assign different failure types to some traces they share (Appendix B).
