CLUE and Accuracy-Only RL share a curricu- lum that begins with full conversations, then mixes them with shorter contexts ending at the target. Groups with identical total rewards or incomplete feedback are resampled or replaced within the same curriculum segment for CLUE; groups that remain unusable are masked. At inference, the trained policy generates a reasoning trace and pre- diction from the supplied interaction without the judge. 4 Experiments To study the effectiveness of CLUE and its gener- alization to new languages and conversational set- tings, we compare performance across supervised initialization and RL. We also examine reasoning token use and MMLU performance to assess ef- ficiency and the retention of broader capabilities. Evaluation data, inference settings, and scoring conventions are detailed in Appendix B. 4.1 Experimental Settings We train Qwen3.5-9B on the dev split of CCU Mandarin instant-messaging conversations, using 2,568 instances for emotion detection, dialog act recognition, and norm violation detection. Evalu- ation covers held-out Mandarin conversations and five out-of-domain settings: emotion detection on CCU Japanese and MELD (Poria et al., 2019), emotion detection and dialog act recognition on CPED (Chen et al., 2022), and power prediction in Wikipedia discussions (Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil et al., 2012). We compare the base checkpoint with Label SFT, trained on task labels, and Reasoning SFT, the first-stage checkpoint trained on refined evi- dence reasoning traces. Both RL variants start from Reasoning SFT: Accuracy-Only RL rewards task correctness alone, while CLUE uses the full second-stage training with IUG. We also include Qwen3.8-27B as a larger open-weight reference: it has the highest Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index score among 4–40B open-weight models, close to that of GPT-6-Luna (Artificial Analysis, 2026b,a). We report mean weighted F1 and sam- ple standard deviation across three sampled evalu- ations of each checkpoint, on target sets excluding catch-all labels where applicable. Standard devi- ations reflect decoding variation at a fixed check- point. 4.2 Performance of CLUE As shown in Table 1, CLUE achieves relative gains of 17.3% in-domain and 9.3% out-of-domain in av- erage weighted F1 over base Qwen, with each task weighted equally within its domain.
