In summary, our diagnosis suggests that exploration faces inherent limitations in shared-expert MoEs. Takeaway. Shared experts can dominate representation learning, limiting representational diversity and thereby weakening routing exploration. 2.3 Consequences of the exploration dilemma The preceding discussions reveals a fundamental exploration dilemma in shared-expert MoEs. On the one hand, the combinatorial growth of the routing space induced by fine-grained expert segmentation demands greater exploration to identify (near-)optimal expert combinations. On the other hand, shared experts reduce feature variation, thereby weakening exploration. This mismatch gives rise to several practical inefficiencies. We reveal them by evaluating models ranging from 100M to 2.4B total parameters. We set 𝑅∈{64, 128} and 𝑆= 2 for shared-expert MoEs, and match the total number of experts for MoEs without shared experts. We vary the number of activated experts as 𝐾∈{6, 8, 10}. In this scenario, increasing 𝐾improves expressiveness but also enlarges the exploration space. Inefficiency 1. Benefits of shared experts are eroded. The first consequence is that shared-expert MoEs do not consistently improve upon standard MoEs. As shown in Fig.1, when 𝐾is small and hence demanding less exploration, shared-expert MoEs outperform their non-shared counterparts. As 𝐾increases, however, the benefits of shared experts diminish or even disappear. Inefficiency 2. Shared experts can diminish the expressive benefits of increasing 𝐾. Across the four model scales in Fig.1, increasing 𝐾from 6 to 10 yields perplexity gains of {2.23, 1.32, 1.08, 0.87} for MoEs without shared experts. With shared experts, however, these gains drop to {0.29, −0.03, 0.58, −0.03}, i.e., negative at two scales. These negative numbers indicate that additional expressiveness from activating more experts is not fully realized with shared experts. Inefficiency 3. Shared experts slow routing specialization. As shown in Fig.3a, replacing the learned router with a uniformly random one leads to a steadily increasing relative perplexity gap across training stages for MoEs without shared experts.
