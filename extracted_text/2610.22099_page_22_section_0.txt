B.3 Supplementary analyses of visibility and revision Protocol. These experiments use TIPSfull and TIPSblock checkpoints with training seed 123, one evaluation seed, and the full GSM8K and MATH-500 test populations. Except for the explicitly selected corruption- reference pools, each reported mathematical-reasoning accuracy is micro-averaged over all 1,819 problems. The decoder remains frozen and uses greedy token proposals. We preserve each checkpoint’s native schedule and timing convention, including the block policy’s next-state local time input. All budgets count decoder calls, including deterministic finalization. B.3.1 Does TIPS select useful reveal and remask positions? We examine a low-budget case, TIPSblock at T = 16, by intervening on one decision along an otherwise on-distribution trajectory. At within-block phases 0.25, 0.5, and 0.75 in each of the two blocks, we preserve the numbers of revealed and remasked positions but randomize the reveal positions, remask positions, or both. The random selections remain within the corresponding eligible masked or visible sets. Each continuation is evaluated on all test problems, including cases where an intervention leaves the action unchanged. As seen in figure 10 and table 13, randomizing both sets reduces accuracy at all six sites in this case study. For example, at phase 0.25 of the first block, accuracy falls from 44.42% to 40.52%; at phase 0.5 of the second block, it falls to 40.79%. The separate interventions are less uniform: randomizing reveal or remask positions at phase 0.75 of the first block slightly improves accuracy. Thus, the selected example provides evidence that the locations chosen by TIPS can matter beyond their counts, particularly early in the trajectory, while retaining cases where a particular native decision is not beneficial.
