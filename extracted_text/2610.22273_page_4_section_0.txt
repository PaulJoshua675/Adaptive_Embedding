Faithful Fallback. No finite memory can anticipate every query, and synthesized artifacts can omit details that are critical in hindsight. The workspace must therefore preserve reference links and raw source paths so the answering agent can verify claims or bypass the synthesized layer when needed. In the enterprise example, a rare query about a single customer may not appear in the high-level summary, but the agent should still be able to read any existing issue thread. As an extreme example, such a fallback is important for needle-in-a-haystack queries where the answer depends on a localized, very specific fact that is unlikely to survive compression (Wang et al., 2024). Amortized Reuse. The builder’s construction cost should pay off across many downstream queries. For example, a compact release timeline written by the builder agent may help answer many questions about regressions, roadmap changes, customer messaging, and dependency updates. In contrast, a one-off answer draft may surface similar information but help only a single request. This creates a tradeoff between the construction cost ∑︀𝐻 𝑖=1 Cost(𝒜𝑖) and the downstream utility. Overview of Anticipatory Workspace Desiderata An effective anticipatory workspace should make likely queries easy to answer, preserve fallback paths to raw documents, and pay off across many downstream queries. 2.3. Workspace MDP Formalization. In Librarian Agents, anticipatory workspace construction proceeds in two phases: an upfront synthesis phase and a downstream answering phase. The synthesis phase is analogous to the offline construction of a retrieval index or knowledge graph, but instead produces a flexible filesystem-based workspace.
