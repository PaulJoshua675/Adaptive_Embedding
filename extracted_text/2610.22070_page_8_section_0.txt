Similar to our design, these methods retain most recent observations in a local context window while compressing historical information into a more compact memory representation, such as textual sum- maries or condensed visual features. Among them, the concurrent work StreamTTT is most relevant. StreamTTT stores historical information in an online-updated test-time-training (TTT) branch while retaining the most recent visual tokens as the current observation. MaLoW achieves state-of-the-art results. As shown in Table 1, MaLoW establishes strong performance across streaming video benchmarks. MaLoW (MOSS-VL-Realtime-11B) and MaLoW (Qwen3-VL-4B) achieve new state-of-the-art scores of 74.5% and 84.8% on OVO-Bench and StreamingBench, respectively, while MaLoW (Qwen3.5-9B) attains the highest EPM score of 63.6% among all compared methods. We highlight key comparisons at matched model scales. At the 4B scale, the MaLoW (Qwen3-VL-4B) surpasses the concurrent work StreamTTT-4B (Chen et al., 2026) by 3.9% and 3.5% on the backward split of the OVO-bench and StreamingBench, respectively. At the 7-9B scale, the MaLoW (Qwen3.5-9B) outperforms the previous memory-based method OASIS-8B (Liang et al., 2026) and local window-based method AURA-8B (Lu et al., 2026) by 9.2% and 6.0% on the backward split of the OVO-bench, respectively. At the 11B scale, MaLoW (MOSS-VL-Real-11B) improves over its base MOSS-VL-Real-11B by 2.2% and 1.4% on the Real-Time split and EPM task of OVO-Bench, respectively. We recognize that on the OVO-Bench real-time split, MaLoW (Qwen3-VL-4B) and MaLoW (Qwen3.5-9B) underperform StreamTTT-4B and AURA-8B, respectively. We hypothesize that these baselines may have benefited from fine-tuning their base VLMs, whereas MaLoW keeps the base VLMs intact. Collectively, these results show that MaLoW is particularly effective on memory-critical tasks while retaining competitive performance on real-time perception. Importantly, the gains are consistent across model scales from 4B to 11B and across base VLMs pre-trained for both offline video understanding (Qwen series) and streaming video understanding (VideoChat3 and MOSS-VL-Realtime). MaLoW offers a lightweight memory patch. Across different base VLMs, MaLoW introduces only 1.4%-4.0% additional parameters. The pretrained backbone architecture and parameters remain unchanged, while historical information is encoded entirely through the evolving LoRA weights. Preserving the backbone helps retain its pretrained capabilities and minimizes interference with real-time perception. Moreover, widespread LoRA support in modern inference frameworks facilitates efficient deployment. MaLoW incurs minor inference overhead.
