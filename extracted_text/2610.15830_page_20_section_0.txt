E Network, acquisition, and measurement details (IV) The central IV network has the structure 6 →32 →2 with tanh after each encoder layer, followed by linear reconstruction heads. In IV-B, training/probe/test sample counts are 3,072/768/1,536; batch size is 256, learning rate 0.002, weight decay 10−5, and post-hoc readout ridge coefficient 10−3. Noisy target channels are divided by √ 1 + σ2 before the orthogonal mixing. Training targets are noise-free. IV-B uses 100 initialization and 500 formation updates; IV-C continues formed states for 1,000 updates. These models are nonlinear shared encoders even though their training and post-hoc readout heads are linear. IV-A follows 40 seeds with two mirrored formed states through four acquisition–update cells for 400 updates. Under the original probe, crossing the pre-specified aligned threshold −0.08 takes 40.6 updates with reference acquisition versus 22.2 with alternative acquisition, holding alternative-target updating fixed. The difference is 18.4 ([13.2, 25.8]). Remeasuring stored trajectories with common low-noise inputs expands the allocation range from approximately [−0.154, 0.160] to [−0.451, 0.462]. The original-input audit rows are bit-identical to the original trajectories; this is a measurement addition, not a new learning run. First-zero-crossing differences are 3.50 under the original probe and 3.44 ([2.81, 4.00]) under the common probe. Changing the original threshold from 0.04 to 0.06 to 0.08 changes the time difference from 6.50 to 8.44 to 18.38. Thresholds at or above 0.10 increasingly produce non-arrivals and sensitivity to right censoring. The pre-specified 18.4 estimate is therefore threshold-specific; agreement of zero-crossing and continuous-trajectory effects supports the direction of the acquisition effect across measurements. The exploratory terminal-depth alignment contrast is 0.0376 with the original probe and 0.1411 ([0.1370, 0.1454]) with the common probe. The common-probe acquisition main effect is 0.0002 ([−0.0011, 0.0015]); conditional effects reverse sign, +0.1413 under reference updating and −0.1410 un- der alternative updating. Symmetric acquisition/update continuation reduces original-probe |s| from 0.1326 to 0.0146 and common-probe |s| from 0.4067 to 0.0413, with mean residual ratio 0.1009. These outcomes distinguish terminal depth, trajectory, and maintenance rather than assigning all three to an acquisition main effect. IV-B’s common low-precision probe retains the acquisition contrast at 0.2237. IV-C retains it at 0.2344 ([0.2322, 0.2365]), compared with the common high-precision formed-start contrast of 0.7161. These cross- probe agreements support the favored-target comparison while demonstrating why allocation-index magnitudes should not be treated as measurement-invariant physical quantities.
