fMRI representation models differ first in what they model. Time-series methods recon- struct masked BOLD signals, predict masked latent targets, or align representations across temporal views (Ortega Caro et al., 2024; Dong et al., 2024; Gijsen et al., 2026). They preserve temporal dynamics, but interregional connectivity remains an implicit property of the learned activity representation. Voxel-level models instead operate directly on 4D fMRI volumes (Wang et al., 2026b; 2025; Xia et al., 2026b; Wang et al., 2026a). They retain fine-grained spatial information without relying on a predefined atlas, but require high-dimensional inputs and do not use connectivity as the primary modeling unit. FC models represent each scan as a connectivity matrix or graph, directly encoding interre- gional coupling (Kawahara et al., 2017; Li et al., 2021; Kan et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2026a;b). Most treat the resulting Pearson-FC matrix as the object to encode, reconstruct, augment, or align. This overlooks that FC is a finite-sample estimate whose values depend on its supporting frames (Noble et al., 2019; Zamani Esfahlani et al., 2020; Faskowitz et al., 2020). Dynamic-FC methods instead model temporal variation across win- dows or graph sequences (Hutchison et al., 2013; Kim et al., 2021), but address evolving connectivity states rather than reproducibility across alternative estimates. CoFlu-Brain retains the interpretability of FC modeling while extending its learning unit from one scan-level estimate to multiple related estimates. These views arise from comple- mentary frame observations and preserve a direct relationship to FC estimation, promoting representations stable across frame compositions and predictive of held-out observations. Self-supervised methods also differ in how they define their learning signal. Masked au- toencoding reconstructs omitted input content, whereas JEPA predicts masked targets in latent space (He et al., 2022; Ortega Caro et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2026a; Dong et al., 2024; Assran et al., 2023). Joint-embedding methods such as BYOL and DINO instead align representations across views, while VICReg and SIGReg regularize the fea- ture distribution against collapse (Grill et al., 2020; Caron et al., 2021; Bardes et al., 2022; Balestriero & LeCun, 2025). Other directions include metadata-conditioned diffusion pretraining across brain states (Xia et al., 2026a), organizing pretraining domains and downstream transfer through measured learning relations (Xia et al., 2026c), and jointly examining data size, model size, and training duration (Ye et al., 2026). These studies complement the design of FC-specific training views explored here. CoFlu-Brain integrates these principles through FC-specific view relations. Consistency across retained estimates promotes stability under frame resampling; prediction from re- tained to complementary dropped estimates captures information predictive of held-out observations; and SIGReg maintains representation diversity. Together, these components use the finite-sample structure of FC to define which views should agree and which comple- mentary evidence should be predicted. 3 Method 3.1 Overview CoFlu-Brain constructs multiple retained–dropped FC pairs from each fMRI segment. As illustrated in Figure 2, a shared encoder maps each estimate to a scan-level representa- tion. Training combines consistency across retained-frame representations, prediction of the paired dropped-frame representation, and distributional regularization. After pretraining, the projector and predictor are discarded, and the shared FC encoder provides representa- tions for downstream tasks. 3
