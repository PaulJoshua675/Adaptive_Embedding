Time-Varying Sensitivities, Noise, and Hybrid Systems. Although Theorem 1 establishes the Action Error via a local LTI approximation, de- ployments involve dynamic Jacobians that de- mand incremental stability (δ-ISS) or bounded- variation arguments. In non-autonomous regimes, unmeasured process noise amplifies parametric sub-optimality, and discontinuous hybrid switch- ing thresholds in exoskeletons (Lippi et al., 2021) can induce impulse-like jumps in state sensitivity. Limitations and Future Work. While the 1-DOF Duﬀing oscillator provides a geometric testbed to isolate the Action Error without the confounding variables of multi-DOF dynamic cou- pling, the present work functions as an analyti- cal framework. Scaling this time-scale separated adaptation to multi-DOF hardware (e.g., lower- limb exoskeletons or humanoid robots) introduces coupled, time-varying Jacobians. In these higher- dimensional regimes, the sensitivity matrix Sk be- comes non-diagonal, and unmodeled cross-joint inertia amplifies parametric sub-optimality. The gradient clipping threshold must be scaled by the spectral norm of the coupled mass matrix to en- sure the slow-loop Lipschitz continuity (Condi- tion 2) is maintained across all joint axes simul- taneously. To elevate this taxonomy to a com- prehensive control methodology, future work pri- oritizes extending empirical validation to such hardware-in-the-loop deployments. This requires experimental benchmarking of our time-scale sep- arated adaptation against classical robust archi- tectures (MRAC, L1) and sequential aggrega- tion (DAgger). Theoretically, while Remark 1 addresses continuous saturation-induced instabil- ity, efforts are required to formalize sensitivity bounds under discontinuous hybrid switching and time-varying references.
