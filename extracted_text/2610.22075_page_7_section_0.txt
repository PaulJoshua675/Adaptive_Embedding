We determine the effect of the text-only stage S0 and the vision-language align- ment stage S1 on the resulting recurrent VLM in Table 2. We find that S0 is crucial to the recovery training, as removing it reduces the performance of the resulting model by 3.68 pp on average. Re- moving S0 primarily affects the reasoning-oriented datasets, with 7.3 pp lost on CV2D, 6.8 pp on MMB and 4.6 pp on AI2D. We attribute this to the fact that without the repair of the pruned language backbone in S0, the stages S1 and S2 have to repair the language backbone and align the projector at the same time. We further find that removing S1 costs 1.28 pp, again primarily on the reasoning- oriented datasets. We attribute this to the fact that S1 performs the vision-language alignment, and without it the stage S2 has to perform both the alignment and the instruction tuning. In summary, both S0 and S1 contribute to the performance, with S0 having the larger impact, which shows that the text-only recovery is necessary for retrofitting recurrence into a pruned VLM. Layer allocation. We first ablate the design of the recurrent block in Table 3(a). To this end, we fix the depth of the recurrent model at mr=56 layers, thus smaller recurrent blocks are executed with more recurrences compared to larger ones. All models are trained on a 7.5B S0 text-token budget. We find that a larger recurrent block performs better than other variants given the same compute setting. Namely, the 4/8/4 allocation outperforms a 6/4/6 allocation at r=11 by 3.19 pp. A narrower 4/6/4 block at r=8 prunes two additional layers, and the 4/8/4 configuration outperforms it by 1.60 pp. Adapters. Next, we consider the adapter design in Table 3(b), comparing the proposed linear adapter of Eq. 3 against multiple adapter variants, including the case of no adapter. The gated adapter Asc adds the context h to the state s, multiplied channel-wise by a learned factor (1 + α) with α initialized at zero. Thus the adapter starts as a plain sum: Asc(s, h) = s + (1 + α) ⊙h. The Parcae adapter Apc follows the adapter design of Prairie et al. (2026). It multiplies the state by a learned per-channel factor a ∈(0, 1)H, so that the state of the previous recurrence is damped and cannot grow across recurrences, and adds a learned projection W ′ of the normalized context, weighted by a learned per-channel scale δ: Apc(s, h) = a⊙s+δ⊙W ′ LN(h). The no adapter variant A∅starts the loop from s0 = h instead of noise. The evaluation is done on a 4/6/4 architecture with r = 6 and all models are trained on a 7.5B S0 text-token budget. Overall, it shows that the linear adapter Alin (Eq. 3) achieves the highest performance. The adapter-free configuration A∅initializes the loop directly from the prelude output h and noticeably performs nearly as well as Alin with a performance gap of only 0.73 pp. Furthermore, A∅outperforms both the gated adapter Asc and the Parcae adapter Apc by 1.31 pp and 0.53 pp, respectively.
