These results demonstrate that PathFM generates high-fidelity trajectory forecasts without sacrificing marginal accuracy, making it a strong candidate for forecasting applications. Our contributions are as follows: • Fast and calibrated trajectory generation. We introduce PathFM, a joint flow foundation model for fast, calibrated trajectory generation with accurate small-ensemble marginals (Figure 2). Distillation enables one- or two-step generation, marginal-CRPS refinement improves empirical coverage, and stratified sampling reduces finite-ensemble marginal error. • A benchmark for trajectory distributions. We introduce fps-bench, to our knowledge the first dedicated benchmark for evaluating path distributions from time series foundation models on real-world data. It reveals how strong marginal accuracy in existing models can mask errors in temporal dependence and uncertainty over trajectory-dependent quantities. • State-of-the-art performance. PathFM outperforms leading foundation models on downstream probabilistic forecasting tasks and stays among the top on marginal benchmarks. It also achieves the highest forecasting accuracy across strong baselines on chaotic systems. 2 PROBLEM SETUP AND PRELIMINARIES Forecast object and downstream query. Let H ∈RT ×C be the observed history and Y ∈RL×C the future. The available information I includes H, observation masks, and known future covariates. A predictive law Pθ(Y | I) supports queries about individual coordinates or a trajectory functional g(Y ), such as cumulative sum or autocorrelation. A trajectory generator represents this law through X = Gθ(I, Z), where each noise draw Z produces one complete future path. Perez-Diaz et al. (2025) distinguish point, quantile, parametric-distribution, and trajectory forecast products by the decisions they support. Coordinatewise quantiles Qτ(Yℓ,c | I) and marginal distri- bution parameters leave temporal and cross-variable dependence unspecified. Consequently, they do not determine predictive autocorrelation or distributions of sums, maxima, and path-dependent events without an additional dependence assumption. Joint trajectory samples support direct esti- mation of those quantities by applying g to each generated path. Existing benchmarks and their scope. GIFT-Eval reports point-forecast metrics and approximates univariate CRPS with weighted quantile loss (Aksu et al., 2024); fev-bench uses scaled quantile loss across heterogeneous forecasting tasks (Shchur et al., 2025). TIME evaluates zero-shot forecasting on freshly curated datasets, reporting MASE and quantile-based CRPS (Qiao et al., 2026). These summaries assess central predictions and marginal uncertainty, including on multivariate tasks, but cannot distinguish joint laws with identical coordinatewise marginals. We introduce fps-bench to address this gap by evaluating temporal dependence and trajectory-dependent quantities (Section 4).
