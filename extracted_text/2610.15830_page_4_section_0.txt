Balanced acquisition contains exactly 250 updates with each target observed precisely, in randomized order; every condition receives the same total amount of high-precision information. Under equal loss weights, precise acquisition of A yields mean s = 0.3385, while precise acquisition of B yields s = −0.3338. The paired contrast is 0.6723 ([0.6660, 0.6794]), above the pre-specified 0.30 criterion. Every confirmatory seed favors the precisely acquired target; balanced acquisition remains near zero. Because all conditions are evaluated using common inputs and newly fitted readouts, the difference persists beyond the momentary advantage of seeing a cleaner signal during training. Acquisition precision is sufficient to select specialization under symmetric explicit updating in this system. The full factorial explains why this is not a fixed division of labor between information and learning (Figure 1a). Either control can favor a target. Agreement strengthens specialization, while conflict weakens it; at the tested levels, a strong opposing update weight can overcome the acquisition advantage. Both controls act through the gradients of the same network, so distinct interventions need not have orthogonal effects. These are 500-update outcomes, not estimates of limiting equilibria. 3.2 Acquisition also redirects already formed states Does acquisition matter only before specialization develops? We formed A- and B-favored checkpoints by asymmetric updating while both targets were observed at noise standard deviation 0.15. We then fixed update weights at 0.50/0.50 and applied the same three acquisition conditions for 1,000 updates, with neutral checkpoints as an additional reference. Opposite-target acquisition changes the favored target in all 40 seeds for both starting states (Figure 1b–d). The terminal A-precise minus B-precise contrast, averaged across formed states, is 0.7161 ([0.7108, 0.7213]), close to the neutral-start contrast of 0.7086. Balanced acquisition moves both formed states toward the center. The first zero crossings occur at mean recorded times of 16.9 and 16.8 updates, but trajectories can subsequently move nonmonotonically; these times do not measure settled adaptation. Current acquisition can redirect an update-formed representation under equal subsequent loss weights, within the observed 1,000-update horizon. 3.3 Strong one-sided updating changes the comparison A separate formed-state experiment holds the update target strictly on one side and crosses it with precise acquisition of either the previously favored target or its alternative. Each formed checkpoint is cloned into four conditions for 400 updates. Updating only the reference target retains it as favored in 159/160 cells; updating only the alternative makes that target favored in all cells. Holding alternative-target updating fixed, however, acquiring that target precisely accelerates the trajectory relative to acquiring the reference target precisely. Under a common high-precision probe, the mean first-zero-crossing difference is 3.44 updates ([2.81, 4.00]). The larger threshold-specific estimate from the pre-specified protocol and its audit remain in Appendix E. Terminal depth also depends on how acquisition and updating combine. In an exploratory analysis, aligned acquisition and update targets yield greater |s| than crossed targets by 0.1411 under the common probe. The acquisition main effect averages to approximately zero because its conditional effects have opposite signs under the two update targets. Thus the data do not support a general rule that acquisition controls only speed and updating controls only direction. Acquisition can select and redirect the favored target; sufficiently strong one-sided updating constrains the outcome while acquisition still changes the trajectory and terminal depth. 4
