Hierarchical Chunks boosts nDCG@10 for the three dense retrievers on both collections. On NormasTCU, the largest relative gains occur for retrievers with the weakest baselines: 36% for text-embedding-3-small (from 0.263 to 0.358), 16% for jua-4B-mixed (0.305 to 0.355), and 8% for Qwen3-Embedding-4B (0.307 to 0.333). On Ulysses-RFCorpus, the relative gains are more uniform, ranging from 9% to 14% across the three dense retrievers. For BM25, nDCG@10 decreases on NormasTCU (from 0.364 to 0.342) and remains almost unchanged on Ulysses-RFCorpus (from 0.531 to 0.532), i.e., lexical retrieval does not benefit from segmentation in the same way as dense retrieval. The simplified core-based representations with context achieve results comparable to or bet- ter than Hierarchical Chunks while indexing fewer units. With Core Chunks + Full Context, nDCG@10 is close to that of Hierarchical Chunks on Ulysses-RFCorpus (absolute differences of at most 0.006). On NormasTCU, it outperforms Hierarchical Chunks with two of the three dense retrievers, yielding gains of +0.028 for jua-4B-mixed and +0.018 for Qwen3-Embedding-4B, while underperforming by −0.016 with text-embedding-3-small. Core Chunks + Metadata shows a similar pattern, with differences of at most 0.008 on Ulysses-RFCorpus. On NormasTCU, this strategy achieves the highest nDCG@10 among all evaluated dense retrievers, reaching 0.392 with jua-4B-mixed. Removing the context (Core Chunks + No Context) lowers nDCG@10 for all retrievers on both collections compared with Core Chunks + Full Context and Core Chunks + Metadata. The comparison with the whole-document baselines is less consistent: without context, segmen- tation still outperforms the baselines for all dense retrievers on Ulysses-RFCorpus, but only for text-embedding-3-small on NormasTCU. It also substantially reduces BM25 performance on both collections, from 0.364 to 0.255 on NormasTCU and from 0.531 to 0.490 on Ulysses-RFCorpus. The MRR followed a similar trend. Hierarchical Chunks increases MRR compared with whole- document indexing in almost all cases, except for Qwen3-Embedding-4B on Ulysses-RFCorpus. The Core Chunks + Full Context and Core Chunks + Metadata achieve similar results to Hierarchical Chunks on Ulysses-RFCorpus and further improve MRR for jua-4B-mixed and Qwen3-Embedding-4B. As with nDCG@10, Core Chunks + No Context produces lower MRR than Core Chunks + Full Context and Core Chunks + Metadata in all cases. Two cases stand out in the MRR results. The first occurs on NormasTCU with BM25: although segmentation reduces nDCG@10, MRR increases from 0.444 with whole-document indexing to 0.509 with Hierarchical Chunks and 0.524 with Core Chunks + Full Context. Thus, the first relevant document tends to appear earlier in the ranked list even though overall top-10 ranking quality does not improve. The second case occurs on Ulysses-RFCorpus with Qwen3-Embedding-4B: MRR decreases from 0.719 (baseline) to 0.668 (Hierarchical Chunks). For this metric and retriever, none of the evaluated representation strategies outperforms the whole-document result on Ulysses- RFCorpus. Figure 4 summarizes these results by showing the change in nDCG@10 and MRR relative to the corresponding whole-document baseline. For each strategy, retriever, and collection, we compute the difference relative to its whole-document result. Thus, each value represents the gain or loss from changing only the document representation while keeping the retrieval model fixed. The figure shows several patterns: • Dense retrievers consistently improve nDCG@10 with all three context-enriched representations.
