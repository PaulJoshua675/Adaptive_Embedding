The Covariance Matrix Self-Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMSA-ES) (Beyer and Sendhoff, 2008) proposes a simplified CMA mechanism that eliminates the evolution paths. This only relies on the points sampled in the current generation; this makes the estimator for C less stable, but only requires the rank-µ update (Line 16 of Alg. 1). The required decomposition of C is then performed via Cholesky decomposition, yielding a speedup relative to the standard CMA-ES. Notably, the algorithm requires an increased selective pressure, with µ = ⌊λ/4⌋ by default. The CMSA update can be more robust to noise and performs better with larger population sizes (Beyer and Sendhoff, 2008). 6. Natural Evolution Strategies (NES) (Glasmachers et al., 2010b; Wierstra et al., 2014) are a variation on the standard evolution strategy theme, which uses the natural gradient to update the strategy parameters of a continuous search distribution. The method is analogous to natural gradient ascent, and uses the Fisher information matrix F estimated from the sampled points to perform an iterative update: θ ←θ + η F−1∇θJ (4) Here θ denotes the parameters of the search distribution and ∇θJ the search gradient w.r.t. to θ. As noticed by Akimoto et al. (2010), the so-called exponential NES (xNES) for multivariate normal distributions is closely related to the CMA-ES. In fact, the xNES can be considered a principled alternative of the CMA-ES without evolution paths (Glasmachers et al., 2010a). In ModCMA, the matrix adaptation mechanism from xNES is implemented as a module, which updates a transformation matrix: A ←A · exp(η · ∇AJ) (5) Where η is a learning rate, and the gradient matrix ∇AJ is computed via: ∇AJ ←∇MJ −tr(∇MJ)/dI (6) ∇MJ ← µ X i=1 wi(ziz⊤ i −I) (7) From a computational perspective, xNES does not offer any benefit over the standard CMA-ES, as the matrix exponential in Equation 5 incurs a similar cubic time complexity.
