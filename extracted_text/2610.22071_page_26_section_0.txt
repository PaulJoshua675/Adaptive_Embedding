Eight More Scripts and Learning Curves Design. This exploratory extension repeats the retained-parent comparison of Appendix E in eight more languages: Japanese (jpn_Jpan), Thai (tha_Thai), Lao (lao_Laoo), Khmer (khm_Khmr), Burmese (mya_Mymr), Hindi (hin_Deva), Bengali (ben_Beng), and Tamil (tam_Taml). Each predictor is trained on one language only, with the model, optimizer, 2,000 steps of 32 rows, six encoding and lookahead conditions, and 3 seeds of Appendix E; text spans are identical across conditions within each language and seed. For each language, we retain whole documents from the first FineWeb-2 shard until the NFC text reaches at least 8 million UTF-8 bytes; no retained document contains a FLORES-200 devtest sentence of any of the eight languages. Targets keep the Qwen3 cuts that fall on extended grapheme cluster boundaries and delete all others, so outside Japanese they also remove cuts inside multi-scalar clusters. We evaluate all devtest sentences of each language after every 250 updates and report all 144 runs. Intervals use 2,000 paired source-sentence bootstrap draws shared across seeds and conditions and are conditional on the 3 seeds. All evaluation scalars lie in the BMP, so UTF-8 at k = 3 and UBE or UBE-BE at k = 2 see the whole next scalar. Pages and first symbols. The main blocks of Thai (U+0E00–U+0E7F), Lao (U+0E80–U+0EFF), Khmer (U+1780– U+17FF), Myanmar (U+1000–U+109F), Devanagari (U+0900–U+097F), Bengali (U+0980–U+09FF), and Tamil (U+0B80– U+0BFF) each lie on a single 256-codepoint page (The Unicode Consortium, 2025). Every letter of such a block therefore has the same first UTF-8 byte (E0 or E1) and the same first UBE-BE symbol, its page, whereas its first UBE symbol is its position within the page. Japanese text mixes hiragana and katakana from their main blocks, which share one page, with kanji spread over many, so UBE-BE’s first symbol separates these kana from kanji and distinguishes kanji pages. The texts also contain Latin letters, digits, and punctuation, so a constant first symbol within one block does not by itself determine the learned error. Results. Table 23 gives the paired contrasts. UBE at k = 1 has fewer errors than both UTF-8 and UBE-BE in every seed of every language, and every interval for these two contrasts excludes zero. In the single-page scripts, UBE-BE differs from UTF-8 by at most 3.0% at k = 1, and UBE’s reduction relative to UBE-BE is at least 97% of its reduction relative to UTF-8.
