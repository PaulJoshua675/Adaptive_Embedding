Clinical sleep medicine repeatedly documents profound divergences between ob- jective sleep duration and subjective daytime impairment, notably exemplified by sleep state misperception in paradoxical insomnia and boundary instability in narcolepsy type 1. Here, we present DyssomniaNN: a multi-scale biophysi- cal computational framework coupling Borbély’s two-process regulation of sleep homeostasis and circadian drive with Saper’s hypothalamic reciprocal flip-flop cir- cuit and a recurrent corticothalamic leaky integrate-and-fire spiking neural network. We formalize five clinical cohorts across an identical 72-hour photoperiod: healthy control, circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorder (delayed-phase), chronic insomnia, paradoxical insomnia, and narcolepsy type 1. The synthetic local field potential converges closely with empirical human electroencephalography spectra from the PhysioNet Sleep-EDF database across all canonical frequency bands (spectral mean absolute error of 0.33–0.35). Furthermore, we introduce the Well-Rested Index to quantify restorative efficacy. Simulations reveal that sleep duration fails to predict restorative recovery: paradoxical insomnia achieves normal total sleep time (21.15 h) yet yields low restoration (37.6%) from unsuppressed cortical beta-band intrusion, whereas hypocretin deficiency in narcolepsy produces severe nocturnal fragmentation (781 min of wake after sleep onset) and 35 sleep-onset rapid eye movement periods. DyssomniaNN provides an in silico platform for mechanistic sleep research and virtual neuropharmacology. 1
