Data selection and synthesis for post-training. Early works show that curation matters more than scale: a small, well-chosen set can match or outperform a much larger one [Zhou et al., 2023a, Muennighoff et al., 2025], and data ordering alone shapes generalization [He et al., 2025a]. Selection methods prune or reweight examples by information gain or diversity [Agarwal et al., 2025, Chen et al., 2025]. Synthesis methods bootstrap instructions from seed sets, evolve complexity, or extract alignment data from aligned models [Wang et al., 2023, Xu et al., 2024a,b, Mitra et al., 2024, Ren et al., 2026]. Some data curation methods also optimize specifically for preference data, curating feedback or preference training pairs [Cui et al., 2024, He et al., 2025b, Deng et al., 2025].
