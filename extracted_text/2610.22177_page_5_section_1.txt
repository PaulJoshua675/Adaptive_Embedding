3.1 Data Preprocessing 3.1.1 Optical flow estimation and decomposition We perform a preprocessing step to selectively compute the residual optical flow for the training data. For datasets composed of continuous video se- quences, optical flow can generally be estimated between adjacent frames. However, when large temporal gaps or abrupt scene changes occur, flow esti- mation becomes unreliable. To address this issue, we first compute an image similarity score between neighboring frames and only estimate optical flow when the similarity exceeds a predefined threshold. Optical flow is computed using RAFT [27], which produces dense flow fields capturing the overall motion between frames. We assume that the rigid motion induced by camera movement can be approximated by a global affine transformation, which is denoted by fi = Api + t (1) where fi is the optical flow of a pixel i. pi is the [u, v] coordinate of the pixel. A is a 2×2 rotation matrix, and t is a 2×1 translation vector. A and t can be solved by minimizing Σi||fi−(Api+t)||2. Pixels consistent with the affine transformation are treated as static background, while deviations from this model indicate dynamic regions. The residual flow is defined as the difference between the original optical flow and the estimated affine transformation, thereby isolating motion components that cannot be explained by the global camera motion model. Figure 1 shows two adjacent input frames together with the decomposed rigid flow and the resulting residual. Importantly, residual flow does not solely correspond to independently moving objects. The affine transformation provides only a global approx- imation of camera-induced motion and therefore cannot perfectly model perspective projection effects in scenes with significant depth variation. In regions with large depth gradients, structural discontinuities, or partial oc- clusions, the global motion model becomes insufficient even under predom- inantly static conditions. Consequently, these regions yield non-negligible residual flow. Such areas typically coincide with geometrically challenging regions for multi-view aggregation, as illustrated by the traffic light region in Fig. 1. Therefore, residual flow can also be interpreted as a signal high- lighting deviations from the global motion model, which often require more careful geometric reasoning during reconstruction. 5
