Emergent Structure of Environment Latents. We examine whether direct optimization gives rise to structured environment representations without supervision of the identity or value of under- lying factors. In fig. 4, we apply principal component analysis (PCA) to the learned environment latents and reduce them to two dimensions for visualization. We annotate the projected points using their corresponding ground-truth factor values to reveal their relationship with the learned repre- sentation; these values are never provided to the model. Figure 4(a) shows the single-factor friction experiment. The learned latents form a one-dimensional structure for a single factor that corresponds closely to the underlying friction values, as reflected by the point colors: similar values are located nearby, while friction varies consistently x-axis. PCA visualizations for other tasks are provided in the Supplementary Material section E.3. Compounded Environmental Factors. We next evaluate T2-Z under the more challenging set- ting of compounded environmental factors, where object mass and surface friction vary simultane- ously and jointly reflect in object motion. Importantly, T2-Z is never informed that there are two underlying factors, nor given their identities or values. table 1 shows T2-Z achieves the lowest phys- ical errors, while improving action success from 35.0% to 69.7%. More strikingly, fig. 4(d) shows that the learned latents naturally form a two-dimensional structure corresponding to the two underly- ing factors, showing that T2-Z discovers the structure of compounded environmental factors through prediction alone, without knowing in advance how many factors are present or what they represent. Robustness across Diverse Visual Scenes. We test whether the learned environment represen- tation captures underlying dynamics without becoming entangled with visual appearance. Specifi- cally, we ask whether a unified latent structure emerges across visually diverse scenes despite sub- stantial differences in appearance. We construct a multi-background setting in which the same un- derlying friction factor varies across diverse scene layouts and visual backgrounds. table 1 shows that T2-Z outperforms all baselines across every metric. fig. 4(c) further shows that latents from dif- ferent backgrounds organize consistently rather than separating according to scene appearance. This indicates that environment-latent can isolate environment-specific information that is shared across visually different observations, enabling a unified latent structure to emerge across diverse scenes. 4.3 REAL-WORLD ROBOTIC EXPERIMENTS We further evaluate T2-Z on four real-world tasks.
