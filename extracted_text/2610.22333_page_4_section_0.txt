Prompts that re- ceive the same label under both cues carry no information about the cue, so the test uses only prompts whose label changed. It then checks whether these changes lean towards one cue more than the other. We apply it to the primary con- trast, eval_explicit against deploy, on harm- ful and benign prompts, and to the secondary con- trast, eval_log against monitor_log, on harm- ful prompts. We use the Cochran Q test to record refusal un- der 5 cues, for each model, language using the same prompts. We run tests for every model and language separately on the harmful and benign prompts, and use the Holm correction within each arm (24 tests each). We use logistic regression to measure how much each factor (model, language or cue) changes the odds of refusal, while holding the other factors fixed. This is expressed using an odds ratio. Due to the correlation between responses for the same prompt, we fit the regression with GEE, which groups each prompt’s responses together. Changes are given in percentage points. Our pri- mary result is based on eval_explicit against deploy. The test cue raises refusal. eval_explicit raises refusal of harmful prompts in all 24 model and language cells, by 13.8 points on aver- age, and the increase is significant in 23 of them (Appendix J). The rate of refusal increases with the strength of the signal (Table 1, Ap- pendix H). monitor_log has no significant effect for Qwen2.5-72B or Sarvam-M (Appendix F). The eval_log vs monitor_log raises refusal in all 24 cells, by 4.6 points on average (95% CI [3.6, 5.7]). A second judge reproduces these effects (Ap- pendix C). The increase in refusal appears in every language. Averaged over the four models, eval_explicit raises harmful refusal by 14.4 pp in Indic languages and by 10.6 pp in English (Figure 2). Every Indic language the effect increases at least as much as English (11.2 to 16.1 points). The same holds within Qwen3-32B and Sarvam-M, and within Qwen2.5-72B except in Hindi. Gemma-3-27B rises less in every Indic language, most of all in Telugu and Kannada, where it already refuses 91.5% and 93.0% of harmful prompts under deploy. The test cue acts mainly on harmful prompts. eval_explicit also raises benign refusal, but far less. Averaged over models and languages, harm- ful refusal rises by 13.8 points and benign refusal by only 3.9 (Table 1). Harmful refusal rises more than benign refusal in 21 of 24 model–language cells. The two exceptions are Gemma-3-27B in Telugu and Kannada, where harmful refusal is al- ready above 91%. On the odds scale, too, the effect is larger on harmful prompts for all four models (Table 3). This selectivity is weaker in Indic lan- guages. By selectivity, we mean how much more the cue raises refusal of harmful prompts than of benign prompts.
