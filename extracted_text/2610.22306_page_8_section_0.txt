Long horizon: the merge and its gate Fig. 2 quantifies the long-horizon claim on SportsMOT val. On SportsMOT val the online gain is +0.83 pooled (77.97→78.80; mean per- sequence delta +0.69, 95% CI [+0.15, +1.32], paired boot- strap) and passes through the chain undiminished (84.12→ 84.96). Both test servers agree: +1.00 on SportsMOT (80.82 →81.82, 95 swaps across 56 of 150 sequences) and +1.00 on DanceTrack (61.1→62.1), each against the byte- identical system without the mechanism. Three finer-grained checks (numbers in Appendix A): the gain lives where crossings do (basketball +1.68; foot- ball, whose failures are camera exits, +0.18); a per- swap ground-truth (GT) audit on SportsMOT val finds 11 verified-correct swaps and none verified-incorrect; and on Deep-EIoU’s embeddings the same val experiment mea- sures +1.06, so the margin fires whatever the embedding stack (val gains transfer at 53–120% across our two test runs). Identity switches rise under the mechanism (1,238 → 1,414 on test) because each swap relabels both tracks (95 swaps, +176 switches). The trade is deliberate: a bounded mislabeling buys the remainder of both tracks, the associa- tion metrics price it correctly (AssA +1.86, IDF1 +1.30), and the count remains the lowest on the leaderboard as of August 2026.
