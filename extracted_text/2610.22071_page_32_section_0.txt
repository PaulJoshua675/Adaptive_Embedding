Tokenizer Loading in the Second Pair The dolma2 and OLMo-parent tokenizers have matching serialized vocabularies and tokenizer components after merge- format normalization, but the recorded runtime loads different pretokenizer backends. It loads a bare byte-level pretokenizer for dolma2 while preserving the split-then-byte sequence from the OLMo model repository. Both are fast tokenizers; the discrepancy concerns segmentation behavior, not the choice of a fast or slow implementation. To hold bank and prefix construction fixed within the behavioral pair, both arms use the OLMo parent’s loaded tokenizer, overriding the conversion’s declared dolma2 tokenizer. The instability comparison between the two conversions instead uses the default dolma2 convention. Loading checks compare vocabularies, byte mappings, and expansion tries. Flattening pieces into bytes removes their canonical boundaries, while suffix lookup supplies vocabulary identifiers independently of those boundaries. Consequently, identical byte inputs and expanded identifiers can coexist with different canonical tokenizations. The behavioral comparison is conditional on the shared OLMo-parent convention. K. Comparison Pitfalls Align scored bytes and state the conditioning. Under the Qwen tokenizer’s default settings, no BOS is added and the first token provides context. Our protocol scores the remaining tokens and the matching byte suffix in the conversion, with no EOS target. Prepending the declared BOS changes both the conditioning and the scored span: across 51 sentences, the cost of the unchanged suffix targets rises by 74.2%. This increase is 11.0 times the additional cost of scoring the first token. The result demonstrates why a comparison must state its prefix convention; lower cost under one convention does not establish greater fairness. Avoid unequal-patch batching in the measured wrapper. Strings of equal byte length can have different patch counts. The released wrapper’s pooling and padding path can then introduce surplus patch states and shifted positions without a matching padding mask, a sensitivity observed in all three wrappers under this protocol. For Bwen-8B, batching changes costs by up to 12.32 bits and moves the selected boundary path in 10 of 306 variants. The corresponding maxima and counts are 41.28 bits and 11/2,550 for Bolmo-1B, and 20.88 bits and 29/2,550 for Bolmo-7B. Standalone forwards avoid this measured dependence on batch composition. Keep a path cost distinct from a text likelihood. The joint byte/flag score follows the conversion’s selected boundary history. On the matched subset, conditional flag surprisal accounts for 13.0% of Bwen’s path cost as an unweighted mean across languages (Table 30). This decomposition does not imply a segmentation cost unique to byte models: selecting a BPE token also selects its extent during generation.
