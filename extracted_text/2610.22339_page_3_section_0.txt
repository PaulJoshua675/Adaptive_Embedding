Throughout this paper, mathematical search refers to retrieving library declarations for formal prov- ing, using either informal or formal queries. For additional related work, see Appendix A. Search for Formal Mathematics. Prior work shows that retrieving reusable library facts improves formal proof construction (Mikuła et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2023; Shen et al., 2025). Magnushammer learns premise selection through contrastive training and reranking for Isabelle (Mikuła et al., 2024), while ReProver and REAL-Prover condition tactic generation on retrieved Lean premises (Yang et al., 2023; Shen et al., 2025). LeanPremise supports premise selection in user-specific con- texts (Zhu et al., 2026), and LeanStateSearch adapts tokenization and reranking to formal proof-state queries (Tao et al., 2025). Complementary search systems help locate declarations from mathemat- ical descriptions: LeanExplore combines formal statements with informal descriptions for semantic search (Asher, 2025), and Lean Finder learns intent-aware embeddings from synthesized queries and preference feedback (Lu et al., 2026). LeanSearch v2 extends this line of work to global premise retrieval, combining embedding-based search and reranking with iterative sketch–retrieve–reflect cycles to gather supporting premises for an entire proof (Gao et al., 2026). Graph-based Retrieval. Graph structure has been used to guide retrieval in both natural-language corpora and formal libraries. It represents Mathlib as a directed declaration dependency graph (Fig. 2 (a)) and uses query-aware propagation to expand from semantically relevant declarations to their formal prerequisites (Fig. 2 (b)). Semantic relevance and dependency structure jointly guide promising declaration selection, followed by reranking to produce the final ranked list. 3.1 TASK FORMULATION Mathematical Retrieval. We study library declaration retrieval for theorem proving in Lean 4 (de Moura & Ullrich, 2021). Let L = {di}n i=1 denote the Mathlib declaration corpus. Each dec- laration di is identified by its name and associated with its formal statement and, when available, a natural-language description1. A retrieval query q can contain an informal mathematical description, a formal Lean statement, a proof state, or a combination of these. It may describe a target theorem or request a definition or lemma needed at an intermediate proof step. Given a query q, the retriever returns up to K declarations from L ranked by retrieval score, denoted R(q) = {dj}K j=1. We consider two complementary objectives: locating an individual declaration that matches the query and retrieving supporting premises for a target theorem. In the latter setting, a useful declaration may address an intermediate object or a side condition, and several declara- tions may be needed to support a complete proof. Retrieval must therefore capture the relevance of declarations in a proof, even when their statements differ substantially from the query. Theorem Proving.
