This paper studies a narrow but important question: given an image of Arabic calligraphy, can modern vision–language models identify its script style rather than collapse it into a familiar label? More specifically, when the choices are Kufic, Naskh, Thuluth, Diwani, Ruq’ah, and Nasta’liq, do models show stable visual discrimination, or do prompt wording and label order change which traditions become legible? This question sits at the intersection of rapid VLM progress [Radford et al., 2021, Alayrac et al., 2022, Liu et al., 2023], culturally diverse vision evaluation [Liu et al., 2021, Romero et al., 2024, Ramaswamy et al., 2023], and evidence that model outputs can shift with option order and prompt format [Zheng et al., 2024, Pezeshkpour and Hruschka, 2024, Sclar et al., 2024, Lu et al., 2022]. Prior work introduced DuwatBench for Arabic calligraphy understanding [Patle et al., 2026]; we focus on whether VLMs have reliable style priors after rebalancing the evaluation set and testing controlled prompt interventions. The task is culturally grounded for Muslim communities [Muslims in ML, 2026]: calligraphic styles carry historical, religious, regional, and institutional associations, so systematic misclassification can distort access to visual heritage. Our contributions are: • a cleaned and rebalanced 1,195-image benchmark for six-way Arabic calligraphy style recogni- tion; • a controlled evaluation of four closed/API VLMs and eight open-weight VLMs on the same image manifest; • three prompt conditions—numeric, visual-cue, and Diwani-first—that test whether style descrip- tions or explicit attention to an under-recognized class repair errors; and • analyses of default-label collapse, Thuluth over-prediction, Diwani under-recognition, and paired prompt effects. 40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026).
