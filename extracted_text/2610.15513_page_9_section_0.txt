The search space is the set of all natural-language prompts: vast, non-convex, discrete, and inaccessible to gradient methods because we operate in a black-box setting where only model responses are observable. This combination motivates evolutionary search guided by reflective LLM reasoning, with multi-objective selection to maintain diverse trade-offs. 3.2 Framework Architecture A core design principle of REPRO is that optimization intelligence should emerge from algorithmic structure rather than from the auxiliary LLM’s reasoning capability. Prior LLM-based red-teaming methods rely on the attacker LLM to generate effective mutations and degrade sharply when weaker auxiliary models are used. REPRO inverts this dependency: the algorithm provides intelligence through structured feedback, and the LLM serves as a parameterized text-transformation function. Structured failure analysis and strategy success statistics act as a shaped reward signal; the reflective LLM is the policy; evolutionary selection and Bayesian learning filter signal from noise even when individual mutations are low quality. Figure 1 illustrates the closed-loop architecture. REPRO begins from a single seed attack 𝑥0 and bootstraps an initial population of 𝑁variants using the reflective LLM at high temperature. Each subsequent generation runs seven phases: evaluation of all candidates against the target, post-processing to update strategy and ICL sta- tistics, parent selection via the epsilon-Pareto frontier, mutation under feedback-driven strategy selection, semantic crossover to recombine techniques across parents, self-refinement to filter and improve offspring before evaluation, and population selection with elitism. Multi-level stagnation management supervises the loop and triggers restarts or early termination as needed. Algorithm 1 presents the complete loop as a road map; each step references the subsection where its component is defined, and readers may skim the pseudocode now and refer back to it as components are introduced. 3.3 Reflective Machinery REPRO’s optimization loop is driven by two components that produce structured signals from the target model’s responses and feed them into every generation of the loop (Figure 2). Struc- tured failure analysis interprets why attacks fail and routes each failure mode to a ranked list of counter-strategies; Bayesian strategy selection then picks the next mutation strategy using a hybrid Thompson Sampling procedure that combines historical success, forward-looking analysis relevance, and exploration. We describe these components first, before the generation cycle that consumes them. 3.3.1 Structured Failure Analysis. Beyond tracking which behaviors fail, REPRO analyzes why they fail. This is the design element that most clearly distinguishes REPRO from prior iterative methods like PAIR and TAP, which feed raw failure responses back to the attacker LLM and rely on its reasoning to figure out what went wrong. REPRO instead produces a structured, stateful, domain-specialized signal that drives mutation guidance and strategy selection directly.
