What this design does not show bears emphasizing. Best-answer selection is measured on answer texts within threads, engagement on question texts across threads. A cluster positive on one outcome and negative on the other is therefore not evidence that a single post trades one for the other: the two coefficients describe different documents, writ­ ten by different users for different purposes. The recurrence of operational vocabulary on the selected-answer side and the low-engagement-question side is better read as a property of the question types a keyword-defined corpus contains — narrow, decisively answerable queries versus broad, debatable ones. Testing a genuine trade-off would require both outcomes defined on the same unit, for example a thread-level joint model of answer count and best-answer designation. This is consistent with work on content-context congruence in Q&A, where answers matching the question’s language attributes are judged more helpful [26] and thematic consistency predicts helpfulness while sentiment consistency does not [16], and with evidence that question framing shapes the breadth of engagement [8]. Because the asker chooses the best answer, the outcome reflects asker satisfaction rather than verified cor­ rectness [29]. 5.2 An exploratory typology and its limitations The cluster-level patterns of § 4.4 are grouped by the sign and significance of coefficients from separate models. No theory predicted these groupings, and the clusters were dis­ covered from the corpus rather than defined a priori, so the grouping is an exploratory typology that generates hypotheses — for instance, that answerability and discussability are distinct content dimensions rather than opposite ends of one axis. Two clusters (cal­ endar/dates, job-seeking-and-Q&A-framing) are lexical or meta-discursive artifacts and are excluded from substantive interpretation. Five further limitations bound what the results can support. First, the pipeline runs on machine translation. Review of a stratified 50-post sample (§ 3.2) found core topical nouns preserved and losses concentrated in proper nouns and rare loanwords, so error acts mainly as attenuation; systematic differences across term classes cannot be ruled out, and replication with a Japanese morphological pipeline remains the strongest check. Second, the analysis is restricted to nouns, so it characterizes topical content rather than wording in the broader sense; verbs, adjectives, politeness markers, sentiment, and discourse structure are excluded by design and may carry substantial signal. Third, the clusters group word vectors, not documents, and are one reproducible par­ tition of one embedding rather than a claim that twelve is the true number of semantic groups. The underlying vocabulary neighbourhoods survive retraining without reply and thanks posts (Table 10), but the partition is sensitive to the PCA dimensionality and to re-clustering. Roughly half of noun-token frequency remains unclassified background. Fourth, several confounders go unmeasured. Answerer reputation and history, asker characteristics, question category, thread exposure, and formatting features are absent from the scraped records, and because usernames were deliberately not retained (Dec­ larations), repeat-poster dependence cannot be modeled. All results are conditional associations.
