The estimation term, at the far smaller sample size S, dominates; we bound it next, sepa- rating sample-resolved positives from the unresolved tail. Estimation term: the visible mass concentrates. For a positive whose hardness the sample re- solves, the count estimate ˆxq,j = K/S has K ∼Binomial(S, xq,j), so by Hoeffding’s inequality |ˆxq,j −xq,j| ≤ p ln(2/δ)/(2S) with probability ≥1 −δ. This deviation propagates benignly: the top-rank survival (1 −x)N is N-Lipschitz in x (| d dx(1 −x)N| = N(1 −x)N−1 ≤N), and each AP precision j/(j + Nx) is 1 4x-Lipschitz (by AM–GM on (j + Nx)2 ≥4jNx), so resolved positives contribute error controlled by the bound above, and the end-to-end error stays ≤0.008 across all five backbones. Estimation term: the unresolved tail needs the model. The queries that dominate large-N ac- curacy have xq ≲1/S and are invisible in the sample (K=0); counting returns 0, so we estimate their hardness from the fitted upper tail—the gap posed as open by Madigan et al. (2003).
