Preprint Inference. At inference, each frame needs one encoder pass over the current observation and ten Euler steps on the action tokens. The registers do not read the action tokens and skip adaptive normalisation (§4.1), so neither they nor the context depend on the flow time τ or on the state. The registers of the current frame are thus computed together with the action tokens in the first Euler step, and their keys and values are reused at the nine others. The register keys and values are also kept for the frames that follow, (K −1) nr pairs per expert block. The cache is a rolling window, so the registers of the oldest frame are dropped when those of a new frame enter (Appendix Fig. 10). At training, each window of K frames is processed in isolation. At inference, the cached registers were themselves computed with the registers of earlier frames, so information can persist beyond the window. The VLM prefill and the expert are each compiled into a single CUDA graph. With two 256×256 images and a chunk of ten actions, REKI predicts a chunk in 21.8 ms on one H100 at K=3, the lowest latency among the VLAs compared in §6.3. In the measurement of Fig. 5d, the VLM prefill takes 9.5 ms and the Euler steps 8.1 ms, and reading the cached registers adds 0.1 ms. 5 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 5.1 PRETRAINING DATA Robot manipulation. We train on a broad mixture of public teleoperation datasets: RoboSet (Bharad- hwaj et al., 2024), Galaxea (Jiang et al., 2025), AgiBot World 2026 (AgiBot World Team, 2026), RoboCOIN (Wu et al., 2025b), RoboMIND (Wu et al., 2025a), RDT-1B (Liu et al., 2025), DROID (Khazatsky et al., 2024), BridgeData V2 (Walke et al., 2023), and twelve datasets of Open X-Embodiment (Open X-Embodiment Collaboration, 2024) including RT-1 (Brohan et al., 2023) and BC-Z (Jang et al., 2022) (Appendix B.1), complemented by motion-planned simulation from InternData-A1 (Tian et al., 2026) and GR00T-X-Embodiment-Sim (Bjorck et al., 2025) and bimanual YAM demonstrations from ABC (Allshire et al., 2026). These span single-arm, bimanual, mobile, and humanoid platforms across tabletop, in-the-wild, and loco-manipulation settings (Appendix Table 1). Action and state dimensions, control conventions and units vary across sources, and a robot may appear under several conventions. Following Qwen-VLA (Wang et al., 2026a), we keep each dataset’s native control convention rather than remapping to a shared action space, normalise each action dimension to [−1, 1] by per-dataset quantiles, and zero-pad actions to a common width under a loss mask. States are absolute, actions are absolute joint positions or delta end-effector poses, and rotations are harmonised to one Euler parameterisation. The prompt and the per-layout state projections of §3.2 tell the policy which convention a sample follows. In the spirit of Qwen-RobotManip’s multi-stage filtering (Yuan et al., 2026), we curate every source before training: we keep only the degrees of freedom the policy commands, discard static and near-zero-variance episodes, and correct per-dataset sign conventions. Appendix B.2 gives the curation stages and the action representation. Egocentric human manipulation. We add three egocentric human manipulation datasets, EgoDex (Hoque et al., 2026), EgoVerse (Punamiya et al., 2026) and Xperience (Ropedia, 2026), recorded from head-mounted or hand-held cameras. They contribute ∼350k episodes and 170M frames. The action target follows Qwen-VLA’s 32D bimanual layout (Wang et al., 2026a), wrist motion and hand articulation per hand, here with the wrist expressed in the camera frame; no propri- oceptive state is used, and each is resampled to the pace of robot teleoperation (Yuan et al., 2026). Appendix B.4 gives the sources, target definition and resampling. Vision–language data. We train on the FineVision (Wiedmann et al., 2025) and LLaVA-OneVision- 1.5 (An et al., 2025) datasets most relevant for spatial reasoning (Appendix B.5), as well as targeted spatial-grounding and pointing corpora: RoboPoint (Yuan et al., 2024), RefSpatial (Zhou et al., 2025), and PixMo (Deitke et al., 2025). This auxiliary supervision preserves the backbone’s perception and spatial reasoning, and is mixed in at a small ratio with the robot and egocentric data. 5.2 MODELS REKI pairs the Qwen3.5-2B vision–language backbone with an action expert of 10 blocks of width 768, 32 registers per frame and a memory window of K=3 frames. It is pretrained on the mixture above for 200k steps on 32 H100 GPUs with a global batch of 2,880 samples, then finetuned on each benchmark separately.
