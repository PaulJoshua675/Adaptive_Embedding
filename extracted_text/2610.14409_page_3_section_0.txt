(4) Inference is not a forward pass but a relaxation: with the input h0 = x clamped, the states descend E to a stationary point, and outputs are read from the settled state. Two properties of that stationary state are what Section 3.1 builds on. First, with only error terms present the minimum is e = 0, which by induction from h0 = x is exactly the forward pass: left to itself the relaxation reproduces the feedforward network it was built from. Second, stationarity ∂E/∂hk = 0 at an interior node gives ek = J⊤ k+1ek+1 with Jk+1 = ∂fk+1/∂hk, so any error introduced at the top propagates downwards through transposed Jacobians. Each term of equation 4 involves only a node and its predecessor, so both the relaxation and the parameter gradients ∂E/∂θk = e⊤ k ∂fk/∂θk are local: every quantity a weight needs is present at its two ends. This is what makes predictive coding a candidate for analog implementation, where the relaxation is performed by the physics rather than simulated. 2.3 EQUILIBRIUM PROPAGATION Equilibrium Propagation (Scellier & Bengio, 2017; Laborieux et al., 2020) is an alternative learning algorithm to backpropagation for systems whose dynamics settle to stationary points, from which their outputs are read. It uses only local measurements at equilibrium, making it a natural candidate for neuromorphic hardware. Consider a system with state s, parameters θ and energy E(s; θ), relaxing to an equilibrium s∗ whose position we want to train so that the outputs reduce a cost C(s). Adding βC(s) to the energy displaces that equilibrium towards lower cost, and the parameter gradient can be read from where and how far it moves. Each training sample therefore takes three phases: a free phase relaxing to s∗, then two nudged phases relaxing E ±βC to s∗±β, at lower and higher cost respectively. The update is ∆θ ∝−1 2β h∂E ∂θ s∗+β −∂E ∂θ s∗−β i −−−→ β→0 −∇θ C(s∗), (5) which lowers the energy at s∗+β and raises it at s∗−β, so that the free equilibrium s∗itself shifts towards lower cost. For small enough β this is exactly gradient descent on C(s∗). Each ∂E/∂θ is a local quantity — on a PCN, e⊤ k ∂fk/∂θk — so the whole procedure runs without global backward passes. EP has been scaled to CIFAR-10 and ImageNet classification, including on PCN energies (Kerjan et al., 2026), but its generative applications remain at MNIST scale and below (Meersch et al., 2023; Gower, 2026).
