In this work, we study weak-to-strong routing in its general setting, jointly accounting for test-time sampling of candidate weak responses, candidate selection, and escalation to a stronger model. Conventional cascades and standard routing approaches rely on signals of response correctness, which are often costly to obtain and ambiguous to define in open-ended or agentic tasks. To bypass the need for ground-truth supervision, we introduce a perspective based on model disagreement. We derive corrected disagreement, a principled approximation to the optimal routing rule that balances cross-model disagreement against the strong model’s internal uncertainty. This formulation provides an objective learning signal that can be collected at scale from model-generated responses alone.
