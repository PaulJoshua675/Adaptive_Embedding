Table 2 reports three scoring regimes of increasing structural awareness— Resolved (binary full-task correctness), Flat-F2P (frac- tion of attributed F2P tests passed (Thai et al. 2025)), and Graph Credit (§4.1)—together with OverCredit = Flat- F2P −Graph Credit and per-task efficiency (interaction turns, total tokens). Results. The strongest configuration (Claude Opus 4.7 + Cline, 48.7% Resolved) outperforms the weakest (MiniMax- 2.7 + Cline, 9.7%) by >5×, confirming that dependency- structured multi-PR tasks are qualitatively harder than iso- lated single-PR benchmarks where frontier models exceed 50% Resolved (Jimenez et al. 2023). Across all 16 con- figurations, Flat-F2P exceeds Graph Credit by 10.5 pp on average (range 7.2–16.8 pp), exposing substantial phantom credit. The gap is scaffold-sensitive: Cline configurations exhibit larger OverCredit (mean 12.2 pp) than OpenCode (mean 8.6 pp), indicating that Cline’s more exploratory strat- egy produces more downstream test passes that do not sur- vive dependency conditioning. Notably, OverCredit does not correlate monotonically with Flat-F2P: Kimi-K2.6 + Cline achieves the highest Flat-F2P (69.1%) yet exhibits only moderate OverCredit (11.9 pp), while DeepSeek-V4-Flash + Cline reaches 58.1% Flat-F2P with the largest OverCredit (16.8 pp)—suggesting that the former resolves upstream de- pendencies more faithfully despite extensive downstream progress. Replacing the true DAG with node-permuted ran- dom graphs reduces the gap to < 2 pp (Appendix), verifying that the signal arises from real dependency structure.
