Human correspondence and parametric reconstruction. A shared body template allows pixels on different people to be related through common anatomical coordinates (Güler et al., 2018). BodyMap and TemporalUV extend this association to clothing and hair (Ianina et al., 2022; Xie et al., 2022), while HumanGPS learns scan-supervised embeddings shared across subjects (Tan et al., 2021). These coordinates and embeddings establish correspondence but do not themselves reconstruct the individual’s body, garment or hair geometry. Mesh reconstruction methods such as 4DHumans (Goel et al., 2023) achieve body correspondence thanks to the fixed topology of the underlying parametric model, but do not represent the geometry and independent motion of garments or hair. Dense4D provides personalised correspondence: HPMs reconstruct reference-time points and locate later observations on them, without a shared body template. Deformable human reconstruction. ReLoo and DressRecon fit personalised surfaces, appearance and body/clothing deformation models to video through differentiable rendering (Guo et al., 2024; Tan et al., 2025). Their temporal correspondences follow the fitted 3D deformation fields used to render the reconstructed motion. Forge4D reconstructs pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians from uncalibrated sparse-view videos and predicts their motion between adjacent timestamps for novel-view and novel- time synthesis (Hu et al., 2026). Dense4D establishes correspondence across a set of monocular RGB images through a common human reference-time geometry, with correspondence and depth learned from dense 3D supervision. Point tracking and amodal correspondence. From Particle Video (Sand & Teller, 2006) to learned models (Harley et al., 2022; Doersch et al., 2023; Karaev et al., 2025), image point trackers estimate query trajectories through occlusion. Three-dimensional trackers add depth or geometric estimation (Xiao et al., 2024; 2025; Ngo et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025). HPMs also locate points in 3D at the reference time even when hidden in the reference image. Object-centric representations. NOCS predicts category-level object coordinates (Wang et al., 2019); CaSPR learns a canonical spatiotemporal space from dynamic point clouds (Rempe et al., 2020). DualPM pairs posed and rest-pose maps for quadrupeds (Kaye et al., 2025); Face Anything predicts template-aligned facial coordinates and depth (Kocasari et al., 2026). Dense4D instead matches observations of a deforming person using that person’s own reference-time geometry. Scene reconstruction. Point-map models represent pixels from different images as 3D points in a shared reference camera frame (Wang et al., 2024a; Leroy et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025a). Human- aware models add person outputs: HAMSt3R adds segmentation and DensePose to MASt3R (Rojas et al., 2025), while Human3R recovers SMPL-X bodies with scene geometry and cameras from monocular video (Chen et al., 2026). Feed-forward models extend point-based reconstruction to dynamic scenes (Feng et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026; Jiang et al., 2026a), often jointly training geometry, motion and camera predic- tors (Karhade et al., 2025; Luo et al., 2026; Sucar et al., 2026). Building on predicted geometry, 4D Primitive-Mâché uses dense image correspondences to optimise rigid-primitive motion and aggregate observations across time (Mazur et al., 2026). Dynamic Point Maps and V-DPM are closest to our representation: they express observations at a shared reference time and viewpoint, allowing correspondence to be established by matching shared-space coordinates (Sucar et al., 2025; 2026). Dense4D tracks by matching rather than by decoding point motion: every observed person pixel is mapped to the reference surface, correspondences are found among the target frame’s observed pixels, and each match is lifted with that frame’s own predicted depth. Tracks therefore lie on the target frame’s predicted geometry by construction; visibility is estimated from the matching residual, and no motion or camera-pose prediction is required for camera-space tracking. 3
