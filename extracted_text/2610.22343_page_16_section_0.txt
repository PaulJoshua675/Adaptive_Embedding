Each token is first linearly projected from 512 to a model width of 1,152. A learned mask token is then replicated over 512 slots to construct a fifth state, which serves as the prediction position. To encode token identity, we use learned temporal and slot embeddings of 576 dimensions each. Their concatenation forms a 1,152-dimensional positional embedding, which is added to the projected tokens. Each Transformer block factorizes attention over time and space. Temporal attention is first applied across the five time positions independently for each of the 512 slots. Spatial attention then operates across the 512 slots independently within each time position. The two attention operations are followed by a 1152 →4608 →1152 feed-forward network with GELU. All sublayers use pre- normalization, residual connections, and dropout. After the final block, the tokens at the fifth, masked prediction position are mapped back to 512 channels by a bias-free linear residual head. The head is zero-initialized, such that the model initially predicts a zero residual and therefore exactly recovers the Copy-Last prediction. A.2 CAMERA PREDICTOR The camera predictor forecasts future FRONT-camera poses from the observed ego-motion and the current FRONT image. The predicted trajectory is used as the future query-camera sequence for both GeoState (Pred. Camera) and Copy-Last (Pred. Camera). A.2.1 ARCHITECTURE The camera predictor contains 8.99M parameters and follows a motion-prior-plus-visual-refinement design. Given four historical metric FRONT poses at t−0.6, t−0.4, t−0.2, and t seconds, we first compute the three consecutive relative transformations and map them to six-dimensional motion vectors using the SE(3) logarithm. The motion vectors and their first differences are standardized, projected to width 256, and encoded by a four-layer Transformer with eight attention heads, an MLP expansion ratio of four, and dropout 0.1. Future poses are initialized from a constant-body- twist extrapolation of the latest observed motion. Learned horizon queries attend to the encoded motion features through three decoding layers and predict residual corrections to this extrapolated trajectory. Future time is represented using linear and quadratic terms together with eight Fourier bands. To incorporate scene-dependent cues, the predictor additionally conditions on the current FRONT image. Frozen VGGT-Ωspatial features are extracted from a separate forward pass and pooled to a 6 × 8 grid, yielding 48 tokens of width 2,048. After projection to width 256 and the addition of learned spatial embeddings, two cross-attention layers compress these features into eight visual tokens. The visual tokens are appended to the encoded motion memory, and two additional horizon- query decoding layers, with eight attention heads and an MLP expansion ratio of two, predict a six-dimensional visual correction ∆ξh for each requested horizon.
