Using RUSTKNN as a controlled experimental platform, we have shown that cover tree perfor- mance depends on interactions between query strategy, k, base parameter, and micro-optimizations in ways not apparent from cross-codebase comparisons. The best query mode depends on k and on the dataset—single-tree search at k=1 on most of our larger datasets, dual-tree search at k≥5 on the largest—and choosing the wrong mode costs up to 7.5×; base 1.3 is not a safe default for dual-tree search (2.0 or more is optimal in 22 of 24 dataset–k cases); early-exit distance is the one micro- optimization that matters, and its benefit grows with dimension (1.31× at d=54, 1.49× at d=784) while leaving the traversal unchanged; and all-NN and held-out benchmarks produce different im- plementation rankings, so both should be reported. Reporting the main results over 20 independent allocations with bootstrap intervals is what separated these effects from measurement noise. Our conclusions are scoped to exact nearest neighbor search under Euclidean distance, single- threaded, on datasets up to n≈581K. We do not study approximate methods; whether these effects transfer to HNSW/IVF-style indexes is open, as are non-Euclidean metrics, parallel scaling, and n > 106. Timings on the smallest datasets are noise-limited (Section 4.2); cross-codebase tim- ings carry an instruction-set disparity (AVX-512 vs. SSE2; Appendix B), though within-RUSTKNN comparisons are unaffected. Large-dataset times were measured on fully loaded nodes, at least ∼2× an idle machine’s (Appendix B); we interpret only ratios measured under the same load.
