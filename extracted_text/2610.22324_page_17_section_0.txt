B.3 DARLING DARLING (Li et al., 2025) optimizes quality and diversity across the rollouts of one prompt, whereas our reward enforces diversity within a single generated set. We re-implement it in our setting so that the only intended difference from our method is where diversity is enforced. We keep DARLING’s group-partition diversity term and its multiplicative reward, and we replace its learned equivalence classifier and quality reward with the judges used elsewhere in this paper. Setup (no SFT). DARLING is trained with K=1: each rollout is a single idea card, and the group of rollouts for a prompt provides the diversity signal. Following their recipe, we start GRPO directly from Qwen3-8B, with no SFT warm-start. The model therefore learns the card format only from the schema check in the reward and from the prompt (identical to Appendix B). This matches the original method, which also applies RL to an instruction-tuned model without a task-specific SFT stage. The training instances, prompt, and judges are the same as for our method. Reward. For a group of n=8 rollouts {y1, . . . , yn} for the same prompt, the reward of rollout i is ri = q(yi) · divi, divi = max  0.1, n −|C(i)| n −1  , (8) where q(yi) is the same pairwise impact win against the gold idea as in Appendix C.1 (SciJudge- 30B), and C(i) is the cluster containing i. Clusters come from union-find over a pairwise equiva- lence test, as in DARLING’s released code. Two rollouts are merged if the distance between their ideas is below a threshold of 0.5. Instead of DARLING’s trained Qwen3-Embedding classifier, we test equivalence with our distance judge (gpt-oss-120b, Appendix C). A rollout in a large group of near-duplicates therefore gets a low diversity score, and a unique rollout gets a score close to 1. With K=1 there is one idea per rollout, so no set-level aggregation is needed. An invalid rollout receives reward 0. Optimization. We follow DARLING’s recipe: n=8 rollouts per prompt, 12 prompts per step, advantages without standard-deviation normalization, symmetric PPO clipping at 0.2, no entropy bonus, KL coefficient 0.001, learning rate 1 × 10−6, and rollout temperature 1.0 for training and validation. Evaluation. At test time we sample three independent generations per instance and treat them as the set, since the policy was trained to produce one idea per generation. All metrics in Section 5 are computed on this set with the same judges as for the other models. B.4 OLMO-3 DETAILS To test that the recipe is not specific to one model family, we repeat it on Olmo-3-7B (Olmo et al., 2025). We initialize from allenai/Olmo-3-7B-Think-SFT, and run the same SFT (with and without thinking) and GRPO settings as Table 7. The length penalty is measured with the Olmo tokenizer. The two tokenizers count the card text within about 0.5%, so L0 is unchanged. Results are in Appendix E (Transfer of recipe to other models). B.5 COMPUTE All runs use a single node with 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs. SFT uses all 8 GPUs and takes about 46 minutes per epoch on 7,172 instances. For GRPO, 6 GPUs hold the policy and the vLLM rollouts, and 2 GPUs serve SciJudge-30B (tensor parallel size 2); the gpt-oss-120b distance judge is served through an external API and needs no local GPUs.
