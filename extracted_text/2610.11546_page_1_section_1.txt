1.1 Background and existing studies Structural model updating, as an essential back-analysis strategy within Structural Health Monitoring (SHM), has been studied for decades for its effectiveness in assessing a structural system's condition through tracking key physical parameters. In recent years, the field has continued to evolve in response to the growing population of aging structures requiring condition assessment, and, more importantly, in response to continually and explosively advancing data-driven and Artificial Intelligence (AI)-driven techniques [1, 2]. One of the earliest and most influential stages in the evolution of structural model updating methods centers on optimization, since most model updating and related back-analysis tasks are fundamentally minimization problems over the discrepancy between a model’s predicted response and the observed response of the target structure [3]. This framework is typically built upon a physical model, e.g., a Finite Element Model (FEM), whose structural parameters are optimized until the simulated output matches the observations, giving rise to the well-known Finite Element Model Updating (FEMU) framework [4, 5]. Accordingly, a wide range of optimization algorithms has been extensively studied. Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs) were among the earliest and most thoroughly investigated optimizer classes [6], with representative examples including Genetic Algorithms (GA) [7], Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) [8], Differential Evolution (DE) [9], Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) [10], and their many subsequent variants and hybrids [11].
