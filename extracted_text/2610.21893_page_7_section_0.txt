The ad- vantage is especially clear for goal-level Macro accuracy: at a 20% budget, the goal-conditioned setting achieves 61.44% Micro and 19.35% Macro accuracy, compared with 42.55% and 6.45% with- out a goal. The Micro curve improves steadily as more tokens become available, whereas Macro accuracy remains much lower at tight budgets be- cause it requires all questions associated with a goal to be answered correctly. The token statistics further show that, under the same token-budget in- struction, compression without a goal uses more to- kens on average, yet achieves lower accuracy. This highlights the importance of goal instructions for directing information retention: goal-conditioned compression uses the budget more effectively by prioritizing goal-relevant evidence rather than sim- ply producing longer compressed memories. 4.5 Comparison of Compression Mechanisms We compare GACC Prompt with LLMLingua, LLMLingua-2, and LongLLMLingua on GACC- Bench-testmini under a nominal 5% retention bud- get. All methods use the same fixed DeepSeek- V4-Pro reader and judge. GACC Prompt receives the target goal and performs prompt-based com- pression followed by prefix truncation, whereas the baselines use task-agnostic or query-conditioned token pruning. As shown in Table 5, GACC Prompt achieves the highest Micro and Macro ac- curacy, reaching 27.04% and 4.84%, respectively. LLMLingua-2 is the strongest external baseline in Micro accuracy at 11.52%, while all external baselines obtain zero Macro accuracy. This Micro– Macro contrast suggests that token-pruning meth- ods may preserve evidence for individual ques- tions but fail to retain the complementary evi- dence required for complete goal coverage.
