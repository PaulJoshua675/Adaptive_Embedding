Beyond EA-based model updating, which optimizes structural parameters directly and deterministically, Bayesian model updating offers a probabilistic and more information-rich alternative that fully incorporates observation data: a prior distribution over the unknown model parameters (informed by expert judgment) is updated to a posterior distribution once the observations are taken into account [13]. Classical approaches to estimating these posteriors are dominated by Monte Carlo methods, including Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) [14], Transitional MCMC (TMCMC) [15], and particle filter-based approaches (also known as Sequential Monte Carlo) [16]. Recent studies cover some relatively more advanced approaches such as those based on variational Bayesian inference [17, 18], which are generally based on approximating the target posterior density with a variational density searched from a rich family of parameterized densities by minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the true posterior density and the variational one. With the rapid growth of AI, AI-driven methods have become increasingly prominent across engineering fields, and data-driven surrogate modeling has emerged as another major thrust in structural model updating research [19]. Surrogate models have strong potential to reduce reliance on high-level FEM expertise and to lower the computational cost of repeated FEM evaluations, thereby facilitating practical FEMU implementation [20]. Neural network-based surrogates in particular, enable efficient inverse analysis and rapid, adaptive parameter identification [20]. Sabamehr et al. [21] combined neural networks with GAs to relate natural frequency shifts to sectional property changes in bridges, outperforming traditional matrix-based FEMU methods. Yin et al. [22] developed a Bayesian neural network for FEMU of a pedestrian bridge that achieved greater accuracy and efficiency than finite-difference approaches. Zhang et al. [23] introduced a physics-guided neural network integrating data-driven learning with FEM principles to improve damage detection in both simulated and experimental settings. Despite these advances, existing data-driven surrogates still fall short of fully integrating physical principles with measured response data, and cannot yet reliably substitute for FEMU or other physics-based models in capturing the full complexity of structural dynamic behavior [17]. This gap has also been observed in many advanced Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs), which, despite explicitly embedding physical constraints into the learning process, continue to struggle under ambiguous boundary conditions or noisy measurements [24]. These limitations, however, also mark out a broad and promising direction for further development of data-driven surrogate modeling. This AI-driven mindset in model updating has so far concentrated mainly on data-driven surrogates, with comparatively less attention paid to the optimization side of the problem. One representative optimization strategy that does incorporate AI is Bayesian Optimization (BO), which uses an acquisition function to decide where to sample next, generally in conjunction with a data-driven surrogate such as Gaussian Process models [25, 26]. Notably, many of these BO methods accelerate convergence and reduce the number of required FEM evaluations, but remain fundamentally a data-driven (probabilistic) surrogate-based model updating framework rather than a direct or efficient advance in optimization strategies. More recently, Reinforcement Learning (RL), emerging from optimal control theory and increasingly driven by AI, has been extensively developed and applied across engineering fields, including SHM [27]. Among them, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) [28], as the state-of-the-art integration of RL with deep learning, has become a leading paradigm for agent-based learning and has been actively explored for developing intelligent, manual-operation-free optimization algorithms, showing clear advantages over traditional optimization methods in dynamic and complex environments. Within SHM, Meng et al. [29] developed a Double Deep Q-Network (DDQN)-based DRL algorithm to solve the discrete combinatorial optimization problem of vibration sensor placement. Zhong et al. [30] proposed a heuristic-enhanced DRL algorithm for efficient optimal placement of multiple sensor types. Guo et al. [31] proposed a DRL-based control strategy for the dynamic response of building structures during earthquakes and other natural disasters. Nishat et al. [32] proposed a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)-based DRL framework for autonomous control of Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) tailored to SHM applications. However, existing studies remain limited in developing DRL-based optimization algorithms suited to direct mathematical optimization and structural model updating tasks, which are inherently continuous-variable problems.
