Under this general population setting, the distri- bution of individuals corresponding to different tasks and the dynamic and complex nature of cross-task knowledge transfer have not been studied in ex- isting theories. How to establish the mathematical runtime analysis for the evolutionary multitask multi-objective optimization remains an important open problem. Our contributions. This work takes the first step. We analyze the ex- pected runtime for the representative MO-MFEA to cover the Pareto fronts of two similar bi-objective optimization problems simultaneously. For the two-task problem that consists of the classic OMM and a similar but more complex multimodal OJZJ problems of size n and gap size k, we prove that the MO-MFEA with a population size N = Ω(n) requires O(nN log n) expected number of function evaluations to cover the Pareto fronts of two task simultaneously (by the individuals corresponding to each respective task) (see Corollary 12). Thus, the expected runtime for the MO-MFEA with N = Θ(n) to cover the Pareto front of OJZJ is O(n2 log n), which achieves a speedup by a factor of Θ(nk−1/ log n) compared with the gen- eral runtime O(nk+1) for either toy or popular multi-objective evolution- ary algorithms solving OJZJ alone, see [ZD23b] for GSEMO, [DQ23a] for NSGA-II, [BZLQ23] for SMS-EMOA, [WD24] for NSGA-III (setting m = 2 for mOJZJ), and [RBLQ24] for SPEA-II (setting m = 2 for mOJZJ). Mean- while, this runtime O(n2 log n) for the MO-MFEA with N = Θ(n) on the simple OMM benchmark is the same as optimizing OMM alone (see [GL10] for (G)SEMO, [ZLD22, ZD23a] for NSGA-II, [ODNS24] for NSGA-III (set- ting m = 2 for the mOMM), [ZD24b] for SMS-EMOA, and [RBLQ24] for SPEA-II (setting m = 2 for the mOMM). Similarly, when simultaneously optimizing the classic LOTZ and a similar but complex multimodal DLTB benchmarks, the MO-MFEA with popula- tion size N = Θ(n) requires the runtime O(n3) to cover the Pareto fronts of LOTZ and DLTB simultaneously (see Corollary 18). For DLTB, it achieves a speedup of Θ(n), compared with solving it alone (see [ZLDD24] for GSEMO, NSGA-II, NSGA-III, and SMS-EMOA). For LOTZ, this runtime matches that of single-task optimization (see [LTZ04] for (G)SEMO, [ZLD22] for NSGA-II, [ODNS24] for NSGA-III (setting m = 2 for the mLOTZ), [ZD24b] for SMS-EMOA, and [RBLQ24] for SPEA-II (setting m = 2 for the mLOTZ)). In summary, our theoretical results provide the first formal evidence that positive knowledge transfer in multi-objective multitask optimization can provably reduce the runtime of a complex problem to that of a simpler, related partner task. 3
