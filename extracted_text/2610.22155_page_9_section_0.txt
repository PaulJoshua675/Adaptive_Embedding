Preprint 7.2 CONTINUOUS PERCEPTION AND ACTION Adapting deliberation also depends on receiving timely observations and updating actions as the environment changes. At the 1 FPS observation rate used in our experiments, brief events or rapid changes may occur between observations, even when inference itself incurs no temporal cost. For example, an enemy may change direction between two frames, preventing the agent from adjusting its action until the next observation. Higher observation rates could provide more timely feedback, but realizing this benefit requires models and harnesses that can process incoming information and revise actions with sufficiently low latency. (1) Model design. Higher temporal resolution presents two coupled challenges: processing more frames increases inference cost, while compressing visual inputs risks discarding brief changes that determine the next action. Addressing these challenges requires optimizing both (1) processing ef- ficiency, for example by replacing cascaded architectures with an end-to-end single Transformer using block-causal attention (Huang et al., 2026), and (2) the preservation of action-relevant infor- mation through compact temporal representations (Ding et al., 2025) or codec-aware visual encod- ing (Sarkar et al., 2026). Future work should explore these two directions to support higher frame rates with lower latency while preserving important information. (2) Interaction harnesses. Real-time harnesses must manage context and memory while coordinat- ing observations and actions in a continuously changing environment.
