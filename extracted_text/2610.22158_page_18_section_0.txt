C.4 Consensus Coverage The four mechanisms are high-precision response- level attributions rather than an exhaustive parti- tion of failure behavior. Of the 8,000 sampled fail- ures, 1,305 (16.31%) receive no strict-consensus M01–M04 label. This total should not be read as 1,305 cases with no cause. In 591 cases, at least one attribution judge supports a mechanism but the two judges share no positive label: Claude assigns no label while GPT assigns at least one in 222 cases, GPT assigns no label while Claude assigns at least one in 318 cases, and both assign positive but disjoint label sets in 51 cases. The remaining 714 cases (8.93%) are jointly assigned no covered mechanism by either judge. Among these 714 jointly unlabeled cases, 687 (96.22%) have the validated behavioral profile SQL Behavior= 0, Detection= 0, and Type Identifi- cation= 0: the model produces complete SQL without explicitly recognizing the premise flaw or identifying its intended type. This profile es- tablishes a recognition failure, but it does not by itself identify how the response transformed or misused the flawed premise. For example, a re- sponse may leave an unspecified condition broad, preserve contradictory predicates literally, or fol- low a context-grounded reading without injecting a concrete assumption, reinterpreting the contra- diction, misusing bounded context, or deleting a complete explicit constraint. We therefore treat Detection= 0 as a behavioral outcome rather than relabeling it as M01–M04. Accordingly, an empty strict-consensus mechanism set means no observ- able covered mechanism, not an error-free response and not an absence of a behavioral failure. C.5 Prompt-Type, Benchmark-Type, and Error-Type Analyses Prompt and benchmark breakdown. The prompt-level reversal between M01 and M03 oc- curs in both source benchmarks. From Base to Explicit prompting, M01 declines from 72.10% to 25.30% on BIRD and from 88.99% to 39.45% on Spider; over the same contrast, M03 rises from 1.94% to 64.99% and from 1.03% to 48.14%, re- spectively. The Implicit setting lies between these endpoints in both datasets. M02 and M04 remain rare across prompting settings relative to assump- tion injection and context/evidence misuse. Be- cause this analysis is conditioned on eligible fail- ures, these percentages describe the composition of observed failures rather than the unconditional probability that a prompt produces an error. For the prompt-association tests, each of the 8,000 sampled responses is one observation. We construct a separate 3 × 2 contingency table for each mechanism, crossing prompting setting (Base, Implicit, Explicit) with the mechanism’s strict- consensus presence or absence, and apply Pear- son’s χ2 test with Cramér’s V as the effect size. Because mechanisms are non-exclusive, a response may be positive in more than one table. We adjust the four mechanism-specific p-values as one fam- ily using Holm’s method; the reported p < .001 results for M01 and M03 remain below .001 after adjustment. Mechanisms by source error category. Ta- ble 15 reports strict-consensus incidence within each represented source error category in the an- alyzed sample. M02 is sharply concentrated in internal textual contradictions, where it appears in 50.69% of sampled failures. M04 is rare overall and is most frequent for question–schema contra- dictions (4.51%). M01 dominates semantic gaps and both beyond-ability categories, whereas M03 is most common for ambiguity, unclear intent, and missing constraints. Thus, the source taxonomy and response mechanisms are related but not inter- changeable: their mapping is many-to-many, be- cause one source category can yield multiple mech- anisms and the same mechanism can recur across categories.
