Consequently, a critical bottleneck stems from the lack of an effective caption representation that unifies video-grounded structural semantics with general language modeling. To address this issue, we propose framing video captioning as assembling discrete, atomic audiovisual event sequences through joint optimization of event content and their correspondences. As illustrated in Fig. 1, a video consists of multiple audio and visual events, where each event is represented as a tuple of type, character, and content. These events can be aligned temporally to form a coherent caption clip, linked via shared semantic content and explicit synchronous connectors (e.g., “as”, “at the same time”). Crucially, such an event-centric perspective bridges audiovisual events and captions by modeling both intra-event attribution (i.e., whether the attributions of a single event are correct?) and inter-event synchronization (i.e., what are the temporal order and synchronization of different events?). While such an event-centric perspective provides a structured semantic understanding of videos beyond mere language modeling, effectively integrating such insights into OLMs post-training remains an open question. This gap is largely due to (1) the scarcity of high-quality audiovisual caption data with event-level fidelity, and (2) the absence of dedicated event-centric frameworks for leveraging supervision with event-level heuristics. To this end, we propose EVA-Cap, which builds upon Qwen2.5-Omni [45] and unifies event-centric alignment within the language modeling post-training phase. Specifically, we first construct EVA-Data, a 63K audiovisual video–caption dataset, via an automatic initialization–refinement pipeline followed by event-centric quality control. In this process, timestamped audiovisual events are extracted from raw videos and subjected to per-event verification to ensure high fidelity. Furthermore, we introduce a two-stage post-training strategy to facilitate event-centric alignment, which consists of Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) [27] on the EVA-Data. During the GRPO stage, three event-centric rewards are designed to enforce global completeness (via event recall), intra-event attribution (via character matching), and inter-event synchronization (via cross-modal retrieval). Collectively, these post-training objectives enhance the fine-grained alignment of audiovisual events, enabling factually accurate, event-grounded audiovisual caption generation. Extensive experiments demonstrate that EVA-Cap outperforms existing open-source models across 6 audiovisual benchmarks. For example, on general captioning benchmarks, EVA-Cap achieves a total error rate of 34.6% on video-SALMONN-2 testset [29] and surpasses existing methods by a clear margin of 3.3% on WorldSense [15]. Most importantly, on event-centric evaluation with ChronusAV [7] and AV-SpeakerBench [23], EVA-Cap exhibits substantial advantages with an accuracy of 55.4%, 56.2%, and 40.1% for Visual-to-Audio (V2A) inference, Audio-to-Visual (A2V) grounding, and speaker attribution, respectively. These results underscore the capability of EVA-Cap in generating highly faithful captions with precise temporal alignment and accurate event attribution. 2
