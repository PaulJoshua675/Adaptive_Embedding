Beyond these advances in broader data utilization, stronger encoders, knowledge integra- tion, or finer-grained alignment, SCCL addresses a distinct source of supervision error: treating semantically compati- ble off-diagonal pairs as negatives. 2.2. False negatives in contrastive learning Instance-discrimination objectives treat unmatched samples as negatives, so semantically compatible pairs may receive repulsive gradients [4, 10, 23, 32]. Unlike valid hard negatives, false negatives should not be repelled. Prior work addresses this issue through distribution correction, hard-negative mining, progressive false-negative removal, or hardness scheduling [5, 7, 15, 26]. Medical VLP methods instead exploit clinical knowl- edge or report semantics. MedCLIP and MLIP construct knowledge-guided targets [19, 30]; SAT, SENSE, and FaNe use report- or study-level similarity to mine or reweight pairs [20, 21, 33]; and SAHN separates hard from false neg- atives using embedding and semantic similarity [18]. SCCL differs by applying modality-specific adaptive thresholds, hard-masking selected pairs without relabeling them, and distilling its rule-specific teachers into one student. 2.3. Knowledge distillation Knowledge distillation transfers predictive distributions from a teacher to a student, commonly to compress a model or retain teacher behavior in a more deployment-efficient network [11]. Deep mutual learning extends this idea to online peer supervision [36], while ALBEF uses a momen- tum teacher to provide stable targets for noisy image–text alignment [17]. These formulations differ in teacher con- struction but use distillation to transfer or stabilize predic- tive behavior. In SCCL, standard distillation consolidates two rule-specific teachers—an inclusive teacher and a con- sensus teacher—into a single student for efficient inference.
