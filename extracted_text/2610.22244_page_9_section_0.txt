A guard that is nearly invariant to how a request is worded but not to which language it is written in has learned the language, more than the register, as a feature. 5 Discussion What to deploy. For a guard that must serve non-English users, size is a proxy for the failure mode to expect: 17–30B open models tend to over-block, the guards that under-block are all below 10B, and neither shows up in an English-only evaluation. Purpose-built agent guards are not exempt; they are the most sensitive class we measured, and their direction has to be measured per guard. We evaluated no mitigation; translate-then-guard or per-language thresholds are untested candidates for the over-blocking regime. The concentration in enumerated multi-task instructions (§4.6) suggests a cheap diagnostic: evaluate guards on translated long instructions specifically, and on a low-resource language, where the effect is largest. The request field is not what strong guards use. The Gemini/scanner identity and the leaky-step mechanism point the same way: on trajectory-rich benchmarks the agent’s own reasoning restates the request, and strong guards decide from the trajectory. This is good news for robustness and bad news for benchmark validity: a benchmark cannot test a guard’s use of the trusted channel if the untrusted channel already contains it. It also reframes language robustness: a guard is hurt by translation to the extent that it compares channels, not to the extent that it reads the request. Limitations. The LLM paraphrases and all five translations were generated by Gemini 3.8 Flash, which is also a guard; its measured invariance is therefore a lower bound, although the scanner identity makes generator overlap an unlikely explanation. Nobody on the team reads Swahili or Amharic, so those rows were accepted on the strength of two judges and back-translation, and the low-resource pair was not run on the partner-hosted guards (Claude, Llama, GLM, gpt-oss). The small tier and the low-resource pair were added after the pre-registered runs (ten guards, three languages), each with its own frozen-before-inference file. The alignment prompt gives open models high false- positive rates (0.26–0.37) at the original wording; effects are reported as shifts from each guard’s own operating point. gpt-oss-120b was quota-throttled and completed 7 of the 11 pre-registered perturbed conditions. ShieldAgent, Safiron and AgentDoG were built to judge whole interaction records, plans or trajectories; we feed them the prefix up to the candidate action, and Safiron’s input format has no observation channel, so our adapter places each step’s thought, tool call and observation in its action list. Their error rates against step labels are therefore not comparable with the step-level guards; their flip rates are.
