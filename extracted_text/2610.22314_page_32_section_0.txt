Three clinical psychologists, with extensive annotation ex- perience, followed written guidelines and annotated con- versations in a web interface. The pool mixed real sessions with sessions from our simulator, MindEval and Patient- Ψ, held with four counsellor LMs: (1) the production ser- vice’s counsellor LM, a proprietary model that roughly matches the setup of the real sessions; (2) GPT-5.6 Terra and (3) GLM-5.2-FP8, both with the benchmark’s generic counsellor prompt (Figure 13); and (4) Claude Sonnet 4.6 with its consumer-app system prompt. The overall anno- tation pool contains 180 windows from 84 source conver- sations: 113 single-turn windows, 19 nine-turn windows covering turns 2–10, and 48 five-turn windows covering turns 6–10. Each turn pairs a counsellor LM message with the patient’s reply, and both are annotated in the same pass but independently: the counsellor LM message for clinical issues and the patient reply for realism. Fifteen items (71 turns) were annotated by all three annotators to measure agreement. Clinical issues. For each counsellor LM turn, annotators assign one severity (no concern, minor, major or critical) and flag which of fourteen clinical issues are present, following the taxonomy and guidelines in Appendix A.
