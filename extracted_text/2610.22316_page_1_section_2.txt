Large language model (LLM)-based agents (Yao et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024; Xi et al., 2025) and multi-agent sys- tems (MAS) (Wu et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024) increasingly tackle complex tasks through long-horizon tool use (Schick et al., 2023) and inter-agent collaboration (Hong et al., 2024), spanning information seeking (Team et al., 2025), software engineering (OpenAI, 2025), and scientific discovery (Qu et al., 2026; Zhu et al., 2026). How- ever, these systems are prone to goal drift, where agents deviate from the overall goal as execution unfolds (Arike et al., 2025; Cemri et al., 2025; Laban et al., 2026). A single agent may persist along an unproductive chain of actions, while an agent in a MAS may prioritize its local objective over the collective goal. Goal drift can waste execution resources and lead to undesired outcomes, even when individual actions appear locally reasonable (Cemri et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026a).
