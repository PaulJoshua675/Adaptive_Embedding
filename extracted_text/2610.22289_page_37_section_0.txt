D Motivation for the RHAE70 Reference Factor According to the AGI Maze team, the benchmark’s max_steps budgets were defined with some slack. The team shared results from internal playthroughs covering eight labyrinths, conducted by one practiced player who knew the mechanics but had not previously seen those labyrinths. Four labyrinths had an action budget of 180 and four had a budget of 230. The mean per-labyrinth ratio of actions used to budget was approximately 0.617. Seven playthroughs used fewer than 70 percent of the budget; the remaining ratio was 162/230 ≈0.704. These observations informed our choice of 0.7 as a rounded reference factor for awarding full credit. These internal tests were not designed to estimate representative human performance or calibrate a reference across all labyrinth types. RHAE70 is an author-defined, RHAE-inspired score using a proxy action reference, not a measured human-baseline score. A value of 100 means that the target was solved within the chosen threshold; it does not establish human-level or superhuman performance. The score saturates at that threshold and does not distinguish faster solutions. Score selection and sensitivity. RHAE70 was adopted during analysis and manuscript preparation, after examining mean step fractions, to give the reported benchmark scores a consistent higher-is-better direction. The internal playthrough results shared by the AGI Maze team informed the choice of reference factor. We also examined mean step fraction and analogous scores with reference factors of 0.6 and 0.8. The broad comparison favors Stop-and-report, but the ordering is not invariant to normalization: for GPT-5.5 xhigh with curriculum, RHAE80 slightly favors Baseline, at 67.14% versus 66.54%.
