These runs show that research can continue through a revised task network and reach a correct answer after the original execution encounters a recovery condition. Ablation Studies. To isolate the contribution of each com- ponent, we evaluate three ablated variants, removing result- informed planning, local multi-agent orchestration, and task- network rebuilding in turn while preserving the remaining mechanisms (Appendix C gives detailed definitions and ex- tended results). All three reduce accuracy (Table 4), but with distinct effects on execution behavior (Figure 4; counts cover all epochs, with No rebuilding restricted to its single epoch). Upfront decomposition is the most direct test of the central mechanism because it changes only whether decomposi- tion occurs before or after prerequisite results return, while retaining dependency-based execution, local multi-agent or- chestration, and rebuilding. Without current-epoch research results to narrow subsequent decomposition, accuracy de- creases from 92.0% to 90.5%. This less targeted planning also increases coordination effort at the median, with worker invocations rising from 14 to 16, Orchestrator rounds from 14 to 17, and the rebuilding rate from 17.5% to 22.5%. Me- dian completion time also rises from 25.23 to 30.02 minutes. The full system therefore achieves higher accuracy with less coordination and lower latency, indicating that intermediate findings guide more effective task decomposition. Single-agent leaf execution replaces each leaf’s local orches- tration with a single ReAct researcher while retaining HTN planning, synthesis, and task-network rebuilding.
