The O-RAN ALLIANCE enables flexible, multi-vendor RAN ecosystems through open interfaces and programmable control [1]. Central to this architecture, the RAN Intelligent Controller (RIC) provides near-real-time closed-loop optimiza- tion for diverse service types, including eMBB, URLLC, mMTC, and ISAC [2]. The O-RAN paradigm integrates AI directly into the control loop, requiring autonomous adapta- tion, real-time responsiveness, and intelligent decision-making under uncertainty [3]. However, realizing this AI-native vi- sion faces several fundamental challenges [4], [5], including: (i) real-time responsiveness, requiring decisions within strict latency bounds (sub-1s for Near-RT RIC) while adapting to dynamic conditions; (ii) self-optimization under uncertainty, as static policies cannot scale across heterogeneous network conditions; and (iii) explainability and trust, since AI-driven decisions must exhibit transparent behavior to satisfy oper- ational and regulatory mandates. Traditional rule-based and supervised learning methods struggle with dynamic network- ing environments, while Reinforcement Learning (RL), despite offering promise through sequential decision-making, suffers from fundamental limitations: (i) poor sample efficiency, re- quiring millions of interactions infeasible in live networks; (ii) limited generalization under unseen traffic patterns; and (iii) lack of interpretability, problematic for safety-critical operations [6]. To address these limitations, this paper introduces X-RIC, a novel RIC framework integrating explainable agentic in- telligence for AI-native RAN control. Unlike conventional RL approaches relying solely on reactive policies, X-RIC employs a hybrid architecture combining fast reactive learning with strategic reasoning. Built on agentic AI principles— augmenting learning systems with reasoning, temporal mem- ory, and task-level understanding [7]—X-RIC enables contex- tual abstraction, reflective learning, and decision traceability. The X-RIC architecture comprises three components: (i) au- tomated demonstration harvesting that initializes RL poli- cies through Behavior Cloning (BC) [8] using trajectories from a fine-tuned reasoning model, improving sample effi- ciency without manual supervision; (ii) a transformer-based reasoning module providing strategic oversight and natural language explanations; and (iii) closed-loop co-adaptation enabling joint optimization while maintaining transparency. To further address sample inefficiency, X-RIC employs a dual-filtered harvesting pipeline: episode-level policy-impact selection identifies valuable trajectories, while critic-guided pruning filters suboptimal actions. Related Work. We review here the O-RAN architecture and how RL has been used within O-RAN, followed by online learning and LLM-based approaches. O-RAN Architectures: Recent O-RAN developments demonstrate AI integration potential [9]. EdgeRIC [10] enables TTI-level decision-making through near-RT applications interfacing with RAN infrastructure. To enhance granular control, [11] embeds Central Units (CUs) and Distributed Units (DUs) within the real-time RIC as dedicated zApps for MAC scheduling and beam shaping. TinyRIC [12] operates near CU/DU components, hosting tApps for non-3GPP interface management and energy optimization. While advancing O-RAN programmability, these frameworks lack integrated AI reasoning and autonomous adaptation.
