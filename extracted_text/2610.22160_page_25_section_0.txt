B.4.3 Component Ablation on Qwen3-32B Table 9 repeats the component ablation of Table 2 on Qwen3-32B. The cross-scale comparison distinguishes three forms of component dependence. First, steering and external judging can contribute differently even within AIME. With the external judge, the M-step ties context-only optimization on AIME-2025 (43.3) but adds 10.0 points on AIME-2026 (53.3 vs. 43.3). With steering retained, external judging adds 13.3 and 6.6 points over self-judging, respectively; neither component is uniformly dominant across editions. Second, steering on GPQA-D loses 2.5 points under both judge choices (62.1 vs. 64.6 externally judged; 61.6 vs. 64.1 self-judged). This differs from Qwen3-8B, where replacing the self-judge with the external judge changes the steering increment from −3.5 to +2.1. Feedback quality and steering utility can interact, but external judging does not universally resolve an unfavorable steering effect. Third, with steering retained, the external-judge increment on MMLU-Pro changes from +2.3 at 8B to −1.1 at 32B, where self-judging reaches 75.4 versus 74.3. This reversal motivates model- and task-dependent judge allocation, but does not establish better intrinsic self-evaluation at 32B: judge replacement changes both feedback and candidate selection, and the greedy comparison does not estimate uncertainty across runs.
