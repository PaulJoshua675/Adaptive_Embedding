Experiment statistical significance Question: Does the paper report error bars suitably and correctly defined or other appropri- ate information about the statistical significance of the experiments? Answer: [Yes] Justification: Every timing table in the main text, and the held-out, waterfall and k=10 tables in the appendices (Tables 5, 7 and 10), reports the median over 20 independent SLURM allocations with a bootstrap 95% confidence interval (4,000 resamples, seeded by the data so a cell repeated across tables is identical). The base-parameter and per-k ablation tables report the same 20-allocation medians without intervals, and the KD-tree ablation, naive and synthetic appendix tables are medians of several runs within one allo- cation; each caption says which. The factor of variability captured is between-allocation: each allocation is a separately scheduled job, reduced to one number (the median of its internal timed runs), and the interval is taken over those 20 per-allocation values. Intervals are percentile bootstrap and therefore asymmetric; no normality is assumed. Guidelines: • The answer [N/A] means that the paper does not include experiments. • The authors should answer [Yes] if the results are accompanied by error bars, confi- dence intervals, or statistical significance tests, at least for the experiments that support the main claims of the paper. • The factors of variability that the error bars are capturing should be clearly stated (for example, train/test split, initialization, random drawing of some parameter, or overall run with given experimental conditions). • The method for calculating the error bars should be explained (closed form formula, call to a library function, bootstrap, etc.) • The assumptions made should be given (e.g., Normally distributed errors). • It should be clear whether the error bar is the standard deviation or the standard error of the mean. • It is OK to report 1-sigma error bars, but one should state it.
