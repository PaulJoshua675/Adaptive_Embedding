Audio-video generation has evolved from unidirectional driving toward joint generation with in- creasingly strong generative capabilities. Earlier audio-driven video generation (e.g., Wav2Lip (Pra- jwal et al., 2020)) and video-to-audio generation (e.g., HunyuanVideo-Foley (Shan et al., 2025)) were centered on more constrained consistency relations: the former mainly focused on lip syn- chronization (Chung & Zisserman, 2016), while the latter mainly focused on simple temporal align- ment (Iashin et al., 2024). In contrast, joint generation removes the constraint of a fixed modality, with recent models such as Veo 3 (Google DeepMind, 2025), MiniMax H3 (MiniMax, 2026) and Seedance 2.0 (Seedance et al., 2026) extending this paradigm to richer and more complex scenes. These richer audio-visual scenes exhibit three forms of complexity: multi-event composition, multi-layer audio mixture, and asymmetric cross-modal relations. First, a clip can contain mul- tiple events that together form a complete scene, with several events potentially occurring at the same time. Second, its audio track can mix physical scene sounds with post-produced audio such as voice-over and background music, which instead support the video’s narrative or atmosphere.
