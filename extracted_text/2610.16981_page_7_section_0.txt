This drop is expected by construction, since the cutoff graph is a subgraph of the all edges graph. It measures sensitivity to the cut- off rather than its validity, because it cannot distinguish flows that violate the assumed ordering from funds that leave the observed accounts or time window. Restricting inter-transaction edges to the ego account further reduces reachabil- ity, though more modestly. The combined restriction (no teleport, ego account, LIFO-cutoff) yields the worst reachability, at 42.42% and 30.70% for borrow and borrow–repay paths, respectively, compared to 57.87% and 47.15% with all ac- counts. Teleportation can thus bridge otherwise disconnected components, while ego-only restriction and the LIFO-cutoff both reduce reachability. Table 1 also shows a consistent divergence in path length. Weighted shortest paths are longer than unweighted ones in every configuration. For instance, under no teleport, all accounts, all edges, the mean rises from 4.13 (P hop s,d ) to 7.57 (P w s,d) as expected, since the weighting scheme favors additional low-cost transfer and swap hops over fewer, costlier inter-transaction or teleport hops. Teleportation, by contrast, shortens and stabilizes unweighted paths (mean 4.13 to 3.93, std. 2.18 to 1.07 under all-accounts, all-edges) by providing direct shortcuts in place of long fallback detours, whereas LIFO-cutoff lengthens and destabilizes them (mean 4.13 to 7.16, std. 2.18 to 12.34 under all accounts, no teleport) by forcing longer, more circuitous paths when no direct chronological match exists. Because these statistics cover only reachable pairs, cross-configuration comparisons also reflect changes in which pairs remain connected. These results reveal a trade-off between reachability and path construction assumptions. Teleport edges maximize reachability at the cost of unverified con- nections, whereas ego-only linkage and the LIFO-cutoff reduce reachability but avoid permissive or partially observed links at the cost of coverage. Weighting balances the two by keeping teleportation as a fallback while favoring lower-cost transfer, swap, and inter-transaction edges whenever available.
