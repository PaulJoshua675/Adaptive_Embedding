Temporal costs limit success in evolving environments. As shown in Figure 5, with the environment paused during in- ference, Claude Opus 5 and Gemini 3.8 Flash each complete 70.0% of tasks in PH. At the same high reasoning effort, their success rates fall to 14.0% and 32.0% in WH, respectively. This decline reflects the difficulty of completing tasks when the en- vironment advances during actual inference and serving. To examine performance independently of physical serving speed, we turn to Token Clock, which assigns a standardized temporal cost to generation. Success remains substantially below Paused Clock at 30.0% for Claude and 34.0% for Gemini in TH. The gap therefore persists when action delay is determined by token consumption rather than actual serving latency. Together, these comparisons highlight a central challenge of real-time capabil- ity: sustaining effective interaction when deliberation consumes time and the environment continues to evolve. Reasoning effort is a tradeoff under time pressure. In Paused Clock, higher reasoning effort improves success for all six models. GPT-5.6 Sol rises from 42.0% in PL to 58.0% in PH, and Claude Opus 5 from 62.0% to 70.0%. However, the effect reverses under Token Clock for Claude, with success falling from 44.0% in TL to 30.0% in TH. Kimi-K3 instead improves from 24.0% to 36.0% under the same clock. Wall Clock shows similarly mixed effects: GPT-5.6 Sol drops from 16.0% in WL to 8.0% in WH, whereas Gemini 3.8 Flash improves from 24.0% to 32.0%.
