The corre- lation between the baseline control input and un- measured environmental disturbances introduces an estimation bias during the offline learning phase. The scope of this paper focuses on super- vised learning paradigms for neural network con- trol. While Reinforcement Learning architectures incorporate online adaptation, the mathematical dynamics of offline-to-online distributional shift in supervised contexts remain a challenge. Re- cent advances in closed-loop system identifica- tion, such as Dual Input-Output Parameteriza- tion (IOP) (Chen et al., 2023), System Level Pa- rameterization (SLP) (Srivastava et al., 2023), and Neural Identification of stabilized models (Boroujeni et al., 2025a), have proposed struc- tural frameworks to mitigate this initial estima- tion bias while guaranteeing closed-loop stability. However, even if this initial bias is eliminated, the transition from offline training to static on- line deployment induces a secondary mathemati- cal challenge. Definition 1 (Identification Bias). The Closed- Loop Identification Bias is the parametric devi- ation of the offline controller θof f from the opti- mal open-loop parameters, caused by overfitting to the correlated process noise in the training dataset. Empirical risk minimization yields a biased representation of the system dynamics, a chal- lenge coupled with conditions for convergence in feedback optimization (Løvland et al., 2025). While structural frameworks—such as the aﬀine constraints of Dual Input-Output Parameteriza- tion (IOP) and System Level Parameterization (SLP) (Chen et al., 2023; Srivastava et al., 2023), which restrict the network’s hypothesis space to ensure the extracted model commutes with the stabilizing controller, alongside the nonlin- ear generalization of Neural Identification (Borou- jeni et al., 2025a) and continuous-time instrumen- tal variable (IV) consistency methods (González et al., 2024; Classens et al., 2025)—mitigate this bias by ensuring the learned model remains stabi- lized by the known controller, they operate under the assumption of continuous loop closure. If an offline model is frozen and deployed as a static feed-forward policy, it acts as a catalyst for the distributional shift defined as the Action Error.
