However, recommen- dation systems can amplify existing popularity biases and may dis- proportionately favor frequently interacted-with items, potentially reducing exposure to less popular or tail items. HASTE explicitly incorporates attribute-aware supervision to improve tail-item repre- sentation, but this does not guarantee fairness across all products or user groups. In addition, the use of catalog and behavioral data may introduce privacy concerns depending on the data collection and deployment setting. In practice, appropriate data anonymization, access controls, and privacy-preserving data practices should be applied. Finally, the framework should be evaluated for unintended recommendation biases before deployment, particularly in settings where recommendations can influence user choices or commercial outcomes. 9 Appendix 9.1 Dataset Details We evaluate HASTE on five datasets spanning public Amazon rec- ommendation [13] and a production-scale internal fashion dataset: Sports & Outdoors (Sports), Beauty, Toys & Games (Toys), Fashion- 140k, and Internal-fashion. Table 6 summarizes their catalog sizes, user sequences, interaction volumes, conversational training data, and available metadata. Detailed preprocessing, semantic-ID con- struction, and attribute availability are described below. 9.1.1 Sports, Beauty, and Toys. Source and preprocessing. Sports, Beauty, and Toys use the Amazon- 2014 5-core P5 release [10]. Each category contains one row per ASIN, while user interaction sequences are the chronological P5 5- core sequences, retaining users with at least five reviews in the cor- responding category. The resulting catalogs contain 18,357, 12,101, and 11,924 items for Sports, Beauty, and Toys, respectively. Table 7 provides metadata coverage. Approximately 0.5% of Sports and Toys items have no title in the upstream metadata (Product: N/A); these items produce near-degenerate embeddings and account for a disproportionate share of semantic-ID collisions. Toys has depth-3 taxonomy labels for only 28.6% of items; therefore, statistics conditioned on this taxonomy level apply only to that subset. Evaluation split. The datasets are prepared using a leave-one-out (LOO) strategy for sequential evaluation. For each user sequence, the last item is held out for testing, the item immediately preceding it is used for validation, and all earlier items are used for training. Thus, each sequence contributes a training history, a validation target, and a test target while preserving chronological order. For the conversational SFT corpus, examples are independently shuffled and split 92%/5%/3% into training, validation and test set. Sequences use a leave-two-out protocol. For each user, the training example uses seq[:-2], validation predicts seq[-2] from seq[:-2], and test predicts seq[-1] from seq[:-1]. Metadata and attributes. In addition to title, description, category, and brand, the Amazon categories provide price, sales rank, and related-item information. They do not contain the dense structured attributes required by our attribute-specific objectives, such as fine- grained color, material, style, and occasion. Consequently, these datasets are used primarily to evaluate hierarchical supervision. 9.1.2 Fashion-140k. Source and preprocessing. Fashion-140k contains 141,300 items selected from an internally processed 799,953-item Amazon Fash- ion pool. The subset is enriched with Gemini-extracted structured attributes that are not available in the raw Amazon metadata. User sequences are obtained using 5-core filtering on Amazon Fashion reviews, restricted to ASINs in the item pool. Sequences shorter than three items are removed and sequences are capped at 100 items, resulting in 21,326 sequences with mean length 3.33, median length 3, and maximum length 15. Evaluation split. Sequences use a leave-two-out protocol. For each user, the training example uses seq[:-2], validation predicts seq[-2] from seq[:-2], and test predicts seq[-1] from seq[:-1]. All 21,326 users are retained across the splits. Structured attributes. Fashion-140k provides dense structured attributes, including product type, gender, brand, occasion, pri- mary and secondary color, style, material, and fit.
