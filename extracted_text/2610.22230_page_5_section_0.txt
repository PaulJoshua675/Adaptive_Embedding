A 4×5 output (20 tiles) on 8 CUs requires 3 timesteps with 20 mod 8 = 4 underutilized CUs in the final timestep. Origami addresses this via two strategies: tile selection to change the number of output tiles, and Stream-K to partition work along the K dimension. The number of output tiles Tout = ⌈M/MT ⌉· ⌈N/NT ⌉ determines timestep count ω = ⌈Tout/NCU⌉. Total latency is Ltotal = ω · Ltile. Fig. 4 illustrates the tail effect: when tiles do not evenly divide across CUs, the final timestep underutilizes the GPU. This static analysis is enabled by the regularity of GEMM workloads: uniform tile shapes and predictable access patterns. Origami evaluates all candidate configurations and selects the one minimizing Ltotal. Each candidate configuration evaluates in nanoseconds; the full search across all candidates completes in microseconds. Grid Size Selection. Beyond tile configuration, Origami selects the optimal Stream-K grid size. Stream-K partitions work in the K dimension across CUs, and Origami balances three considerations: parallelism to maximize CU utilization, locality to enable data reuse across CUs iterating over distinct K slices, and scheduling to load balance across XCDs and minimize the wave quantization problem at each level of the chiplet hierarchy. Chiplet-Aware Mapping. MI350X comprises 8 XCDs, each with private L2 cache. Origami extends hierarchical tiling to the chiplet level: a device-scope tile is composed of 8 XCD-scope tiles, each containing workgroups that share L2. XCD-aware workgroup ID swizzling maps spatially adjacent output tiles to the same XCD, improving L2 hit rates. Origami also selects the L2 tile shape dynamically based on the number of CUs per chiplet, arranging the concurrent workgroups into an MC ×NC grid to maximize the cache hit rate predicted by Equation 3. Origami is also integrated into tritonBLAS [32], enabling zero-warmup kernel selection for arbitrary matrix shapes at runtime, eliminating the need for offline autotuning entirely. Generality. Although calibrated for MI350X, Origami’s for- mulation is general: because GEMM has regular tiling and predictable data movement, its cost reduces to a hardware- parameterized roofline. Retargeting another accelerator is there- fore largely a matter of substituting a handful of hardware constants (cache sizes, achievable bandwidths, matrix-unit throughput, and chiplet topology) rather than re-deriving the model from scratch. GPU Power Excursion Boost The Open Compute Project (OCP) is a collaborative initia- tive between hyperscale datacenter operators and technology vendors to standardize interfaces and specifications across infrastructure domains, including power delivery. The MI350X accelerator is implemented as a 1000 W open accelerator module (OAM) [33]. While the OAM must comply with the nominal total board power (TBP) over a 1s window, the specification permits transient power excursions over shorter intervals as defined by the OAM’s excursion design power (EDP). These excursions range from 1.1× TBP over 20 ms up to 2.0× TBP over 20 µs. Although the OCP specification defines minimum electrical, thermal, and power-delivery capabilities, system designers may provision additional margin. Meta’s AI hardware platform, for example, supports average power levels of up to 1.36× TBP over a 50 ms window for a 1000 W OAM configuration.
