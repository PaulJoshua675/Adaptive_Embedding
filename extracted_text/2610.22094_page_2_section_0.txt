This assumption is particularly fragile in radiology, where reports from different patients frequently describe overlapping findings using similar or interchangeable ex- pressions. As illustrated in Figure 1(a), the two batch items underlying an unmatched image–report candidate may share the same report-derived finding set. At a mini-batch size of 256, an exact corpus-level calcula- tion under uniform random batching estimates that 66.8% of pathology-positive anchors would encounter at least one cross-patient batch mate with an identical CheXpert present-label set, with 2.78 such matches per anchor on av- erage (Figure 1(b)). A related enrichment appears in the recorded online masking decisions. Within the trace pre- fix ending at the best checkpoint, the inclusive rule used in the trace—which masks an off-diagonal pair when ei- ther the corresponding image–image or report–report sim- ilarity exceeds its modality-specific adaptive threshold— flags pair-observations with 3.96% exact present-label-set agreement, compared with 1.83% across all eligible pair- observations. Here, eligibility requires different patients and at least one positive label among the 12 pathology findings in each report; exact agreement is then evaluated over all 14 labels. The resulting 2.16× enrichment remains above one throughout five consecutive training windows (Figure 1(c)). Although exact CheXpert present-label-set agreement does not establish clinical equivalence, its en- richment among flagged pair-observations suggests that this subset may contain a higher proportion of semantically compatible pairs. Consequently, semantically compatible pairs may still receive negative contrastive targets, giving rise to false-negative supervision. Such false negatives are known to degrade generic contrastive learning [5, 7]. We refer to this mismatch between semantic compatibility and the assigned contrastive target as a semantic conflict. Prior work has attempted to mitigate false negatives by incorporating external clinical knowledge [19, 30], mining additional positive pairs from report- or study-level seman- tics [20, 21, 33], or softly reweighting suspected false neg- atives during optimization [15, 18]. Despite their effective- ness, two gaps remain. Detection: rules based on single- modality evidence or fixed, globally normalized similarity thresholds can become miscalibrated as modality-specific similarity distributions shift during representation learning. Optimization: additional positive mining may promote par- tial semantic compatibility to full equivalence, whereas soft reweighting still allows suspected false negatives to exert residual repulsive gradients unless their weights become ex- actly zero. An effective solution must therefore identify semantic conflicts adaptively and prevent them from be- ing pushed apart without indiscriminately relabeling them as positives. To address both gaps, we propose Semantic-Conflict- Aware Contrastive Learning (SCCL), which dynamically flags potential semantic-conflict candidates using evidence from both modalities and removes them through adap- tive hard masking. For detection, SCCL maintains sepa- rate exponential-moving-average (EMA) statistics for intra- batch image–image and report–report similarities, allow- ing each threshold to track its modality-specific distribu- tion as training evolves. Flagged candidates are excluded from contrastive normalization, eliminating their probabil- ity mass and repulsive gradients rather than treating them as additional positives. To account for uncertainty in the modality-specific intra-modal cues, we instantiate two rule- specific teachers: an inclusive (OR) teacher that accepts ev- idence from either modality to increase candidate coverage, and a consensus (AND) teacher that requires agreement be- tween both modality-specific cues for more conservative selection. Standard distillation then consolidates the two teachers’ cross-modal rankings into the single deployable student SCCL-DT. SCCL thus adapts candidate selection to evolving similarity distributions while explicitly preventing flagged candidates from being optimized as negatives. Our contributions are: • We propose SCCL, a contrastive framework that adap- tively hard-masks similarity-flagged off-diagonal can- didates using per-modality EMA thresholds, without adding trainable parameters to the masking module. • We instantiate SCCL with inclusive and consensus rule- specific teachers and use standard distillation to consoli- date their cross-modal rankings into SCCL-DT, a single deployable student. • On five public chest X-ray benchmarks (Open-I, PadCh- est, ChestX-ray14, CheXpert, and ChestXDet10), SCCL- DT reaches a mean zero-shot AUC of 0.850, the highest among the compared methods, at single-model inference cost.
