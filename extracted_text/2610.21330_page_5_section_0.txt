Structure strategies modify page organization and pre- sentation without changing the underlying factual content. Both strategies follow C-SEO [25]. Content Improvement improves the organization and presentation of the page through clearer sections, more logical ordering, stronger emphasis on key information, and reduced redundancy, while preserving factual information, exist- ing citations, and overall length. LLMs.txt reformats the page into an LLM-oriented layout. It adds an LLM Guidance block with the topic, key facts, and citation priority, reorganizes the body using Markdown headings, key points, summaries, lists, and bold text, and adds a final citation section explaining why the page should be cited and what information it uniquely provides. Evidence. Evidence strategies strengthen support for existing claims by adding quotations, quantitative evidence, or source attri- butions. All three follow GEO [1]. Quotes inserts direct quotations attributed to credible figures while preserving the remaining con- tent and formatting. Statistics inserts persuasive numerical evidence, such as percentages, growth rates, and counts, at relevant positions while modifying only the sentences receiving the numbers. Au- thoritative adds five to six relevant attributions to credible sources while preserving the core content, length, and local structure. Style. Style strategies rewrite the same information at the lexical and sentence levels without adding new substantive content. All four follow GEO [1]. Fluency improves sentence flow, clarity, and readability. Simple Language paraphrases sentences using simpler and clearer language while keeping the information and length unchanged. Technical Terms rephrases individual sentences using more technical terminology without adding or removing content. Unique Words introduces more distinctive vocabulary while pre- serving the original meaning and core information. 5.2 Argumentation Strategies Existing GEO strategies primarily modify page structure, support- ing evidence, or linguistic style. Voting webpages may additionally contain competing political claims or recommendations. Motivated by political persuasion research on argument framing and counter- argument [11, 16], we introduce two argumentation strategies. Comparative Distinction. This strategy strengthens a compar- ative case by emphasizing what makes one option distinctively strong relative to plausible alternatives. It foregrounds concrete distinctions in policy positions, commitments, timelines, mecha- nisms, or other actionable details that can make the option stand out rather than appear merely acceptable. The rewriting prompt instructs the model to preserve the original factual information and maintain a similar overall length. This design is motivated by prior work on contrast advertising in political campaigns, which compares candidates or positions to differentiate the favored option from alternatives [23]. Refutational. This strategy strengthens a position through refu- tational two-sided argumentation [11, 21]. It introduces plausible opposing arguments and explicitly rebuts them with reasoning or supporting information. Each opposing argument must be substan- tive and directly addressed rather than weakened into a straw man. The original core content and overall position are preserved. 6 VoteGEO-Bench This section evaluates VoteGEO through three research questions: • RQ1: Does Target Specification Matter? We compare rewrit- ing with and without an explicitly specified target party. • RQ2: Which Rewriting Strategies Work? We compare the 11 rewriting strategies across engines and elections. • RQ3: How Do Voter Personas Affect GEO? We examine how voter personas affect baseline exposure and rewriting gains. We report the main results below and provide full results across the evaluated settings in Appendix A. 6.1 Benchmark Setup Datasets. We retain 109 U.S. queries from 5 subcategories that directly evaluate or compare candidates: Candidate positions (51), Comparison (29), Public opinion (17), Evaluation (9), and Vice pres- ident (3). For each query, we independently sample 7 collection days uniformly at random from its available days using a fixed seed. Together, the selected query cells cover the full 106-day collection window. For Japan, we use all 25 queries and all available collection days. We use gpt-4o-mini, gpt-5-mini, and gemini-2.5-flas h as generative engines from OpenAI and Google. Following the fixed-pool GEO setting in Section 2.1, web search is disabled and responses are generated from the provided webpage pools. For the U.S., all three engines use the same pools constructed from histor- ical Google-SERP results. For Japan, gpt-4o-mini and gpt-5-mi ni use their corresponding GPT pools. Because the original Grok models are no longer available, gemini-2.5-flash is evaluated separately on both Grok webpage pools, and the two sets of results are combined for reporting. All engines use temperature 0. This yields 2,289 U.S. and 650 Japan query cells, for 2,939 in total. Benchmark Procedure. We define targets at the party level to keep the U.S. and Japan settings comparable, with candidate men- tions attributed to their corresponding parties. For the U.S., we use the Democratic Party as the primary target. Results using the Republican Party as the target show the same overall pattern. For Japan, we divide the 11 parties into Top, Mid, and Low tiers of sizes 4, 4, and 3 based on final proportional-representation vote share.
