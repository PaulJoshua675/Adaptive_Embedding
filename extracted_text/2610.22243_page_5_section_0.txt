Each benchmark exposes a different opportunity; there is no evidence for one universal rule. 4.1 LinuxArena: separable collection, not a length cutoff Two controls test whether duplicated traces or pooled agent models explain the separation in Table 1. Exact trace deduplication leaves the pooled LOEO lexical AUC at 0.934. Because that AUC combines scores from separately fitted folds, we also inspect each held-out environment: all ten AUCs lie from .900 to 1.000 (macro mean .951). A model-matched Haiku-only cohort gives lexical AUC 0.955 versus length AUC 0.943; their difference is 0.012 with interval [−0.003, 0.027]. Thus neither duplicated monitor runs nor agent-model pooling explains the pattern. The length result uses negative mean shell-command characters, not a hard cutoff. In the same- monitor rerun cohort, we max-pool action scores for an exploratory trajectory diagnostic; shell-derived comparisons use the 70 human-crafted attacks and 200 Opus-honest trajectories containing shell commands. Action count is more label-predictive than mean command length (AUC .947 versus .905). Within coarse length quartiles, the monitor score remains informative (weighted AUC .896 versus .607 for length). This is not causal adjustment, but it is inconsistent with that monitor merely reproducing the length ranking. Operational metric-unit reconstruction is reported in the appendix; the public sample cannot reproduce LinuxArena’s official pre-harm metric exactly.
