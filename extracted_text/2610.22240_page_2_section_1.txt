Long-Horizon Multimodal Search. MMSearch- R1 and WebWatcher train multimodal agents to interleave reasoning with external retrieval (Wu et al., 2025; Geng et al., 2025). Recent work ex- tends search horizons and manages growing inter- action histories (Huang et al., 2026; Du et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026). DeepVoyager-VL uses interme- diate visual evidence to guide subsequent search (Zhang et al., 2026b). These developments call for training questions requiring sustained, cross-modal evidence acquisition. Multimodal Search Data Construction. Ques- tions solvable through parametric knowledge or short retrieval provide limited supervision for long- horizon search. Existing pipelines increase dif- ficulty through multi-hop composition and en- tity obfuscation (Huang et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026), graph-structured synthesis (Chu et al., 2026; Mei et al., 2026), and visual evidence integration (Zhang et al., 2026c,b). These constructions re- quire evidence collection and validation. Our Re- fine pipeline reuses existing easy questions and candidate reasoning chains, applying Expand, Blur, and Visualize to control chain length, disambigua- tion, and visual requirements without building a large graph from scratch. Reinforcement Learning for Multimodal Search. GRPO-based training improves search policies through interaction (Shao et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2026), but terminal scores offer limited feedback on partially useful trajectories. Process rewards, adaptive sampling, and finer credit assignment ad- dress this limitation (Chen et al., 2026; Yan et al., 2026; Dai et al., 2026; Jiao et al., 2026; Mei et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025). HyperEyes combines ef- ficiency rewards with teacher distillation on failed rollouts (Li et al., 2026).
