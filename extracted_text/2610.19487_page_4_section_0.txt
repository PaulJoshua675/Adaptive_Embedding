The interaction order changes when the same distribu- tion is viewed through a different ruler. To demonstrate how this interaction order shifts under coarse-graining, we write bi = (si + 1)/2 and retain only x = b1 and a compressed variable z = b2 ∧b3. Because x and z de- pend on disjoint fine coordinates, they are independent at K = 0 (zero coupling, where Eq. (16) is uniform). This provides a clean baseline: any mutual information observed at nonzero coupling reflects the coarse-grained effect of the triadic interaction, rather than a spurious dependence introduced by overlapping projections. At K = 1.8, however, the ordinary mutual informa- tion between these same projected variables is 0.261 bits (Fig. 1). Part of the third-order dependence quantified by Eq. (17) has therefore become an ordinary pairwise dependency after projection. No new information has been created; the many-to-one map has changed the or- der at which part of the existing statistical structure is represented. The dependence on the ruler is even clearer under a second projection. If instead z = 1[b2 = b3], the pro- jected pairwise information is 0.823 bits, equal to the full value of I(3) in Eq. (17). This occurs because the equality variable encodes the product s2s3. The fine-scale inter- action s1s2s3 is thereby represented at the coarse scale as a dependency between two observables. Under this pro- jection, the entire third-order dependence survives, but its apparent interaction order has changed from three to two. The same fine distribution can thus exhibit no pairwise dependence in its original coordinates, a weaker pairwise dependence under one projection, and the full depen- dence as a pairwise relation under another. Interaction order is therefore not invariant under a many-to-one mea- surement map. It is a property of the statistical structure relative to the observables through which that structure is resolved. A strong pairwise dependency at coarse res- olution cannot, by itself, identify a pairwise mechanism at finer resolution. The ruler can change the apparent order of the computation. D. The Branch-To-Soma Cascade Having established that interaction order can change under mathematical projection, we now translate the same logic into a neuron-like architecture. The purpose is not to construct a compartmental model of a particu- lar dendrite. It is to show, with an exactly enumerable example, how familiar biophysical operations—local co- incidence detection, saturating integration, and somatic mixing—can act as successive coarse-graining maps. We therefore follow the same signal through three observa- tional rulers. Individual synaptic contacts, two dendritic branches, and a single somatic variable. The underlying input–output law is fixed throughout. What changes is the set of distinctions available to the observer, and un- der coarse-graining a neuron with genuinely synergistic substructure can masquerade as an additive unit. 1. The fine-scale ground truth: NMDA coincidence detection We begin at the finest resolution of the construction. Six independent binary inputs, x ∈{0, 1}6, are divided evenly between two three-input subunits. Think of a neu- ron receiving six independent binary inputs, distributed evenly across two dendritic branches. In this construct, the output probability is governed strictly by third-order interactions of the two input branches: P(Y = 1 | x) = 0.06 + 0.40(x1x2x3 + x4x5x6). (18) Thus the baseline firing probability is 0.06, one active triplet raises it to 0.46, and simultaneous activation of both triplets gives the maximum probability 0.86. All 26 = 64 input configurations can therefore be enumerated exactly. The two products in Eq. (18) are deliberately sharp abstractions of local dendritic coincidence detection. NMDA-dependent dendritic events provide the biological motivation: because NMDA conductance is strongly volt- age dependent through extracellular Mg2+ block, suffi- ciently coordinated local excitation can recruit a regener- ative response that isolated inputs do not produce12,13,22. The number three is not intended as a literal universal threshold for an NMDA event. Its role is mathemati- cal: x1x2x3 is nonzero only when all three members of the local group are active, providing the simplest exact representation of a third-order coincidence. At this contact-level ruler, the generative law is there- fore explicitly organized around third-order products. This does not mean that individual contacts carry no predictive information. Marginalizing over the other in- puts produces direct one-coordinate effects that an ad- ditive model can capture. What it cannot reconstruct is the full dependence on the joint configuration. For the coordinate-invariant categorical-additive family defined in Eq. (1), the direct fraction is consequently only 55.0%. In other words, slightly more than half of the available information can be assigned to direct single-coordinate effects, while the remainder requires their joint configu- ration.
