However, these geometric features are primarily learned for 3D reconstruction, an objective different from semantic understanding in VLMs. Integrating them therefore requires the model to align two different representation spaces while learning spatial relations. With limited spatial supervision, this additional adaptation may limit the effective use of geometry and interfere with the model’s existing semantic representations. Beyond token content, VLMs already use positional encoding to guide attention. In the Qwen-VL family (Wang et al., 2024, Bai et al., 2025b,a), multi-modal rotary position embedding (mRoPE) encodes sequence order, time, and 2D image grid coordinates, so that attention between two tokens depends on their relative positions in the input. Position thus offers a second interface for geometry: instead of being fused into token content, geometry can define relations between tokens that directly shape attention (Miyato et al., 2024, Li et al., 2025c, Wu et al., 2026, Xiang et al., 2026). However, input positions describe where a token appears in the input rather than where its content lies in the 3D world. As the camera moves, the same 3D surface appears at different image locations across frames, while neighboring 2D patches can lie at very different depths (Figure 1). Existing geometric attention mechanisms (Miyato et al., 2024, Li et al., 2025c, Wu et al., 2026, Xiang et al., 2026) introduce 3D relations directly into attention by applying geometric trans- formations to queries, keys, or values. However, replacing a pretrained VLM’s native positional encoding with geometric encoding may disrupt the semantic attention patterns learned under the original encoding—patterns that remain useful for spatial understanding. We therefore bring scene geometry into attention through a separate kernel while retaining the native attention and positional encoding.
