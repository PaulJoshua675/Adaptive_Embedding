AI Consilium: a Benchmark of LLM Clinical Multidisciplinary Second Opinions A PREPRINT 4.3 Physician Ratings and Judge Calibration Six physicians supplied criterion-level reference ratings for Judge-guidance development and internal validation within the fixed 110-case sampling frame. DOI-disjoint development subsets and independent optimization of each criterion preserved case separation and prevented a favorable result for one clinical dimension from compensating for another. This design produced a Judge that could characterize factual grounding, reasoning, management, safety, and conclusion quality separately rather than collapsing them into one undifferentiated score. Criterion-local optimization reduced macro-mean validation MAE from 0.8601 without additional guidance and 0.9033 with the initial prompts to 0.7798 with the selected guidance, a 9.3% improvement over the no-additional-guidance configuration. The clearest validation gains occurred for factual grounding, differential diagnosis, and management. The held-out benchmark used one fixed Judge configuration across all candidate outputs. Calibration performance remained criterion specific, as intended by the evaluation design. Criterion 6, which jointly assesses conclusion clarity, diagnostic concordance, evidence grounding, and uncertainty, was the most demanding target (MAE, 1.6667). We therefore use the Judge to identify consistent criterion-level and model-level patterns, while interpreting small differences in this composite criterion conservatively. The evolutionary search improved or preserved training re-evaluation MAE for every criterion and transferred its gains to disjoint validation cases for factual grounding, data use, differential diagnosis, and management (Table 5). The largest validation reductions were observed for factual grounding (1.2222 to 0.6296) and management (0.8889 to 0.5000). These results show that criterion-local guidance can produce substantial numerical agreement gains for clinically interpretable dimensions even when a single universal prompt would obscure them.
