As illustrated in Figure 7, our framework is divided into three main components. First, we construct the Vias-to-Refine Dataset (Figure 7A), which labels the vias that need reassignment compared to the initial solution to achieve optimal results. Second, we train a GNN model using the dataset (Figure 7B), encoding ILP-derived features into a graph to learn which vias need reassignment. Third, during inference (Figure 7C), vias requiring reassignment are sampled according to the probabilities predicted by the GNN, and only the variables associated with the sampled vias are re-optimized, yielding a high-quality solution with significantly reduced runtime compared to solving the full ILP from scratch. 4.2 Vias-to-Refine Dataset Preparation Training a GNN model to predict vias requiring reassignment de- mands layouts with sufficient diversity in conflict graph topologies to ensure good generalization, but most real-world designs are pro- prietary and not publicly available. Following [15], we generate synthetic layouts track-by-track based on design rules extracted from the open-source ASAP7 process design kit (PDK) [16], includ- ing wire width 𝑤and track pitch 𝑝. Candidate vias are placed at intersections of adjacent metal layers, with enclosure and pitch constraints enforced via an assist wire layer. Wire lengths, spac- ings, and via placements are randomized within design-rule con- straints, resulting in layouts with diverse yet design-rule-compliant conflict graph topologies. This produces a diverse set of layouts L = {𝐿1, . . . , 𝐿𝑁} covering a wide range of local conflict structures for GNN training. For each layout 𝐿𝑖, the ILP formulation from Section 3 is solved with a time-limited early stop to obtain the initial ILP-solve features.
