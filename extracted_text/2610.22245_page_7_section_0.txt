Thus, the approximation gap naturally shrinks as sp approaches qp. 4.2 Population Comparison of Routing Scores In this section, we compare the quality of different routing scores at the population level. We use extensive Monte Carlo sampling to accurately estimate the probabilities defining each score, allowing us to compare the underlying routing signals independently of how they are learned in practice. For simplicity, we consider the one-shot setting σ(P) ≡1. We compare four routing signals: Figure 1: Corrected disagreement better approximates optimal routing than alternatives. • Optimal routing gain: ∆(p, yw) = Pr(Ys = Y | P = p) −Pr(Y = yw | P = p). This is the exact gain defined in Theorem 3.1. It serves as an oracle upper bound representing full knowledge of both models’ correctness, and requires ground-truth supervision. • Weak-model correctness: Scor(p, yw) = 1 −Pr(Y = yw | P = p). This represents optimal confidence-based routing, routing when the weak model is likely incorrect. It serves as the population oracle for confidence- and consistency-based methods and requires ground-truth labels. • Weak-strong disagreement: Sdisagree(p, yw) = Pr(Ys̸ = yw | P = p). This baseline routes purely based on raw cross-model disagreement, without using ground-truth labels. • Corrected disagreement: SCD(p, yw) (Equation 7). This represents our proposed approximation and requires no ground-truth supervision. Figure 1 reports results across the three varying benchmarks.
