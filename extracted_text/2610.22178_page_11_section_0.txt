Solid bars give full successes, hatched bars partial credit. 7 REAL ROBOT EXPERIMENTS Experimental setup. We use a 6-DoF UR5 robotic arm with a Weiss WSG50 gripper and UMI-style parallel jaws. The action loop runs at 15 Hz. The robot operates over a 1 m×1 m tabletop workspace observed by a fixed Orbbec Femto Mega RGB camera, see Fig. 8, left. Observations include an RGB image, and a 7D absolute joint state. Actions are specified as 7D absolute joint state. Tasks and protocol. We evaluate performance across five manipulation tasks, and report task success rates and partial subtask success rates. Tasks span diverse action primitives, including pick, place, stack, and sweep. The tasks and partial success scores are defined in Appendix E. We collect 50 training episodes per task with a Meta Quest controller, and evaluate on 10 test episodes per task, with randomised initial object placements and distractors in each episode. Baselines. We finetune the pretrained checkpoints of REKI and π0.5 on the five tasks for 3,000 optimiser steps, with a global batch of 360 samples. Both models use a learning rate of 5 × 10−5, held constant after warmup. The action chunk has 50 steps (3.3 s) for π0.5, fixed by its pretraining, and 30 steps (2 s) for REKI. In both models, only the last four vision encoder blocks are trained. The backbone is trained at 0.1× the learning rate of the action expert. REKI is finetuned with a memory window of K=3 and without memory (K=1). π0.5 has no memory. Results. Across the five tasks, REKI outperforms π0.5 by 7 to 37.5 percentage points (Fig. 8, right). Removing the memory leaves performance on the shorter-horizon tasks mainly unchanged (65 against 65 and 57.5 against 57.5), but consistently degrades it on the longer-horizon tasks, where REKI outperforms its memoryless variant by 12 to 17.5 percentage points, supporting that memory matters most when tasks require tracking progress over many steps.
