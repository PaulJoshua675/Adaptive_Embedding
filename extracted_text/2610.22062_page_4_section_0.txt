4 MATERIALS AND METHODS Study design and population We conducted a retrospective observational study of patients with Rett syndrome identified from Rett Center clinical visits at Texas Children’s Hospital (TCH) who were prescribed or considered for trofinetide (Daybue). We selected this population because the TCH Rett Center is a dedicated center of excellence whose standardized note templates capture comprehensive disorder-specific information over office visits of at least one hour. An initial 115 patients were identified on this basis. To verify sustained drug exposure, an independent quality-control (QC) review triangulated three sources per patient: pharmacy orders, medication-administration records, and longitudinal note mentions. This confirmed sustained exposure in 108 of 115 patients (94%), with 65 by pharmacy order and 43 by clinical documentation alone (≥2 note mentions over >60 days). The remaining 7 were unconfirmed (4 with only isolated mentions, 3 with no evidence) and were therefore excluded. This 108-patient QC-confirmed cohort is the analysis cohort throughout. Cohort characteristics for the 108 QC-confirmed patients and the 56 diarrhea-validation patients are summarized in Table 1. The subset is comparable to the remaining QC-confirmed patients on age, sex, and follow-up, but has more notes per patient (median 171 vs. 99, p = 0.07). The full cohort’s median is 148. The per-patient note count is right-skewed (range 7–2,546), summing to the 26,763 analyzed notes, and the cohort is young (median age 13 years) and almost entirely female, consistent with Rett syndrome epidemiology. The rare male patients with MECP2-related disorders can follow a different disease course, but we retained them because this analysis documents phenotype presence rather than disease severity. All four reside as ontology-mapped tables in Texas Children’s Hospital’s Palantir Foundry environment. The registry is curated by the Rett Center clinical team of two pediatric nurses and two pediatric neurologists, who together performed all clinical annotation and review reported here, and the notes and medication tables are drawn from the institutional EHR. The QC review found that 43 of the 108 confirmed patients had no matching row in the Epic order table.
