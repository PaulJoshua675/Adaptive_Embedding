D Case Studies To make the mechanism concrete, we trace two Belebele queries under mE5-large through the base- line, mean removal, and disagreement-guided steer- ing. In both, the gold answer exists as a parallel passage in every language, so a language-agnostic retriever should surface those copies rather than fill- ing the ranking with same-language distractors. Ta- bles 6 and 7 show the top-5 retrievals with a snippet of each document. In both examples the baseline returns only same-language passages and recov- ers no cross-lingual gold, while steering keeps the same-language answer and surfaces parallel copies in other languages. Mean removal also recovers cross-lingual golds in the first example, but at the cost of dropping the same-language passage from the top rank. E Demotion vs. Loss: Larger Cutoffs If the correct foreign documents were absent rather than demoted, widening the retrieval window would not recover them. It does. Raising the cut- off from K=20 to K=100 increases cross-lingual recall by 11–21 points on the parallel collections, while nDCG rises far less, since the recovered docu- ments sit deep in the ranking (Table 8). The correct documents are therefore present and retrievable but demoted. On the non-parallel Multi-EuP collection, many golds fall beyond rank 100, consistent with it being the hardest setting.
