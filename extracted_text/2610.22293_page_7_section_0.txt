A non-thinking instruct model, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, shows the same learning–retention pattern across three domains including Tool Use (Appendix B.4). 3.2 SST scales well SST reduces forgetting as parameters increase. Figure 2 shows the effect of scaling model size on the Science dataset, with each model trained for five epochs. Across model sizes, unconstrained SFT forgets substantially, losing 30-40 points of prior capability. By contrast, SST preserves the base model better as scale grows. SST also remains competitive with or improves upon SFT on the new task, so the retention benefit is not obtained by suppressing learning. Training details are in Appendix B.5, and a separate two-epoch sweep through 32B in Table 3 shows the same trend. SST prevents monotonic decline in capability as the training data scales. Figure 3 shows the impact of scaling data on performance on prior and new tasks. We compare two setting under a 45,000-example budget. In the first, we have a novel stream of data and in the second we look at the multi-epoch setting: a 9k example subset repeated for five epochs. In both settings, SFT moves progressively farther from the base model as training proceeds, while SST maintains prior capability and continues improving on the target task. SST therefore remains effective in both the single-epoch novel-data setting and the multi-epoch repeated-data setting. The corresponding table can be found in Appendix B.5. 3.3 SST stays close to the base model SST keeps the model close to its base distribution. Figure 7 measures divergence from the base model on WikiText- 2 [32], a general-domain corpus. Across the training-data (Figure 7) and model-scale studies (Figure 14, Appendix B.7), SST remains substantially closer to the base distribution than the corresponding unconstrained model. The right panel of Figure 7 traces new-task accuracy against drift as training proceeds, and shows that SST improves accuracy with roughly 3× less movement away from the base policy. Across model sizes and training recipes, the reduction is 2-4× (Appendix B.7). A natural alternative is to enforce closeness directly, by adding a reverse-KL penalty toward the base model during SFT. Appendix B.10 (Table 6, Figure 16) shows that this does not recover SST’s trade-off. Weak regularization fails to mitigate forgetting, and stronger regularization suppresses task learning without preserving prior capability. SST reduces hallucinations induced by self-distillation.
