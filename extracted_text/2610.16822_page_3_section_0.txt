Together, these modules prevent directional information from being reduced to an isolated structural cue. We evaluate DTA-GT on four prediction settings spanning accuracy and latency prediction over NAS-Bench-101 and NAS-Bench- 201. Across multiple limited-budget settings and evaluation metrics, DTA-GT consistently improves over representative predictors. Stage-wise ablations assess the contribution of the coordinated direction-aware stages, Magnetic spectral controls isolate the effect of the edge-orientation-sensitive spectral prior, and mechanistic diagnostics examine whether the internal behaviors align with the intended directed computational semantics. Our contributions are: • We formulate encoder-wide directional consistency as a design principle for architecture DAG representation, shifting direction modeling from isolated structural cues to coordinated preservation of directed computational semantics throughout the encoder. • We introduce DTA-GT, which integrates direction- and topology-aware node initialization, Magnetic Laplacian spectral encoding, direction-dual structural attention, and a Direction- Sensitive MoE-FFN. These modules jointly model directed node roles, a global edge- orientation-sensitive spectral prior, relation-aware interactions, and predecessor/successor- aware updates, thereby strengthening topology-aware architecture representations and pro- viding a stronger inductive bias for limited-budget prediction. • We conduct a unified evaluation on accuracy and latency prediction over standard NAS benchmarks, with stage-wise ablations, Magnetic spectral controls, and mechanistic diag- nostics to analyze the contribution and behavior of the proposed direction-aware stages. 2
