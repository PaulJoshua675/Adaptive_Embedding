This sequen- tial curriculum allows the model to stabilize one privileged modality before integrating the second, ensuring coherent multi-modal feature fusion. 4.3 Dense Multi-View Evaluation We evaluate our method under the dense multi-view protocol using both π3 and VGGT backbones. Figures 3 and 4 report performance across a varying number of input views. On both architectures, injecting residual flow consistently outperforms the baselines across all key metrics, including depth relative error, ray align- ment, and inlier-based statistics. These gains confirm that explicitly mod- eling dynamic motion is highly beneficial for feed-forward multi-view recon- struction. Similarly, the introduction of gravity priors yields results compa- rable to residual flow, further validating the role of global orientation cues in enhancing structural alignment. Crucially, models fine-tuned with both modalities outperform those using any single injection, demonstrating that these priors are complementary rather than redundant or contradictory. Notably, the performance gap between our method and the baseline widens as the number of input views increases. In dense settings, cross- view aggregation becomes the dominant factor, which simultaneously am- plifies geometric inconsistencies caused by independently moving objects or misaligned camera orientations. By effectively suppressing these inconsis- tencies, motion and gravity awareness leads to increasingly pronounced gains as view redundancy grows. This trend indicates that our approach actively enhances cross-view geometric aggregation rather than merely compensating for sparse observations. Furthermore, the consistency of these improvements across both π3 and VGGT demonstrates that our proposed learning framework generalizes ef- fectively across different feed-forward reconstruction backbones without re- quiring specialized architectural modifications. 11
