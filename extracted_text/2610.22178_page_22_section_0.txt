(a) In the first Euler step of a chunk, the VLM encodes the current frame t. The registers of frame t read its context and the cached registers of frames t−1 and t−2. The action tokens read the context and the registers of the three frames; past frames are not encoded again. (b) In the N−1 other Euler steps, only the action tokens are updated, and they read the context and the register keys and values from the cache. The registers do not read the action tokens and skip adaptive normalisation, so they do not depend on the flow time and are computed once per frame. When the next frame enters, the registers of frame t−2 are dropped. What it costs. Figure 5d gives the measurement. On one H100, a chunk takes 22.1 ms without registers, 22.6 ms at K=1 and 22.8 ms at K=3, the window used for training and evaluation. Even at K=32, a chunk takes only 24.6 ms. The cache adds about 2 MiB of GPU memory per past frame, 4 MiB at K=3, on a policy of 4.7 GiB. Protocol. Every measurement is taken on one H100, with two 256×256 cameras, a chunk of 10 actions and N =10 Euler steps, in bf16, with the expert and the VLM prefill run as CUDA graphs. Each value is the median of two interleaved repetitions of 200 chunks. The split by phase comes from a separate pass with a synchronisation after each phase, scaled to the median total.
