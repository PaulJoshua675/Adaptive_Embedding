Intern-Decision: Efficient Multimodal Decision Model models must additionally integrate visual observations, task instructions, and changing interface states, where action selection depends jointly on visual evidence and contextual constraints [28, 29]. Moreover, practical agent requests often require multiple decisions over a shared state, creating a need for efficient multi-field decision models that can preserve the capabilities of pretrained multimodal language models while providing structured outputs. To address these challenges, we introduce Intern-Decision, a family of multimodal decision models based on autoregressive masked language modeling. Our approach reformulates structured decision making as next-token prediction over masked decision fields within a predefined assistant output schema. By retaining the pretrained causal language model backbone and native language-model head, Intern-Decision evaluates multiple candidate decisions in a single forward pass and produces both structured predictions and candidate probabilities without iterative text generation. This design separates decision scoring from free-form response decoding, enabling efficient inference while preserving compatibility with existing multimodal language models. To adapt pretrained models to this decision-oriented interface, we construct a unified multimodal decision corpus consisting of textual states, optional images, typed questions, and target decisions. The corpus combines teacher-constructed challenging decisions with public task distributions, covering textual reasoning, instruction- following decisions, visual judgments, interface actions, and interactive control scenarios. We instantiate Intern-Decision with 0.8B, 2B, and 4B Qwen3.5 backbones [23], fine-tuning the language backbone while keeping the vision tower and projector frozen. To improve the reliability of confidence estimates, we further apply post-hoc temperature scaling [13] using a separate calibration split. We evaluate Intern-Decision from three perspectives: decision accuracy, inference efficiency, and probability quality. Across seven evaluation suites, including public Jevbench subsets [10], Intern-Decision-4B achieves a 90.02% unweighted mean accuracy, compared with 88.74% for Jev. For a fixed 289-token request containing three decision fields, Intern-Decision-4B achieves a mean latency of 44.16 ms on a single RTX 4090. We further evaluate probability calibration through a separate known-distribution pilot and study confidence-based model routing, in which requests are passed to a larger model when the decision model’s confidence falls below a threshold. Qualitative demonstrations on visual interaction and browser assistance further illustrate how compact decision models can serve as local decision modules within hierarchical agent systems. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We introduce Intern-Decision, a family of compact multimodal decision models that formulate structured decision making as next-token-aligned masked prediction, enabling multi-field candidate scoring with a native language-model head in a single causal forward pass. • We develop a unified multimodal decision framework, evaluate accuracy, latency, and calibration under separate protocols, and illustrate visual interaction through qualitative demonstrations. • We investigate confidence-based model handoff that passes low-confidence requests to a larger model, and demonstrate how compact structured decision models can complement larger models in interactive agent systems.
