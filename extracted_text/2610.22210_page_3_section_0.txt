We represent the target connectivity to be preserved using a class-labeled carrier S : Ω→{0, . . . , C −1} derived from Y ; its construction is described in Sec. 3.2. The carrier is a thin representation that preserves both foreground and class-specific topology, and its local branches define the connectivity requirements that TRACE supervises. For each foreground class c, define Kc = {x ∈Ω| S(x) = c}, K = C−1 [ c=1 Kc, (1) where Kc denotes the class-c carrier and K is the union of all foreground carriers. Removing a carrier point v ∈Kc reveals the neighboring same-class branches that should remain connected. For each x ∈Ωwith Y (x) = c, let gc(x) denote the centered logit-margin score defined in Sec. 3.3, which ranks each target point by its rival-to-target margin relative to the carrier average. Let ∼d denote 8-connectivity for d = 2 and 26- connectivity for d = 3. For a carrier point v ∈Kc, define its local 3d neighborhood as N(v) = {x ∈Ω| ∥x −v∥∞≤1}. (2) Let Pv1, . . . , Pvmv be the connected components of (Kc ∩ N(v))\{v} under ∼d; these are the neighboring same-class branches. When mv ≥2, these branches define a local reconnection event: the target requires a connected set of class-c points within N(v) that reaches every branch. Carrier points with mv < 2 do not define a reconnection event. Define the local class-c target support Tv = {x ∈N(v) | Y (x) = c} and the family of valid local reconnections Rv = {R ⊆Tv | R is connected under ∼d, R ∩Pvk̸ = ∅, ∀k ∈{1, . . . , mv}}. (3) The local connectivity bottleneck is then rv = min R∈Rv max x∈R gc(x). (4) For mv = 2, each R ∈Rv contains a same-class path connecting the two branches; at a junction, one connected re- connection reaches all branches simultaneously. Thus, rv is the lowest achievable bottleneck score among valid local re- connections. A large rv means that every valid reconnection contains a point with a large rival-to-target margin relative to the carrier average. Eq. (4) has an equivalent threshold-connectivity form that enables exact bottleneck selection. For τ ≥0, let Tv(τ) = {x ∈Tv | gc(x) ≤τ} and let Cv(τ) denote the connected components of Tv(τ) under ∼d. Then rv = min  τ ≥0 : ∃Q ∈Cv(τ), Q ∩Pvk̸ = ∅, ∀k ∈{1, . . . , mv} . (5) This characterization in Eq. (5) yields an exact finite solver. We activate at most 3d points in Tv in nondecreasing order of gc, with ties resolved by a fixed local order, and merge adjacent active components until a component reaches every branch. If x⋆ v is the last activated point, then rv = gc(x⋆ v). We perform the combinatorial search using detached scores to select x⋆ v, while propagating gradients through the corre- sponding non-detached score gc(x⋆ v). Because the carrier means in Eq.
