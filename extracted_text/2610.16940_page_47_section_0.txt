J Synthetic Manifolds with Known Intrinsic Dimension The cover tree’s O(c6 log n) query bound is stated in terms of the doubling dimension, not the ambi- ent dimension, but on real datasets the intrinsic dimension is unknown, so the distinction cannot be tested directly. We therefore generated synthetic data where it is known by construction: n=20,000 points sampled on a d-dimensional latent manifold, then linearly embedded in ambient dimension D ≥d: latent coordinates are uniform on [0, 1]d, the embedding is a fixed random D × d matrix with N(0, 1) entries, and isotropic Gaussian noise (σ=0.01) puts points near rather than exactly on the manifold, with a fixed seed per configuration. Sweep B raises the ambient dimension 5× at fixed intrinsic dimension: distance computations do not rise monotoni- cally (9.8M→11.0M→8.8M—variation is about 25%, consistent with different random embedding matrices across configurations rather than an intrinsic-dimension effect). This is direct empirical support for the claim that cover-tree query cost is governed by intrinsic rather than ambient dimen- sionality, which the theory asserts but which, to our knowledge, has not previously been isolated experimentally. Wall-clock behaves differently from distance count in Sweep B, and the difference is informative: cover-tree time still rises with D (501→1,203 ms) even though the number of distance computations does not, because each computation is D-dimensional. This is the distance-count-versus-runtime decoupling discussed in Section 4.2, and it is why we report both throughout: a paper reporting only distance counts here would conclude, incorrectly, that ambient dimension is free. 47
