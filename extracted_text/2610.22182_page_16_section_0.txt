Calibration and layer weighting. We next restore bandwidth selection and simplify the full LAMP model on the same four datasets. S1 replaces regional sets with whole-map descriptors; S2 additionally removes support centering and diagonal normalization; S3 further fixes the three layer weights to 1/3. LAMP, S1, and S2 each search 337 configurations, while S3 searches 22. S1 thus uses the WHOLE representation with a larger bandwidth search. The clearest calibration effect occurs with RN50: removing both operations reduces mean accuracy by 0.627 points, with an adjusted 99.1667% interval of [−0.979, −0.277] for S2 minus S1. On EuroSAT, accuracy falls from 82.802% to 80.811%. The ViT change is +0.215 [−0.075, +0.514], showing a less consistent benefit from calibration on this backbone. Replacing regional sets with whole-map descriptors also has task- dependent effects: ViT accuracy on DTD falls from 66.923% to 66.056%, while Pets and Food101 improve slightly. Equal layer weights reduce the means by a further 0.331 points with ViT and 0.180 with RN50. Their adjusted intervals include zero; this comparison assesses the complete selected-weight and equal-weight strategies, including their search budgets. Even after all three simplifications, S3 exceeds the paired global baseline by 2.179 and 1.117 points. These results locate the main gain in native-layer information and the strongest calibration benefit in RN50. 4.6. Performance gains of LAMP-F over LAMP This experiment isolates prototype adaptation by retaining LAMP’s selected ker- nels and mixture weights. Across eleven datasets and five support budgets, LAMP-F improves the three-seed mean in 53 of 55 settings with ViT and 52 of 55 with RN50. With ViT, there is one tie (Pets, 16-shot) and one decrease (Caltech101, 8-shot; −0.068 points); the three RN50 ties all occur on Pets, at 1, 8, and 16 shots. Mean gains decrease from 1.097 and 1.463 points at 4-shot to 0.274 and 0.366 at 16-shot for ViT and RN50, respectively. SUN397 benefits most, with gains concentrated at 1–4 shots. Comparison with an independently adapted global predictor. We also train a separate ProKeR+CLAP model [4, 6] on the ten non-ImageNet datasets at 4-shot. It shares the support data, prompts, validation labels, and checkpoint-search budget with LAMP-F. Both methods evaluate 33 learning-rate/checkpoint candidates; LAMP-F additionally inherits the 337-candidate regional-mixture selection. LAMP-F improves mean accuracy by 1.840 points with ViT and 1.949 with RN50; the family-adjusted intervals are [1.451, 2.283] and [1.483, 2.417], respectively. This comparison assesses the complete LAMP-F adaptation procedure against a global predictor with independently optimized prototypes. 4.7.
