Inconsistent completion across visibility boundaries. Completing unseen surfaces is particularly challenging when text or patterns extend beyond the visible region. For example, lettering that wraps around a can may be only partially visible in the input. The model may preserve the visible fragment yet fail to continue its strokes or layout coherently onto the hidden side, producing inconsistent text or patterns across the visibility boundary. Errors in 2D material estimation. The 2D material estimator can misinterpret material properties, for example by assigning high metallic values to a non-metallic surface. These estimates condition the 3D generator, which may retain the error in the completed asset and produce implausible reflections under rendering or relighting. Accurate transfer of reference details thus does not guarantee correct material decomposition. Failure cases. Figure 6 illustrates these limitations. Reference fidelity depends on usable image–surface correspondence and reliable material observations, while fine detail on unseen surfaces must be generated. The plate shows a printed motif reconstructed as protruding geometry, and the sign retains spurious structures after texture refinement. The owl separates recognizable appearance details from an ambiguous reflectance estimate; the teapot separates sharp observed patterns from less precise unseen completion. These are qualitative cases, without corrected-geometry controls or measured unseen-surface ground truth. A back view may help the last case; we report no paired two-view result.
