Retrieval for the QC-confirmed patients returned 91,453 notes, of which the 26,763 falling inside the six evaluation windows constitute the analyzed corpus. Retrieval was not restricted to the treatment period, so the retrieved notes span November 1998 to August 2026, while the analyzed notes span July 2021 to August 2026. The retrieved corpus spans 67 note types (Figure 1C; full distribution in Supplementary Table S1), the largest being telephone encounters at 22.7% and progress notes, followed by a large “unspecified” group and letters. Because telephone encounters are often the first channel through which adverse effects like diarrhea are reported, we retain all note types rather than restricting to progress notes. Pipeline architecture The extraction pipeline follows a five-step architecture (Figure 1): Step 1: Medication Timeline. Every follow-up window is measured from the patient’s exposure anchor, meaning the identified date on which trofinetide was started. The anchor is taken from the Epic medication-administration record where available, and otherwise from the earliest note documenting active dosing, adjudicated by LLM review; no patient anchored on a dispense record, because trofinetide fills bypass the institutional tables. Across the 108 patients, 8 anchored on an administration record and 91 on an adjudicated note, while 9 lack a reliable date and are excluded from the timepoint analysis. Step 2: Note Bucketing. Each note is assigned to a six-month bucket (baseline through 30 months) by the days between its contact date and the exposure anchor. Notes are then pre-filtered by phenotype-specific keyword lists (Supplementary Table S2), producing 60,537 note–phenotype matches across the 17 phenotypes scored by the presence prompt, all but 7 of which returned a valid YES/NO in the structured output from both models. Those 7 were scored negative (Supplementary Note S1, which also reports inter-LLM agreement). For diarrhea the filter matches 7,111 of the 91,453 retrieved notes, and the 2,260 of those falling inside an evaluation window are the corpus every diarrhea comparison below is scored on. Step 3: Dual-LLM Classification. Each keyword-positive note is independently classified by two LLMs with complementary perspectives. The first, a clinical NLP expert, judges whether the phenotype is documented in the note itself, applying explicit rules to separate findings recorded at that visit from historical, negated, anticipatory, and template mentions against a written per-phenotype definition. For diarrhea that definition adds a severity threshold. The second, a domain specialist (gastroenterologist for side effects, pediatric neurologist for the Rett clinical features), weighs clinical significance and attribution. Prompt templates, YES/NO criteria, and a contingency table of the two agents’ calls (evidence they work independently) are in Supplementary Notes S3 and S1. Both output a structured YES/NO call with supporting evidence, and a note is positive only when both models agree YES. The record also carries the note’s contact date, which is known from EHR metadata rather than extracted from the note text (Supplementary Note S3). The evidence spans were spot-checked but not systematically validated. Detection is thus note-local in time, so historical mentions score negative, at the cost of missing purely retrospective ones (quantified in the error analysis).
