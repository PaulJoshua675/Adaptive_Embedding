Whole-slide images may contain coexisting lesions, yet weak super- vision typically provides only the most severe diagnosis. We formu- late this setting as dominant-label multiple instance learning, where the observed label is the maximum-severity element of a latent le- sion set. Standard multiclass MIL treats it as a complete one-hot description and therefore suppresses lower-severity evidence when- ever it co-occurs with a dominant lesion. We propose DomSet- MIL, a label-aware framework that aligns the annotation mechanism across bag and instance levels. Dominant-Label Censored Likeli- hood models the reported diagnosis without declaring unreported le- sions absent. Severity-Ordered Residual Routing assigns patch ev- idence from high to low severity and preserves residual support for secondary findings, while top-q pooling links local routing scores to bag prediction. Experiments across natural and controlled set- tings show that DomSet-MIL achieves state-of-the-art performance in dominant-label prediction while more effectively recovering latent coexisting-lesion evidence. These findings demonstrate that model- ing the dominant-label mechanism can improve diagnostic predic- tion without sacrificing clinically meaningful secondary findings. Index Terms— multiple instance learning, whole-slide image classification, weak supervision, breast pathology, censored labels
