In other words, Tag- Insert tends to defer difficult3 decisions towards the end of decoding, exploiting its flexible ordering. 3Here, difficult as defined by the strong BERT model being wrong on them. 5.2 CCG Supertagging (CCG Rebank) Table 2 reports the performance of the three models on CCG supertagging: the BERT encoder achieves the highest accuracy across all data sizes, similar to the POS experiment. In this case too, TagInsert outperforms the Vanilla Transformer for all training sizes (The improvement is statistically significant in all cases except the full data setting). 4 Analyses To better understand these results, we first analyzed performance by supertag frequency bins (Table 3). These highlights the long-tail chal- lenge of CCG: although BERT maintains strong accuracy on frequent categories (≥100 in training data), its performance degrades substantially on non-frequent ones. Interestingly, the L2R Vanilla model achieves higher accuracy on rare tags than both BERT and TagInsert. TagInsert, by contrast, collapses on rare categories and instead overfits to frequent tags. An analysis of the cases where TagInsert cor- rectly supertags a word but BERT makes a mis- take shows the average normalized position of such words is 89%, with an average sequence length of 29, showing that TagInsert has deferred these deci- sions closer to the end of decoding, similar to the POS case (Fig. 4).
