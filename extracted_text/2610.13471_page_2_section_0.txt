Restricted function-evaluation budgets are a defin- ing constraint of expensive optimization [10], and methods aimed at that setting, such as emulation-based adaptive differential evolution [11], target the small-evaluation regime directly. On the application side, metaheuristic STAT- COM tuning has been reported with hybrid ACO–PSO and differential-evolution/harmony-search approaches [12, 13], broader VSC-based FACTS control is reviewed in [14], and for electric-arc-furnace service, STATCOM flicker mitiga- tion has been addressed through conventional and energy-storage STATCOMs [15], nonlinear control [16], and more recently multi-objective evolutionary tuning [17]. The cited STATCOM and EAF studies report performance at a fixed experimental setting and do not address the budget-resolved and transfer questions examined here. The methodological gap addressed here is the combined evaluation of a refined optimizer across matched FE budgets, against its parent, at the exact application budget, and on unseen stochastic application trajectories. This paper makes three contributions. 1. A five-optimizer, thirty-run CEC2022 study over a common set of independently configured evaluation budgets at D=10 and D=20, including the exact 1040-evaluation budget used in the application (Sections 4–5). 2. A parent-relative analysis of REMBGO against EMBGO, together with a 1040-evaluation component-removal analysis and an associated supplementary sensitivity study. 3. A 210-run fixed-gain EAF–STATCOM generalization study, in which one selected gain vector per optimizer and controller is replayed across twenty unseen stochastic arc realizations (Section 5). The comparison methodology follows Derrac et al. [18], with Holm correction [19] applied within each planned func- tion family. 2 Application Context and Objective Function This section gives the application detail needed to read the fixed-gain transfer study of Section 5; the paper is otherwise self-contained. A STATCOM compensates a point of common coupling (PCC) that feeds an electric arc furnace (EAF) whose time-varying nonlinear arc dynamics produce rapidly varying reactive power and PCC-voltage modulation. The application uses an 11 kV, 50 Hz EAF–STATCOM EMT plant with 5 MVA grid short-circuit capacity and X/R = 7; a 500 kVA two-level VSC-STATCOM is connected in shunt at the PCC through its coupling transformer and LCL filter. Two controllers are considered. The DQ-PI controller uses decoupled current PI control with filtered dc-link regulation, split-frequency reactive-power control, bounded slow integral action, current limiting and anti-windup. The Lyapunov controller is a nonlinear design with full reactive-power PI regulation, voltage droop, dc-link regulation and nonlinear inner current feedback with the associated limiting. Each controller exposes six tunable parameters, gDQPI = [Kp,id,Ki,id,Kp,iq,Ki,iq,Kp,Q,Ti,Q], gLyap = [Kdroop,Kq,p,Kq,i,k1,i,ki,i,k2,i], (1) where for DQ-PI the reactive integral gain follows Ki,Q = Kp,Q/Ti,Q under the parameter transformation used during optimization and the remaining structure is fixed. The five optimizers (PSO, GWO, FP-SHADE, EMBGO, REMBGO) tune each controller. Every run uses population N = 40 and 25 update iterations, so, including initialization, each run spends N(25 + 1) = 1040 objective evaluations (Section 3); this is the exact application-aligned budget carried into the benchmark study. During tuning the arc-noise stream is held at a single stochastic realization, so repeated optimizer seeds measure optimizer stochasticity rather than different furnace trajectories, a distinction central to the transfer experiment. The 1040-evaluation limit is set by the cost of the objective, not by an arbitrary benchmark choice. In the archived EAF–STATCOM tuning campaign, one complete 1040-evaluation optimization seed required approximately 98–273 min of wall-clock time depending on controller, worker count and computing platform. Among the fourteen archived cases executed with eight workers, runtimes were approximately 98–131 min per seed. These measurements are reported only to establish the practical cost of objective evaluations and are not used to compare optimizer execution speed. The twenty archived optimizer–scenario combinations correspond to five optimizers evaluated across four controller–EAF scenarios (two controllers × two EAF conditions). Each candidate is scored by an EMT simulation over the post-transient window t ∈[0.50,1.00] s. The performance term combines a reactive-power fluctuation feature and a one-cycle-RMS PCC-voltage feature, each normalized by a common uncompensated deterministic reference so that the score measures disturbance severity consistently, TQ = Qfluc Qref fluc , TV = Vpcc V ref pcc , Jperf = 0.60TQ +0.40TV, (2) and the scored scalar adds graded quality and safety penalties, Jtot = Jperf +PQ +PVdc +Psafety.
