Random links behave as graph-free shrinkage at the weight implied by their lifetime (P-MNIST, 20 tasks, 10 seeds). (a) Shrinkage weight implied by the link process, from a simulation of the process alone (Appendix B). (b) to (d) Final accuracy against the shrinkage weight for graph-free shrinkage of hidden-layer importance (blue, where 𝛽= 0 is tag only) and for random links placed at their simulated weight (orange), at three penalty strengths 𝜆. In (d) the labels give the link lifetime, and lifetimes 50, 500, 1000, and 2000 come from Study 1. Error bars show ±1 standard error over seeds. Lines connect measured weights only, so the blue curve was not sampled between 0.5 and 1. First, graph-free shrinkage reproduces the gain of random links. At 𝜆= 0.003, shrinkage with 𝛽= 0.25 improved ACC over tag only by 2.74 ± 0.43 points (mean ± SE, Holm 𝑝= 0.002), and random links with 𝐿= 25 improved it by 2.64 ± 0.41 (Holm 𝑝= 0.002). Shrinkage with 𝛽= 0.5 improved it by 2.41 ± 0.50, and links with 𝐿= 200 by 2.58 ± 0.43. Paired directly, the matched arms differ by at most 0.18 points at any 𝜆. The 95% confidence intervals of these differences lie within ±0.8 points at 𝜆= 0.003 and 0.01, a third or less of the gain over tag only, and within ±1.3 points at 𝜆= 0.001, where the paired differences were noisier. Second, the pairing is predicted rather than fitted. The simulation of the link process alone gives 𝛽eff = 0.23 for 𝐿= 25 and 0.52 for 𝐿= 200 (Figure 2a), close to the graph-free weights that match each lifetime. The consolidated importance agrees. At 𝜆= 0.001, 0.003, and 0.01 its CV was 0.394, 0.413, and 0.454 for links with 𝐿= 25 against 0.389, 0.407, and 0.444 for 𝛽= 0.25, and 0.295, 0.313, and 0.333 for links with 𝐿= 200 against 0.302, 0.320, and 0.342 for 𝛽= 0.5. In the lifetime sweep of Study 1 (Figure 2d), lifetimes of 25 and 200 steps land within 0.2 points of the matched shrinkage arms.
