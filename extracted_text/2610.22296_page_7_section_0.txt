Backward Compatibility in Zero-Bit Neural Audio Watermarking 1. Activation rotation. Within a fixed embedder/detector size, we rotate the activation function in the order in which it became standard in the deep-learning literature: ReLU [26] first, then Swish [27], then GELU [28]. This mimics a team that periodically swaps the activation function for a more recent one without otherwise changing the network. 2. Capacity step-ups. After the activation rotation has cycled through ReLU/Swish/GELU at the original size, we step up the network’s capacity (more dense blocks / more encoder-decoder layers) while keeping the most recent activation. This mimics a team that, after exhausting easy activation upgrades, decides to scale the model. The same two-axis schedule is applied first to the convolutional family (𝑣1–𝑣4) and then repeated on the Transformer family (𝑣5–𝑣8), since Transformer-based audio models entered the field later than convolutional ones. The transition from 𝑣4 to 𝑣5 simulates the discrete jump that occurs when a deployment switches to a fundamentally newer architecture family rather than enlarging the existing one. Training Details. We evaluate every detector on the unwatermarked test set and measure the TNR. All detectors, across both training approaches, achieve 100% TNR. Results Without Diversity Loss. Our base setup pairs each new detector with the legacy chain using performance-weighted sampling and the detection loss alone, with no diversity term. Fig. 1 shows its accuracy matrix. In-chain TPR is near-perfect, between 99.3% and 100% (mean 99.8%), so performance-weighted sampling alone lets each detector serve its whole chain. The version boundary, however, fails catastrophically: mean out-of-chain TNR is only 11.3%, with 24 of 28 incompatible pairs at ≤3%. The collapse is total within the ViTMAE block (0% TNR) and near-total across cross-family transitions (0.7–1.4%). The sole exception is embedder 𝑣4, rejected by earlier detectors at high TNR. Residual-Similarity Evidence for Embedder Collapse. The accuracy matrix shows that the version boundary collapses without a diversity loss, but not why. Our hypothesis is that, with no pressure to differ, a new embedder learns watermark residuals that resemble its predecessors’, so their detectors accept it as one of their own.
