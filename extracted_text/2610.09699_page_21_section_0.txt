Configuration Objective. During the SMAC runs, configurations are evaluated using a nor- malized log-precision measure derived from the best-so-far function values. For each run, precision values are clipped to the interval [10−8, 102], transformed to a logarithmic scale, and averaged over the evaluation budget. This yields a scalar performance metric that reflects the convergence speed of a configuration: the area over the convergence curve, equivalent to the area under the empirical cumulative distribution L´opez-Ib´a˜nez et al. (2024). This objective is to be minimized, such that lower values correspond to faster convergence. Selection and Validation. After optimization, we take only the configurations that have been evaluated for 25 repetitions by the intensification procedure. The configurations are ranked by their average SMAC objective value, and the five best configurations per function are retained. Each of these candidates is then evaluated on 50 independent BBOB instances that were not used during tuning. For each function, the reported tuned configuration is the candidate with the lowest ERT on this validation set. 9.2 Benchmark Results For the performance comparison, we again use ERT (see Section 8.2), with a predefined target precision of 10−8 following the standard COCO/BBOB Hansen et al. (2020) methodology6. We compare three settings: 1. Default: the default ModCMA configuration. 2. Tuned: the per-function tuned configurations obtained via SMAC. 3. bbob2009: a reference baseline constructed from the per-function best-performing algorithms in the BBOB-2009 benchmark Hansen et al. (2010). For both the ‘Default’ and ‘Tuned’ ModCMA configurations, we calculate ERT over 50 validation runs. For the reference algorithm, we extract all data from the BBOB-2009 benchmark from the COCO data archive COCO Platform (n.d.), and calculate ERT over 15 runs. For each function, the virtual best-solver, i.e., the solver with the lowest ERT, is selected for comparison. Results Figure 3 shows the ERT values for all 24 BBOB functions. A larger version of this figure, including individual entries for each baseline algorithm, is provided in Appendix A. The benefit of automated configuration is clearly visible across the benchmark. The default configuration fails to consistently reach the target on a substantial subset of functions (e.g., f3, f4, and f15–f24), resulting in infinite or very large ERT values. In contrast, the tuned configurations substantially reduce ERT on most functions, often by orders of magnitude. For some functions, the improvement is more modest, such as on f2 and f9–f14, but even in these cases the tuned configuration improves upon the default by at least a factor of two. On several functions where the default has very low success (e.g., f15–f23), the tuned configurations reliably reach the target, yielding finite ERT values.
