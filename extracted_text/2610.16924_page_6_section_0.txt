Fidelity to LIF Dynamics We evaluate the fidelity of FidLIF on a single layer using Gaussian input currents c ∼N(0, 1). Except for the repre- sentative trajectory, results are averaged over five trials with shaded regions denoting one standard deviation. Fig. 2 compares FidLIF directly with the Heaviside-gated hard-reset LIF dynamics. As the number of iterations in- creases, both the membrane potential error and the spike mismatch rate decrease. The residual error arises from two sources: the finite-iteration error in recovering the sigmoid- relaxed trajectory, and the discrepancy between the sigmoid- relaxed and original Heaviside-gated dynamics. These results confirm that FidLIF faithfully recovers the dynamics of the Heaviside-gated hard-reset LIF within very few iterations. To isolate the solver error from the discrepancy in the underlying dynamics, Fig. 3 compares each method against its own matched serial sigmoid-relaxed baseline. FidLIF is compared against the sigmoid-relaxed hard-reset LIF while FPT is compared against the sigmoid-relaxed subtractive- reset LIF. The results show that FidLIF converges substan- tially faster, reducing the mean absolute error of the mem- brane potential below 10−7 with five iterations. This shows that, under the same iterations, the Newton-based FidLIF re- covers the target relaxed dynamics more accurately than the repeated substitution method used in FPT. Backward Correctness and Computational Efficiency Given an input current sequence, we use the gradients ob- tained via serial step-by-step forward propagation and auto- matic differentiation (i.e., BPTT) as a reference. Then, we run FidLIF on the same current sequence and compute backprop- agation gradients using our adjoint scan after each iteration. Finally, we compare the gradient calculated after each itera- tion with the reference gradient. As shown in Fig. 4(a), the gradient error decreases rapidly with K, while larger values of K may yield identical errors after convergence due to tolerance-based early stopping. At K = 3, the gradient error is less than 10−6. Since the backward adjoint scan is BPTT- equivalent for an identical forward trajectory, the remaining gradient discrepancy at finite K mainly reflects incomplete forward-trajectory recovery. Finally, we test computational efficiency on a single layer with parameter generation disabled, using an RTX 4090 in FP32 (TF32 disabled). The implementation of PLIF comes from SpikingJelly (Fang et al. 2021). Figures 4(b) and 4(c) report backward and combined forward–backward latency across T at K = 3. The latency advantage of FidLIF in- creases with T. Figure 4(d) shows peak memory across K at T = 256. While FPT’s memory grows near-linearly, FidLIF maintains a nearly constant memory usage because it does not need per-iteration computation graphs. Task Performance We evaluate FidLIF on sequential CIFAR10/100, CIFAR10- DVS (Li et al. 2017), and SHD (Cramer et al. 2022), cover- ing sequential images, event-based vision, and long-horizon spiking speech. Table 1 compares task accuracies and the features of different methods. The task accuracies for other methods are taken from (Fang et al. 2023; Xue et al. 2026; Li et al. 2024; Feng et al. 2025), respectively. The ConvNet used for sequential CIFAR10/100 tasks and the VGG architecture used for CIFAR10-DVS task both use a modified version implemented by PSN (Fang et al. 2023). For SHD, we use the FCNet with learnable delays (Hammouamri, Khalfaoui- Hassani, and Masquelier 2024), which is the same approach as MF-PSN (Xue et al. 2026). All FidLIF task-performance experiments in Table 1 set the maximum number of forward iterations to K = 3 and are repeated three times, reported as mean ± std. Most parallel neurons in Table 1 break the serial form, pre- venting incremental step-by-step inference. Methods using temporal matrices cannot be reduced to a recurrence because their entries are learned independently, breaking the struc- ture of recurrence. Sliding PSN and MF-PSN restore serial ability by restricting temporal dependence to a finite window.
