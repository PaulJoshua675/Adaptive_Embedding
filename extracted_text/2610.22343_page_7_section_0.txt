We report frame-wise mean AbsRel (↓) and δ1 (↑) (Eigen et al., 2014) over valid pixels. Our method flexibly adapts VGGT-Ω’s default balanced resolution preprocessing to both training and evaluation, while baselines follow their original resolution settings. For scale handling, we follow the original evaluation procedure of each baseline: VGGT-World performs per- target-frame scale alignment, while FR3D performs per-target-frame scale-and-shift alignment. For DINO-Foresight, we fit its relative inverse-depth predictions to the LiDAR measurements of each target frame. In contrast, we calibrate metric scale once using LiDAR at the last observed FRONT frame and retain this scale throughout the entire forecasting rollout without target-frame alignment. We additionally measure cross-query geometric consistency using symmetric reprojection AbsRel. Baselines. We compare with DINO-Foresight (Karypidis et al., 2025), which forecasts seman- tic image features, and geometry-based world models FR3D (Morbitzer et al., 2026) and VGGT- World (Sun et al., 2026). We train DINO-Foresight and VGGT-World on the same Waymo training data as our method, but following their respective training procedures. All FR3D results are taken directly from the original paper. We also include three Copy-Last baselines. Fixed View repeats the last observed VGGT-Ωdepth at every future timestamp, while GT Camera and Pred. Camera decode the last observed scene state from ground-truth or predicted future cameras. These baselines allow us to assess the effects of future camera motion and scene-state prediction. Implementation Details. We pretrain the Scene Tokenizer and ray-conditioned decoder on the multi-view pretraining data for 200k steps with an initial learning rate of 10−5, and then finetune on the static Waymo subset for 50k steps with an initial learning rate of 5×10−6. Both stages use a global effective batch size of 36, with the VGGT-Ωencoder frozen. We sample one to five source views per scene and supervise depth reconstruction at held-out query views. For static Waymo sequences, source and query views may span both cameras and timestamps. We then train the World Model in two stages: 20k steps of one-step prediction with an initial learning rate of 10−5, followed by 35k steps of seven-step autoregressive rollout training with an initial learning rate of 10−6. VGGT-Ω, the Scene Tokenizer, and the decoder remain frozen throughout World Model training, while gradients propagate through the decoder to the predicted states.
