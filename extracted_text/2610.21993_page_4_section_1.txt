2.1. Masked Image Modeling Building on masked language modeling (Devlin et al., 2019), masked image modeling (MIM) has become the leading approach to self-supervised visual pretraining. MAE (He et al., 2022) showed that reconstructing raw pixels from a small visible subset (as little as 25%) of ViT patches produces representations that transfer well at scale, and SimMIM (Xie et al., 2022) car- ried the same reconstruction objective over to hierarchical Swin Transformers (Liu et al., 2021). I-JEPA (Assran et al., 2023) took a different route, predicting latent representations of the masked regions instead of pixel values, which pushes the model toward semantic structure over low-level texture. These ViT-based approaches are effective but data- and compute-hungry. Convolutional al- ternatives have narrowed that gap: MCMAE (Gao et al., 2022) combines convolution and trans- former blocks in a hybrid masked autoencoder, while SparK (Tian et al., 2023), the framework this work builds on, shows that sparse masked modeling works directly on purely hierarchical CNNs (ResNet (He et al., 2016), ConvNeXt (Liu et al., 2022)) with no transformer component at all. MMEarth (Nedungadi et al., 2024) is a concurrent example in the electro-optical remote sensing domain, pairing a ConvNeXt V2 (Woo et al., 2023) backbone with multi-modal MIM objectives on electro-optical Sentinel-2 imagery; we pursue the same CNN MIM direction on a different modality, applying it to SAR-only pretraining under the SparK framework. 2.2.
