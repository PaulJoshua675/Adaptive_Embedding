Dense4D separates correspondence from camera-pose and scene-motion estimation while keeping tracks consistent with its predicted human geometry by construction. Compared with the baselines, Dense4D produces more precise trajectories and a better fit to measured human geometry, with videos showing robust tracking through articulation and clothing deformation. Dense4D can also be integrated with scene point clouds simply through shared image pixels. Limitations. Self-contact can make reference-space correspondence ambiguous when distinct surface points coincide. Selecting a reference frame without self-contact, when available, is a relatively straightforward mitigation that we do not pursue here. A broader challenge is evaluating correspondence on real people: 4D-DRESS measures proximity to scans rather than point identity, while Panoptic Studio Humans relies on reconstruction-derived trajectory labels that may inherit reconstruction errors.
