In Legal IR, passage-based retrieval has also been explored. Rosso et al. applied it to treaties, patents, and contracts in their experiments to find relevant sections rather than full documents [14]. Aumiller et al. study the trade-off between document-level and smaller representations using a collection of English Terms of Service documents [4]. They argued that document-level repre- sentations may be too broad, whereas sentence- or paragraph-level representations may omit the contextual information needed to understand a topic. They proposed structural text segmentation based on topical coherence, identifying coherent sections that may span multiple paragraphs. Sim- ilarly, Malik et al. investigated semantic segmentation of legal documents using rhetorical roles [15]. Their corpus consists of English-language documents from the Indian legal system, and their method identifies semantically coherent units corresponding to functions such as facts, arguments, issues, precedents, and rulings. Nguyen et al. investigated the retrieval of sets of legal articles related to a query or its negation in Japanese statute law, using individual legal articles as retrieval units rather than entire legal documents [16]. Another approach uses structural information that is already present in legal documents. Structure-aware retrieval methods consider organizational or functional information to represent legal documents and their components. SAILER, for example, incorporates the structure of legal case documents into a pre-trained language model for legal case retrieval [17]. The authors argued that legal cases contain logical structures and key legal elements that are important for deter- mining relevance, and they designed the model to exploit relationships between these structural components. Yang et al. also incorporated structured information into legal case retrieval, com- bining textual representations with structured factual representations based on event chains and participant roles [18]. These studies show that document structure can provide information about how different parts of a legal document should be represented. The importance of document structure is especially clear in legal acts that follow formal drafting conventions. In such documents, structure is part of how information is organized. Brazilian legal acts provide an example. Complementary Law No. 95/1998 establishes drafting rules for Brazilian laws, including rules concerning their organization and hierarchical structure [6]. Other normative documents, including regulations, resolutions, ordinances, and legislative proposals, are organized according to similar principles (e.g. [7, 19]). This formal organization provides explicit boundaries that may be used for document segmentation and retrieval. Legal IR has developed around a range of specialized tasks and benchmarks, with much of the established literature focusing on judicial case retrieval and legal datasets from a limited number of jurisdictions. More recently, interest in Legal IR for Brazilian Portuguese has grown, with the development of publicly available datasets, retrieval pipelines, and benchmarking initiatives [8–10, 20–22]. These resources have enabled systematic comparisons of retrieval methods across different collections. However, relatively little work has considered the internal organization of Brazilian legal documents for retrieval [23, 24]. In the Brazilian context, Lima proposed a multi-layered representation for Brazilian legal acts [23]. Originally developed for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) applications, it exploits their hierarchical organization to represent each document at different structural levels. In this work, we investigate its use for Legal IR, where the goal is to retrieve the source document rather than individual chunks. We also conduct ablation studies to evaluate simplified represen- tations derived from Lima’s approach by varying which chunks are indexed and which contextual information is included with them.
