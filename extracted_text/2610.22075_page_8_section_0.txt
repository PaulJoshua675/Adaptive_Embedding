Knowledge distillation. We further evaluated the impact of knowledge distillation, especially at the training stage S2, and compared this setting with no knowledge distillation, a knowledge dis- tillation based on image-text samples only, as well as a knowledge distillation with text-image as well as text-only training data in Table 4. Overall, it shows that knowledge distillation in S2 always improves the overall performance, and that having both image-text as well as text-only data leads to the most significant improvement, improving over the undistilled version by 1.48 pp. The unpruned baseline gains only 0.25 pp from the same S2 distillation (first two rows). We attribute the gain of the recurrent model to the fact that the knowledge distillation acts as a form of regularizer for the new recurrent architecture, which keeps the model close to the pretrained one and prevents it from overfitting on the image-text data. This is in line with the large effect of removing S0 in Table 2, which also anchors the retrofitted model to the pretrained one. Appendix A.2 reports the placement of the distillation term across the training stages at the 7.5B budget. Fixed versus sampled depth. We further evaluate the effect of the number of recurrences during training in Table 5. To this end, we compare a model trained with a fixed number of recurrences r=6 against a model trained with a variable number of recurrences, sampled per training step with mean 6 (Sec. 3.1). Both models are evaluated at test-time depths from r=2 to r=16. We find that the model trained with a fixed recurrence only works at the depth it was trained for, degrades at the neighbouring depths and collapses beyond r=8 or below r=5. Its GQA accuracy drops from 59.7 at r=6 to 43.1 at r=5 and 50.9 at r=8, and the output degenerates at all other depths. In contrast, the model trained with a sampled recurrence regime also works at depths other than the one it was trained for, staying within 2 pp of its best value from r=2 to r=16. This can be attributed to the fact that the sampled regime exposes the model to a different number of recurrences in every training step, so that its readout is trained for every depth in that range. However, we find that its performance saturates and does not improve beyond the trained depth. Consequently, we do not observe test-time scaling. The fixed and sampled recurrence models perform on par at the trained depth, with 55.75 against 55.83 on Avg. We therefore find that a fixed recurrence model is the less expensive approach to train a recurrent VLM, but it comes at the price of test-time stability to varying recurrences.
