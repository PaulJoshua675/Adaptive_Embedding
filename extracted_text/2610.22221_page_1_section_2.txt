A robot clearing a table observes two cups that look alike, one ceramic and one plastic. A vision model might correctly label their materials, but this categorical distinction does not reveal how they behave, since the ceramic cup shatters when dropped while the plastic cup bounces. Without physical grounding, vision cannot predict these divergent futures. This appearance–reality gap pervades modern computer vision. Large vision models achieve strong recognition performance, but what they encode is how things look rather than how things be- have. Physics benchmarks confirm this gap. PhysBench [Chow et al., 2025] found that GPT-4o achieves only 46.99% on physical dynamics (49.49% overall, against 95.87% for humans), and Physion++ [Tung et al., 2023] showed that all models remain well below human performance on latent properties such as mass, friction, or elasticity. ∗Equal contribution. 40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026).
