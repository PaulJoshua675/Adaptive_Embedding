Transformers underpin modern vision, language, and multimodal models, but their intensive matrix operations impose substantial computation, memory, and data-movement costs. Analog Compute- in-Memory (CIM) performs matrix-vector multiplications (MVMs) in situ and therefore offers an energy-efficient deployment sub- strate [11, 22, 25]. However, device variation, write noise, and other analog non-idealities perturb stored weights and can severely de- grade Transformer inference. To mitigate such degradation, noise-aware training (NAT) meth- ods incorporate hardware noise injection during training to improve model robustness [12, 19, 26]. While effective for conventional con- volutional neural networks (CNNs), these approaches treat the model as a black box and optimize solely for correct output predic- tions, overlooking the distinctive role of attention mechanisms in Transformer inference. This output-centric paradigm leaves a criti- cal gap: the internal semantic reasoning pathways of Transformers remain fragile under CIM noise, even when final predictions appear correct. For clear spatial visualization of attention routes, we conduct a detailed analysis of Vision Transformers (ViTs) [9]. (L) Output- centric NAT misses semantic attention routes. Existing NAT meth- ods supervise only final predictions, allowing models to produce correct answers via incorrect attention routes.
