A fixed-point curve in the CTM The cascade account, and why it fails. The balanced-amplification structure of §3 suggests a compelling alternative account of the waves. If the tick map is an almost-pure feed-forward cascade that also organizes the observed dynamics, then the traveling waves should appear when neurons are ordered by their position in that cascade. They do not. Ordering neurons by their position in J’s feed-forward chain — each neuron’s mean index in the Schur basis J = UTU∗, weighted by its participation P k U2 ik, using J at the mid- trajectory tick — peak activation time does not advance: the rank correlation between cascade position and peak tick is −0.08 over all D neurons, and between cascade position and activation phase +0.01 (medians over 16 images). The reason is structural: J — and with it its Schur ordering — is redefined at every tick, so no single instantaneous cascade can organize a trajectory that runs over all ticks; and because the state sits on the moving fixed point, the bulk of the activation is carried by where that fixed point is, while the balanced-amplification transient governs only the one-step residual. The traveling waves are thus a signature of the moving fixed-point curve, not of the instantaneous feed-forward cascade; the balanced-amplification structure of J stands as a property of the operator whose functional role is left open. 6. The curve carries class identity Every trajectory starts from the same learned z1, so class identity must enter the curve along the way. It does so at once. Images and recordings. 50 classes with 50 validation images each (2500 images), drawn at random from the 1000 ImageNet classes. The model is rolled out once per image, record- ing the state zt, the fixed point z⋆ t (iterated to relative tolerance 10−5) and the readout memory St out at 15 ticks. When class becomes decodable. At each tick, a linear probe (standardization, 128 principal components, multinomial logistic regression; 5-fold cross-validation) decodes class from each representation. With about 40 training images per class the probes are a lower bound: the model’s own readout, trained on all of ImageNet, reaches 0.92 where the probes plateau near 0.86. Comparisons are therefore made between representations under the same probe. The fixed point leads: 0.82 ± 0.02 at tick 1 (50-way; chance 0.02) against 0.79 ± 0.02 for zt and 0.79 ± 0.03 for St out, and above the token-bank mean of the image features (0.75 ± 0.02). The state’s accuracy at tick t+1 tracks the fixed point’s at tick t (0.81 vs 0.82, 0.83 vs 0.84, 0.84 vs 0.85 over ticks 1–4), the one-tick lag of §3 seen in class information. The fixed point and the state peak near 0.86 by tick 4–6, and St out near 0.87 by tick 10; thereafter St out holds its level while z⋆ t and zt drift slightly lower (to about 0.83), with transient dips (ticks 25 and 36) that we do not interpret. The model’s readout, restricted to the 50 classes, rises from 0.73 at tick 1 to 90% of its plateau at tick 3, as the memory accumulates, and plateaus near 0.92 from tick 13. Where class sits relative to the curve. We write z⋆ i,t = ct + dk(i),t + ri,t, with ct the mean over images (the shared curve), dk,t the offset of class k, and ri,t the image residual. Over images and ticks, the shared curve carries 0.66 of the variance, class offsets 0.105 (against 0.007 when labels are shuffled) and the image residual 0.24.
