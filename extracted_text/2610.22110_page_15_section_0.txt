On the illustrative seed set used during development, mean DBS shifted from −1.313 for the base model to −0.380 after specialization. Under the sign convention in Eq. 3, both values favor the sovereign candidate, but the specialized score is closer to zero. It would therefore be incorrect to describe this shift as a stronger sovereign preference. The result instead reflects a reduced magnitude in the modeled contrast. We retain the metric because it exposes candidate-level probability distributions, but we interpret direction and magnitude explicitly rather than attaching an uncritical label to every numerical movement. This distinction is methodologically important. In production evaluations, DBS can be reported alongside bootstrap confidence intervals over contrast pairs, sign tests for the proportion of instances with DBS < 0, and stratified subsets across specific phenomena, including lexical borrowing, kinship terminology, toponyms, and ceremonial register.
