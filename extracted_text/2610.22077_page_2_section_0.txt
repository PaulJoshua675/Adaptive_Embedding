Tropical cyclones are among the most damaging weather hazards globally (WMO, 2021). Evacuation and impact preparedness depend on forecasts of tropical cyclone location and intensity several days ahead, including the uncertainty of those predictions, issued by weather agencies. Yet cyclones occupy only about 0.5% of grid points in global climate simulations (Lacombe et al., 2023), and the proportion of intense cyclones is projected to increase under continued global climate warming (IPCC, 2023; Knutson et al., 2020). Their rarity and consequences make them an important testbed for learned forecast models, particularly as these models enter operational and cyclone forecasting trials (Lang & Magnusson, 2025; Google DeepMind, 2025). We revisit the ability of AI models at forecasting extreme weather through a systematic evaluation of three recent leading probabilistic AI weather models: GenCast (Price et al., 2024), FourCastNet 3 (FCN3; Bonev et al., 2025), and FGN (Alet et al., 2025). Across 178 tropical cyclones in two evaluation settings, we intercompare these three models’ ability to predict location and intensity with respect to reanalysis and best track data. In addition, we benchmark these models against IFS-ENS, ECMWF’s numerical operational ensemble model, to assess how far recent progress extends to cyclone forecasting and where limitations remain. Our evaluation examines both forecast skill and the properties of the predicted storms, combining probabilistic track forecasts, intensity, ensemble calibration, and wind–pressure dependence. This allows us to assess both forecast accuracy and the models’ ability to reproduce the physical relationships that characterize these extreme events. Among our findings, operational AI models improve cyclone forecasts in several respects. FGN outperforms IFS-ENS on probabilistic track scores for about 90% of the 41 retained storms in 2025, and its advantage extends to the spatial location of strong winds and low pressures. Both operational AI models show larger relative CRPS gains over IFS-ENS near cyclones than at global scale. Yet improvements in aggregate scores do not imply improvements in every aspect of the storm: GenCast outperforms FCN3 on cyclone-local CRPS despite a larger peak-wind bias, while FGN’s better spatial scores coexist with intensity biases similar to GenCast’s and persistent ensemble calibration errors. Finally, the operational AI models retain intensity distributions close to ERA5 despite fine-tuning on HRES-fc0, yet reproduce HRES-fc0’s wind–pressure dependence once marginal differences are removed.
