We examine how reliable verification feedback can help 3D systems reflect on their outputs. Parallel selection measures the value of recognizing successful candidates, while human-guided recursive refinement examines how explicit failure feedback can guide subsequent revisions. Parallel selection. For 40 source images, we fix seven factual requirements before generation and obtain eight candidates each from Pixal3D and Hunyuan3D-2.1 (Team Hunyuan3D et al., 2025), forming a sixteen-candidate pool per input. All candidates are reviewed by multiple humans with adjudication. Table 4 shows that human-oracle selection raises full factual success from 26.7% under uniform random selection to 90.0%, while Strict Full rises from 16.6% to 85.0%. Selection by GPT-6 Astra also improves over random, approaches human-level performance in factual success rate, but stays below the human oracle. Thus, the oracle results quantify the selection headroom available from reliable verification rather than the performance of an existing automatic verifier. Selection cannot recover a requirement that is absent from all sampled candidates. We therefore study eight inputs whose initial Hunyuan3D-2.1 generation violates at least one of the seven facts. After each generation, a human verifier identifies unmet requirements and guides either regeneration from a revised 2D condition or targeted mesh editing with VecSet- Edit (Hsiao et al., 2026). Recursive refinement satisfies all seven facts for all 8/8 cases, although only 7/8 final assets avoid severe defects and 6/8 additionally preserve source fidelity. We additionally compare against a parallel oracle baseline, Parallel Oracle@8, which uses a fixed pool of eight independently generated candidates and selects a successful candidate whenever one exists.
