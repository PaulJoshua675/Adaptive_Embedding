The ability to systematically vary group size and computational capacity also offers a framework that can be readily extended to test factors in optimal team composition. Prior work on team composition suggests a number of factors impact group outcomes, including relative performance of individuals, group size, and group structure (Lamberson & Page, 2012). These factors can be translated into testable predictions within our model and used to investigate potential underlying mechanisms systematically (McClelland, 2009). ESNs implement well defined and parameterized processes that can be precisely tuned for computational budget and individual training as well as leveraged across many tasks. This framework could thus contribute new mechanistic links between cognitive processes and collective outcomes (Scott-Phillips & Nettle, 2022; Smaldino et al., 2023). 5 General Discussion In this paper, we ask what dynamics enable dyads and groups to perform better when deliberating together versus independently and how interaction can be leveraged for efficient collective decision-making. We offer a novel model of joint decision-making using coupled ESNs that shows how dyads can outperform their best individual member. They can do so somewhat through statistical facilitation, but interaction through step-by-step pooling of read-outs yielded even better performance and also better accounted for the data. This benefit of coupling ESNs via interactions grew further still with larger ensemble sizes, in terms of absolute performance as well as computational efficiency. Our model shows how interactions among reservoir networks can shape their individual dynamics in the service of collective decision-making. Reservoir computing is general-purpose and our model could be used to investigate the role of dynamics in many different kinds of collective tasks and performances. 5.1 Interaction and Crowd Wisdom In our model, pooling feedback offers a simple mechanism to fulfill the function of weighted confidence-sharing proposed by Bahrami et al. (2010). On each time step, read-out vectors encoded both a decision (most active node) and a degree of confidence in whether the vowel token came from each of the nine speakers–specifically, unit magnitudes encoded confidence because their expected values were zero when the reservoir provided no reduction in the uncertainty of speaker identification. As identification information becomes available in the reservoir, units move away from their zero baselines towards identification of one or more speakers over others. Therefore, a more confident ESN contributed larger read-out magnitudes compared with a less confident ESN, and larger magnitudes exerted more influence on collective decisions. Weighted confidence-sharing does not specify how interaction alters decision-processing over time. Individual guesses could be combined after independent decision-making has terminated, which would predict that integrating decisions across independent networks would show the same dyadic advantage as coupled networks, or better. Instead, we found that pooling feedback step-by-step as inputs came in over time provided a better account of the data, which we take as evidence that the collective benefit in perceptual decision-making and similar tasks can emerge from interactions that shape both individual and collective processing dynamics.
