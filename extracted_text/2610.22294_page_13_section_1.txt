We introduce and study multi-target few-shot prompting with masked DLMs, where demon- strations guide the infilling of multiple regions within a shared canvas. One-token-at-a-time decoding with deterministic position selection defines a normalized distribution over complete infills, enabling exact sampling, scoring, and likelihood-based example guidance. Across few-shot prompting benchmarks and Java refactoring, we find that demonstrations improve infilling accuracy, with example guidance providing further gains in several settings. Our experiments further investigate the benefits of example guidance relative to likelihood sharpening, while identifying opportunities to improve target-length allocation for practical editing. Together, these findings provide a foundation for more effective example-guided multi-site infilling. Limitations. Our framework assumes that target sites and demonstration-output positions E are specified in advance, leaving their automatic identification for future work. The deterministic, one-token-at-a-time decoding used for exact sampling and scoring does not exploit parallel generation, while the induced distribution may depend on the reveal order and model interface for learned DLMs. Moreover, target lengths must be allocated before infilling, making improved length prediction an important direction for practical use. Example guidance measures relative support from demonstrations rather than correctness, so its effectiveness depends on how the reward ranks sampled infills. Increasing guidance strength can also concentrate resampling weights without necessarily improving accuracy (Section B.5). Our experiments include an autoregressive comparison for single-target prompting, while systematic comparison for multi- target infilling remains future work. Finally, the Java refactoring study uses an LLM judge rather than code execution, includes commits examined during development, and contains some prompts in which reference text appears elsewhere in the context. 13
