TLM candidates across four dimensions: latency, compliance, determinism, and policy impact. For a given network state s, the TLM is queried N times under fixed sampling parameters, temperature T ∈[0, 1] and nucleus probability p ∈[0, 1], yielding structured responses E = {en}N n=1. i) Latency is measured by the end-to-end response time ℓn, from which we report the mean µℓ, standard deviation σℓ, and empirical 95th percentile ℓ95. Responses with ℓn > 600 ms violate the sub-second budget of Near-RT RIC control loops. ii) Compliance is enforced by validating each reply against a strict Draft-7 JSON schema. The schema error rate E is the fraction of invalid outputs; if E > 0, BARX assigns a suitability score of 0. iii) Determinism is quantified by pairwise comparison of valid responses, with consistency score C defined as the proportion of identical reply pairs. Scores C < 1 indicate non-deterministic behavior due to sampling noise or soft hallucinations. iv) Policy impact is assessed by parsing valid replies into control directives executed under a TLM-guided Near-RT RIC policy. The average episodic reward under TLM guidance, ¯RTLM, is compared with the RL-only baseline reward ¯RRL. The differ- ence ∆¯R quantifies the functional impact of agentic reasoning in the control loop. BARX aggregates (µℓ, σℓ, ℓ95, E, C, ∆¯R) into a normalized score [0, 100], with thresholds calibrated us- ing foundation-model evaluation practices [27]. Scores above 80 indicate deployment-grade performance; scores from 60 to 79 indicate marginal reliability requiring monitoring; scores from 40 to 59 require refinement; and scores below 40 are unsafe for production. X-RIC Agent System Architecture Our X-RIC framework in Fig. 1(a) employs two coordinated agents with critic-guided selection for autonomous slice man- agement. Coordination Architecture. The architecture con- sists of: (1) the Monitoring Agent, implemented as the TLM (based on LLaMA3-8B with LangChain tools), which contin- uously observes network performance using a two-tier tem- poral strategy: 1 s polling frequency for real-time monitoring from the Kinetica time-series database and 10 s aggregation windows for statistical smoothing. This design implements the periodic intervention strategy validated as optimal in Sec- tion IV-B. The TLM monitors network state continuously and generates supervisory resource allocation recommendations aTLM every 10 s.(2) The Control Agent, implemented as a BC- trained actor-critic policy πθπ with critic network QθQ, serves as the primary low-latency controller (< 10 ms) for resource allocation. Action Selection and Demonstration Harvesting. At each 10 s decision epoch, both agents observe the same network state sk: the Control Agent proposes aRL = πθ(sk) while the TLM proposes aTLM. The critic evaluates both candidates and executes the TLM recommendation when ∆Rk = Qϕ(sk, aTLM) −Qϕ(sk, aRL) > 0; otherwise, the RL action proceeds autonomously as the primary real-time controller. A dual-filter agentic BC layer, detailed in Sect. II-C, generates high-quality demonstrations from positive TLM in- teractions, storing only critic-validated state-action pairs in the demonstration buffer Dfil for behavioral cloning updates. Policy updates flow back to the RL agent with minimal overhead (median TLM latency: 465.6 ms vs. sub-10 ms RL latency), forming a closed-loop learning system. C. Automated Demonstration Collection for BC Demonstration-augmented RL has shown promise in im- proving sample efficiency, particularly in complex decision- making tasks [28]. However, it still faces key limitations: expert demonstrations are often collected through manual teleoperation, or scripted policies. Although helpful in learning complex behaviors, the acquisition of high-quality demonstra- tions is often costly and impractical to scale [29], [30]. To address these limitations, we propose automated demonstra- tion harvesting using a TLM with episode-level policy-impact gating ∆¯Rk and critic-validated action pruning. Our approach integrates BC as imitation learning to enable agents to mimic high-quality TLM-guided behaviors, accelerating learning by bootstrapping policies with expert-like actions. Episodes with positive ∆¯Rk values indicate improved performance from the use of agentic reasoning in the control loop, qualifying them as candidates for demonstration collection. However, these demonstrations may still contain suboptimal actions due to partial observability or ambiguous instruction mappings. Blindly cloning all actions from episodes with positive ∆¯Rk values could reinforce suboptimal behaviors and lead to performance degradation. To address this, we propose a dual-filtering mechanism that automates demonstration collection and retains only the most informative state–action pairs for training. The first filter operates at the episode level using a policy-impact metric, while the second operates at the action level using a critic- guided Q-filter. This design addresses two critical challenges in demonstration-augmented RL; (i) reducing the cost of demon- stration collection by leveraging TLM-generated trajectories, and (ii) improving demonstration quality through critic-guided filtering of suboptimal actions.
