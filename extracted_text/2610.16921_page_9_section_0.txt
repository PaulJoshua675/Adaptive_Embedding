A representative single-trial example is included in the Graphi- cal Abstract for illustration. The analysis is then supplemented by class-wise averages to assess the generalizability of these findings beyond a single trial. Only Left-hand and Right-hand tasks are reported because these classes exhibit the clearest contralateral ERD/ERS patterns over the sensorimotor cortex. The visualizations of Feet and Tongue imagery show weaker contralateral effects with similar temporal decision patterns and are therefore omitted. All visualizations use the same configuration as in the online experiment. Fig. 2 and Fig. 3 present averaged decision process for Left-hand and Right-hand motor imagery respectively. In both figures, row (a) displays induced ERD/ERS maps computed using the method from Graimann et al. [35], where phase- locked components are removed by subtracting the across- trial mean, followed by short-time Fourier transforms, baseline correction relative to the 0-1 s, and statistical validation via t-percentile bootstrap testing. Black contours highlight time- frequency regions with statistically significant deviations from baseline (α < 0.05). Row (b) visualizes the action probability distribution over time as a stacked profile, where the vertical axis represents the mean proportion of each action. The stack is ordered from bottom to top as Discard, Onset, Sustain, and Offset. Row (c) reports the mean confidence of the true class across windows, visualizing the confidence evolution throughout the trial. The three rows reveal knowledge alignment between in- duced ERD/ERS patterns and the learned policy. Row (a) exhibits induced ERD/ERS over the contralateral sensorimotor cortex within the mu-beta range, with stronger desynchroniza- tion occurring during MI performing periods. In Fig. 2(a), Left-hand imagery exhibits a clear µ-band desynchronization over C4 (around ∼10 Hz). In Fig. 3(a), Right-hand imagery shows a prominent β-band desynchronization over C3 (around ∼20 Hz). Row (b) demonstrates that early time windows are dominated by Discard or Onset decisions. From approximately 0.8 s to 2.5 s, the agent’s selections consist mainly of Onset and Sustain actions, indicating that discriminative evidence is concentrated in this period. Beyond approximately 2.5-4.0 s, Offset becomes the prevailing action, reducing the inclusion of low-information windows, which is consistent with typical motor imagery durations. In row (c), the mean probability of the true class rises at onset, surpasses 0.7 during the sustained desynchronization stage, and then gradually declines as neural activity returns toward baseline.
