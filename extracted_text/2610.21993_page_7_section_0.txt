We did not log gradient norms directly, but the timing is consistent with gradient magnitudes in the high- dimensional skip connections exceeding the FP16 dynamic range (≤65,504). At the reduced pretraining learning rate lr=10−4, UNet-768 trains stably under FP16 mixed precision pretraining, but yields inferior downstream performance compared to UNet-128 at the same learning rate (Table 7), confirming that decoder capacity itself, not solely the lower learning rate required for stability, contributes to UNet-768’s inferior downstream performance. To characterize its VRAM requirements beyond our production hardware’s 32 GB ceiling, we profiled UNet-768 on two A100 80 GB GPUs (Table 1). Running in FP32 (80,599 MiB/GPU) or BF16 (45,091 MiB/GPU) on this hardware eliminates the instability, but at VRAM costs that would still exceed our production 4×V100 32 GB budget. The near-identical VRAM consump- tion of UNet-768 under FP16 (45,037 MiB) and BF16 (45,091 MiB) confirms that the FP16 instability is a numerical precision issue rather than a memory constraint. We therefore adopt UNet-128 and ConvNeXt-512, which remain stable under FP16 at lr=10−3 and achieve superior downstream performance. UNet-128 keeps the same hierarchical, multi-scale aggregation as UNet-768 but shrinks the channel width to 128; that reduction in decoder capacity is enough on its own to keep FP16 pretraining stable at lr=10−3.
