We introduce TDAL to learn a dynamic acquisition function online from a single perturbation screening trajectory. Building on surrogate-estimated hit probabilities, TDAL uses temporal difference learning to incorporate observed discoveries and feedback-weighted successor value, while LBO reconstructs additional training transitions from measured outcomes. By learning how to select experiments from feedback accumulated within the ongoing screen, TDAL adapts candidate selection to both immediate discoveries and subsequent discovery opportunities under limited experimental feedback. Across eight public screens spanning single, double, and triple perturbations, TDAL achieves an average rank of 1.63 among eleven methods, demonstrating consistent discovery performance across diverse response landscapes. Limitations. Our evaluation is retrospective, and the additive acquisition function does not explicitly model interactions among candidates within a batch. Feedback weighting also relies on surrogate-estimated hit probabilities, motivating further investigation of its robustness to prediction errors. Future work will explore batch-aware selection and alternative feedback weighting strategies, alongside prospective validation in closed-loop biological experiments.
