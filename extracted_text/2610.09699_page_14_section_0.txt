The second approach, Restart, resets the CMA-ES using the same parameters as in the initial run (Auger et al., 2004). The initial position x0 can be reused or resampled uniformly from [lb, ub]d. This flexibility is also present in the next two strategies: 1. Increasing Population (IPOP) (Auger and Hansen, 2005): In order to achieve fast convergence on simple problems, the CMA-ES has a relatively small population size compared to traditional ES (Beyer, 2001; Hansen and Ostermeier, 2001). However, on multimodal functions, a larger population size λ is required to solve them effectively (Hansen and Kern, 2004; Lunacek et al., 2008). The IPOP strategy first attempts to optimize with the default small λ, then increases λ by a factor of 2 at each restart. The population size is limited to a maximum of 29λ0, where λ0 denotes the original population size specified by the user. This maintains competitive performance on simple functions that do not require restarts, while improving performance on complex multimodal landscapes in subsequent restarts. 2. Bi-Population (BIPOP) (Hansen, 2009a): BIPOP extends the IPOP concept by alternating between two regimes: one with an increasing population size (as in IPOP), and one with a smaller, randomly parameterized population size. Each regime tracks its own evaluation budget independently. On each restart, the regime with fewer total evaluations is selected. Initially, the CMA-ES is started with standard parameters, and the increasing regime is used for the first restart. The balance between the exploratory, increasing, and exploitative smaller regimes yields a highly effective strategy. The BIPOP CMA-ES is currently one of the top-performing algorithms on BBOB (Hansen et al., 2022). 6.2 Repelling Native restart strategies can be effective for multimodal landscapes but typically ignore information gained from previous runs. In contrast, state-of-the-art evolutionary algorithms for multi-modal optimization (MMO), such as HillValley-EA (Maree et al., 2018) and RS-CMSA (Ahrari et al., 2017), explicitly leverage such historical knowledge. Inspired by these approaches, the Repelling Restarts CMA-ES (de Nobel et al., 2024c) maintains a taboo archive of previously visited basins of attraction and actively repels new candidate solutions away from these regions.
