SST, by contrast, constrains weight update, and as a result can compose with on-policy methods (Table 4). We defer discussion of many other related works to Appendix A. 4.1 Comparison with subspace-protecting methods Two recent methods share our starting point of protecting the top singular directions of pretrained weights while adapting elsewhere. Both differ from SST in the same structural way. They rewrite the update as a product of trained factors and constrain the factors, whereas SST trains the weights directly and constrains the one quantity that reaches them, the applied update. Sculpting [34] freezes the top singular vectors of each weight matrix and projects the gradient before every AdamW step. Its projection removes only block (1) of Eq. 2, the component that reads from and writes onto protected directions at once. The cross blocks (2) and (3), which touch a protected direction on one side, are never removed. The projection moreover acts on the gradient, rather than the update as suggested by our controlled study. Even removing all three blocks from the gradient leaves 69% of the applied update in protected directions (Figure 4). OPLoRA [54] freezes the weights and trains a LoRA adapter, the product BA of two low-rank trainable matrices, sandwiched between fixed projectors, ∆W = QU BA QV in our notation. This is the projection Π of Eq. 3 applied to a low-rank product rather than to the optimizer’s step, and it confines the update fully to the free subspace. The amount protected is set by the projector rank k, and their experiments protect only the top 16-128 directions while adapting at low rank in everything that remains, nearly the inverted regime from SST, which protects 99% of the spectrum and adapts densely in the tail. By the chain rule through the fixed projectors, the gradient components along protected directions never reach the trainable factors, and the applied update is projected again by construction. This corresponds to scheme (3) in Sec. 2.2, which our controlled study found mis-scales the step (Figure 4), with Adam’s step sizes moreover set in factor space rather than for the weight update. Another difference is that neither method considers refreshing the protected subspace. SST introduces refresh as part of the method, re-anchoring the basis every K steps, and studies it as an explicit retention-adaptation control - swept from no refresh to R10 throughout our experiments (Sec. 2.3). We compare both methods on the four-domain setup under their official settings (Table 5). Both retain more capability than SFT but lose 18-26 points of prior capability on average where no-refresh SST loses about one, at comparable in-domain accuracy. Giving OPLoRA our protection level (k=0.99d) recovers much of the gap, yet it still trails SST on three of the four domains (Table 4). Under data scaling across Medicine, Pathology, and FinQA with official settings, both OPLoRA and Sculpting forget substantially more as training continues, while SST stays much closer to the base model (Appendix B.9, Figure 15). We therefore keep SFT and SDFT, the dominant fine-tuning recipes in practice, as the primary baselines throughout the paper, while comparing against Sculpting and OPLoRA in this main setting and on data scaling. 5
