Agentic Reinforcement Learning (Agentic RL; Gao et al., 2025; Jin et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025) trains Large Language Model (LLM) agents (Shinn et al., 2023; Yao et al., 2022) for multi-turn reasoning, tool use, and interaction with external environments via online rollouts and reward feedback, yielding broad performance gains. More recently, LLM agents have incorporated advanced inference techniques, such as context management (Mei et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026a) and harness engineering (Lin et al., 2026; Zhou et al., 2026), enabling agentic workflows to tackle increasingly complex and long-horizon tasks. However, most agentic RL methods assume a single-session setting, where each rollout unfolds as a coherent multi-turn trajectory within a continuously accumulated context and yields a single training segment (Wang et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026b). We argue that the single-session setting is not sufficiently general for optimizing LLM agents. For example, context compaction compresses the execution history to free up context space while rewriting the prefix that conditions subsequent actions (Lu et al., 2025; Nguyen et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025), thereby initiating a new session. Similarly, subagent forking delegates global goal to subagents which creates branch sessions (Song et al., 2025; Team et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026d). Those resulting trajectories no longer satisfy the single-session assumption, creating a mismatch between training and inference that compromises consistency and limits optimization potential.
