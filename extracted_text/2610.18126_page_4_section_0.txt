Routing uses no model calls; the model is called again only for decisions that receive news. What the entry indexes sets what the router can reach. Routing sets ρ and the items each decision sees; judgment sets S, L and K (Appendix A derives Eq. 3 and the trade-off between routers it implies). A routing failure leaves a decision stale; a judg- ment failure ignores decisive news or abandons a decision that should stand. A decisive item reaches the chosen option in one of four ways: it names the option (Lovage Corner), the dependency stated in the evidence (the Bellhaven district), another name for that dependency (Chime Green, as the district is known locally), or an entity upstream of it (the Gorran substation, which supplies the dis- trict). Each step down this list moves the link from the decision’s text into background knowledge. Predictions. The theory makes four predictions. P1: without post-decision routing (ρ = 0, L = 0, K = 1), accuracy equals 1 −π, the share of de- cisions that should stand, for every model. P2: delivery is sufficient for change (S ≈1), so the accuracy of changing decisions tracks ρ, and ρ is set by whether the router’s index contains the entity a decisive item names. P3: each misleading item delivered lowers K at a rate set by the model’s judg- ment, so routers trade recall against exposure, and broadcasting, which maximises both, helps only models that judge well. P4: with oracle routing (ρ = 1, every related item and nothing else), the residual error concentrates on temporal judgment: deciding whether Ij overlaps the activity. 4 The Decision Ledger Each ledger entry is persistent state that outlives the session in which the decision was made (Bakkali and Merimi, 2026). When the model decides at td, it writes the entry: the chosen option and its alter- natives, the action time ta, the revision deadline tr, the evidence about the chosen option, and a dossier of background facts, such as other names of the en- tities the option depends on and what those entities rely on in turn. In deployment, the dossier holds what the assistant looked up while deciding, in- cluding much that is irrelevant to the user’s activity. When an item xj is published, a router scores the open entries and sends the item to the K highest- scoring ones. At the revision deadline, the model reads the entry and the routed items in order of publication and keeps or switches. Decisions that receive no items keep their choice without a model call. Routers differ in what they index, which by P2 sets what they can reach. Decision text. BM25 or dense embeddings com- pare the item with the decision’s request and its evidence about the chosen option, reaching news that names the option or its stated dependency. Whole dossier. The decision text is expanded with every dossier fact, reaching news about other names and upstream entities together with news about everything else the dossier mentions. Watch conditions. At td, the model reads the entry and writes three to eight conditions wi1, . . . , wik describing events that would stop the user’s activity. The conditions name every entity the activity depends on, including other names and upstream entities, and leave out what the user does not need (Appendix C). The router in- dexes the decision text with the conditions (BM25) or scores each decision by its best condition, maxk cos(e(xj), e(wik)), with a sentence encoder e. Watch conditions are document expansion (Nogueira et al., 2019) performed once, at write time, by the component that knows why the option was chosen; by P3 they should match the dossier’s reach with less exposure. Time filter. A rule drops an item for a decision when the item states a start time after the deci- sion’s activity ends, performing by rule the tempo- ral check of P4. 5 Controlled Decision Streams Testing the predictions requires exact control over which items are decisive, how they refer to the option, and when changes take effect.
