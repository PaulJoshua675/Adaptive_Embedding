The refiner reconstructs its scene representation from proposal coordinates, confidence scores, and original scene inputs; it does not require the proposal backbone’s exported embed- dings, queries, or intermediate activations. 2.3. Marginal and Joint Motion Forecasting Marginal forecasting predicts each agent’s future distri- bution independently, whereas joint forecasting represents scene-level hypotheses whose agent trajectories are mu- tually consistent. Many high-performing forecasters fo- cus on marginal prediction [28, 33], while FJMP [17], Fu- tureNet [25] explicitly model multi-agent futures. This dis- tinction constrains relational refinement: equally indexed modes from independent marginal predictions need not de- scribe the same joint world, and connecting them can there- fore introduce artificial dependencies. FutureLift preserves this distinction through conditional graph construction. Marginal proposals are refined with- out cross-agent mode correspondence, whereas scene- consistent joint hypotheses additionally receive social fu- ture edges. This design supports both settings without im- posing joint semantics on marginal predictions. 3. Method FutureLift is a post-hoc refinement module that turns tra- jectory proposals into an explicit hypothesis of the future scene. Its central operation is lifting: rather than compress- ing a complete proposal into one latent token, we instanti- ate its temporal segments as spatially anchored graph nodes. This representation makes the proposal geometry available to map, history, modal, and social reasoning. A single bidi- rectional graph update then predicts residual corrections to the proposal coordinates and confidence scores. We first formalize the proposal interface, then describe proposal-to- graph lifting, one-shot refinement, and the training objec- tive. 3.1. Problem Formulation and Proposal Interface Let a scene contain observed agent histories H, an HD map M, and A agents to be predicted over T future steps. A proposal backbone B produces K candidate trajectories and their logits, ( bY, ℓ) = B(H, M), bY ∈RA×K×T ×2. (1) For marginal forecasting, k indexes an agent-specific mode and ℓa,k is an agent-level logit. For joint forecasting, k in- dexes a coherent scene-level future shared by all predicted agents and ℓk is a scene logit. FutureLift accesses only bY, ℓ, and the original scene description; it does not consume backbone queries, decoder features, or intermediate activa- tions. We therefore treat B as frozen and stop gradients at the interface in Eq. (1). This contract permits the same refiner architecture to operate on structurally different pro- posal generators after setting-specific training. FutureLift outputs coordinate and logit residuals, eY = bY + ∆Y, eℓ= ℓ+ ∆ℓ, (2) which preserves the backbone’s modes while allowing their geometry and ranking to change. The residual parameteri- zation is important for a reusable refiner: the proposal re- mains the reference solution, and FutureLift learns only the correction supported by the lifted scene context. 3.2. Scene nodes and relative geometry. The lifted future nodes are combined with a heterogeneous scene graph con- taining historical agent states, lane elements, and fine- grained map points. FutureLift uses the QCNet scene en- coder [33] to encode this context. The encoder is trained as part of FutureLift from the original scene inputs; Future- Lift does not reuse encoder activations or other latent fea- tures from the frozen proposal backbone.
