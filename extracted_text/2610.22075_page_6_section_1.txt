Benchmarks. We evaluate the models on the following nine benchmarks: GQA (Hudson & Man- ning, 2019), POPE (Li et al., 2023b), TextVQA (TVQA) (Singh et al., 2019), and ScienceQA-IMG (SQA) (Lu et al., 2022), MMBench (MMB) (Liu et al., 2024), RealWorldQA (RWQA) (xAI, 2024), SEED-Bench (SEED) (Li et al., 2023a), AI2D (Kembhavi et al., 2016), and the 2D split of CV- Bench (CV2D) (Tong et al., 2024). We use the VLMEvalKit (Duan et al., 2024) library for all model evaluations. We define the Avg metric as the arithmetic mean of the nine VQA benchmarks. We set a fixed eval seed to make evaluation deterministic. 5.1 COMPARISON TO BASELINES Baselines. We evaluate the proposed recurrence retrofitting approach against five baselines and report the results in Table 1. The first baseline is the pretrained TinyLLaVA-1.4B checkpoint without any modifications. The second and third baselines fine-tune this model without pruning, either with the image-text stages S1 and S2 only or with all three stages S0, S1 and S2 of our training strategy. The fourth baseline applies all three stages to the pruned model without recurrence, which corresponds to the single-pass model F 1 of Sec. 3.1. The baselines with the text-only stage apply the distillation term of Eq. 4 in S0, and the unpruned Baseline+(S0, S1, S2) also applies it in S2, so that it is trained with the full strategy of our model. We include those retrained versions to allow a direct comparison between the retrofitted recurrent model and the same non-recurrent backbones trained on the same data. To allow a comparison to a setup that starts from a recurrent language model and adds the vision backbone after recurrent training, we convert a pretrained recurrent TinyLlama checkpoint of McLeish et al. (2025) into a recurrent VLM by adding the pretrained image encoder from TinyLLaVA-1.4B and train the model with the same image-text data on the training stages S1 and S2. For our retrofitted recurrent VLM (RR-VLM), we use a fixed recurrence of r=6 and report results for an 8-layer recurrent block architecture (F 6 4/8/4). We see that the training improves the baseline models by around 2.4 to 2.9 pp, while the performance of the pruned model is slightly below that of the base model. The performance of the converted recurrent VLM is in the same range as the retrained baselines. Compared to that, the proposed RR-VLM model is able to outperform all baseline models on average by at least 2.3 pp. Analyzing the performance per dataset, we see that more basic visual understanding datasets, such as GQA, POPE, TVQA and SQA, do not profit as much from the recurrent architecture, and that the RR-VLM performs on par with the baseline model. This can be attributed to the fact that those datasets usually have single-step QA type questions, which do not profit from more complex reasoning. Recurrence adds computation without adding information, so no gain is expected on such questions. Compared to that, we see a clear trend of performance increase on more reasoning-oriented datasets such as MMBench, RealWorldQA, SEED-Bench, AI2D and CV2D. Note that on the latter four datasets, both recurrent models, the converted VLM as well as the proposed RR-VLM model, per- form consistently better than the non-recurrent baselines, with RR-VLM still outperforming the post hoc converted VLM on three of the four datasets. We thus find that retrofitting a pretrained VLM results in better performance than introducing a vision encoder into a recurrent language model to create a recurrent VLM.
