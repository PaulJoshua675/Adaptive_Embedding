Flag when the model: ■References a prior patient detail that is not in the clinical memory or discussed in the conversation. ■Misremembers the patient’s goal, concern, relationship, activity, or preference. ■Presents an inference as if it were established history. ■Says “you mentioned...” when the patient did not mention it. Do not flag when the model: ■Accurately reflects prior information. ■Uses tentative language for an inference. ■Asks for confirmation rather than asserting memory. ■Summarizes context that is clearly present in the transcript. ■The reference seems questionable but cannot be verified as unsupported from the available context. 8. Under-guiding Definition: This issue tends to show up when the model acknowledges the patient’s concern or goal but does not provide enough active, concrete guidance when guidance is clinically indicated. A missed opportunity is clinically meaningful enough to tag when the patient actively surfaced the need (rather than mentioning it in passing) and a concrete, available form of support clearly fit the moment. Under-guiding occurs when the model fails to orient the patient to what is happening next, explain why it is asking a new line of questions, or articulate the next step when guidance is required. In these cases, the issue is not that the model failed to recommend an activity, but that it left the patient without enough structure to understand the purpose or direction of the interaction. Under-guiding can co-occur with Over-exploring. Use both labels when the model continues unneces- sary questioning and also misses a clear opportunity to provide concrete support. Use Under-guiding alone when the main issue is the absence of structure, direction, or next-step support, even if the model is not repeatedly questioning. This may show up in the following forms: ■Asking the patient to identify next steps or how to approach addressing their need/challenge without collaborative guidance (e.g., “what do you think you should do to improve your sleep?”) ■Develop a future oriented goal with a patient, but not help them plan for when and how they will be successful in performing it (e.g., “ok, try the breathing exercise whenever you like later and let me know how it goes”) Flag when the model: ■Responds with questions or generic encouragement instead of helping the patient move forward. ■Leaves the patient to figure out the next step alone. ■Says “try that sometime” without shaping a realistic plan. ■Misses an opportunity to translate the patient’s goal/concern into action, skill use, or next-step sup- port. ■Offers the patient well wishes with a symptom or issue in place of guidance ■Continues a pattern of insufficient support across later turns after the missed opportunity has be- come clear. Do not flag when the model: ■Offers a warm well-wish to close a topic that was already worked through, or in response to a passing mention the patient is not raising as a current problem. ■The patient is not seeking guidance and needs validation or exploration first. ■The model offers a specific next step, skill, plan, or recommendation. ■The model appropriately asks one clarifying question needed before guidance. ■The model is intentionally slowing down because of emotional intensity, safety, or ambiguity. 24
