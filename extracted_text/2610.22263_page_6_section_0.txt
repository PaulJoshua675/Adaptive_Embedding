One can see from (11) that ˆJ(θ) is affine in πθ. Informally, ˆJ can be viewed as a linear approximation of J in the policy space, up to an additive constant. The relationship between ˆJ and ˆV0 reveals why ˆJ is a useful surrogate objective to optimize. Because ˆJ(θ) and ˆV0(πθ; ˆπ) differ only by a constant independent of θ, maximizing ˆJ(θ) is equivalent to maximizing ˆV0(πθ; ˆπ). If πθ improves over ˆπ in ˆV0(·; ˆπ), i.e., ˆV0(πθ; ˆπ) > ˆV0(ˆπ; ˆπ) = V0(ˆπ), this implies DV0(ˆπ) · (πθ −ˆπ) > 0, indicating that πθ −ˆπ is an ascent direction in the policy space. When πθ is sufficiently close to ˆπ, it follows that V0(πθ) > V0(ˆπ), i.e., πθ improves over ˆπ in the true objective. In the constrained version of trust-region policy optimization (TRPO) (Schulman et al., 2015), this condition is enforced by a trust-region constraint that restricts the divergence Dmax KL (πθ ∥ˆπ).
