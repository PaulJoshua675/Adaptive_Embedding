In general, the CMA-ES generates λ offspring every generation, and uses the µ best individuals to perform weighted intermediate recombination (see line 11 in Algorithm 1). 4.1 Elitism The elitism module determines whether CMA-ES uses (µ + λ) selection instead of the default (µ, λ) scheme. In (µ + λ) selection, both the µ parent individuals, i.e., the µ selected individuals from the previous generation, and the newly generated λ offspring are considered during selection (Igel et al., 2007). Elitism ensures that the best solution found so far is preserved, thereby improving local convergence. However, in real-valued evolution strategies, non-elitist selection is generally preferred because it exhibits stronger exploratory behavior (Schwefel, 1987; Beyer, 2001). Additionally, solutions sampled in a previous generation must be appropriately transformed for the parameter update to work. This is achieved via the same procedure as with boundary correction, where the values y′ i and z′ i are recomputed from an elitist xi (see Equation 1). 4.2 Recombination Weights This module determines how the recombination weights w ∈Rµ are generated. The default option generates w according to the procedure defined in Hansen (2016), where the weights wi decrease logarithmically with the rank of the solution. ModCMA provides two alternative options. The first is to use equal weights, wi = 1/µ for i = 1, 2, . . . , µ, effectively performing intermediate recombination without weighting. The second option introduces exponentially decreasing weights, using wi = 1/2i + 1/(µ2µ) for i = 1, 2, . . . , µ. When active update (see Section 5.3) is enabled, we also require weights for the λ −µ individuals that have not been selected. In general, these weights are generated by the same procedure as the positive weights, sequentially reversed and with their sign inverted, i.e. w−= (−wµ, −wµ−1, . . . , −w1). For example, for equal weights, assuming µ = λ/2, the complete weight vector equals [w; −w].1 4.3 Sequential Selection In standard CMA-ES, each generation samples and evaluates a fixed number λ of candidate solutions.
