Household manipulation has been a long-standing goal in robotics and embodied AI, with the promise of improving daily life by enabling robots to perform chores from natural language instructions (e.g., “put an apple, bread, and milk on my plate”). Recent progress in generalist robotic manipulation has been driven in large part by Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models [1, 2, 3, 4], which show strong generalization across diverse multimodal inputs [5, 6] and heterogeneous embodiments [7, 8]. However, real-world deployment demands more than completing the nominal task: embodied agents must also execute tasks in ways that align with the preferences, habits, and constraints of individual users, a requirement we refer to as personalization. Preferences introduce diverse execution requirements for identical goals. For instance, placing a mug might require a specific handle orientation or a designated landing spot. Consequently, personalized manipulation must satisfy user-specific constraints on both what strategies are selected and how actions are performed.
