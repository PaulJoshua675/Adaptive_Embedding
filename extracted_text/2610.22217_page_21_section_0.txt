A.8 DETAILS OF EXPERT-RETENTION ANALYSIS We provide the computation protocol for the expert-retention analysis in Figure 3a. For each ca- pability, the independently trained capability expert without additional skill conditioning is used as the reference. All comparisons are performed using fixed-trajectory teacher forcing so that different unified models are evaluated under identical response states. For each input xi, the base policy πbase greedily generates a trajectory τi = (τi,1, . . . , τi,Ti), hi,t = (xi, τi,<t), (23) and all compared policies are evaluated on the same states {hi,t}Ti t=1. This avoids confounding functional differences with changes in generated trajectories. Functional Expert Retention. For any policy π, we represent its functional deviation from the base policy along τi using the observed-token log-probability changes: a(i) π = (log π(τi,t | hi,t) −log πbase(τi,t | hi,t))Ti t=1 . (24) Let a(i) c denote the vector produced by the corresponding capability expert πθc, and let a(i) θ denote that of a unified model πθ. We measure directional agreement with the capability expert using cosine similarity: Ci(θ, c) = D a(i) θ , a(i) c E a(i) θ 2 a(i) c 2 . (25) A larger Ci indicates that the functional change induced by the unified model is more closely aligned with that of the corresponding capability expert. We further measure the projected amplitude retained along the expert direction: Ri(θ, c) = D a(i) θ , a(i) c E a(i) c 2 2 . (26) Here, Ri = 1 indicates that the unified model preserves the same projected magnitude as the expert, 0 < Ri < 1 indicates attenuation, Ri > 1 indicates amplification, and Ri < 0 indicates an opposing component. Both metrics are first averaged over samples within each capability and then macro-averaged across the four capabilities to obtain the results reported in Figure 3a. These metrics quantify functional agreement with independently trained capability experts under identical trajectory states rather than directly measuring downstream task accuracy. 21
