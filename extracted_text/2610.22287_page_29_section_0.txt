F Extended Literature Review Probabilistic Strategic Logics and Synthesis Tools. Hahn et al. [25] translate reachability-normal patl objectives into rewards for Min- imax Q-Learning. Their model-checking procedure recursively re- labels states for nested modalities, rather than finding one strategy witnessing all subformulae. It considers a single coalition in turn- based games. Bozkurt et al. [8] similarly study model-free learning for ltl objectives in two-player turn-based zero-sum games, using deterministic Rabin automata. The zero-sum fragments of PRISM-Games [31] synthesise strategies for probabilistic and reward-based rpatl objectives from known transition models. Single- and multi-objective properties concern one coalition against its complement; where optimal strategies need not exist [12], the tool computes 𝜀-approximations of Pareto-optimal strategies. Equilibrium-based extensions synthesise subgame-perfect, social-welfare-optimal Nash equilibria in concurrent stochastic games [32–34], also assuming known models. Varricchione et al. [53] construct reward machines from atl specifications for a fully cooperative grand coalition. Rational Synthesis. Rational synthesis [20, 52] considers agents pursuing their own temporal goals. Fisman et al. [20] reduce ltl rational synthesis to satisfiability in Extended Strategy Logic, ob- taining 2EXPTIME-completeness for dominant-strategy, Nash and subgame-perfect solution concepts. Ummels [52] study pure strategies in infinite multiplayer turn-based games with 𝜔-regular objectives, establishing exis- tence, payoff-constrained decision procedures and finite-state implementations for subgame-perfect Nash equilibria. Condurache et al. [14] study complexity refinements and cooperative and non-cooperative variants. Our setting combines per-agent probabilistic ltl𝑓requirements with learning from unknown dynamics under a single committed profile. Policy Space Response Oracles [35] instead train approxi- mate best responses against a meta-strategy over a policy popula- tion. Rational Verification. Rational verification [58] asks whether a temporal property holds on some or all equilibrium behaviours. Equilibrium Logic expresses such properties through modalities [NE] and ⟨NE⟩over Nash-equilibrium computations. Model check- ing is 2EXPTIME-hard in general, with matching upper bounds for restricted fragments. Commitment-aware Logics. Strategy Logic (sl) explicitly quanti- fies and binds strategies, subsumes atl⋆, and can express equilib- rium through stability against deviations. Model checking for full sl is decidable but non-elementary. Extensions of atl evaluate nested formulas under previously selected strategies, including strategy contexts (atlsc) [15], explicit strategies (atles) [55], and irrevocable semantics [2].
