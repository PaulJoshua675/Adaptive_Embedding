Not Just a Subject: Capturing Adaptive Scene-Specific Variance for Visual Storytelling SeungJu Cha1 , Ye-Chan Kim1 , Kwanyoung Lee1 , and Dong-Jin Kim1 Hanyang University, Seoul 04763, South Korea {sju9020, dpcksdl78, mobled37, djdkim}@hanyang.ac.kr § https://github.com/SeungJuCha/EOS-Story Abstract. Recent advancements in diffusion models have significantly improved text-to-image generation, enabling their application to visual storytelling, where coherent image sequences are generated from nar- rative prompts. However, existing methods primarily focus on identity preservation and often fail to adapt to diverse scene variations, resulting in semantic misalignment and structural inflexibility. We propose EOS- Story, a training-free framework that harmonizes identity consistency with scene-specific adaptation across text and vision spaces. In the text space, we decouple the narrative into identity and scene sub-prompts to prevent information dilution, but this leaves the scene without access to appearance cues. To bridge this gap, we introduce Cross-Contextual Bridging, which softly injects a phrase-level appearance residual into the scene’s ⟨EOS⟩embedding. In the vision space, we find that scale mis- match between identity and scene subjects causes a few identity tokens to be repeatedly transferred, producing structural artifacts. We address this with Adaptive Identity Preservation, which anchors identity fea- tures at the scene-specific scale (Identity Anchoring) and integrates them via Identity-Aware Self-Attention. To evaluate performance under varied spatial compositions, we extend the Consistory+ benchmark with di- verse framing scenarios (Consistory+_+). Extensive experiments show that EOS-Story achieves competitive or superior performance on both benchmarks. Keywords: Storytelling · Text-to-Image Generation 1
