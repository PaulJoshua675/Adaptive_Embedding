Web search allows LLMs to access up-to- date information, but it remains unclear why they sometimes produce outdated answers even when relevant evidence is available. To study this problem, we construct temporal facts whose values changed after the mod- els’ training cutoffs. We evaluate one propri- etary model under deployed web search and six open-weight model configurations under controlled retrieval. We find that models of- ten answer from search snippets without open- ing the source pages, which is associated with higher rates of outdated answers and unfaith- ful citations. Our controlled experiments and layer-wise analysis reveal a gap between the availability of evidence and its effective use. Even when retrieved evidence influences inter- nal predictions, models may still favour out- dated information in their final answers. We hope these findings will help advance research on the reliable use of retrieved evidence in search-augmented LLMs. 1
