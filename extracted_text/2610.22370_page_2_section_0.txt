However, as shown in Fig. 1(a), CFG- based merging tends to underprotect non-prompt regions of the image, losing fine details and structural coherence. We attribute this to CFG’s prompt-centric bias: by focusing exclusively on text-conditioned influence, CFG overlooks two key aspects of how diffusion models generate images. First, CFG fails to capture time-varying token importance. Diffusion generation follows a coarse-to- fine progression [15, 16], where tokens undergoing large inter-step changes represent regions in which the model is actively injecting details [17]. In this context, prematurely merging such still-evolving tokens directly attenuates their ongoing refinement by averaging their features with neighboring tokens. Crucially, the spatial distribution of these actively evolving tokens is time-varying: while it remains concentrated on foreground objects in early steps, it progressively expands toward broader spatial regions, including non-prompt areas, at later timesteps (Fig. 1(b)). High-ranked tokens in CFG importance signal, however, remain anchored to foreground objects throughout the entire timestep, confining its search for important tokens only to prompt-aligned regions. As a result, CFG fails to identify the very tokens that should be protected: actively evolving tokens outside the foreground are prematurely merged into nearby or similar tokens, suppressing their distinct updates. Second, CFG is insensitive to prompt-independent structural importance. To produce natural and coherent images, diffusion models actively generate scene elements that are not directly mentioned in the prompt such as background objects and supporting structures [1, 2]. Although these prompt- independent structures contribute little to text guidance, they carry essential structural information such as boundaries between homogeneous regions, edges of background objects, and fine textures that anchor visual coherence. However, because CFG only measures text-conditioned discrepancy, structural boundaries in such non-prompt regions tend to receive low scores across all timesteps (Fig. 1(c)), making them vulnerable to merging into neighboring homogeneous areas. Taken together, these observations indicate that token importance should not be defined solely by text relevance, but by diffusion dynamics: how unresolved a token remains and how much structural information it carries at the current denoising step [18]. To faithfully capture diffusion dynamics, we define two complementary signals that quantify the spatiotemporal dynamics of each token during denoising. For temporal dynamics, we start from the score function [19], which quantifies how each token is updated at the current denoising step. In particular, we project this score into clean-image space via the Tweedie denoised estimate [20, 21], and use its inter-step difference as the temporal signal, assigning high values to tokens that are unresolved. Unlike CFG, this score-derived signal reflects the actual update applied to every token, regardless of prompt relevance, providing an image-wide view of generative activity (Fig. 1(b)). For the structural signal, we measure the residual between the Tweedie estimate and its coarse-scale smoothed version, which acts as a high-pass filter highlighting structural components throughout the image.
