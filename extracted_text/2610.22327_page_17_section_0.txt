We next train the STFT-based alternative under the full training setup and compare it with the proposed delay-based model. As shown in Table 12, neither representa- tion uniformly dominates across all evaluations. STFT is slightly stronger on ChatTS numerical alignment and the ChatTS reasoning average, whereas the delay-based model is slightly stronger on TSExam and substantially stronger on the broader TSRBench evaluation. On ChatTS categorical alignment, the two models are effectively tied. These results suggest that the benefit of complemen- tary views is not tied to uniform superiority of the delay representation on every task; rather, the choice of secondary representation changes the inductive bias available to the language model. C.6 VISUAL-TOKEN BUDGET AND SAME-VIEW CONTROLS For each input series, the line-plot representation is encoded by the native Qwen3-VL vision encoder into 230 visual tokens, while the delay representation contributes 64 visual tokens, for a total of 294 visual tokens per series in the full model. The Plot+Plot and Delay+Delay controls preserve this budget and the complete dual-tower architecture, changing only the input to one tower. In Plot+Plot, the DINOv3 tower receives the line plot, rasterized at 256 × 256 px, in place of the delay map, yielding 64 tokens.
