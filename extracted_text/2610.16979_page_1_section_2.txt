As illustrated in Figure 1, modern industrial search systems typically organize candidate generation, pre-ranking, and ranking as a multi- stage cascade. Such systems maintain multiple retrieval channels and pass their merged candidate set through downstream ranking stages, increasing both serving complexity and computational cost. Generative retrieval (GR) offers an alternative paradigm by rep- resenting each item as a sequence of discrete tokens, commonly referred to as a Semantic ID (SID), and directly generating candi- date SIDs conditioned on the query and user context [1, 25, 29, 33]. Realizing this potential in large- scale industrial search system, however, depends on learning be- haviorally informative SIDs and aligning the policy model with observed user feedback in a robust and transferable manner. The first challenge lies in constructing SIDs that preserve collab- orative semantics while maintaining effective codebook utilization. Existing quantization methods mainly follow two paradigms. RQ- VAE-based methods support end-to-end learning and allow task- specific supervision to directly shape SID construction, but discrete assignment and successive residual decomposition can produce imbalanced codeword usage and codebook collapse [25, 31]. RQ- Kmeans-based methods provide more explicit control over cluster assignments and can incorporate balancing strategies or alterna- tive quantizers [2, 9]; however, collaborative and business-specific signals are usually encoded only in upstream item representations and may be attenuated or distorted during multi-level residual quantization. Consequently, existing paradigms offer complemen- tary strengths but do not fully reconcile collaborative-information preservation with stable codebook utilization, limiting the repre- sentational capacity of the resulting SID space. The second challenge lies in aligning the generative policy with user preferences. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) maximizes the like- lihood of observed positive SIDs but does not explicitly distinguish the relative value of generated candidates. On-policy reinforcement learning (RL) facilitates preference optimization by leveraging re- ward signals from user interactions [12, 37]. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a widely used optimizer that learns from the relative advantages of multiple sampled trajectories. However, when using outcome-based rewards from clicks and orders, GRPO requires generated SIDs to exactly match positive items, resulting in severe reward sparsity and insufficient learning signals [35].
