TransX: Technical Report • TransX-GenRet. Generative retrieval retains the shared TransX behavioral memory but replaces the discriminative readout with an autoregressive decoder over item semantic IDs (SIDs). At decoding step 𝑘, the evolving generative query cross-attends to the same reader memory, allowing the projected memory KV to be reused throughout generation. The decoder predicts one SID token per step until a complete item identifier is produced. We construct SIDs hierarchically from pretrained item embeddings using balanced 𝑘-means. The first three levels each use a vocabulary of size 2,048; a fourth hash-based token is appended to disambiguate items that collide under the three-level semantic code. The autoregressive decoder is trained independently rather than initialized from the TransX-Base candidate readout. • TransX-GenRank. Generative ranking first computes the candidate-conditioned TransX represen- tations and base relevance logits for the complete candidate set. These representations are reused throughout slate generation. At decoding step 𝑘, a lightweight autoregressive slate decoder summarizes the previously selected items into a position-conditioned slate state ℎ𝑘. Each remaining candidate is scored by adding a slate-conditioned residual to its base relevance logit, 𝑠𝑘,𝑖= 𝑏𝑖+ (𝑊𝑞ℎ𝑘)⊤(𝑊𝑐𝑧𝑖) √𝑑slate , where 𝑧𝑖is the precomputed candidate-conditioned TransX representation. Previously selected items are masked from subsequent steps. This formulation preserves the strong pointwise relevance learned by TransX-Base while concentrating the autoregressive capacity on dependencies among slate selections. Candidate representations and behavioral memory are computed once and reused across the maximum eight decoding steps. Post-training settings. TransX-GenRet and TransX-GenRank analysis. Figure 10 shows that both generative specializations benefit from the TransX-Base backbone. For TransX-GenRet, post-training converges faster and consistently improves held-out preference quality, Recall@𝐾, NDCG@𝐾, valid-SID rate, and generation diversity relative to matched training from random initialization. TransX-GenRank shows the same pattern, with faster DPO convergence and stronger held-out preference separation. Together, these results indicate that the behavioral representation learned by TransX-Base transfers effectively to both autoregressive retrieval and slate generation, allowing post-training to focus on task-specific decoding rather than relearning user behavior from scratch. Despite effective DPO optimization, the two generative specializations exhibit a consistent trade-off relative to their discriminative counterparts: greater structured-output expressiveness comes with higher sensitivity to representation design, supervision quality, and autoregressive decoding. • Where TransX-GenRet improves over TransX-Retrieval, the gains are concentrated among long-tail users and items, suggesting that hierarchical SID decoding can capture item structure not fully expressed by a single embedding-space similarity. However, invalid SIDs still occur after convergence and directly reduce Recall@𝐾without constrained decoding; generated results are also more concentrated on popular items. • TransX-GenRank is broadly comparable on average with the conventional learning-to-rank variant, but exhibits higher variance and weaker quality at some later slate positions as early autoregressive decisions propagate forward. Its performance is also more sensitive to the coverage and fidelity of slate-level preference supervision, with additional risk from exposure bias and overfitting to teacher or preference signals. 72
