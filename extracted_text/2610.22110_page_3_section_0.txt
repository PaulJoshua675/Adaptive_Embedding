Single-Variant Specialization for Masewalkopa 3 In this paper, we develop a single-variety approach to neural machine trans- lation (NMT) for Masewalkopa, using a relatively small community dataset to specialize an existing multi-variety model. By combining parameter-efficient LoRA fine-tuning with rule-based finite-state morphology and authentic target-side oral narratives, we show that targeted specialization resolves variant fragmentation while respecting the linguistic autonomy of Santa Ana Tlacotenco speech. 1.2 Core Contributions This paper makes four contributions: 1. Single-Variant Specialization Pipeline: We specialize a 1.2-billion-parameter NMT model with 2,665 Masewalkopa pairs, restricting short dictionary en- tries to training so that development and test benchmarks evaluate only full sentence translation. 2. Morphosyntactic NMT Conditioning: We expand a finite-state analyzer beyond 6,500 lexicon lines to derive surface-validated functional syntax prefixes, supplying the neural translation encoder with structural grounding to resolve morphological and lexical ambiguities. 3. Ablation Suite and Post-Mortems: We ablate asymmetric backtranslation, cold special tokens, sparse versus dense syntax, and syntax stripping in the final lora_v9c experiment. 4. Decolonial and Production Evaluation: We combine chrF++, linguis- tic diagnostics, DBS, human comparison, and measured CPU deployment behavior. Together, these experiments demonstrate that targeted single-variant spe- cialization overcomes the pitfalls of multi-variant data aggregation. The final specialized model (lora_v9c_b) achieves 58.46 dev chrF++ and 58.20/57.90 direc- tional test chrF++, gaining over 28 points over the mixed-variant base. Ablation results show that plain-text syntactic prefixes successfully recover from the severe optimization stalls of frozen special tokens, while dense morphological tagging yields measurable structural gains (+1.33 test chrF++). Culturally, decolonial DBS probes and human A/B ratings confirm a strong reduction in colonial Span- ish substitutions and improved naturalness on elder-transcribed oral narratives. Finally, 8-bit quantization and widened beam search reduce inference latency to under 2.6 seconds on standard CPU hardware, demonstrating that single-variant models can be operated within strict community budget constraints. 2
