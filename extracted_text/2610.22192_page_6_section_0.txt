Long-Reward uses SPR; fixed-trajectory rescoring isolates reward allocation rather than the training effects of reward, critic, or optimizer changes. 3 Implementation and Empirical Results 3.1 Infrastructure and phase-adaptive training configuration Phase-adaptive training safeguards inherited value baselines against policy-drift shock by decou- pling actor learning scales from critic recalibration. Backbone and asynchronous infrastructure. We instantiate Terminal-RSI on Qwen3.8-27B with a 262,144-token model context capacity, adopting T1’s slime coordination, Megatron optimization, SGLang sampling, and Harbor-based execution [12, 38, 46, 55, 56]. Rollouts overlap optimization in a one-rollout- batch-ahead pipeline, corresponding to the step-wise off-policy setting of one. This setting does not fix per-token lag: realized mismatch combines policy drift and training–inference differences [47]. Training data. Our 3,000-task pool combines 2,400 semantic-verifier tasks and 600 supplementary terminal tasks. Scientific Workflow Reconstruction (SWR) supplies execution-grounded supervision through hidden semantic checks on reconstructed scientific programs [20]. SWR100 has no task overlap with this training pool. Semantic grading resolves partial progress at verification, not necessarily after every action. Stage-specific hyperparameter curriculum. We adapt refinement to each stage’s starting policy (Table 1). RPR-1 uses actor rate 5 × 10−7 and critic rate 1.5× 10−5, updating values on a faster optimization scale. After RSR-2, RPR-2 reduces the actor rate fivefold to 1 × 10−7 while retaining the critic rate; both actor schedules are constant within phase. The smaller actor rate is intended to limit disruptive changes while the inherited critic recalibrates. This schedule accompanies FDR in RPR-1 and SPR in the retained Long-Reward RPR-2 branch; ordinary RPR-2 retains FDR. Critic preparation and phase-local calibration follow Section 2.1, building on value-initialization precedents [46, 49, 50].
