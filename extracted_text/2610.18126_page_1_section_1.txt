Freshness shapes an LLM decision at the mo- ment it is made; the action it commits to is la- tent, carried out later, in a changing world. We therefore introduce a timestamp theory of deci- sion validity and a decision ledger that realises it. Six timestamps define a decision; a fresh- ness ceiling bounds what up-to-date evidence guarantees at action time, placing the remain- ing risk in information published afterwards. Accuracy then factorises into two capabilities: routing, which delivers that information to af- fected decisions before their revision deadlines, and temporal judgment, which reads it for the time of action. The ledger is persistent state holding each decision’s times, evidence and background knowledge, indexed by watch con- ditions written at decision time. Controlled decision streams with exact labels confirm four predictions across five LLMs, from 8B to fron- tier scale: freshness alone yields exactly the accuracy the ceiling predicts; models act on de- cisive news once it arrives, so accuracy follows routing; the ledger’s index sets what routing reaches, and watch conditions reach indirect dependencies at low exposure; and the remain- ing errors concern when a change takes effect. Freshness makes a decision right when it is made; routing and temporal judgment keep it right when it is carried out. 1
