A.5 SKILL CONSTRUCTION DETAILS We provide additional implementation details for the capability-wise skill abstraction procedure introduced in Sec. 3.2. Skill construction uses only training data and training-set rollouts, while the held-out evaluation set is used exclusively for final evaluation. For each capability, a single skill is constructed from the trajectories generated by its corresponding optimized expert. Trajectory Curation. For each training instance, the optimized capability expert samples multi- ple rollout trajectories, each of which is evaluated by the corresponding capability-specific reward function. To reduce redundancy, we deduplicate repeated trajectories within each task subtype and balance the retained trajectories across subtypes so that high-resource tasks do not dominate the subsequent skill construction process. Reward-Based Trajectory Grouping. Following Sec. 3.2, the curated trajectories are divided into successful and failed cohorts according to their capability-specific rewards. This grouping provides complementary evidence about effective solution patterns and recurring failure modes. Trajectory-Level Reflection. The optimized capability expert performs trajectory-level reflection over the successful and failed cohorts using outcome-specific reflection prompts. Successful tra- jectories are used to identify reusable solution strategies and verification behaviors, while failed trajectories are analyzed to diagnose recurring failure mechanisms and derive corrective guidance. The resulting reflections are aggregated into a capability-specific evidence ledger containing both effective behaviors and failure-avoidance guidance. Capability Skill Synthesis. Based on the aggregated trajectory-derived evidence, the same capa- bility expert synthesizes a single capability-level skill containing reusable procedural guidance for the corresponding capability. The resulting skill is subsequently used to condition the expert during skill-decoupled on-policy expert distillation. This procedure produces one skill for each of the four capabilities. Detailed reflection and skill-induction prompts are provided in Appendix A.12. Ablation of Trajectory Sources. Table 7 examines the contribution of successful and failed tra- jectories to skill construction. Using either successful or failed trajectories alone improves the cor- responding capability experts, while combining both sources achieves the strongest overall perfor- mance.
