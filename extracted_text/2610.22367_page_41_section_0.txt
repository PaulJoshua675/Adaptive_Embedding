Taking y = aP SP + aRSR therefore applies the desired phase to the link registers while leaving the phase-gradient state unchanged. This reduces the compilation task to constructing the underlying reversible addition |g0 · · · g191⟩|k⟩7−→|g0 · · · g191⟩|k + aP SP + aRSR mod 251⟩, (82) for arbitrary computational-basis values k. The baseline treats all 576 loops independently, combining the inversion and multiplication circuits of Osorio Perez et al. [37], whose inner Σ(36 × 3) circuits come from Gustafson et al. [76]; we implement these circuits from the published descriptions. For a loop L, let a(L) = aP for a plaquette and a(L) = aR for a rectangle. For each loop, the baseline starts with W = 1 and successively multiplies the four or six directed links into it, so W is the running product of the links traversed so far. After completing the loop, the baseline looks up the 51-bit addend a(L)f(W) by QROM [4], adds it to k, and reverses the lookup and loop product. Each multiplication costs 146 Toffolis and each link inversion costs 87. A plaquette costs 1,702 Toffolis and a rectangle costs 2,460, so the 192 plaquettes and 384 rectangles cost 1.3 million Toffolis. The baseline peaks at 1,927 qubits. The discovered circuit combines three optimizations. First, it uses different factor orders for the links and the running product, which makes updates of W cheap. Second, it reuses partial loop products across the layer instead of computing every loop from the identity. Third, it avoids QROM by reading the trace value directly from the coordinates of W and using the result to control the addition of a known constant. Specialized running-product representation. Repeatedly multiplying the running product W by links dominates the cost, so the circuit uses a representation tailored to the restricted update W ←Wg rather than to arbitrary group multiplication. Multiplication in the original normal form is costly in part because X has a complicated conjugation action. However, the normal form can use any element of the u = 1 coset in place of X. We replace X by Xo = CEX. Because Xo differs from X only by factors of C and E, rewriting a link in terms of Xo changes only p, q, r. Xo is the unique such element that satisfies XoV X−1 o = V −1, X2 o = V 2. (83)
