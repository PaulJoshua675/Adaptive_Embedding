Moreover, previous studies indicate that modifying the distance met- ric used to calculate the membership of data points can significantly im- prove clustering outcomes (Bezdek et al., 1984). FCM and FGWC tradi- tionally use Euclidean distance; however, evidence suggests that the use of alternative distance metrics could improve clustering performance (Srivas- tava et al., 2011; Singh et al., 2013; Gueorguieva et al., 2017; Wu et al., 2012). Currently, the only publicly available package that specifically ad- dresses FGWC is spatialClust, which provides limited options, primarily the classical FGWC and a version optimised with GSA (Pamungkas and Pra- mana, 2019)—and uses solely Euclidean distance. On the other hand, the ppclust package offers a variety of alternative distance metrics, but lacks integration with spatial clustering methods or optimisation algorithms. To address these gaps, this article aims to introduce our new R package called naspaclust, which comes from the abbreviation of nature-inspired spatial clustering. The main contribution of this study lies in the adaptation of several previous works on fuzzy clustering, particularly in the spatial con- text (Rao and Patel, 2012; Rao et al., 2012; Bhadoria et al., 2016; Wijayanto, 2015; Wijayanto and Purwarianti, 2014a; Wijayanto et al., 2016; Pamungkas and Pramana, 2019; Wijayanto and Purwarianti, 2014b; Nasution and Kurni- awan, 2018; Nasution et al., 2020; Caraka et al., 2020; Nasution and Siregar, 2022; Nasution et al., 2022; Sumargo et al., 2024). Moreover, this package can behave simultaneously like its predecessors such as spatialClust regarding the optimisation in FGWC and ppclust regarding the distance adjustments. To address these gaps, this study introduces a novel package named naspaclust (nature-inspired spatial clustering), which significantly extends existing packages by incorporating multiple nature-inspired optimisation al- gorithms and diverse distance metrics into fuzzy spatial clustering methods. Specifically, naspaclust implements seven metaheuristic algorithms: Ar- tificial Bee Colony (ABC), Gravitational Search Algorithm (GSA), Flower Pollination Algorithm (FPA), Firefly Algorithm (FA), Harris-Hawk Optimi- sation (HHO), Particle Swarm Optimisation (PSO), and Teaching-Learning- Based Optimisation (TLBO).
