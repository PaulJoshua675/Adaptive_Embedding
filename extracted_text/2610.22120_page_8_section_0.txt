Across all metrics, our framework achieves the strongest performance, substantially outperforming both raw-measurement baselines and improving on the marginal-probability variant. To the best of our knowledge, these are the strongest scene reconstruction results reported from communication-infrastructure measurements, and substantially improve on prior attempts at this problem. Several conclusions follow from the results. The comparison with Diffusion + Raw Meas. isolates the benefit of the physics-grounded repre- sentation. The decoder, noise schedule, and sampler are otherwise identical, yet conditioning on raw channels collapses to near-chance performance. This indicates a failure of learnability rather than information: raw channels contain at least as much scene information as their learned rep- resentations, but the decoder must simultaneously discover the propagation-to-geometry mapping and perform posterior generation from a high-dimensional input. In contrast, the foundation model provides scene-aligned representations that substantially simplify posterior inference. Marginal Prob. + Raw Meas. fails for a different reason. Its full-grid cross-entropy requires ev- ery observation to predict every scene cell, even though a measurement is informative only about propagation-relevant regions. The resulting overconfident predictions in uninformed regions accu- mulate during fusion, where observations that never sensed a cell still contribute votes to it. Marginal Prob. + FM uses the same fusion but forms each per-observation belief on the physics-grounded representation and around the propagation-relevant cells of that observation, abstaining elsewhere; each vote is therefore informative where it is cast, and the aggregation better recovers the scene. Our proposed design improves over this baseline by 22.1% in balanced accuracy and 67.0% in F1 score. Lastly, the results demonstrate that the joint posterior outperforms fused marginals in this setting. The main advantage is concentrated in the upper tail of the per-scene distribution: the median IoU of the two models differs by three percent relative, while at the 90th percentile the gap widens to sixteen percent. The marginal variant treats observations independently and combines them through a fixed rule: confidence gating discards evidence that is weak in each observation but informative in combination, and nothing in the fusion enforces that the result be a plausible scene. The diffusion decoder instead learns the joint posterior over the entire grid given the full set of observations. It fuses evidence in representation space, and it completes the scene through the learned prior where the measurements are silent, the second factor in equation 2. We examine the physical interpretabil- ity of the per-observation marginal pre- dictions using Marginal Prob. + FM, which exposes a belief for each mea- surement individually. Specifically, we ask whether each belief captures the environment regions implicated by its propagation paths while remaining un- certain elsewhere. Figure 5 visualizes the marginal occupancy probability for two measurements in the same environment, to- gether with the ground-truth layout and corresponding propagation paths. The belief re- covers the free-space corridors along the direct and reflected paths, the reflecting struc- tures, and the obstructing component when the direct path is blocked. Elsewhere, predic- tions remain near one-half, indicating uncertainty in regions not informed by the measurement. What the beliefs recover is therefore not an arbitrary partial reconstruction but the specific geometry that is physically responsible for shaping the observation, leaving unrelated regions unknown. This behavior, induced by the physics-guided pretraining, makes the marginal predictions physically in- terpretable: each belief can be read back as a statement about which structures the link encountered. 8
