Limitations and Further Controls The cumulative ladder measures successive changes in a particular order. It does not by itself establish the contribution of each component in the full architecture or resolve interactions among components. Further controls would include removing individual components from the full model, crossing query–key normalization with conditioning width 128/256, and crossing shortcut detachment with calibration at a fixed conditioning width. A backbone expansion with a comparable parameter increase, together with a separate compute-matched comparison, would further clarify whether gains arise from the placement of conditioning capacity. The roles of the two gates also warrant separate tests. Comparing a fixed unit multiplier, a learned constant, and a learned noise-dependent multiplier would isolate the value of noise-dependent shortcut calibration. Comparing confidence gating with no gating, constant attenuation, and gates shuffled within noise bins would distinguish token-specific confidence information from generic attenuation. These controls are not included among the reported results. Such comparisons would require matched data exposure and optimization schedules, comparable tuning budgets, and explicit accounting of parameter counts and FLOPs. Independent baseline and modified-model runs across a shared seed set would assess training variability; intervals obtained by resampling generated texts do not capture that source of uncertainty. Potential failure modes include suppressing useful uncertain self-conditioning inputs or removing shortcut gradients that aid optimization. Lower feature variance alone does not establish better gen- eration. These possibilities limit the generality of conclusions drawn from the present configurations and motivate evaluating the interventions across broader settings.
