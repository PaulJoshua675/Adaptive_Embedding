Figure 1: Optical flow decomposition. 3.1.2 Gravity direction estimation We estimate 3D gravity vectors in the camera coordinate system for all training images using the pretrained GeoCalib model [28]. The model is applied offline to each image to extract its corresponding gravity direction. GeoCalib first predicts a dense perspective field from a single image, which encodes geometric cues such as vanishing directions and horizon struc- ture. Based on this representation, gravity direction is recovered through a Levenberg–Marquardt optimization procedure. To ensure reliability, we re- tain only those gravity estimates for which the optimization converges within a predefined number of iterations. In practice, non-convergence occurs only in a very small fraction of images. Samples for which the optimization fails to converge are excluded from gravity supervision. The resulting gravity vectors, expressed in the camera coordinate system, are used as orientation priors during training. 3.2 Privileged Information Injection To incorporate additional geometric cues during training, we introduce a privileged information injection mechanism that integrates residual optical flow and gravity direction into the transformer-based reconstruction back- bone. These signals are encoded into token-aligned representations and in- jected into the aggregation process with minimal modification to the original architecture. Residual Flow Encoding. Residual optical flow is first partitioned into patches using the same patchification scheme as the input images.
