Which information actually changes a language model’s market prediction? A plausible financial forecast may follow recent returns, react to the emotional framing of an announcement, or compare newly released earnings with expectations. Natural financial text usually combines these signals. If they all agree, the final forecast cannot distinguish their influence. An earnings announcement makes the distinction concrete. Higher earnings than a year earlier can still miss consensus; a loss can beat an even worse expected loss. Upbeat commentary can accompany either numerical outcome. We hold actual earnings fixed and reverse the surprise by changing the stated consensus, while independently reversing momentum and commentary. This identifies the effect of the information supplied to the model under the tested protocol. This paper expands an audited two-checkpoint pilot. The primary experiment now covers six checkpoints in four families, a wider size range, six commentary pairs, losses, annual declines, and small as well as large surprises. Label reversal is part of every primary case. The pilot’s raw results are retained separately, and a conversion/runtime bridge compares saved full-precision scores with the new inference setup. The original expanded protocol was internally frozen before expanded inference, after the pilot had already been inspected; it is not a public preregistration or a fresh confirmation of pilot-independent hypotheses.
