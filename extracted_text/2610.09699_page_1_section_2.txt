Many real-world optimization problems can only be accessed as black boxes: the objective function is expensive, noisy, or lacks analytical structure, and gradient information is unavailable or unreliable. Even when the problem is not purely black-box in nature, methods that treat it as such can be effective, e.g., when problem knowledge is not linked to existing solution techniques, or when the effort required to integrate problem knowledge into a custom solver is too large. Evolution strategies (ES) (Rechenberg, 1973; Schwefel, 1965) provide a robust class of methods for such settings, with the Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) (Hansen and Ostermeier, 2001) having emerged as one of the most widely used algorithms for continuous black-box optimization. CMA-ES combines strong empirical performance on benchmarks (Hansen, 2009b), a comparatively small set of sensitive hyperparameters, and strong invariance properties.
