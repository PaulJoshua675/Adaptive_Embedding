The remaining risk lies in information published inside that gap. Decision accuracy then factorises into routing, whether that information reaches the decision before its revision deadline, and judgment, whether the model reads it correctly for the time of action (Section 3). The theory comes with a model of the system that realises it. A decision ledger is persistent state (Bakkali and Merimi, 2026) that stores each deci- sion with its times, its evidence and its background knowledge, and a router delivers each new item to the open decisions it matches (Section 4). A query retrieves what the user asks about today; a ledger retrieves what yesterday’s decisions still need. The ledger also makes the theory testable: what a router can reach follows from what the ledger indexes, and what the model does with a delivered item follows from its judgment. We derive four predictions and test them in con- trolled decision streams, in which every label fol- lows from timestamps and stated facts, under three conditions that vary how news refers to a deci- sion and how hard its negatives are: decisive news that names the option or its stated dependency (DIRECT), the same with hard negatives (STRESS), and news that reaches the option through other names and upstream entities (INDIRECT). The tests confirm all four predictions across five LLMs, from Ministral 8B to GPT-6.1 Sol. • P1, the freshness ceiling holds. With perfect evidence at decision time and nothing after- wards, accuracy equals the share of decisions that should stand. • P2, delivery is sufficient for change. Once decisive news arrives, models switch in 91– 100% of cases under every router, so the ac- curacy of changing decisions tracks routing recall, and recall follows the ledger’s index: the decision text reaches 37.5% of news that uses another name for a dependency, and back- ground knowledge in the ledger reaches all of it. • P3, misleading deliveries cost correct de- cisions. Every misleading item delivered is a chance to abandon a decision that should stand. Watch conditions written at decision time reach 92.2% of decisive news with 20 misleading deliveries against 89 for the whole background, and broadcasting helps only mod- els that judge well. • P4, the residual error is temporal judg- ment. Given exactly the related news, Minis- tral 8B and Qwen3-235B abandon every de- cision whose related change ended before the activity, Qwen3-8B abandons 94% of those whose change starts after it, and Claude Son- net 4.6 keeps all of them. This is a theory paper with controlled empiri- cal tests. It contributes a temporal theory of LLM decisions with a freshness ceiling and a routing– judgment factorisation (Section 3), the decision ledger as a model of the system that realises it, including watch conditions (Section 4), and con- trolled tests that confirm the theory’s predictions across five models (Sections 5–7). 2
