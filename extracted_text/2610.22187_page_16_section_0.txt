For these tasks, we report Spearman’s rank correlation between LLM-generated scores and hu- man labels, which is unaffected by normalization to a common scale range. In contrast, the politi- cal leaning labels in MBIC (Spinde et al., 2021) are categorical. They indicate whether the outlet that published each sentence is left, center, or right, based on media ratings rather than a judgment of the sentence itself. We map each LLM-generated score to the three categories used for SSswitch and report macro-F1 against the human labels. Agreement with human labels varies substan- tially across tasks. Spearman’s correlation ranges from 0.87 to 0.95 for sentiment and from 0.24 to 0.38 for toxicity, while macro-F1 ranges from 0.34 to 0.65 for political leaning. The lower correla- tions for toxicity may reflect what the human labels measure, namely the proportion of annotators who considered a text toxic rather than the intensity of its toxicity. Similarly, the lower agreement for po- litical leaning may reflect that its labels describe the outlet rather than the sentence. MBIC also includes sentences on non-political topics, and a sentence from a left- or right-leaning outlet may express no leaning of its own. Agreement also varies with rating scale design. For sentiment, 3-point ordinal scales yield the low- est correlations (0.87–0.90). For political leaning, average macro-F1 on ordinal scales decreases from 0.58 with 3 points to 0.48 with 21 points. Within each combination of model and prompting strat- egy, the difference between the highest and lowest macro-F1 across the 16 scales ranges from 0.09 to 0.23. Rubric-based prompting yields lower macro- F1 for political leaning than zero-shot prompting for every model and scale, averaging 0.51 com- pared with 0.56. For sentiment and toxicity, the average change in correlation between prompting strategies is less than 0.01. F Results Details This section provides additional statistical details for the results presented in Section 4. All analyses were conducted in Python 3.9.6 with NumPy 2.0.2 and pandas 2.3.3. We fit the weighted least squares regressions with NumPy linear algebra routines and computed Spearman correlations with SciPy 1.13.1. Figures were created with Matplotlib 3.9.4. First, Table 6 reports the interaction coefficients from the weighted least squares (WLS) regression. Each sensitivity estimate is weighted by the in- verse of the number of minimal pairs that vary the same design element, giving format, range, and sign equal total weight. Format is the reference level of the design element changed. Each interac- tion coefficient indicates how the difference in sen- sitivity between sign or range and format changes at a given moderator level compared with the mod- erator’s reference level. Next, Table 7 gives the full variance decomposi- tion illustrated in Figure 5. In the variance decom- position, scale design distinguishes all 40 minimal pairs, so it covers which design element changes, the values compared for that element, and the val- ues of the other two elements held fixed.
