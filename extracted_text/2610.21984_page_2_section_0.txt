Interaction-centric methods, such as Game- Former [10], improve multi-agent relation modeling, but mainly at the representation level, without explicitly using relational struc- ture to determine trajectory value or optimization priority. Diffusion- based planners, such as Diffusion Planner [31], provide flexible multi-modal trajectory generation, while reward-guided variants such as Diffusion-ES [28] demonstrate the promise of reward-driven optimization. However, existing approaches still rely either on be- havior cloning objectives, which suffer from compounding errors and exposure bias in closed-loop inference, or on generic reward- driven optimization, which does not explicitly encode planning- oriented relational semantics. This leaves open how relational se- mantics should shape both the training distribution and the opti- mization objective, rather than serving only as scene representa- tions or sampling-time guidance. To address this gap, we propose GRID-Planner, a Graph-Reward Integrated Diffusion planner that uses graph-structured scene se- mantics to directly shape the optimization signal of generative planning. Rather than treating graph structure as just an input representation, we use it to define both trajectory importance and planning-oriented return. Our framework answers three questions left open by prior planners: which trajectories should matter more during training, how their quality should be evaluated for plan- ning, and how relational signals should reshape diffusion-based policy learning. Specifically, our framework contains three tightly coupled components: ❶Graph-Aware Importance Weighting, which upweights trajectories from complex, high-risk scenes us- ing scene-interaction centrality and collision-risk cues; ❷Rela- tional Reward Surrogate (RRS), which evaluates candidate roll- outs through time-indexed ego-centric scene graphs, producing a dense trajectory-level signal with smooth bounded penalties over safety, lane/route consistency, and comfort; and ❸Graph-Reward Integrated Diffusion Objective, which incorporates structured returns into a low-variance advantage-weighted diffusion objec- tive, coupled with an advantage-gated on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) mechanism. The resulting advantage reweights diffusion denoising and gates OPSD teacher selection and updates, forming a rollout–return–advantage–update loop in which graph-reward signals directly drive policy improvement. The main contributions of this work are: • We propose GRID-Planner, a unified graph-reward inte- grated diffusion framework that redefines how scene graphs are used in autonomous driving planning: rather than serv- ing only as input representations, they directly shape opti- mization priority and trajectory value for closed-loop deci- sion making. • We introduce a planning-oriented relational learning mechanism, instantiated by Graph-Aware Importance Weighting and a Relational Reward Surrogate, which together focus training on planning-critical trajectories and score candidate rollouts using safety, lane/route consistency, and comfort criteria. • We develop a unified on-policy diffusion objective that combines advantage-weighted diffusion with advantage-gated self-distillation to support stable on-policy refinement and consolidate high-return behaviors, yielding consistent gains over strong baselines on nuPlan and interPlan. 2
