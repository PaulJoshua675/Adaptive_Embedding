An edge within the cutoff costs 1, whereas an edge beyond it costs 10. This penalty steers the path toward the earliest plausible subsequent expenditure rather than a distant one. Edges involving alter accounts incur an additional cost of 50 (51 within the cutoff, 60 beyond), prioritizing ego-account continuity, since alter accounts’ his- tories are only partially observed and could otherwise create artificial shortcuts (e.g., a deposit followed by a later withdrawal). These weights are heuristic traversal costs rather than probabilities or empiri- cally calibrated confidence scores. They were set manually to encode an ordinal preference with roughly order-of-magnitude gaps, so rank order matters more than exact values. The weighted path should be read as the one that best satis- fies our preference hierarchy, rather than as definitive evidence that the traced funds followed that exact route. 4 Results We analyze 1,505 borrower accounts on Aave v3 on the Ethereum mainnet, each with a cumulative borrowed amount of at least 1 million USD between the protocol’s launch on January 27, 2023, and April 9, 2026. The dataset com- prises 175,601 borrow events and 288,764 borrow–repay matches identified using recency-based matching, which we refer to as the LIFO-cutoff. Four inter-transaction network variants are evaluated: (1) all accounts and all candidate edges, (2) only ego-account edges, (3) all accounts with LIFO-cutoff pruning, and (4) ego-account edges with LIFO-cutoff pruning, each without and with teleportation edges. For every configuration, we compare the unweighted shortest path P hop s,d with the weighted shortest path P w s,d to examine how edge pruning and weighting affect reachability, path length, and path composition. 4.1 Path Reachability and Shortest Path Lengths First, we examine reachability between borrow and repay nodes. Table 1 reports the share of borrow events with at least one reachable repayment (“Borrow”) and of individual borrow–repay pairs connected by a path (“Borrow–Repay”) across the eight conditions. Teleport edges yield the best reachability, with nearly 100% of paths found in both metrics, although this reflects more permissive connec- tivity rather than validated correctness. Under the least restrictive edge set (all accounts, all edges), borrow-event reachability rises from 85.91% to 99.97% and borrow–repay reachability from 81.62% to 98.57% relative to no teleportation. This comes at the cost of unverified connections: teleport edges appear in 30.23– 91.68% of unweighted but only 17.22–63.84% of weighted paths. LIFO-cutoff has the opposite effect, substantially reducing reachability by removing candidate temporal links.
