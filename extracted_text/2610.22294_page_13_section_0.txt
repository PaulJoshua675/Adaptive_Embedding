5.2 Qualitative Analysis The limited benefits of guidance may arise because the sampled rewrites offer little room for improvement, or because the reward fails to identify correct candidates. With oracle lengths, only 23 of 127 sample pools contain both judged-correct and judged-incorrect rewrites, so even a perfect selector could improve pooled success by at most 8.3 percentage points. With learned lengths, 72 of 127 pools contain both outcomes, and the corresponding upper bound rises to 45.2 points (Section C.3). Thus, limited selection opportunities help explain the oracle-length results, whereas learned-length sampling leaves substantial room for better selection. A post-hoc Checkstyle example illustrates how the reward can favor an incorrect rewrite. An incorrect edit to logMessage changes the logged line number and message type, yet receives a higher example reward than the developer-consistent rewrite (8.69 versus 2.05 nats). At β = 0.5, this gives the incorrect rewrite approximately 28 times the resampling weight of each correct occurrence. Despite appearing only once among K = 16 samples, compared with seven occurrences of the correct rewrite, it receives 61.8% of the total weight, reducing expected LLM-judged success from 43.8% to 15.7%. Here, correct rewrites are available, but the reward disproportionately favors an incorrect one (Section C.3). 6
