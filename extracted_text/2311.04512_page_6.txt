EXPERIMENTS A. Experimental Settings Dataset. Our FFINet is evaluated on the Argoverse 1 and Argoverse 2 Motion Forecasting Dataset, which contains high- definition (HD) maps and trajectories of agents collected from real traffic scenarios. Argoverse 1 consists of 324,557 inter- esting agent trajectories, in which the first 2 seconds (T = 20) of each trajectory are used as the observations, and the next 3 seconds (Tp = 30) are leveraged to supervise the predic- tions. Argoverse 2 selects 250,000 scenarios for training and validation, where multiple high-quality trajectories relevant to AV are selected as ”scored actors” in each scenario for multi-agent prediction. The first 5 seconds (T = 50) of each trajectory are used as the observations, and the next 6 seconds (Tp = 60) are utilized for predictions. Evaluation Metrics. We predict multi-modal trajecto- ries (K = 6) for each agent and evaluate our FFINet with single-agent and multi-agent metrics, respectively. As for single-agent evaluation, we adopt the standard metrics in Argoverse Motion Forecasting Competition [41], includ- ing minimum Final Displacement Error (minFDE), minimum Average Displacement Error (minADE), Miss Rate (MR) and Brier minimum Final Displacement Error (brier-minFDE). For multi-agent evaluation, we take the metrics of Argoverse2 Multi-World Forecasting Challenge [42], including Average Brier Minimum Final Displacement Error (avgB-MinFDE), Average Minimum Final Displacement Error (avgMinFDE), Average Minimum Average Displacement Error (avgMinADE) and Actor Collision Rate (actorCR). Implementation Details. The distance threshold of A2L, L2A and A2A are set to 7, 6, and 100 meters, respectively. The hidden size of the MLP Block is set as 128. We take the Pytorch toolbox and two RTX 3090 Ti GPUs to train our model with a batch size of 64 for 40 epochs, which takes around 19 hours. Besides, we adopt the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 1e-3, which is decayed to 1e-4 at 32 epochs following [19]. Since our model can simultaneously predict the trajectories of multiple agents, we just calculate the loss of trajectories longer than 40 timestamps for a balance between the quantity and quality of training data. We didn’t adopt any data augmentation method in this paper. B. Comparison with State-of-the-Arts Comparison on Single-agent Performance. We com- pare our method on the test set of Argoverse 1 with various state-of-the-art methods, including LaneGCN [19], DenseTNT [6], TPCN [34], Scene transformer [22], GO- HOME [12], HiVT [20], DSP [35], TENET [36], Multi- path++ [2], GANet [28] and Wayformer [37]. For fair com- parison, we further apply a simple yet effective ensemble strategy following [36] with the submissions on leaderboard. The results are shown in Table I, where our method consis- tently outperforms the other methods on all metrics except for MR (K=6). Although we were narrowly defeated by GOHOME on MR (K=6), we outperforms it in other six metrics by a large margin.
