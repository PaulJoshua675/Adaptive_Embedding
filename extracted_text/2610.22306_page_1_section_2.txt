Tracking-by-detection splits multi-object tracking (MOT) into two steps: a detector finds object boxes in every frame, and an association step links those boxes across frames into tracks that keep their identities. The SORT family [7] per- forms the second step with a Kalman filter, which predicts where each track will be, and a matching cost, usually the intersection over union (IoU) between predicted and de- tected boxes, solved as a linear assignment. This paper is about the association step only: ARC-SORT takes the out- put of a fixed detector and trains nothing. Sports broadcasts concentrate the two failure modes of the SORT paradigm into one domain: athletes sprint, cut, and collide, violating the Kalman filter’s constant-velocity assumption, while the broadcast camera pans and zooms on top. The dominant response is to make association more tolerant, enlarging boxes by a fixed margin [43], sched- ule [20], or velocity heuristic [40], or else to learn motion per domain [3, 22, 27]. Meanwhile, two quantities that re- port how much the tracker should trust itself are computed every frame and discarded: the filter’s predictive covari- ance says how far the motion prediction can be trusted, yet overlap-cost pipelines either ignore it at association time or consume it as a hard Mahalanobis veto [42] (ground-plane costs [45] abandon overlap entirely); and the assignment’s own cost margin says how nearly it preferred the swapped pairing, yet nothing in the tracker’s state distinguishes a confident match from a coin flip. This paper is built on a single principle: a tracker should let its own uncertainty govern its own decisions. We instantiate it twice, as search breadth and commitment firmness, and follow it to its limit. At short horizons the mo- tion covariance acts as tolerance, not veto: our uncertainty- adaptive Expansion IoU dilates each track’s matching re- gion by a continuous function of its predictive standard de- viation, supplied by an Interacting Multiple Model (IMM) filter [6, 8].
