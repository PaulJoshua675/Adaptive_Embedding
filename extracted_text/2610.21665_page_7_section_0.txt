Population averaging provides a complementary intervention: ten perturbations in Scen1 yield accuracy comparable to a reduced-learning-rate reference, while four perturbations stabilize the wide five-hidden-layer configuration where preceding single-perturbation settings failed [4, Sec. 3.4.3]. Indeed, the thesis’s final comparison states that the deeper reference had not achieved BP-comparable accuracy within the considered budgets [4, Sec. 3.6]. 4 Discussion WP can attain near-BP accuracy on MNIST in a fully connected network trained through scalar loss feedback. Extended large-batch training reaches approximately 97% test accuracy, within about 0.5–1 percentage point of the early BP reference (Fig. 2). This result demonstrates that the lower accuracies observed in the small-batch runs are not an intrinsic ceiling of the WP learning rule. A substantial part of the accuracy deficit can be recovered through stabilization and additional training. The experiments therefore separate two properties that are easily conflated when evaluating biologically motivated learning: attainable accuracy and learning efficiency. The high-accuracy regime extends the earlier MNIST comparison of Züge et al. [12]. Their network with one hidden layer reached 92.3% with WP and 97.7% with SGD after 50,000 updates at a batch size of 1,000. Our extended experiments show that WP can move substantially closer to the BP reference when larger batches and longer training are available. The contribution is this empirical demonstration of near-BP accuracy in the hidden-layer setting. It complements the established interpretation of WP as a stochastic gradient estimator [2, 3, 11] by showing how much performance can be recovered in a nonlinear classification network. Computational efficiency remains the principal practical limitation. In the ten-epoch comparison, WP reaches 90.2%, whereas the reported BP reference reaches 97.4% (Table 1). Approaching the latter accuracy with WP requires substantially more training. This behavior is consistent with the variance introduced when a high-dimensional update is estimated from a scalar loss difference [11, 12]. Smaller learning rates limit destabilizing parameter changes, while averaging independent perturbation estimates reduces update variability [10]. These mechanisms support continued learning, but they incur costs through additional updates or additional forward evaluations. The batch-size comparison further shows why more averaging is not automatically more efficient. Larger batches expose each perturbation to a greater fraction of the task, while population averaging combines independent perturbation realizations. In the update-indexed curves, batches from 4,000 to 10,000 approach similar accuracies. When performance is indexed by epochs, the largest batch loses its advantage (Fig. 3). The useful operating regime therefore balances update stability against the amount of computation devoted to each step. Maximizing batch size or population size alone can yield diminishing returns; the objective is sustained learning with an affordable amount of averaging. Network depth makes this balance more demanding. In the tested deeper architectures, settings that support shallow learning can produce unstable updates, and recovering useful learning requires smaller learning rates or stronger averaging. These interventions improve trainability, although the deeper configurations remain below the BP comparison within the investigated budgets. The results identify stabilization and slow convergence as central challenges for scaling WP. A larger representation hierarchy increases the demands on the learning process, so architectural capacity and effective training progress must be considered together. Improving the information extracted from each perturbation is consequently an important direction for extending WP to deeper networks. Hybrid learning provides a complementary direction.
