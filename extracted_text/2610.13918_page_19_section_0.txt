A.2 List of Neurotransmitters and Neuromodulators Table 4: Reference guide to key neurotransmitters and neuromodulators in sleep-wake neurobiology. Chemical Class & Receptors Anatomical Projections Primary Functional Role GABA Amino Acid GABAA (ionotropic Cl−) GABAB (metabotropic Gi) VLPO, basal forebrain, local cortical interneurons Primary central nervous system inhibitory neurotransmitter. Hyperpolarizes target neurons, gates slow-wave sync, and inhibits ascending monoamines to initiate sleep. Galanin Neuropeptide GalR1, GalR2, GalR3 (Gi/Gq) Co-localized with GABA in VLPO neurons Co-released with GABA; provides sustained slow inhibition onto orexinergic and monoaminergic wake systems to maintain sleep continuity. Orexin/ Hypocretin Neuropeptide OX1R (Gq), OX2R (Gq/Gi) Lateral hypothalamus (LHA) to brainstem, cortex, and thalamus Essential wakefulness stabilizer. Projects excitatory drive to monoaminergic nuclei, reinforcing flip-flop hysteresis and preventing cataplexy/SOREMPs. Glutamate Amino Acid AMPA, NMDA, Kainate, mGluRs Cortical pyramidal cells, thalamic relay, parabrachial nucleus Primary fast excitatory neurotransmitter. Mediates cognitive processing, intracortical communication, and ascending reticular arousal drive. Acetylcholine (ACh) Quaternary Amine Muscarinic (M1–M5), Nicotinic Basal forebrain, PPT/LDT brainstem nuclei Desynchronizes cortical EEG. Active during waking cognition and elevated during REM sleep; quiescent during deep N3 slow-wave sleep. Noradrenaline/ Norepinephrine Monoamine α1, α2, β1, β2 adrenergic Locus coeruleus (LC) to neocortex, thalamus, spinal cord Mediates vigilance, environmental alertness, and stress responses. Discharges rapidly during wakefulness; quiescent during NREM and silent during REM sleep. Serotonin (5-HT) Monoamine (indolamine) 5-HT1–5-HT7 Dorsal and median raphe nuclei to forebrain Promotes wakefulness and behavioral motor activity; directly suppresses REM sleep by inhibiting cholinergic brainstem centers. Histamine Monoamine H1, H2, H3 receptors Tuberomammillary nucleus (TMN) of hypothalamus Sustains tonic wakefulness and cortical vigilance. Antagonism of H1 receptors (e.g., first-generation antihistamines) induces drowsiness. Adenosine Purine Nucleoside A1 (inhibitory Gi), A2A (excitatory Gs) Ubiquitous extracellular accumulation across cortex Master endogenous homeostatic somnogen. Accumulates with metabolic wakefulness; excites VLPO (A2A) and inhibits wake nuclei (A1); blocked by caffeine. ATP Purine Nucleotide P2X (ionotropic), P2Y (metabotropic) Astrocytes and active neuronal terminals Primary cellular energy currency. Released via gliotransmission and hydrolyzed extracellularly into adenosine to signal metabolic sleep pressure. 19
