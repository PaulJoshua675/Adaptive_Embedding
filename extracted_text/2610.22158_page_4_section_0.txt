Consequently, instructions that encourage scrutiny or assert the presence of a flaw should not alter the expected response to a given query. 3.2 Premise Taxonomy We further organize flawed premises into a unified taxonomy. (1) Expression Error refers to errors caused by ambiguous or unclear natural language expressions that prevent the query intent from being uniquely mapped to SQL; (2) Premise Contradic- tion captures queries whose assumptions are logi- cally inconsistent or incompatible with the database schema; (3) Missing Premise refers to queries that omit essential assumptions, constraints, or schema information required for reliable SQL generation and execution; (4) Beyond Ability includes queries whose requirements exceed the expressive or rea- soning capabilities of SQL and the given database, making them unanswerable under the available ex- ecution assumptions. A complete taxonomy with fine-grained subcategories and definitions is shown in Table 1. 3.3 Benchmark Construction Figure 2 shows that PremiseSQL pairs 1,800 flawed queries with their unmodified originals from Spider 1.0 (Yu et al., 2018) and BIRD / BIRD-filtered (Li et al., 2023). Each pair combines an original query with a taxonomy-guided perturbation of the query or supplied schema. The BIRD portion of E3.2 (Missing Schema Premise) uses BIRD rather than BIRD-filtered because it requires access to gold schema annotations. To improve diversity and linguistic naturalness, perturbations are generated using GPT-5.1 under predefined error constraints (see Table 1 and Ap- pendix A.3.1). All instances are assigned corre- sponding error categories and subcategories, and further verified using a two-stage process consist- ing of automatic evaluation by Gemini-2.5-Pro- Thinking and subsequent human review.
