In parallel, query expansion techniques based on pre-trained language models and LLMs have been extensively explored to alleviate lexical mismatch and enrich retrieval representations, as systematically reviewed by Li et al [16]. Large decoder-based LLMs offer substantially stronger semantic modeling capabilities, but integrating them into sparse retrieval remains challenging due to structural differences between continuous hidden representations and discrete lexical signals. Direct projection often leads to unstable sparsity and inefficient representations. This raises a key question: Can we retain LLM-level seman- tic expressiveness while preserving the efficiency and interpretability of sparse retrieval? Motivated by recent advances in neural sparse retrieval and the powerful se- mantic modeling capabilities of large language models, we investigate whether LLM-enhanced representations can be effectively integrated into sparse retrieval without sacrificing its inherent efficiency and interpretability. Drawing inspi- ration from the compatibility of sparse lexical systems with practical inverted indexing, we propose a lightweight alignment framework that bridges LLM se- mantic representations with high-dimensional sparse vocabulary spaces.
