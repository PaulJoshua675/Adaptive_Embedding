A paragraph that only concludes, summarizes, synthesizes, or maps interpretations already established in earlier paragraphs onto a task label is NO_GAIN. This holds even when it states the candidate's final answer, and even when it restates a correction or reversal made earlier. NOVELTY RULES 5. Compare meanings, not wording. A different quotation, synonym, label, or confidence level does not make the same interpretation new. 6. Compare against the entire earlier reasoning prefix. A supported insight remains established even if the candidate later takes an unsupported detour. Returning to that insight is not another gain. 7. Unsupported earlier claims do not consume novelty. A later paragraph may establish a warranted interpretation that an earlier paragraph merely asserted without support. CORRECTION AND SELF-CRITIQUE RULES 8. A critique can itself make progress. Identifying and withdrawing a specific unsupported inference, correcting a speaker attribution, narrowing a claim, or reducing unjustified certainty can qualify. The critique must explain the particular error or limitation and establish a supported change in interpretation. It does not need a new quotation or a replacement final answer. 9. General doubt is not progress.“ Wait”, “I may be wrong”, “let me reconsider”, an answer reversal, or a promise to check something does not qualify without a specific supported improvement. 10. Credit the resulting understanding, not the act of correcting. Count the corrected interpretation or limitation once. Do not give an extra bonus because the candidate first made a mistake. Repeatedly introducing and repairing the same error does not create new progress. MIXED-PARAGRAPH RULE 11. Evaluate contributions individually before deciding the paragraph's verdict. A paragraph can contain both valid reasoning and errors. Give GAIN if at least one independently supported, new, task-relevant contribution remains. Do not reject an otherwise qualifying contribution merely because an unrelated clause is wrong. However, an error that undermines the proposed contribution prevents that contribution from qualifying. Do not credit a claim the paragraph ultimately withdraws or leaves directly contradicted. GAIN does not certify the whole paragraph or every quotation within it. 12. Do not infer usefulness from evidence length. Greetings, short replies, address terms, and other brief spans can support contextual interpretations. Long quotations can contribute nothing. Evaluate the expressed inference. ASSESSMENT AND OUTPUT For each paragraph: - FIRST write a short assessment in English. - For GAIN, name the specific new contribution. - For NO_GAIN, name the main reason there is no supported improvement. - THEN write the verdict. For a mixed paragraph receiving GAIN, the assessment must identify the valid contribution rather than endorse its errors. Base each verdict on whether the paragraph's own contribution is new and supported by the source and task information. Do not decide it by whether the candidate's final label matches the label you would choose, and never mark a paragraph NO_GAIN only because you disagree with its conclusion. Return exactly one line per paragraph, in order, with no blank lines between them. Each line has one of these two forms: <number>: <short assessment> | GAIN <number>: <short assessment> | NO_GAIN Do not put a vertical bar inside the assessment. Do not output headings, JSON, code fences, belief lists, blank lines, or additional explanation. CALIBRATION EXAMPLES Each example below is independent.
