CEG underperforms CIG despite using the same computational budget: given a fixed number of gradient evaluations, CEG trades path steps for additional baselines, and as discussed in Section 3.4, path steps are critical for accurately approximating the causal path integral, so reducing them degrades attribution quality. Finally, on all nodes (Figure F.1), CIG still performs better than all other methods. 4.2 Causal gene recovery in a simulated biological dataset We evaluate our method on a regression task using simulated single-cell gene expression data, predicting each cell’s simulated differentiation time from its trajectory of activated and repressed transcription factors. The dataset is generated with Dyngen [Cannoodt et al., 2021] under a known gene regulatory network (GRN) with a backbone of transcription factors (TFs) that causally regulate downstream genes. Unlike the per-sample CATEs of the synthetic benchmark above, ground truth here is exact but global: the backbone TFs are the known drivers of the simulation, letting us test recovery at realistic single-cell scale and complexity. After filtering and normalization, a fully connected neural network is trained to predict simulated time. The GRN defines both the feature ordering and surrogate structure for our method. We evaluate several CIG/CEG variants (linear or expanded-basis surrogate, first- or second-order terms), against attribution baselines from the captum and shap packages [Kokhlikyan et al., 2020, Lundberg and Lee, 2017], over five random seeds. Conditional SHAP and causal Shapley values were excluded due to their computational infeasibility at this scale. Performance is evaluated by backbone-TF recovery: area under the recall curve (AUC) (treating backbone TFs as positives), and discounted cumulative gain (DCG) over the top-ranked genes (relevance scores assigned according to each gene’s role in the GRN). We also evaluate our method’s ability to prioritize the causal regulators for each transition state. Dataset, model, surrogate, and evaluation details are given in Appendix G. Results and discussion. Our methods achieve the strongest recovery of backbone TFs (Figure 4A). The expanded-basis with second-order terms is the best overall, and SHAP’s partition explainer is the best baseline. Both second-order terms and the expanded–basis improve performance over the linear and first-order variants. Similar trends are observed for the DCG scores (Figure 4B): Our method variants are near-optimal at a threshold of 10 genes, and the expanded-basis CIG remains the best overall at 50 and 250.
