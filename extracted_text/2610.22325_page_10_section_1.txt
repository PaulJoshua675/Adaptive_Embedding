Temporal and multi-view consistency losses on large corpora of real video could improve correspondence and reduce reliance on synthetic supervision. Developing real-data bench- marks with independently measured surface-point identities would strengthen evaluation beyond surface proximity and reconstruction-derived tracks. The alignment provided by Dense4D could support learning appearance models based on Gaussian splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) from humans in motion, while its accurate tracks could control human motion in generated video (Geng et al., 2025). Dense4D provides a foundation for augmenting scene understanding systems with more faithful human geometry, dense correspondence and robust tracking. Building on this foundation, future work could develop richer models of people and their surroundings that remain coherent through prolonged motion and interaction. ETHICS STATEMENT This work uses existing research datasets and publicly available video footage, including clips from Pexels (n.d.), in accordance with their respective terms of use. No new recordings of people were collected. Training uses BEDLAM2, THuman2.1 and MammaSyn; evaluation includes existing benchmarks of real videos and 3D scans. Dense4D estimates human geometry and correspondence without predicting biometric identity. Applications should respect the consent and privacy of the people depicted. Our experiments do not assess demographic differences in performance or suitability for consequential decisions about individuals. The underlying datasets and pretrained models remain subject to their respective terms of use.
