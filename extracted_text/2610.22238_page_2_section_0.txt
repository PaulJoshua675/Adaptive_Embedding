However, parallel sampling ignores the dependence among the tokens revealed in the same step. For example, in “he plugged the ⟨key, plug⟩into the ⟨lock, socket⟩”, each slot is plausible on its own, yet independent draws produce “key into socket” with the product of the two marginal probabilities. This error persists even when the score function is exact, and it has been identified as a source of quality loss in parallel decoding (Zheng et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025). A growing line of work characterizes this error through the generation order and the dependence within a block (Zhang et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2025; Wen et al., 2026; Zhao and Cai, 2026), and reduces it by choosing schedules or blocks whose tokens are nearly conditionally independent (Park et al., 2025; Kim et al., 2025; Lavenant and Zanella, 2025; Azangulov et al., 2026) or by learning a joint correction on top of the marginals (Liu et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025). A complementary approach is to correct the sampling of a given block directly, using only the trained score function. Recently, Anari et al. (2026) achieved this exactly by rejection sampling. Their key observation is that the likelihood ratio between the joint distribution of a block and the product of its marginals can be decomposed into token-level conditional probabilities along an ordering of the block, all of which are given by the score function. However, evaluating this decomposition requires one score query per token in the block, which offsets the savings of parallel sampling. This motivates the following question: Can we correct parallel sampling in MDMs with a number of score queries that does not grow with the block size? In this paper, we give an affirmative answer by proposing the Single-Increment Rejection Sampler (SIRS). Instead of evaluating the full log-likelihood ratio, SIRS estimates it with a single randomly sampled token-level increment, which costs one score query. However, the acceptance probability is the exponential of the log-likelihood ratio, and exponentiating an unbiased estimate does not give an unbiased estimate of the acceptance probability. We therefore adopt the Poissonized Bernoulli factory of Chen et al. (2026): it draws a Poisson number of increments and combines them into a random acceptance probability whose expectation equals the target one. Since the output of rejection sampling depends only on the expected acceptance probability, the output distribution remains exact, provided that this random probability lies in [0, 1]. We ensure this by clipping the increments, which is the only source of error when the score function is exact. Moreover, since one query returns the conditional distributions of all remaining tokens in the block, we Rao-Blackwellize each increment by averaging over these tokens at no extra cost. We show that, when the score function is exact, the error of SIRS is controlled by the parts of the increments that exceed the clipping range, rather than by the full dependence within a block as for parallel sampling. We summarize our contributions as follows: • Query-efficient correction of parallel sampling. We propose SIRS, which corrects parallel sampling by rejection sampling with a Poissonized Bernoulli factory. With clipping threshold B, each unmasking step costs at most (1 + 2B)e2B score queries in expectation, independent of the block size, whereas the exact correction of Anari et al. (2026) requires one query per token. We further propose the Rao-Blackwellized increment, which reuses each query for all remaining tokens in the block. 2
