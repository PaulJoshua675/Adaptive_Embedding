Reasoning-conditioned embeddings can im- prove retrieval by progressively refining seman- tic constraints. However, additional reasoning may introduce over-generalized or semantically noisy abstractions that weaken retrieval speci- ficity. Existing methods largely supervise rea- soning through final retrieval outcomes, pro- viding limited guidance over the refinement process itself. We propose AURA, a frame- work that directly supervises intermediate rea- soning states according to how well they sepa- rate relevant documents from hard distractors. AURA further trains a lightweight utility esti- mator over prefix-conditioned embeddings to adapt reasoning depth at inference time. On the reasoning-intensive BRIGHT benchmark, AURA-4B achieves 34.7 average nDCG@10 while using 2.8 reasoning steps and 96 gen- erated tokens per query. Under matched de- ployment conditions, adaptive stopping reduces latency from 4.6 to 0.76 seconds relative to full- trajectory reasoning. Results on MS MARCO further suggest that this specialization need not substantially degrade conventional in-domain retrieval. 1
