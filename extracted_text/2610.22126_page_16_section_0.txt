CIG’s delta is consistently one or more orders of magnitude larger than IG’s, as expected rather than indicative of worse attribution quality: IG’s path derivative is closed-form, while CIG additionally estimates the surrogate-residual derivative via finite differences, an extra discretization-error layer IG lacks. Real-data residuals exceed the synthetic ones: on synthetic data the mechanism is the exact generating function, so hi(α) reduces to pure exogenous noise, whereas on real data hi(α) additionally conflates the surrogate’s fitting error, which need not vary smoothly with α along the path. Since all our evaluation metrics depend only on how attributions rank features, a residual of this size is unlikely to affect them. This residual converges as expected for a first-order discretization scheme: Figure C.4 sweeps K at 100 synthetic features, showing the relative completeness delta falling off linearly on a log-log plot, roughly halving with every doubling of K — the textbook O(1/K) signature shared by both the Riemann sum and the finite-difference derivative. This sweep does not isolate which of the two dominates, only that the combined numerical error vanishes at the expected rate. Implementation invariance Statement. Attributions are invariant to the implementation of the model, depending only on the input-output behavior of F. Precondition. The causal graph and the mechanism’s input-output relation (ci as a function, or si if using surrogates, not its specific implementation) are held fixed; only the model F varies. 16
