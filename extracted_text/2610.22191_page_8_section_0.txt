The pairwise curves bend down with α, as in [13]: at ρ = 0.06 the magnetization drops below 0.9 at α ≃0.14 and reaches 0.65 at α = 0.5, at ρ = 0.25 it is below 0.9 already at α ≃0.015 and reaches 0.38, at ρ = 0.5 it reaches 0.24. The attention curves are flat over the whole range, at 1.0, 0.90 and 0.75 respectively, which are their α →0 values, i.e. the majority-rule values erf(1/√2ρ) lowered by the partial pooling Meff/M ≃0.65 at this β. The flatness is more robust than the saturation of W: at ρ = 0.5 the correct class carries only W ≃0.3 of the weight at α = 0.1 and W ≃0.1 at α = 0.5, yet m(1) does not move, because the weight lost to the K −1 wrong classes is spread over all of them and its contribution to the noise, the term (1 −W)2/[(K −1)W 2] in (15), stays well below ρ (0.04 at W = 0.1, K = 2000); in the pairwise model the same crosstalk is of order α. The dependence on the load returns only when the crosstalk term of Corollary 1(i), (1 + ρ)(1 −W)2 (K −1)W 2 ≃(1 + ρ)(K −1)e−2Nβr2 (q = r), becomes comparable with ρ, which is the crosstalk edge of (17): there W ∼K−1/2. Panel (b) shows the de- pendence on ρ at the fixed load α = 0.2: the atten- tion layer stays above the pairwise network at every ρ, and both follow their one-step theories. The triangles are the fixed point of the iterated attention map: for ρ ≲0.06 the archetype itself, which is not one of the keys, is an attractor of the softmax dynamics and is retrieved exactly; for larger ρ the fixed point is a train- ing example (nmax = 1, m(∞) ≃r, the dotted line). Iterating a transformer layer memorizes unless the ex- amples are good enough for the pooled majority vote to be self-consistent; a single application generalizes over a wider range. This is a departure from the attractor paradigm of associative memory: when the keys are ex- amples rather than archetypes, the fixed points of the map are the examples, and the archetype is reached by stopping after one step rather than by converging. It is also, we note, how attention is used in practice, where each layer is applied once in a feed-forward pass rather than iterated to a fixed point (iterated variants exist [11]); the present setting offers one reason why the sin- gle application is not a loss.
