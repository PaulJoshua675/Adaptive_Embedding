Against the same Ginibre and entry-shuffle nulls, and against the random core (the trained backbone with the recurrence at initialization), J is near-maximally feed-forward (0.99 vs 0.71/0.81/0.93) with a chance-level rotational part (0.71)—the balanced-amplification signature of Murphy and Miller (2009); reading a recurrent operator’s hidden feed-forward organization off its Schur form follows Goldman (2009). This structure is visible directly in the materialized operator (Fig. 7). What the Schur matrix does not show. Two features of it are conventions rather than findings, and we read neither. The Schur vectors are each defined only up to sign—U 7→UD with D = diag(±1) sends Tij 7→didjTij—so the sign pattern is gauge-dependent, and we plot |Tij|, which is not. The eigenvalue ordering along the diagonal is likewise arbitrary, and reordering it yields a different U and a different T rather than a permutation of the same entries; the placement of individual modes, including the densely coupled leading rows and trailing columns, therefore carries no information. What does not depend on either convention is the diagonal itself—the eigenvalue set in any ordering—and hence the diagonal/off-diagonal split that the feed-forward fraction measures.
