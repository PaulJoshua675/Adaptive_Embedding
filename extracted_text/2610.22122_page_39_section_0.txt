The two single perturbation tasks each use 60 screening rounds, whereas the two combinatorial tasks each use 200. More rounds provide the acquisition network with more feedback and training opportunities. The explicit hit probability term is more beneficial on the two tasks with 60 rounds. This pattern is consistent with a useful probability reference early in learning and motivates gradually reducing its contribution as feedback accumulates. Testing such a schedule within the same task would separate the effect of screening horizon from differences between datasets. F.2 Training Dynamics and Candidate Reranking Candidates analyzed. On IL-2, 85 hit candidates are discovered during active screening in all five TDAL runs and none of the five Surrogate only runs. We compare their discovery times and selection ranks across the five random seeds. Training and discovery across seeds. Figure F1a–b shows TD loss per round and cumulative discoveries among these 85 candidates. Early loss changes vary in direction and magnitude across seeds. For all five seeds, fluctuations are smaller in the last 30 than in the first 10 rounds. Discoveries concentrate in the second half, with 74.1%–87.1% of the set discovered in the last 30 rounds (Table F1). This common timing pattern shows that these candidates mostly enter selected batches after feedback accumulates. Candidate reranking at selection. We compare each candidate’s TDAL score rank and concurrent surrogate hit probability rank at selection, using the same unmeasured pool and surrogate predictions. Across seeds, mean TDAL ranks are 16.1–17.2, whereas mean surrogate ranks are 9,442–9,780. Of 425 selection records, 413 have surrogate ranks outside the top 𝐵= 32 (97.2%, Figure F1c–g). Across seeds, the acquisition network consistently promotes candidates ranked low by concurrent surrogate predictions into the selected batch.
