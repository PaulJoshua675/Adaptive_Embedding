2.1 AI Tutors Since the debut of Large Language Models (LLMs), researchers have consistently explored their potential applications in computing education. Beyond aiding student learning by providing thorough code explanations [12], step-wise worked examples [5], and prompt problems [1], as well as facilitating various instructional activities, LLMs have proven effective as the basis for educational question- answering (QA) systems, or in other words, AI tutors. Hicke et al. [3] investigated the effectiveness of various optimiza- tion techniques in LLMs for answering questions collected from a CS1 course Piazza. They enhanced Llama-2 using Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) on textbooks and lab manuals, supervised finetun- ing with selected Piazza QA pairs, and reinforcement learning with human feedback; and applied RAG to GPT-4. They show that these techniques resulted in substantial improvements: Llama-2’s answer accuracy (factual correctness & groundedness) increased from 0.30 to 0.58, and GPT-4’s from 0.40 to 0.73, while usefulness (practical values to human instructors) increased from 0.18 to 0.50 for Llama-2 and from 0.28 to 0.65 for GPT-4.
