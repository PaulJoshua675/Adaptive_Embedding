We presented the first release of Open Generalist CUA, an ongoing project to develop and open the full stack for generalist computer use. Our GCUA formulation centers on three model decisions: what to observe, how to act, and how to organize work. Code execution lets models use intermediate results to coordinate GUI and programmatic operations and apply their existing programming capabilities to computer interaction. Harness-to-model decision transfer complements this design by moving selected observation and task-state decisions from fixed harness rules to the model. On the offline subset of OSWorld-v2, the full OpenGCUA configuration improves task scores across all eight evaluated models, raising mean partial score by 19.6 points and reducing reported steps per task by an average of 53.0% relative to batched GUI-only execution. Ablations show additional benefits from code execution with the same GUI and CLI access, indicating that execution design matters beyond interface access. We release our implementation, prompts, and evaluation trajectories, alongside preliminary versions of a real-device harness and cross-device SDK. Evaluations on four macOS tasks reveal substantial differences in reported reasoning-token usage and task completion under the same harness. This report studies GCUA designs rather than introducing a newly trained model. OpenGCUA will continue through further research and releases on GCUA models, reasoning and execution efficiency, and real-device environments and benchmarks. 25
