Other approaches reconstruct milestone DAGs or mine explicitly referenced PR chains (Deng et al. 2026; Jiang et al. 2026). These bench- marks commonly measure progress through flat test comple- tion, stage- or component-level scores, or milestone resolu- tion. Related work has also explored denser signals based on patch similarity, weighted tests, learned reward models, pro- cess feedback, enhanced test oracles, and multidimensional evaluation (Wei et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2026; Shum et al. 2025; Dai et al. 2024; Yu et al. 2025; Tao et al. 2025; He et al. 2025; Miserendino et al. 2025), while graph structures and cross-language code representations have been used to organize agent workflows or support structural program anal- ysis (Chen et al. 2024; Song et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2025b; Vogel et al. 2026). WEAVE complements these directions by constructing executable PR-DAG tasks from repository- calibrated code couplingand deriving graph-conditionedpar- tial credit from node-attributed tests, which can also serve as a dense, verifiable outcome signal during optimization. 3 Dependency-Structured Task Construction 3.1 Overview and Task Definition WEAVE constructs executable dependency-structured tasks from validated atomic PR instances. As illustrated in Fig- ure 2, the construction contains two stages. First, WEAVE represents each PR by the program entities it modifies and aggregates strongly coupled PRs into bounded depen- dency DAGs. Second, each candidate graph is instantiated as a single-environment task with a unified specification, executable tests, and node-level test attribution. The re- sulting graph is retained as evaluator-side metadata and is subsequently used for the graph-conditioned credit and reinforcement-learning methods in Section 4. A final WEAVE instance is defined as I = (Sbase, q, G, TF2P, TP2P, A) , (1) where Sbase is the repository state presented to the agent, q is a unified task specification, G = (V, E) is the depen- dency DAG, TF2P and TP2P are the functional and regression test sets, and A denotes test-to-node attribution.
