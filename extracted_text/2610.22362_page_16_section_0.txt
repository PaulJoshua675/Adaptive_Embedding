Broader Impact The ability to generate environments and training sig- nals automatically has far-reaching implications for robot learning, simulation, and embodied AI. CoDA contributes to this space by turning environment generation into a closed loop that responds to what a policy has learned, which lowers the cost of producing diverse, verifiable training data. This can accelerate the development of capable manipulation policies, improve the fidelity and coverage of simulated benchmarks, and support scal- able data generation for research groups without large teleoperation budgets. While lowering the barrier to training capable robot policies raises potential concerns about misuse and about biases inherited from the un- derlying foundation models, our framework is designed for research use, judges success only through machine- checkable verifiers, and audits every automated diagnosis with control experiments. We encourage responsible de- ployment practices that keep such audits in place. Com- pared to collecting real-robot demonstrations at scale, the approach is computationally efficient and requires no physical data collection, and we view its benefits in controllable, verifiable data generation as outweighing its risks when applied responsibly. 16
