In this paper, we show that CIM noise induces depth-accumulated, head-dependent attention drift, while output-centric NAT may re- produce answers through routes that deviate from the clean model. We propose ABNAT, which aligns headwise attention between clean and noisy paths from the same checkpoint to preserve task-relevant content processing rather than merely match answers. Across vi- sion, language, and multimodal Transformers, ABNAT improves noisy accuracy by up to 11.28 percentage points, reduces attention drift by 85.77%, generalizes under train–deploy noise mismatch, and adds no inference cost; selective variants retain near-full robustness with lower training overhead.
