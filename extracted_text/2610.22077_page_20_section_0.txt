The dashed line is the uniform frequency of a calibrated ensemble. B.4 CORRELATION BETWEEN TRACK AND INTENSITY ERRORS We check whether a member that misplaces a storm also misjudges its intensity. For each matched ensemble member of the 106 (2021–2023) and 41 (2025) retained storms, we average over its forecast times the distance between its storm centre and the IBTrACS position, and the absolute error of its regional pressure minimum and 10m wind maximum against the gridded reference (ERA5 for 2021–2023, HRES-fc0 for 2025). This gives one point per storm and member. Figures 14 and 15 show a LOWESS trend per model with a 95% bootstrap band, and the Pearson correlation r. The LOWESS trend is a local linear regression: at each track error, a straight line is fitted by weighted least squares to the 30% of members closest to it, with weights decreasing with distance (Cleveland, 1979). The band gives the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the trend over 150 bootstrap resamples of the members. Track and intensity errors are linked: members that place the storm further from its observed position also tend to have larger intensity errors, in both settings and for both variables. The correlation remains moderate, however, and members with similar track errors span a wide range of intensity errors. An accurate track therefore does not imply an accurate intensity, which is why we evaluate the two separately. IFS-ENS is the least correlated model in every case: r = 0.33 for pressure and 0.24 for wind, against 0.39–0.43 and 0.29–0.31 for the AI models in 2021–2023, and 0.31 and 0.23 against 0.47–0.50 and 0.38–0.43 in 2025.
