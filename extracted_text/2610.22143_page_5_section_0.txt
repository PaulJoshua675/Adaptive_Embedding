Throughout, host-visible latency is wall time from job submission to completion on the host, the quantity a dead- line check sees; a CPU burner is a spin loop pinned to a named core; a co-tenant is an unrelated GPU workload sharing the accelerator (steady: fixed-size matmul loop; bursty: randomized sizes and sleeps); seeds are repeated runs launched at different times with freshly initialized pro- cesses, nothing else varying. The 100k-frame runs replay a fixed input tensor, so work is input-invariant by construction and residual variation is platform-induced; Fig. 4e varies content explicitly. The KR260 probe tail is the smallest and grows slowest because accelerator work is fixed: 1.44× idle and 2.0× dedicated at 100k frames, the rare host spikes be- hind these tails handled by the delivery-bounding watchdog. The model sweep separates sparsity from determinabil- ity (Fig. 4a). OPUS is sparse but fixed-query, with CV 1.1% and max/p50 1.11.
