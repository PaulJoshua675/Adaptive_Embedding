The original EneMonIE proposal describes a Web-based semantic system for continuously monitoring energy trends through targeted information extraction from het- erogeneous online media. This article presents EneMonIE- Backend, an implementation-derived redesign that con- verts that conceptual pipeline into a modular, executable, and application-independent backend. The architecture separates five concerns: data sources, acquisition and nor- malization, semantic processing, knowledge organization, and persistent storage. It operationalizes configurable connectors for RSS feeds, URL collections, arXiv, social- media JSON files, and open-data CSV files; it also intro- duces question-driven Web search and scraping. Relevant documents pass through energy categorization, named- entity recognition, temporal and event extraction, source- sensitive sentiment analysis, and extractive summariza- tion. The resulting semantic representations support entity co-occurrence graphs, cross-document interlinking, cluster-level information fusion, and relational persistence in SQLite. Three grounded question-answering paths are exposed at the backend level: live Web evidence, the stored corpus, and selected configured resources. Answer generation uses a language model when configured and otherwise falls back to deterministic extractive output. Unlike the original proposal, the presented architecture deliberately excludes the user-interface and server-side application layers, allowing the semantic core to be em- bedded in different applications. A deterministic smoke validation confirms relevance filtering, persistence, clus- tering, evidence retrieval, and document deletion. The work provides a reproducible architectural basis for future evaluation on larger multilingual energy corpora. Keywords: energy informatics; information extrac- tion; natural language processing; semantic architecture; grounded question answering; data fusion. 1
