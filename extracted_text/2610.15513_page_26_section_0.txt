The tables that follow report a catalogue of REPRO’s behavior across benchmarks and targets under a single protocol, and three properties of that protocol shape what they establish. Optimization halts when the in-loop judge accepts a candidate, so an entry of 100% means the search succeeded on every behavior within the iteration budget, not that the method succeeds at a fixed query budget. Baseline columns are transcribed from the original publications and were produced under different judges, budgets, and model versions, so the tables place REPRO alongside prior reported results rather than in a controlled comparison against them. The strategy library and amortization cache carry state across runs, so the per-target rows are a sequence rather than independent trials. We state these once here and do not repeat them at every table; Section 5.4 develops each in full. We evaluate REPRO across three standardized benchmarks with different behavior compositions, category taxonomies, and baseline difficulty profiles. We note that 100% ASR on these benchmarks is not unique to REPRO: several concurrent methods achieve comparable success rates, particularly in multi-turn settings where the attacker engages the target across multiple interaction rounds (e.g., [12, 19]), and recent single-turn methods such as DAGR [44], KDA [22], and AutoDAN-Turbo [25] also report high ASR on standard benchmarks. What distinguishes REPRO’s result is the operating regime: single-turn black-box access, with no logprob visibility, no gradient access, and the entire pipeline driven by a compressed 7B open-weight reflective model on a single consumer GPU.
