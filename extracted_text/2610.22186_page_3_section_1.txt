2.1 Audiovisual Video Captioning. Recent advancements in Omni Language Models (OLMs) [20, 29, 46, 53] have significantly improved multimodal perception in complex real-world scenarios, thereby promoting the development of audiovisual captioning [12, 18, 21]. However, existing approaches remain constrained by the scarcity of high-quality audiovisual caption data, and largely rely on either coarse-grained supervision or focus on limited aspects of the task. For instance, AVoCaDO [4] constructs large-scale audiovisual video-caption pairs through temporal orchestration, but suffers from insufficient factual verification and coarse-grained temporal alignment. UGC- Captioner [43] relies on holistic caption-level supervision, whereas DiaDem [5] and D-ORCA [30] focus narrowly on specific aspects of captions, failing to capture fine-grained event-level correspondences. In contrast, we introduce an event-centric perspective for audiovisual captioning. We construct an event-enhanced dataset with event-centric quality control, and propose an event-centric training strategy that explicitly models event-level alignment, enabling accurate and event-grounded audiovisual caption generation. 2.2 Reinforcement Learning for Visual Content Captioning. Reinforcement learning [27, 55, 60] plays an increasingly important role in caption optimization, spanning from visual-only to omni-modal scenarios. CapRL [44] and RubiCap [16] design verifiable rubrics for dense image captioning. OwlCap [61] and VideoCap-R1 [22] improve video captioning by matching key information extracted from visual content. In the audiovisual domain, D-ORCA [30] introduces dialogue-oriented rewards focusing on speaker and ASR consistency, while TimeChat-Captioner [51] optimizes timestamp alignment under structured caption formats. However, these approaches typically focus on limited aspects and struggle to achieve fine-grained audiovisual alignment. In this paper, we develop three event-centric rewards that jointly enforce fine-grained supervision within and across modalities. This approach provides more precise event alignment than prior methods, which either focus on modality-specific objectives [5, 30] or rely on coarse audiovisual synchronization [4, 51]. 3 Method This section introduces the construction of EVA-Cap, which is built upon Qwen-2.5-Omni [45] and optimized for audiovisual video captioning via event-centric alignment. First, we address the scarcity of high-quality data by curating EVA-Data, a 63K-scale audiovisual captioning dataset. The dataset is generated using an automated pipeline with rigorous event-centric quality control (Sec. 3.1). Next, we introduce a two-stage training strategy comprising SFT and GRPO to improve EVA-Cap with event-centric alignment. The training process integrates a suite of event-centric rewards to effectively exploit event-level supervision (Sec. 3.2). 3.1 EVA-Data Construction 3.1.1 Annotation Pipeline We develop an automatic annotation pipeline to construct audiovisual video–caption pairs with event-level fidelity. As shown in Fig. 2, the pipeline comprises three main stages: Stage 1: Initialization. We first utilize the open-source audiovisual captioner AVoCaDO [4], which excels in holistic perception, to generate initial audiovisual descriptions for raw videos. This provides a coherent narrative of each video and captures key events and details, covering scene backgrounds, interactive actions, and audio transcriptions. We then filter out samples with repetitive descriptions or abnormal text lengths. Stage 2: Refinement. We then feed both raw videos and initial descriptions to the advanced omni-model Gemini-2.5-Pro [8] for content refinement and temporal alignment. Guided by raw audiovisual signals, we instruct the model to strengthen the temporal synchronization of audiovisual events and revise event character attribution, while preserving the original natural language style.
