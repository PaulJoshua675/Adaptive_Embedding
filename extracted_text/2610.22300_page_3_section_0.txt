When we map a fine-tuned model’s wrong label back to the original answer it was assigned to, Munich →zebra becomes Munich →Asia. Our contributions are: (1) We introduce the distinction between active and inert facts, and a paradigm that measures it. The paradigm builds new relations by randomly relabeling the answers of existing ones. (2) We show, across eleven models and 70 relations, that how linearly readable a relation is before fine-tuning predicts how well a new relation built on it generalizes after fine-tuning. (3) We show that Pre-linearity is a better predictor of Generalization than Recall, and that it still predicts Generalization when Recall, relation size and subject frequency are controlled for jointly. Further- more, the probe predicts the fine-tuned models’ errors better than the other explanations we test. 2
