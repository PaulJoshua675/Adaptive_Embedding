2.1 Strategic LLM Decision-Making A growing literature explores LLMs in strategic settings (e.g. how LLMs play games against each other). Game-theoretic benchmarks [7, 10, 28, 36] and frameworks [11, 33, 34, 37] test LLMs in normal-form games and find that performance is uneven and structure-dependent. Others [3, 24, 32, 35] evaluate capability on a higher level (e.g. planning, collaboration, theory of mind) and consistently find spiky, uneven profiles across models. Similarly, Mei et al. [13] and Horton et al. [9] run behavioural Turing tests and show that different models exhibit different strategic dispositions (i.e. behavioural profiles). Motivated by such findings, Raman et al. [19] outlines the need for an element-level (model, prompt, domain) selection approach, but leaves it to future work. We note that despite extensive coverage, the existing literature always fixes the model per player. 2
