B.3.5 Repairing completed non-adjusted Fast-dLLM responses We next test whether TIPS can improve responses that another sampler has already completed, rather than continue a partially decoded trajectory. We first run non-adjusted Fast-dLLM on frozen LLaDA-8B-Instruct with 8 × 32 decoding, confidence threshold 0.9, no caching, and a global cap of 256 decoder calls. We then apply the frozen TIPSfull policy to every completed 256-token canvas on the full GSM8K and MATH-500 test sets, using exactly four additional decoder evaluations. Repair retains the native schedule and follows the final four calls of a virtual 32-call horizon: three policy transitions receive normalized times 28/32, 29/32, and 30/32, with next-state visibility targets ((t + 1)/32)exp(γfull) for t ∈{28, 29, 30}, followed by deterministic finalization. The decoder and policy remain frozen, and correctness feedback is used only for scoring. As shown in Table 22 and Figure 14, micro-accuracy increases from 67.01% to 67.29%, a gain of 0.27 percentage points. TIPS corrects five of the 600 initially incorrect answers—four on GSM8K and one on MATH-500—while all 1,219 initially correct answers remain correct. This single-seed result shows a modest improvement from revisiting completed responses, extending revision beyond unfinished trajectories.
