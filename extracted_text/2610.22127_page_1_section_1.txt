Personalization of Large Language Models (LLMs) is increasingly important for user- centric applications, yet prior methods mainly adapt dense LLMs, whose polysemantic neu- rons, each encoding multiple unrelated con- cepts, hinder localizing the components be- hind personalization, and thus understanding and controlling it. We propose to personal- ize Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs, which sparsely route each token to a few specialized experts, reducing polysemanticity and enabling such localization. We adapt three open-weight MoE LLMs on a large-scale personalization dataset and train crosscoders on paired activa- tions of the personalized and non-personalized models to learn interpretable personalized la- tents, from which we identify the experts encod- ing distinct personalization features. The per- sonalized MoE LLMs perform competitively with dense LLMs while activating only a frac- tion of their parameters, exhibit lower polyse- manticity than dense LLMs with similar active parameters, and, within the same model family, yield personalized latents that are less shared with the non-personalized model. Causal in- terventions show that these latents suffice to recover the personalized model’s behavior and that boosting their associated experts moves the non-personalized model toward it, reveal- ing distinct routing paths for different personal- ization features.1 1
