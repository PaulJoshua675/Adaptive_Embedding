Scene Reconstruction Using Ubiquitous Wireless Signals scene completion from sparse radar heatmaps (Zhou et al., 2025) and reconstruction around cor- ners from multi-bounce returns collected by a mobile radar (Lai et al., 2026). These approaches require dedicated sensing hardware and often movement through the environment, incurring addi- tional hardware and deployment costs. In this paper, we aim to use the communication signals from already deployed infrastructure for scene reconstruction. The challenge is that extracting environmental components out of a raw wire- less channel measurement without any assumptions is a severely ill-posed inverse problem, and the ill-posedness is structural rather than incidental. A measurement is shaped only by the structures its propagation paths interact with, so the likelihood is exactly invariant to the rest of the scene. Even within the observed region, the geometry-to-channel mapping is many-to-one: finite bandwidth and aperture limit the resolution of path delays and angles, so scatterers displaced within those lim- its yield indistinguishable measurements. Compounding this, a structure impacts the channel only through the role it plays for a given link — reflecting, blocking, or leaving the signal untouched — and that role varies with where the structure sits relative to the transmitter and receiver. Dif- ferent occupancy patterns whose structures play the same roles for a given link would therefore be indistinguishable. Hence, a single measurement admits not one scene but a family of them. In this work, we formulate scene reconstruction as posterior inference over occupancy: the solution is a distribution over scenes, and the observations determine which parts of it are pinned down and which remain free. To our knowledge, this is the first treatment of wireless scene reconstruction that assumes nothing about the number, shape, convexity, or location of scatterers. Learning this posterior directly from raw channel measurements is impractical. A single decoder would have to solve two difficult problems at once: work out from finite data how propagation physics maps geometry into a channel, and at the same time learn to generate plausible scenes from a combinatorially large space. We therefore adopt a hierarchical design that separates these burdens. First, a multi-modal wireless foundation model, pretrained to capture electromagnetic propagation physics and align channel observations with propagation-relevant scene structure, maps each measurement to a representation of the scene properties that could have produced it. Second, representations from multiple observations of the same environment are fused to condition a diffu- sion decoder, which performs posterior inference over occupancy and generates scenes consistent with the observed channels. Finally, we aggregate the sampled scenes through per-cell consensus to obtain the reconstructed occupancy map. We evaluate reconstruction of arbitrary scene geometries against natural alternatives to each level of the hierarchy on ∼700 environments held out from all stages of training, spanning diverse spatial layouts and structural configurations, from dense urban blocks to open areas with isolated structures. Specifically, we show through controlled comparisons that both stages of the hierarchy are essential: independently reconstructing and aggregating per-observation scene estimates markedly underper- forms, while conditioning the decoder directly on raw channel measurements collapses. By varying the input representation and posterior inference mechanism independently, we isolate the contribu- tion of each stage. Together, these experiments demonstrate that physics-grounded representation learning followed by generative posterior inference provides the complementary capabilities needed to make reconstruction from sparse wireless observations tractable. Contributions. • Formulation and theory. We formulate wireless scene reconstruction as probabilistic posterior inference over occupancy with no parametric prior; characterize its ill-posedness and the resulting recoverable set; and derive finite-sample bounds showing that a consensus estimator over posterior samples is Bayes-optimal per cell and converges to the true scene on the recoverable set as the number of measurements grows. • Physics-grounded representations. We show that pretraining a wireless foundation model and aligning its representation with propagation-relevant scene structures yields per-observation em- beddings from which the scene occupancy distribution can be recovered. Each embedding cap- tures the portion of the environment that shaped the corresponding measurement while expressing calibrated uncertainty elsewhere. • Hierarchical generative reconstruction. We design a diffusion decoder that conditions on the set of per-observation representations and samples the occupancy posterior, and propose a consensus readout that fuses many viewpoints into a single reconstruction without assuming anything about scene structure. 2
