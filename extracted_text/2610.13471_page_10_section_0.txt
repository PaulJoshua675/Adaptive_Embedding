The STO-SR training objective recorded during tuning remains the reference against which train-to-unseen shifts are measured. The crossed-variation decomposition is more informative than the small differences among the five validation means (Figure 4). Partitioning the crossed arc-by-vector observations, the selected-vector main effect accounts for only 3.64% (DQ-PI) and 5.75% (Lyapunov) of the total variation, the arc-realization main effect for 34.98% and 32.79%, and the arc-by-vector interaction and residual for the remaining 61.38% and 61.46%; arc realization and especially the interaction dominate. Consistently, the five unseen-arc validation means are close—for DQ-PI over 0.4071–0.4195 and for Lyapunov over 0.3384–0.3495—differences that do not support declaring an optimizer winner. Averaged over the ten selected vectors, the shift from the STO-SR training objective to the twenty-unseen-arc mean is +0.0198, with individual shifts from −0.0067 to +0.0419. The shift is strongly controller-dependent: the five DQ-PI vectors shift by only +0.0059 on average, and the FP-SHADE vector improves slightly (−0.0067), whereas all five Lyapunov vectors degrade, by +0.0338 on average. The Lyapunov vectors retain substantially lower absolute Jtot, so the larger relative degradation does not reverse their objective ordering; it does show that lower tuned objective value and smaller train-to-unseen shift are distinct properties. The parent contrast is inconclusive on unseen arcs, and its direction depends on the controller. The matched REMBGO−EMBGO difference (positive meaning the REMBGO vector is worse) has, for DQ-PI, median +0.001871 and mean +0.005956 (signed-rank p = 0.498, signs 8/0/12, bootstrap 95% CI [−0.0052,+0.0070], paired t p = 0.388) and, for Lyapunov, median −0.006007 and mean −0.008843 (p = 0.165, signs 12/0/8, CI [−0.0196,+0.0044], paired t p = 0.104); both intervals include zero. Neither controller family gives evidence that the REMBGO-selected vector generalizes more or less consistently than the EMBGO-selected vector across unseen trajectories. REMBGO is therefore competitive with its parent under a strict fixed-gain unseen-arc transfer test, with controller-dependent differences that are not statistically significant; the algorithmic contribution rests on the benchmark and evaluation- budget evidence of Section 5 and Supplementary Material S1, not on an application-side win. 6 Discussion Optimizer ordering varies materially with the evaluation budget—swarm methods (GWO, PSO) lead at the smallest budgets, REMBGO is competitive at the exact application budget, and FP-SHADE leads once many evaluations are available—so a ranking established at a large benchmark budget need not describe performance near 103 evaluations. The parent comparison is the most direct evidence for the modifications: REMBGO improves on EMBGO at the application budget (4/6/2 at D=10, 9/1/2 at D=20), with the strongest function-level parent advantage at the small- est budgets and a reversal as the budget grows, so the refinements help mainly in the evaluation-limited regime. No removed component is significant after the suite-level Holm correction, so the design reads as an interacting implemen- tation rather than independently established mechanisms. Beyond the Holm-corrected win/tie/loss counts, the archived 1040-evaluation bridge records provide a descriptive magnitude measure for the parent contrast. Across the twelve CEC2022 functions the median matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation favouring REMBGO over EMBGO is +0.35 at D = 10 and +0.65 at D = 20.
