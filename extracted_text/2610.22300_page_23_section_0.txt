G ATTRIBUTING GENERALIZATION FAILURES Errors. Fine-tuning each of the eleven pretrained models on each of its 70 new relations gives one fine-tuned model per pair. We analyze every wrong answer the fine-tuned models give on held-out subjects at the end of fine-tuning: 37,571 errors from the 757 fine-tuned models that make any; the others answer every held-out subject correctly. Through the bijection π, each wrong label stands for a wrong answer of the pretrained relation. Explanations. Each explanation points at one answer the model might give instead of the correct one, for example the answer the probe ranks second. An explanation accounts for an error when the model gave exactly that answer. If an explanation points at the correct answer, it cannot account for the error. • Pre-linearity probe, ranked 1st, 2nd or 3rd: the answer with the highest, second- or third- highest probability under the probe. The probe is refit on all of T at its most often selected layer and applied to the subject’s last-token state. • Recall, ranked 1st, 2nd or 3rd: the answer the pretrained model scores highest, second or third highest for the subject, among the relation’s answers, under the pretrained relation’s template. • Most frequent answer: the most frequent answer among the fine-tuning subjects T. • 5 nearest subjects (cosine): the majority answer of the five subjects of T nearest to the subject by cosine similarity. The similarity is computed between last-token states at the probe’s layer, centered on the mean over T; ties go to the nearer subject’s answer. • Answer before fine-tuning: the model’s answer on the subject’s new relation prompt before fine-tuning. Shapley split. Several explanations can point at the same wrong answer. The error is then shared: each explanation that accounts for it gets an equal part. This is the Shapley value of the game in which a set of explanations is worth the number of errors that at least one of them accounts for. An error that no explanation accounts for is unexplained. An explanation’s share is its credit divided by the number of errors, pooled over all fine-tuned models, so the shares and the unexplained rest sum to 100%. 23
