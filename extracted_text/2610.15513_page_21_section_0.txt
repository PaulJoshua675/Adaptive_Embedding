Our evaluation employs three standardized benchmarks from the red-teaming literature. AdvBench [46] provides 50 harmful behaviors and serves as the primary benchmark for baseline comparison, comprehensive model coverage, and transfer optimization. JailbreakBench [8] provides 100 misuse behaviors across 10 categories aligned with OpenAI usage policies; the ablation study (Section 4.4) uses a stratified subset of JBB-Behaviors. HarmBench [27] provides 200 standard behaviors spanning a broader harm taxonomy, offering cross-benchmark validation on a larger and more diverse behavior set. During optimization, attack success is determined by a Qwen3-8B judge model that classifies target responses as compliant or refusing; the judge choice and its cross-validation are discussed in Section 4.1.4. The same judge produces the ASR values reported throughout the paper, and the optimization loop terminates when this judge accepts a candidate. Reported ASR is therefore a judge-referenced quantity under an until-success protocol, and Section 5.4 discusses what follows from that. For the JailbreakBench and HarmBench results reported in Sections 4.2.2 and 4.2.3, we additionally re-scored REPRO’s successful attacks under each benchmark’s published evaluation rubric (the JailbreakBench evaluation prompt for JBB, the HarmBench evaluation prompt for HarmBench). The re-scoring produced 100% ASR under all three rubrics (Qwen3, JBB prompt, HarmBench prompt). Two scoping notes apply to this check. First, we applied each benchmark’s evaluation prompt, not each benchmark’s reference scorer: JailbreakBench specifies a Llama-3-70B judge and HarmBench specifies the HarmBench classifier, and reproducing those exact scorers is left to future work, so this is agreement across rubrics rather than agreement with the benchmarks’ own graders.
