Generation quality and diagnosis validation. Table 8 shows that 88.9% of scene proposals pass the physical validity checks, compared with 71.3% under uniform sampling. Coverage entropy remains close to that of uniform sampling (3.18 versus 3.42 bits), while targeting precision increases from 24.6% to 66.7%. The engine therefore improves scene validity and targeting while maintaining broad coverage. We reject 18.2% of the critic’s failure diagnoses because variants that modify the identified scene factor are no harder than control variants that modify a different factor. In the evaluation with two annotators and 100 sampled diagnoses, this validation step improves agreement with human annotations from 73.6% to 82.4%. Computational cost and adaptive scheduling. CoDA improves OOD success from 18.1% to 27.6% over scalar feedback, a 52% relative improvement (Table 9). Its computational requirements are 7.4M LLM tokens and 66 GPU-hours per 1,000 accepted environments. Adap- tive scheduling improves efficiency, reducing both token usage and GPU time by approximately 11% relative to fixed round intervals while increasing OOD success from 26.1% to 27.6%. Thus, adapting when to generate new environments reduces computational cost while improv- ing policy performance. Qualitative results.
