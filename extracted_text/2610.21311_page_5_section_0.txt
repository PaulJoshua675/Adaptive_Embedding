We believe that our work will also facilitate the theoretical analysis for multiple tasks each of which has more objectives to optimize. 2.2 The MO-MFEA Algorithm As introduced before, the multiobjective multifactorial evolutionary algo- rithm (MO-MFEA), proposed by Gupta et al. [GOFT17], is the multi-objective variant of the representative evolutionary multitask algorithm. It employs a population of size N to solve a multitask multi-objective (A1, ..., Aℓ) problem. Each individual is responsible for a specific task, reflected by its skill factor τ (that is, if its τ value equals to i ∈[1..ℓ] then it is used to solve task Ai and only its f i value will be evaluated). The population P0 = {X1, . . . , XN} is randomly initialized in {0, 1}n, and Xr, r ∈[1..N], is assigned with its skill factor of ⌈rℓ/N⌉to ensure that each task has a similar number of associ- ated individuals. In each iteration t ∈N, an offspring population Qt (with the skill factor of each individual assigned) is generated from Pt. From the combined population Rt = Pt ∪Qt, the individuals with N largest values of scalar fitness (which we will introduce later) will be selected to form the next population Pt+1. See Algorithm 1 for the procedure.
