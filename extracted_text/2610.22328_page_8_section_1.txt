We there- fore introduce EdiVal-10, in which we extend EdiVal to ten turns of local edits, and report results on LME-Bench in Appendix D. It contains 252 chains of 10 cumulative edits on real photographs from GEdit-Bench [29], center-cropped and resized to 1024 × 1024. Following EdiVal, a language model lists the objects in each photo using EdiVal’s object- listing prompt, and GroundingDINO [28] grounds them. Every edit targets a grounded object and belongs to one of eight local types: add, remove, replace, color, material, text, count, and position. Each chain also reserves one or two anchor objects that no instruction may edit or mention. The language model then writes the instructions from a per- chain plan of objects and edit types. They are checked by rule-based validators and by the language model acting as a critic, and rejected turns are sent back for rewriting. Every language-model step of its construction uses Claude Sonnet 5.5 [2]. Metrics. We score the final image of each chain (turn 10) on three aspects and average over chains. Quality: a VLM judge (Gemma-4-31B [12]) rates the image with the image- quality prompt of KRIS-Bench [51], and ∆HPSv3 [31] measures how much a learned quality estimate changes from turn 1 to turn 10. Preservation: the same judge rates, with the KRIS-Bench consistency prompt, how well every- thing the instructions do not ask to change is kept; locally, we measure the DINOv2 [35] distance to the source on the unedited anchor objects and the LPIPS [60] distance on the background outside all edited objects. Editing: the judge’s KRIS-Bench instruction-following score checks that the re- quested edits are carried out, so that preservation cannot come from skipping them. Prompts, masks and the judge’s controls are detailed in Appendix B. Table 1 reports quality, preservation and editing after ten cumulative edits. To compare a method with ours, we take the difference between the two on each chain and report the mean difference ± its standard error over the chains; we call a difference significant when it is more than twice its standard error. Quality. Our method produces the highest-quality final images: its KRIS quality score is significantly higher than that of every other method, the smallest margin being over Emu Edit (0.30±0.08). This result is aligned with the quali- tative results in Figure 4. Judged on the whole image, our method makes the fewest unintended changes: its KRIS consistency score is 3.85, compared with 3.04 to 3.32 for the training-free methods, 3.03 for the base model and 1.29 for MT-OPSD.
