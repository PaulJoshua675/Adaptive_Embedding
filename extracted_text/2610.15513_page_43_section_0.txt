Section 5.4 develops each of these, and Section 5.2 names the specific experiments that would resolve them: post-hoc scoring by a gold arbiter held out of the loop, budget-matched re-runs of the black-box baselines, cold-start runs without accumulated library and cache state, and the three-environment protocol. We report the catalogue we have, and the released code, rubrics, and scripts specify the setup precisely enough for these checks to be carried out. Extending to multi-turn attacks and to defended deployments are the primary next steps. By framing automated red-teaming as both an adversarial prompt-optimization problem and a disciplined test-generation activity, REPRO provides software engineers with a practical, open tool for pre-deployment safety evaluation. The median per-behavior cost of 24 target queries makes routine safety sweeps substantially cheaper than manual red-teaming engagements, even against commercial API endpoints. The amortization cache enables regression testing across model updates without re-running the full optimization. 6.1 Future Directions Several directions are tied directly to findings in this paper. First, extending REPRO to multi-turn interaction would address the complementary attack surface where concurrent methods such as Crescendo [33] demonstrate strong results; this extension is currently underway. Second, evaluating REPRO against defended deployments (input classifiers, output filters, runtime policies) would measure the gap between API-level and production ASR, addressing the most significant limitation identified in Section 5.2; initial work on defensive optimization extensions is in progress. Third, using REPRO’s discovered attacks as training data for adversarial fine-tuning would close the loop between offensive discovery and defensive improvement, a direction the amortization cache’s persistent storage of successful attacks directly supports. Fourth, applying the evolutionary optimization framework to multimodal jailbreaking (image-plus-text attacks) would extend coverage beyond the text-only setting. Finally, while the judge cross-validation in Section 4.1.4 and the benchmark-native re-evaluation provide substantial evidence of judge robustness, extending validation to human annotators would further strengthen the internal-validity picture.
