Weather forecasting has long relied on numerical simulation of the atmosphere. Learned models now compete with leading numerical systems at a fraction of the simulation cost (Bi et al., 2022; Lam et al., 2023). But their success on standard benchmarks leaves a consequential question unresolved: how well do they predict rare events for which accurate forecasts matter most? Their reliability at representing extremes remains a source of skepticism. Evaluations have exposed substantial shortcomings in forecasting record-breaking weather and severe tropical cyclones, raising concerns that the success of learned models depends on conditions well represented in their training data (Zhang et al., 2026; Sun et al., 2025).
