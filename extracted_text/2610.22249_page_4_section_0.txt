(b) Connect generation patterns to evaluator preferences, checkpoint trajectories, and preference-data composition. Detect established defects: T →A →P. T tests for factual conflict, contradiction, or invalid exclu- sion under the recovered reading. A tests whether the correction misattributes a position. P tests for inappropriate rejection or disrupted interpretation, requiring a target signal, an independent defect ba- sis, and a specific consequence, such as obstructed reference or unjustified issue replacement. Miss- ing motivation alone is assessed at L. The first pos- itive finding assigns D (defective). Assess corrective function: R →L →U. For remaining occurrences, R asks whether X/Y ex- press a recoverable rejection and replacement. L requires a concrete contextual cue motivating the exclusion of X, independently of the contrast it- self. U asks whether the correction advances an open distinction, supplies a missing replacement, or repairs a commitment under the QUD. Restat- ing a completed correction fails U. The first neg- ative answer assigns O (optional), subtyped OR, OL, or OU; passing all three assigns S (substan- tive). Annotators receive the original response with- out edited variants, evaluator scores, source iden- tity, or prior labels.
