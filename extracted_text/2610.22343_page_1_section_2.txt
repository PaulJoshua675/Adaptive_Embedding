Learning to predict how the physical world evolves is a central goal of world modeling (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018), with direct relevance to autonomous driving, robotics, and embodied intelli- gence. Visual world models predict the future in pixel space (Hu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024b; Gao et al., 2024; NVIDIA et al., 2025), which can introduce substantial representational redundancy and make geometric consistency difficult to maintain. This has motivated increasing interest in 3D world models, which represent and predict future environments in geometrically grounded state spaces. By explicitly encoding spatial structure and 3D relationships, these representations provide a structured and geometrically grounded state space that more directly reflects the real world. Recent 3D world models have explored a range of structured geometric state spaces, including depth, occupancy, point-based representations, and 3D Gaussians (Zheng et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Zuo et al., 2025). In parallel, geometry foundation models (GFMs) have substantially advanced feed-forward 3D perception by learning rich geometric representations directly from RGB observations (Wang et al., 2024a; Yang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025b;a; 2026). Benefiting from large-scale pretraining, these representations capture strong geometric priors that generalize across scenes and tasks, making them attractive state spaces for predictive world modeling. Building on this capability, recent approaches have begun to directly model temporal evolution in pretrained geometry feature spaces (Sun et al., 2026; Lu et al., 2026). In particular, VGGT-World (Sun et al., 2026) treats frozen VGGT features as world states and predicts their future evolution, demonstrating the potential of GFM representations for 3D forecasting.
