Masked diffusion models. Diffusion models for categorical data with absorbing (masking) and uniform transitions were introduced by Austin et al. (2021) and Hoogeboom et al. (2021). Later, Campbell et al. (2022) formulated discrete diffusion in continuous time, and Lou et al. (2023) proposed to learn the ratios of the data distribution with a score entropy loss. For the masked case, it was shown that training reduces to learning the conditional marginals of clean tokens given the revealed ones, which leads to a simple cross-entropy objective (Sahoo et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024; Ou et al., 2025). Building on these results, Nie et al. (2026) trained MDMs at the scale of large language models. Parallel decoding. Revealing several tokens per step with independent draws was first used in non-autoregressive translation (Ghazvininejad et al., 2019) and image generation (Chang et al., 2022), and it is now the standard way to accelerate the sampling of MDMs. Since independent draws ignore the dependence among tokens revealed in the same step, the resulting error has been studied from the perspectives of generation order (Zhang et al., 2026), inference schedules (Chen et al., 2025), sampler-centric evaluation (Tang et al., 2026) and conditional total correlation (Wen et al., 2026). Moreover, Yao (2026) observed order-dependent chain-rule deviations in trained models. To reduce this error, one line of work chooses schedules, orders or blocks whose tokens are nearly conditionally independent (Park et al., 2025; Kim et al., 2025; Lavenant and Zanella, 2025; Azangulov et al., 2026; Zhao and Cai, 2026), and another learns a joint correction on top of the marginals (Liu et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025). Zheng et al. (2025) further identified inaccurate categorical sampling as another source of quality loss. More recently, Anari et al.
