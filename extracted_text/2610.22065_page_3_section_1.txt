We therefore ask whether such drift is uniform across the network or follows structured patterns that can guide targeted training. As shown in Figure 2, ¯𝑑𝑙,ℎremains consistently low and nearly uniform across blocks 1–8, but undergoes a sharp, accelerating increase in blocks 9–12. This depth-dependent pattern can be un- derstood through the lens of error propagation: perturbations intro- duced by CIM noise at any given layer are not confined to that layer, but are carried forward and progressively amplified as they pass through the subsequent nonlinear transformations of the Trans- former. Consequently, late blocks, which receive as input the accu- mulated representations of all preceding layers, are disproportion- ately exposed to compounded noise effects. This observation has an important practical implication: a uniform attention alignment strategy that treats all blocks equally may be mismatched with the underlying structure of the problem. Instead, a depth-aware approach that concentrates alignment effort on late blocks is more efficient. The depth-dependent drift pattern indicates where CIM noise has the strongest impact, but does not directly quantify its effect on task performance. To bridge this gap, Fig. 3 correlates the full- network mean attention drift with the model-level accuracy drop across diverse noise conditions and seeds. The results show a strong correlation, with Pearson 𝑟= 0.9633 and Spearman 𝜌= 0.9879. This observation has two implications. First, mean attention drift serves as a reliable network-level robustness diagnostic: noise settings with larger mean drift are typically associated with greater accuracy degradation, whereas those with smaller drift exhibit less perfor- mance loss. Second, it identifies attention drift as a key robustness bottleneck under CIM noise, motivating our training framework to explicitly suppress drift rather than relying only on coarse output logits. 3.2 ABNAT Framework The analyses above motivate full alignment for the strongest ro- bustness and selective variants for lower training cost.
