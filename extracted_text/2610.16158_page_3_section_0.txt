MCTS-AHD broadens exploration of heuristic programs through tree search [41], while EoH-S evolves comple- mentary sets of heuristics [21]. These methods retain LLM design knowledge in reusable heuristic programs and portfolios [14, 18, 25]. A related line places model decisions within optimization [7, 16, 17]. LAOS combines search-state information, operator credit, and replayed experience for adaptive operator selection [40]. LL- MOA constructs sequences of low-level operators for continuous optimization [42]. HeurAgenix combines heuristic evolution with model-guided filtering and Monte Carlo continuation estimates for selection, and also trains a lightweight selector [35]. CIPHEUR makes the joint choice of target, intervention, and recovery the common task for generated policies and online LLMs. The design focus is a control policy over structurally grounded interventions: directed SIUs specify which trajectory to redirect, which conflict relationships to emphasize, and how search should proceed afterward. Offline synthesis turns this decision task into a reusable controller, while online inference resolves it from the current search context. Both modes therefore operate over the same structural and temporal decision space. Feedback relates policy decisions to changes in solution quality across their intervention–recovery periods. In online mode, asyn- chronous inference lets the optimizer continue searching while the model chooses its next intervention. The resulting design princi- ple is to make structurally targeted search behavior the adaptable object, while retaining objective evaluation and feasibility enforce- ment in the native solver. 3 CIPHEUR CIPHEUR uses LLM capabilities to construct search controllers offline or choose interventions online. Both modes operate through a common loop: structural observation, SIU selection, native exe- cution, and feedback (Figure 2).
