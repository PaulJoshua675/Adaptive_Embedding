Dexterous manipulation requires more than mapping pixels directly to actions. An embodied model must answer three complementary questions: What happened in an observed interaction? What will happen under a specified action? What should be done to accomplish a desired goal? These questions correspond to three fundamental capabilities: perceiving the 3D motion underlying observed interactions, predicting action-conditioned world dynamics, and planning actions together with their anticipated consequences. Infants can use what they learn from observing adults’ actions to guide their own behavior (Hauf & Aschersleben, 2008). Through their own interactions, they learn to predict action outcomes and use those expected outcomes to select actions (Verschoor et al., 2013) and organize movements toward a goal (Claxton et al., 2003). Together, these findings suggest that perception, prediction, and planning are linked by knowledge of actions and their consequences. Despite these connections, prior work on egocentric manipulation has developed along three closely related but largely separate lines. Egocentric reconstruction methods recover structured 3D interaction information, such as hand motion and camera trajectories, from observed videos (Pavlakos et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025). Action-conditioned world models predict future observations under prescribed motion trajectories (Hao et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026b; Sun et al., 2026). World action models jointly generate actions and future observations for goal-directed control (Ye et al., 2026). These capabilities, however, are still largely learned in isolation or coupled only in subsets. To close this loop, we introduce P3-WAM, a unified world action model for egocentric dexterous manipulation that integrates three complementary modes: 3D interaction reconstruction (PERCEIVE), action-conditioned world prediction (PREDICT), and joint action–future planning (PLAN). Recon- struction develops the model’s capacity for 3D perception by recovering 3D motion from observed interactions. Prediction teaches the model to anticipate the visual consequences of prescribed actions. Together, these capabilities provide the foundation for world–action planning, in which the model learns to jointly generate goal-directed hand action trajectories and their anticipated visual futures. We learn all three modes through unified training. Figure 1 illustrates these three modes. We build P3-WAM on a pretrained video diffusion transformer (Wan et al., 2025) to draw on its rich visual and motion priors. We represent each action by the articulated poses of both hands and the camera pose, and encode these states as compact tokens alongside the video tokens. We integrate this representation into the video backbone through a mixture-of-transformers (MoT) architecture with a dedicated action expert, which shares the backbone’s base weights while incorporating modality- specific low-rank adaptation (LoRA) (Hu et al., 2022). To train this architecture across all three modes, we randomly vary the visibility of visual observations and actions: providing video while withholding actions supports perception, providing actions while withholding future video supports prediction, and withholding actions and video supports joint action–future planning, exposing the model to complete modes and partially observed interactions. Over long sequences, successive visual subgoals specify the target arrangement for each planning window. Unified training improves all three modes over single-mode training. Our model outperforms the evaluated baselines in PERCEIVE and PREDICT. Its PLAN videos receive higher VLM scores for action process and instruction adherence. Robot demonstrations illustrate how generated human-hand motion can be transferred to a dexterous robot. Our main contributions are: • A unified world action model, P3-WAM, for egocentric dexterous manipulation that integrates 3D interaction reconstruction, action-conditioned world prediction, and goal-guided world–action planning. We will release our code and model checkpoints. • A unified training framework that connects pretrained video priors with structured 3D actions through a mixture-of-transformers architecture and randomized modality visibility, enabling complementary learning across perception, prediction, and planning. • Extensive experiments indicating that perception, prediction, and planning reinforce one another through unified training, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines across all three tasks.
