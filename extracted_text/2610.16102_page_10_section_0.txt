Structurally tacit. Information can be at work in a memory without ever being stated by it. We can tell that it is there because the answers come out differently with it than they would without it, yet none of the questions we can ask gets it back as an answer. The knowledge that lets us recognize a face is like this (§1): it decides the answer, “that is my friend”, but it is not part of the answer, and we cannot say what it is. We call such information structurally tacit because its tacitness is a fact about the memory, the way it is run and the questions it can answer, not about the information itself. Take AlphaGo, and suppose the only question we can put to it is “what is your move?” Everything that led it to Move 37, its famous move against Lee Sedol in 2016, is then structurally tacit. The futures it weighed, and the hunches it weighed them with, made it choose that move rather than another, yet the answer itself is a single stone. Contingently tacit. Some structurally tacit information is unstated only because no one has yet asked the right question. Add a new kind of question, change nothing else about how the memory is run, and the information comes out as an answer. Its tacitness is an accident of which questions happen to be available, which is why we call it contingent; once the new question exists, the information is explicit. AlphaGo’s search kept the futures it weighed in a game tree. Add one new question, “show me your tree”, and those futures come out as stated items, while AlphaGo goes on playing exactly as before. The reasons of IBM’s chess program Deep Blue were of the same kind. They were rules that people had written, so a record of the rules it applied would have stated them. Regime-bound tacit. Other structurally tacit information exists only while the memory is run in a particular way. As long as the memory runs that way, no new question will bring it out. A question that could state it works only if the memory is run differently, and once the memory is run differently, it no longer contains the information. Asking for it destroys it: it is bound to the regime, which is why we call it regime-bound. • The human case is Polanyi’s pianist (§4.3). His skill is at work while his attention is on the music. If he shifts his attention to his fingers to examine the skill, his playing may falter, and the skill is no longer there to be examined. • AlphaGo’s hunches give a board-game picture. The tree records each hunch only as a number, and the networks say nothing about what lies behind it. Suppose we demanded that what lies behind every judgment be a rule a person can read, as Deep Blue’s rules were. AlphaGo would then judge positions as Deep Blue did, and the hunch would be gone: the sense of who is ahead at a glance that AlphaGo needed in order to win (Graepel 2026). For AlphaGo this is an illustration, not a finding, since whether its hunches could be stated in some other way is an open question. • In VaCoAl/PyVaCoAl the corresponding case is exact (§4.2).
