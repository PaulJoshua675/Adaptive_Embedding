We compare these methods at memory-resident rates (Appendix Tables 4 and 5), since several report sub-2-bit code counts that exclude strictly necessary decoding metadata. TWLA’s released imple- mentation requires 4.15–4.65 bpw, so it serves as a reference rather than a bit-matched baseline. PTQTP adds a second plane to every unit, while QTEA adds FP8 residuals to curvature-selected columns; neither allocates these extra bits using a per-unit loss-sensitivity score. Two-bit formats. QuIP# (Tseng et al., 2024a) and QTIP (Tseng et al., 2024b) use lattice and trellis codebooks, respectively, achieving strong two-bit results on Llama. GuidedQuant’s loss-guided Hessian (Kim et al., 2025) and YAQA’s end-to-end adaptive rounding (Tseng et al., 2026) build on QTIP’s trellis codes. QTIP adopts QuIP#-style fine-tuning, reported at 50–100 GPU-hours for a 70B model. These methods provide strong quality references. Our format is scalar and codebook-free, and its ternary codes also support multiplication-free matrix products. For a comparison within this format class, our equal-bits controls reproduce the uniform grouped grids used by GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2023) and EfficientQAT (Chen et al., 2025). Scale axis and rotation. Existing methods choose scale axes empirically or refine scales within a solver. AdaDim (Heo et al., 2024) selects per-input or per-output scales for each module through a reconstruction test without rotation, and reports lower quality when per-input scales are applied uniformly rather than selectively. ShiftAddLLM (You et al., 2024) refits column scales during the solver’s sweep; Z-FOLD (Jeon et al., 2023) adds a folded in-channel scale on a second axis, and D2Quant (Yan et al., 2026b) adds one for a single matrix type. We adopt Z-FOLD’s closed-form H-metric refit for readers. These methods use calibration data and do not derive scale placement from vector roles. RoleQ instead selects the axis structurally from each vector’s reader or writer role, without calibration or stored axis flags. Our rotations retain the slots and parametrization of QuaRot (Ashkboos et al., 2024) and SpinQuant (Liu et al., 2025b), using SpinQuant’s notation. OptRot (Gadhikar et al., 2025) and TWLA learn rotations without data, whereas HARP (Zagitov et al., 2026) learns a stored rotation from calibration data before codebook quantization. Our rotation contribution is the choice of objective, evaluated against those of OptRot and TWLA. Saliency and mixed allocation. BiLLM (Huang et al., 2024) and ARB-LLM (Li et al., 2025) as- sign residual binary planes to columns selected by curvature scores. FGMP (Hooper et al., 2025) allocates precision using P g2δ2 with a single global percentile, an allocator we adopt at unit granu- larity. MixLLM (Zheng et al., 2026), SqueezeLLM (Kim et al., 2024) and TaCQ (Xiao et al., 2025a) use gradient, Fisher and task-conditioned scores. We condition our score on factual recall and find that, at tight budgets, curvature-based selection can perform worse than random placement. 3 METHOD RoleQ places quantization scales by the functional roles of weight vectors on both integer and ternary grids (Section 3.1). RoleQ-T (Sections 3.2–3.4) converts an existing checkpoint to this ternary format in a few GPU-hours, combining a data-free learned rotation, GPTQ adapted to role- aligned scales and brief scale distillation with frozen codes. Optional Fisher-placed secondary planes (Section 3.5) allocate additional precision to units selected by loss sensitivity. Figure 2 summarises the pipeline.
