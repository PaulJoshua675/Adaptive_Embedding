Preprint The law holds (Fig. 2). For DINOv3-L the per-query prediction tracks measured mAP across eight decades to 0.266 at 100M (the value computed on the full official feature release). By contrast, a homogeneous single-x model collapses to zero. Recovering the official number end-to-end validates the pipeline and the law at ground truth. Hardness follows an approximate power law x−α (α ≈1.2; 1.16–1.32 across the five backbones and three benchmarks): a large fraction of queries stay near- immune even at 100M, and the resolved rest bleed accuracy at a near-constant rate per decade—log- uniform (α=1) is the idealized limit of Cor. 1 and gives exactly that. This never-saturating decay is also why reading the metric off a 50k subset is optimistic by +0.34 at 100M (Fig. 2). The independence assumption is benign in practice (Fig. 3). The exact (1 −x)N form assumes independent distractors. Empirically the residual between the independent prediction and the mea- sured incremental curve is at most a few percent at 100M across all 5 backbones, and the prediction is stable to 0.004 across three disjoint 5M sub-pools (App. B). Analytically, near-duplicate clusters of size c leave the expected rank unchanged (1 + Nxq, clustering-invariant by linearity) and inflate only its variance by ∼c; on the real crawl ordering the measured curve sits at c ≈1, so we do not claim i.i.d. is conservative for mAP in general—only that the residual is small here (Fig. 3, App. B). From a 5% sample (5M for ILIAS, a 20× reduction), the tail-aware (EVT) estimator recovers true mAP@100M to mean error 0.005 (max 0.008) across the five ILIAS backbones. The query- bootstrap interval’s overlap with the true interval for every backbone (App. C, Tab. 6) is a weaker check, since prediction and truth share the same queries and their intervals are correlated. Two stronger checks (App. C) agree: the estimate preserves the backbone ranking exactly (Kendall τ = 1.0, no pairwise sign flip; gaps down to ≈0.013 mAP are resolved, and the closest pair— PE-core vs. SigLIP2, gap 0.010—is within the extrapolation error and should be read as a statistical tie), and the paired per-query error has pooled 95% CI [−0.001, +0.004]. At this same 5M cost, naively evaluating on the subset is optimistic by mean 0.11, whereas our prediction is within 0.005, roughly 22× more accurate (App. C, Tab. 8). For DINOv3-L the estimator gives 0.260 vs. 0.266 (err 0.006), while the count estimator over-predicts (+0.050). The estimate is stable across sample size (Fig. 6), and 5% (5M) is accurate for every backbone across all three benchmarks. These intervals resample queries only. Folding in the other two noise sources, namely which distrac- tors land in the sample and the per-query EVT refit, needs a nested bootstrap over all five backbones. It adds only σ ≈0.003 of distractor-sample-plus-fit noise, so the intervals shift slightly rather than widen, and still covers the true 100M value in 5/5 cases (App. C, Tab. 7). Query sampling dominates the uncertainty. Resampling the distractors directly—20 disjoint 5% draws per backbone—places all 100 predictions inside the reported interval (coverage 100/100; std 0.003, worst error 0.013; App. D). The reported interval is a query confidence interval; the remaining noise sources are quan- tified in App. C. We compare against the baselines a practitioner would reach for, all from the same 5% sample. Three fail on the unresolved tail: reporting the metric on the sample (“evaluate-at-S”) is optimistic by 0.08–0.13 (Tab. 8); the tail-blind count (the Krichene–Rendle unbiased estimator), which assigns the tail below 1/S zero mass, over-predicts by 0.037–0.068; and power-law (0.120) and log-uniform survival (0.038) curve fits do no better. The tail-aware estimate (mean error 0.005; the headline paired mean of Tab. 1 is 0.005) beats all of them—only the bounded GPD (ξ ≤0, free scale) excludes the power-law regime and recovers the tail.
