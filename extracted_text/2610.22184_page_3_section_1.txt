We present a pipeline for whole-body dexterous manipu- lation, enabling object repositioning across diverse regions of the body. Beyond end-effector and front-of-torso contact, we define contact surfaces for grasping as any link or link subsection in the robot’s kinematic system. Because the embodiment for whole-body grasping differs greatly from a multi-fingered hand, much of the re-grasping in the whole- body formulation involves sliding and rolling across the torso mesh, which we account for later in this section. Our method trains and deploys controllers with dexterous whole-body re-grasping capabilities in three stages. We first generate a large dataset of whole-body object grasps using energy-based optimization and decompose our torso mesh into subregions to enable diverse whole-body grasping on the robot. Second, we train per-object experts on this grasp corpus to reposition objects around the body, encouraging sliding and rolling contact across the torso. Finally, we distill these experts into deployable controllers without privileged object state, enabling real-world whole-body dexterous ob- ject repositioning. A. Whole-Body Grasp Generation Given a kinematic system, we enumerate configuration types (Fig. 3): which bodies or appendages hold the object and which of their links or subregions should contact it. A grasp mode m ∈M abstracts a configuration type to the appendages contacting the object and is the level at which goals are commanded in Section III-B. The system model, an object mesh, and the choice of contact links are the only inputs, so the procedure is not specific to a humanoid or to a class of objects.
