Checkpoints and Measurement We study three released conversions, two of them paired with their parent models. Bwen-8B is a conversion of Qwen/Qwen3-8B-Base from the Qwen3 family (Yang et al., 2025), although its tokenizer configuration names Qwen/Qwen3-8B; the loaded tokenizers have identical SHA-256 digests in the measurement records. The sec- ond behavioral pair is allenai/OLMo-2-0425-1B, from the OLMo 2 family (OLMo Team et al., 2025), and allenai/Bolmo-1B. We additionally inspect allenai/Bolmo-7B, whose weight lineage starts from Olmo-3-1025-7B (Olmo Team et al., 2025). The latter two conversions share the dolma2 tokenizer but differ in back- bone and training history as well as size. Public revisions and measured runtime identities appear in Appendix M. We audit natural-text boundaries in float32, processing one unpadded sentence per forward pass through the check- point’s local encoder and boundary predictor. The audit reconstructs the exact NFC bytes from parent pieces, vali- dates the cut-offset mapping against shifted alternatives, and verifies the loaded encoder tensors. The embedding-removal ablation drops the residual parent-vocabulary feature with- out retraining. Boundary Retention The audit first measures how much inherited segmentation fragments grapheme clusters. Across the focal languages, Bwen retains at least 97.07% of its parent’s interior cuts in each language, and its cluster-internal cut count Ain differs from the parent’s by at most 1.54%. This agreement emerges from the predictor’s own decisions; the evaluation does not impose parent boundaries. Retention varies more widely across the control languages (Appendix A). Across the full audit, Bwen splits 99.49% of multi-scalar clusters, compared with the parent’s 99.89% (Table 4). The close agreement extends to both boundary positions and the proportion of clusters fragmented in this released lineage.
