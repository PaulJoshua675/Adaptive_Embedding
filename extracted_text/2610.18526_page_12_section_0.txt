Its imbalanced pools and binary relevance make it our realistic stress test. Derived from public parliamentary proceed- ings, it is released for non-commercial research, under which our use falls. FLORES-200 (Team et al., 2022) is a parallel corpus covering 200 languages. We use it to esti- mate the language statistics for XQuAD and Multi- EuP, taking 500 sentences per language from the development split for the languages present in each set. It is released under ODC-By, which permits research use with attribution. TED2020 (Qi et al., 2018) is a corpus of TED and TED-X talk subtitles, crowd-translated into over 100 languages and sentence-aligned across them; this broad, many-way parallel coverage is what lets it serve as an estimation corpus. It is dis- tributed under the TED Talks Usage Policy, under which the talks are licensed CC BY–NC–ND 4.0, permitting the non-commercial research use. Belebele comprises 9,000 queries over a 4,880- document index (10 languages), XQuAD 7,140 queries over 1,440 documents (6 languages), and Multi-EuP 22,504 queries over 25,883 documents (24 languages). For the parallel collections, each query has a single cross-lingual gold per language; on Multi-EuP, the number of relevant documents varies (median: 23, range: 1–188). To our knowl- edge, none of the datasets contain private or sensi- tive personal information, and our method requires no additional data collection. B Model and Experimental Details Models. We evaluate seven publicly available em- bedding models, all accessed through their Hug- ging Face repositories and run in inference only, without fine-tuning. Table 5 summarizes their provenance, size, architecture, multilingual status, and license. All are explicitly multilingual and in- tended for multilingual retrieval, with the exception of e5-mistral, which is trained primarily on English data but is widely used for multilingual retrieval and included here to test whether our findings ex- tend to a decoder not optimized for cross-lingual use. We read out each model in the pooling and prompt configuration, its documentation specifies: mean pooling with the official query:/passage: prefixes for the mE5 models, CLS pooling for bge- m3, last-token pooling for the Qwen and e5-mistral decoders, and mean pooling with the projection heads applied for embedding-gemma. All licenses permit research use. For the layer-wise analysis, we extract a representation at every layer by applying each model’s deployed pooling to that layer’s hid- den states and run retrieval on the pooled vectors. The pooling is identical across layers, so differ- ences between layers reflect the representations, not the readout. Hyperparameters. All retrieval uses cosine simi- larity over the final-layer embedding with a cutoff of K = 20. All correction hyperparameters are se- lected on the estimation corpora, and each is set to the value that performs best there: the steering scale α, the subspace rank r for subspace removal, and the shrinkage weight for mean removal. The sub- space rank peaks at r = 10 for both a representative encoder and decoder, so no setting advantages our method over the baselines. The steering scale α is tuned per model rather than shared across models, since the architectures tolerate different amounts of steering. Disagreement-guided steering (α) is in the range 0.1–0.3, norm-scaled by each query’s magnitude, with the consensus for each language formed by reciprocal-rank fusion (rank constant 60) over the other languages. Compute and training. Our method trains nothing. Every correction, including disagreement-guided steering, is estimated in closed form from precom- puted embeddings: it introduces no learnable pa- rameters, runs no gradient updates, and adds only a per-language vector or matrix applied to queries at inference. The only forward passes through the models are to extract embeddings for documents, queries, and the FLORES estimation corpus, done once per model and cached. All experiments run on a single GPU (NVIDIA A100 40GB); embedding extraction takes minutes to a few hours per model, depending on size, dominated by the 8B decoders, and every intervention on the cached embeddings, including the full alpha sweep and the multi-seed runs, completes in minutes. The per-language steer- ing directions are the same dimension as the em- bedding (up to 4096) and add negligible cost at retrieval time.
