The fixed-ID generations in Appendix F cover all twelve evaluations in Figure 2, with multiple complete outputs per setting; they are not a matched quantitative comparison. The results support improving CDLMs through changes to the denoiser backbone and its prediction and training interfaces. Within the cumulative LangFlow study, the largest adjacent GenPPL reduction at 50k updates comes from confidence-weighted training self-conditioning. The Plaid-style adaptation illustrates how compatible components can be reused while retaining a different continuous state space, objective, and sampler. The study is limited to OWT-1024 at one backbone scale and one training seed. The cumulative ablation measures effects along a fixed design sequence, and wider conditioning increases parameter count. Parameter- and compute-matched comparisons, component-removal ablations, and additional seeds would help isolate individual effects and assess their robustness. Direct measurements of attention behavior and codebook gradients are also needed to test the proposed optimization explanations. The unmatched Plaid comparisons and extended-training results should be interpreted with their respective training budgets. Finally, GenPPL and unigram entropy capture limited aspects of generation quality; evaluation of coherence, semantic diversity, and distributional coverage remains necessary.
