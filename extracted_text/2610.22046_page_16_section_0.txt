Differences below the margin are recorded as failures even when their intervals exclude zero. B Experimental protocols Problem set and sampling. The two scales share one problem set of 35,854 problems. Of these problems, 32,031 come from DeepMath-103k at difficulty at least 7, and 3,823 come from levels 4 and 5 of the training split of Hendrycks MATH. Each problem has four sampled traces, except 146 problems with eight, which gives 144,000 traces at each scale. The traces were sampled at temperature 0.7 with a 16k-token budget. No problem in the problem set matches a MATH-500, MATH-Perturb or AIME 2024–2025 problem after whitespace and case normalization (0 of 35,854 problems). The exact-match check does not detect paraphrased or reformatted duplicates. Monte Carlo collections. The two collections use different numbers of rollouts, and every result identifies its source collection (Table 2). The broad collection labels all incorrect traces and supplies the failure-type shares in Section 2. In a scan, it evaluates steps 0, 1 and 2 and then every second step with 8 rollouts, and in a refinement it re-evaluates some of these prefixes with 16 rollouts. More than 80% of its evaluated prefixes have 8 rollouts, and more than 70% of its PIT steps were evaluated with 16 (Table 2). About half of the 16-rollout prefixes of PITs lie within two evaluated steps of the PIT step. The collection evaluates about 60% of the steps of a trace. Each set of K rollouts contains one greedy rollout and K −1 independent samples at temperature 0.7, so its success count is not binomial. Writing Gt ∈{0, 1} for the greedy result and pt for the temperature-0.7 success probability, the count is Gt + Xt, where Xt ∼Bin(K −1, pt). Thus E[ˆpt | st] = [Gt + (K −1)pt]/K, a K-dependent protocol mean rather than pt. Rollout rates at K = 8 and K = 16 therefore differ in mixture weight as well as sampling variance under the recorded protocol. Conditional on the greedy outcome, the remaining K −1 samples give rate standard deviations of 0.099 at K=8 and 0.073 at K=16 near p=0.1. A depth sweep reports refinement-depth agreement of 0.754 at K=16 against 0.638 at K=8. The two agreement values are point estimates against a deeper reference, with no interval. The envelope is computed over the prefixes evaluated by a collection (Section 2). Rollouts continue from the final recorded prefix, so the value of that prefix is estimated from rollouts and is not set equal to the final label. The rollout search, replayed offline on the gold collection’s stored rollout rates, reads every second step and then bisects the interval that contains the first prefix at which the envelope is at or below θ. No 8B replay exists. Each rollout is capped at 4,096 tokens, a cap read from the stored rollouts at both scales. The temperature of 0.7 is taken from our experiment records. The temperature, nucleus-sampling parameter, random seed and stop strings cannot be recovered from the stored rollouts, and the script that generated the rollouts was not retained. The gold collection contains 5,380 Qwen3-4B traces and supplies 818 PITs, whose PIT steps are the reference PIT steps. It scans with 4 rollouts and refines with 8 (Table 2), so no step in it receives the 16 rollouts of the broad collection’s refinement. At K=4 and K=8, a rollout rate at or below θ means zero successes. The gold collection overlaps the broad collection. Of its 5,380 traces, 2,760 are also broad-collection traces, including 815 of the 818 gold PIT traces. For 84 of these 815 traces, the broad collection assigns a different failure type: 76 capability gaps and 8 near misses. This overlap restricts the traces that can be evaluated at 4B.
