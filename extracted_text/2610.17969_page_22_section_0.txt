E.5 Gauge-canonical factorization What scale separation preserves. For nonzero inputs with independent positive rescalings, canonical coordinates preserve continuous invariant expressivity (Thm. 4; Cor. 3). Targets that depend on absolute magnitude still require the relevant scale information. Coupled unit declarations can also leave meaningful dimensionless scale ratios, which must be retained (Thm. 5). Apps. E.5 through E.7 give the factorization and perturbation-stability details. Factorization and expressivity. For nonzero inputs that can each be rescaled independently by a positive factor, Thm. 4 shows that every equivariant computation factors into a function of RMS-normalized inputs and explicit scale factors that supply the required output unit. Choosing RMS-one coordinates makes this factorization unique. For targets that are unchanged by these rescalings, Cor. 3 shows that normalized coordinates retain all the information needed to represent any continuous invariant function. Targets that depend on absolute magnitude still require the relevant scale information.
