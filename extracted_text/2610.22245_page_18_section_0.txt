Motivated by these works, our supervised weak-correctness probe Proxy Reward serves as a controlled oracle reference to represent this category of quality-based deferral. Our proposed framework assumes no access to ground-truth correctness or improvement signals. Consistency and Ensemble-Based Cascades (N > 1 Label-Free). Consistency-based ap- proaches assess reliability without labels by comparing multiple generated responses. [51] use self-consistency([46]) agreement among reasoning paths for deferral. Semantic Entropy [25] and Kernel Language Entropy [12] measure uncertainty over clusters of equivalent answers which can be used as deferral rules in cascades. Similarly, [53] rely on clustering to compute the semantic entropies of both student and teacher models to construct routing preferences. Other methods evaluate consensus across an ensemble of multiple models: ABC [23] uses pairwise similarities across inexpensive models, SMOOTHIE [14] estimates quality from distances between embedded outputs (which requires generator triplets), and [34] groups queries into skill clusters via confidence-weighted ensemble consensus. These within-model consistency methods (particularly self-consistency and semantic entropy) directly motivate our multi-sample (N > 1) baselines. However, as a broader category, these approaches fundamentally differ from our framework in their operational assumptions and mechanics. First, ensemble routers ([14, 34]) are designed to select among a diverse pool of multiple models, whereas we study a strict cascade between a single local weak model and a more capable strong model. Consequently, our framework only requires the weak model’s internal repre- sentations; the strong model can remain entirely black-box, accessed only for its text outputs during offline training. Second, consistency methods require generating multiple responses at inference time and rely heavily on semantic clustering. As we demonstrate in Section 6.2, grouping responses into equivalence classes is notoriously brittle for open-ended tasks. Learned Label-Free Reliability and Cross-Model Verification. Recent work has explored learning reliability predictors from unlabeled outputs or cross-model comparisons. Semantic Entropy Probes [24] predict semantic-entropy targets directly from model representations to enable single- response uncertainty estimation. HaloScope [10] derives pseudo-labels from unlabeled response representations to train a truthfulness classifier. Additionally, [47], [16], and [13] explore cross-model disagreement and verifier surprise to quantify uncertainty. These works provide strong conceptual motivation for comparing models and utilizing hidden-state probes. However, rather than detecting hallucinations or requiring test-time queries to a second model, our approach uniquely trains a probe on offline strong-model disagreement to directly approximate the optimal cascade routing gain, evaluating it autonomously at test time. Allocating Test-Time Computation. Additional inference computation can significantly improve response quality by generating and selecting among multiple candidates. For example, self-consistency [46] aggregates sampled reasoning paths, while broader test-time compute strategies [43] scale sampling and refinement based on input difficulty. For routing specifically, BEST-Route [9] pre- determines a sampling budget combined with model selection, and ModelSwitch [3] evaluates consistency across multi-model samples to adaptively halt generation.
