The student is the closed-form model built from 128 problems per domain with a limit of 16k new tokens. It answers the same 256 problems under the evaluation settings. In the first variant the student reads its own answers, so that the statistics are those of the student’s activations; a version of it that keeps only the student’s correct mathematics answers is also reported (Table 3). In the second variant the problems are restricted to the 165 that the teacher solves within 12k tokens, the student’s answers to them are kept after removing loops, and the mathematics teacher reads them. This is the closed-form counterpart of the trained on-policy objective, in which the teacher supervises text written by the student [12, 19, 20]. Domain weights. The mathematics Gram matrices of the all-answers statistics are multiplied by three. Mined hard problems. From 2,000 further DAPO-Math-17k problems we take 400 on which a 1.5B reasoning model solves at most two of four samples. The mathematics teacher and a fused student (all answers) answer each once, with a limit of 16k new tokens. Problems solved by the teacher and not by the student, and problems solved by both, give four sets of statistics, all read by the mathematics teacher: the student’s and the teacher’s answers on each class, with loops in student answers cut at their onset. Mix 1 weights the student’s answers on the first class, the teacher’s answers on the first class and the student’s answers on the second by 0.4, 0.4 and 0.2. Mix 2 weights the selected teacher statistics of the final model and the teacher’s answers on the two classes by 0.5, 0.25 and 0.25. Teacher and student solve about the same number of these problems, so the classes reflect sampling luck more than a capability gap. The code statistics are unchanged. Results.
