Unlike prior GEO work that mainly optimizes source visibility or item ranking, we study target exposure within webpages that may discuss multiple candidates or parties. 3 Data Collection and Preprocessing We construct three datasets, us-election-24, ai-persuasion, and jp- election-26, from two electoral settings, the 2024 U.S. presidential election between the Democratic and Republican parties and the proportional-representation component of the 2026 Japanese House of Representatives election involving 11 parties. us-election-24 and jp-election-26 contain election-related query cells and their corre- sponding webpage pools, while ai-persuasion provides question- naire information for constructing richer voter personas in the U.S. setting. Additional details on query composition, persona specifi- cations, and webpage preprocessing are provided in Appendix A. 3.1 Data Sources us-election-24 is derived from the longitudinal corpus for the 2024 U.S. presidential election [7]. The original dataset contains 573 election-related queries collected on a near-daily basis from July 23 to November 12, 2024, together with historical Google Search results. ai-persuasion is based on the voter-persuasion dataset [16]. We use the pre-treatment questionnaire responses of 2,306 U.S. par- ticipants in the 2024 presidential election, covering demographics, political engagement, voting criteria, and other voter characteris- tics. jp-election-26 is derived from the longitudinal factorial dataset of Miyazaki and Hall [19] for the 2026 Japanese House of Represen- tatives election. The dataset contains 25 voting-advice queries, 44 demographic personas, and responses from five web-enabled LLMs collected daily from February 2 to February 8, 2026. 3.2 Query Cells and Voter Personas Query cells. For us-election-24, we retain 260 of the 573 queries that are relevant to voter decision making, covering candidate eval- uation and comparison, policy positions, election predictions, and related news. One example is “Which US presidential candidate do voters believe will handle the economy the best?” We use the 106 days from July 23 through election day on November 5, 2024, with Google Search as the engine. The first collection day contains SERP results for only 250 queries, resulting in 27,550 query cells. For jp-election-26, we retain all 25 voting-advice queries, consist- ing of 12 policy issues with two opposing stances for each issue and one control query without a policy stance. Each asks which political party the voter should vote for, optionally accompanied by a policy stance. For example, one query asks “I think Japan should raise defense spending to at least 2% of GDP. Which polit- ical party should I vote for?” We retain four web-enabled LLMs as engines, gpt-4o-mini, gpt-5-mini, grok-4-1-fast, and gro k-with-x. Voter personas. Personas form an independent conditioning di- mension and can be combined with the same query cells. We con- sider three categories of voter personas: demographic identities, partisan identities, and questionnaire-derived profiles. us-election- 24 provides 11 identity personas [7], which we group into 9 demo- graphic identities, covering 6 racial or ethnic identities and 3 gender identities, and 2 partisan identities, Democrat and Republican. We additionally include an Independent persona representing voters who identify with neither major party as a study-specific parti- san identity. ai-persuasion provides pre-treatment questionnaire responses from 2,306 U.S. participants, from which we use demo- graphics, political engagement, and voting criteria to identify three interpretable subgroups and construct questionnaire-derived pro- files [16]. jp-election-26 provides 44 demographic identities formed by crossing 2 genders, 11 regions, and 2 area types [19]. 3.3 Webpage Pools Following the fixed-pool GEO setting in Section 2.1, we construct a webpage pool D𝑖for each query cell. Voting-related web content can change over time during an election, so our goal is to reconstruct a query-relevant snapshot of the web content available around the collection day. To this end, we use the historical search results and citations available in the source datasets to collect relevant URLs for each query cell. For us-election-24, each pool consists of the persona-free his- torical Google-SERP result URLs for the corresponding query and day. For jp-election-26, each user prompt combines a query with a persona and, where applicable, a policy stance. To construct a comprehensive query-relevant webpage pool while mitigating de- pendence on any single persona or stance, we aggregate citation URLs for the same engine, query, and day across all 44 personas and the two opposing stances of each policy issue. For each URL associated with a query cell, we collect the webpage content from as close as possible to its collection day. We first use the closest available Wayback Machine snapshot, followed by live-page and headless-browser fallbacks when no archived snapshot is available. We then extract the main textual content from each webpage. The resulting pools contain 9.15 usable webpages per query cell on average for us-election-24 and 63.1 for jp-election-26.
