The default backbone is the lightweight GPT-6-Luna (OpenAI, 2026c), and the stronger GPT-6-Astra (OpenAI, 2026b) is the sec- ond backbone, both with high reasoning effort. Each attempt has at most 1,800 s of wall-clock time, and each Pi attempt at most 300 turns. Metrics. Following STaRK, we report Hit@1 (H@1, also called Hits@1), Recall@20 (R@20) and mean reciprocal rank (MRR). Because the agent is asked for 20 IDs, our MRR is a lower bound on the full-ranking MRR. A query whose at- tempts all fail counts as a miss in every metric (retry policy in Appendix C). Overall values are micro- averages over all queries of the three datasets, and differences are computed before rounding. Ap- pendix C reports the cost and resource use of each configuration. Baselines. We report published results and, as in Table 2, separate the systems trained or tuned on STaRK from the training-free ones. The baselines are retrievers, LLM pipelines and agents, and all of them access the KG through predefined opera- tions. The strongest trained system, GRASP (Tao et al., 2026), combines a Cypher query on a graph database with a trained dense retriever and a fine- tuned reranker. Among the training-free systems, KAR (Xia et al., 2025b) and AF-Retriever (Boer et al., 2026) are LLM pipelines. KAR expands the query from the documents of neighboring enti- ties, and AF-Retriever translates it into graph con- straints. The agent ARK (Polonuer et al., 2026) calls predefined global search and one-hop neigh- borhood search tools. We list separately three KG reasoning methods (ToG, RoG and GoG) that Yang et al. (2025) adapt to STaRK and run with GPT-4.
