The velocity likelihood in Equation 5 is a surrogate rather than the exact likelihood of the sampled route. It models the routed deviation as an isotropic Gaussian around the dense velocity, whereas the true deviation is the effect of a discrete route on the attention output. It also omits the pathwise gradient of the generator through later denoising transitions and through the frozen reward model. Finally, the width s2 t is re-estimated on every batch. As routing perturbs the generator less during training, s2 t shrinks, so the same deviation produces a larger gradient. Extensions. Two directions extend the method naturally. A likelihood that adds the probability of the sampled route to the velocity term would also credit the route choice itself, rather than only its effect on the dense velocity. Larger groups would give a leave-one-out baseline with more members. They trade part of the pair’s variance cancellation for a more stable advantage scale.
