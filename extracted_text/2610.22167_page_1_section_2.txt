Existing work on cross-cultural pragmatic in- ference shows the importance of speakers’ back- grounds and shared knowledge for interpretation (Shaikh et al., 2023; White et al., 2024). Stud- ies by Rao et al. (2025); Pujari and Goldwasser (2025) also show that supplying explicit cultural norms or augmenting conversations with cultural context schemas can improve models’ social judg- ments and downstream predictions, which moti- vates training models to discover and use the rel- evant context already present in an interaction. In long interactions, the relevant evidence may be spread across many turns, so a model can pro- duce a plausible explanation while overlooking an earlier exchange that changes the interpreta- tion. Wu et al. (2026) find that stronger ground- ing in relevant visual evidence is linked to better performance, and Zhang et al. (2026) show that citation-aware rewards can improve evidence use in question answering.
