Four benchmarks and four model interfaces, with 64 prompt families per benchmark; DLMs use oracle target lengths, while Qwen3 generates freely. 4 Few-Shot Prompting Experiments with Standard Benchmarks We first quantify the effects of demonstrations and example guidance in single-target infilling (Section 4.1). We then investigate how these effects change when multiple targets are infilled jointly (Section 4.2). Finally, we evaluate the impact of predicting target lengths rather than supplying them through an oracle (Section 4.3). We defer additional experiments to Section B, such as on guidance strength (Section B.5) and likelihood sharpening (Section B.6). Experimental setup. We evaluate four benchmarks spanning different few-shot transforma- tions: SST-2 (Socher et al., 2013; Wang et al., 2018) with demonstration-defined sentiment codes, List Functions–Easy (BIG-bench authors, 2023; Rule, 2020) for list transformations, SALT repetition (Zheng et al., 2025a,b) for artificial-language transformations, and Movie Director (Liu et al., 2012; Zhao et al., 2021) for information extraction. We use three DLMs: LLaDA-1.5 (Zhu et al., 2026), Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion-8B (Fu et al., 2026), and dQwen3.5-9B-Base (Xue et al., 2026), together with Qwen3-8B (Yang et al., 2025) as an autoregressive single-target comparison. Throughout, D and J denote the numbers of demonstrations and targets, respectively. We refer to generation without additional guidance (β = 0) as standard infilling for DLMs, or equivalently unguided sampling, and compare this with example guidance at β = 0.5 by default. Outputs are graded by task-specific exact or parsed matching, and the DLM experiments reveal one target token at a time in ascending canvas order. Further experimental details appear in Section B.1. 4.1 Single-Target Few-Shot Prompting We begin with single-target infilling, which closely resembles conventional autoregressive few-shot prompting, except that DLMs generate a masked output rather than a continuation.
