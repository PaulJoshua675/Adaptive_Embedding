DyssomniaNN establishes an end-to-end multi-scale biophysical framework that bridges molecular, subcortical, and cortical mechanisms of human sleep regulation. By uniting Borbély’s two-process homeostatic and circadian drivers with Saper’s hypothalamic mutual inhibitory flip-flop circuit and a recurrent corticothalamic LIF network, the model reproduces the multi-day electrophysiological and architectural signatures of diverse human sleep pathologies across a standardized 72-hour photoperiod. Synthetic LFPs generated by the spiking network exhibit close convergence against empirical human PSG spectra from the PhysioNet Sleep-EDF database across all canonical EEG frequency bands, confirming that the simulated macroscale dynamics emerge directly from biophysically parameterized neuronal interactions. The multi-cohort simulations resolve the long-standing discordance between objective sleep duration and subjective sleep quality. In paradoxical insomnia, normal macroscale PSG architecture and sleep duration (21.15 h) coexist with severe restorative impairment (R = 37.6%), driven by cortical disinhibition and unsuppressed high-frequency beta intrusions during slow-wave sleep. In narcolepsy type 1, the destruction of orexinergic stabilization collapses subcortical flip-flop hysteresis, producing severe sleep-state fragmentation, nocturnal wakefulness (WASO = 781 min), and daytime SOREMPs despite elevated aggregate sleep volume (26.18 h). Total sleep time represents an incomplete proxy for restorative sleep in the presence of cortical hyperarousal or subcortical boundary instability. DyssomniaNN provides a rigorous computational testbed for evaluating targeted neuropharmaco- logical interventions. By formalizing targeted neurochemical perturbations, including hypocretin loss, preoptic disinhibition, and cortical GABAergic hypofunction, the framework enables predictive evaluation of targeted pharmacological interventions such as dual orexin receptor antagonists and orexin agonists prior to clinical implementation. Future extensions incorporating spatially distributed cortical geometries, explicit PK/PD modeling, and patient-specific parameter tuning will advance personalized computational psychiatry and therapeutic neuromodulation.
