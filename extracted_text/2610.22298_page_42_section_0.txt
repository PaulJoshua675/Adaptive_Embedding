Diagnostic and Treatment Tactics Diagnostics: Tissue diagnosis is mandatory to resolve the differential and exclude malignancy. An ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy is the initial pathologic procedure, with the goal of obtaining adequate tissue for histology and immunohistochemistry while minimizing cosmetic morbidity. Expected result: benign breast tissue consistent with gynecomastia or fibroadenoma. If core needle biopsy is nondiagnostic or shows atypia, an excisional biopsy should follow. If histology confirms benign gynecomastia or fibroadenoma, observation or limited surgical excision may follow based on symptoms. If secretory carcinoma or sarcoma is identified, oncologic surgical planning with margin control and staging is required. Histologic evaluation must assess for ductal versus stromal predominance, cytologic atypia, infiltrative growth, lymphovascular invasion, and mitotic activity. Immunohistochemistry (e.g., S100, cytokeratins, ER, PR, myoepithelial markers) should be performed if morphology is atypical. Radiologic-pathologic concordance is mandatory: if imaging suggests benignity but pathology is discordant, repeat biopsy or excision is required.
