How Long Is the Coast of a Neuron? 6 2. Intermediate projection: sublinear summation and saturation We next change the ruler to an intermediate macro- scopic one. Suppose that individual contacts are no longer resolved and that the observer instead has access to one effective variable from each dendritic branch. We define those branch observables by the saturated sums B1 = min(x1 + x2 + x3, 2), B2 = min(x4 + x5 + x6, 2), (20) so that each branch can now occupy only the states 0, 1, or 2. The hard ceiling in Eq. (20) is a stylized represen- tation of saturating or sublinear local integration, not a quantitative membrane equation. Real dendritic re- sponses can become sublinear through several conduc- tance and driving-force mechanisms12. As a local den- dritic membrane depolarizes toward the reversal poten- tial of its excitatory ion channels, the electrochemical driving force decreases, and voltage summation plateaus. However, active dendrites can also exhibit strongly supra- linear regimes13,14. What matters for the present con- struction is the many-to-one character of the map. Ex- plicitly, distinct microscopic input configurations become indistinguishable once only B1 and B2 are retained. The state collapse can be seen directly. If exactly two contacts on the first branch are active, then B1 = min(2, 2) = 2. If all three are active, B1 = min(3, 2) = 2 as well. The fine-scale states (1, 1, 0) and (1, 1, 1) are therefore assigned the same observed branch value even though Eq. (18) treats them differently. The triplet con- tribution is zero in the first case and nonzero in the sec- ond. The projection has removed precisely the distinc- tion needed to determine whether the local third-order gate was completed. That lost distinction reduces absolute predictive in- formation. The available information falls from Iavail = 0.156742 bits at the six-contact ruler to 0.028607 bits at the two-branch ruler. But the higher-order residual falls much more sharply, from Ihigher = 0.0706 to 0.0011 bits. Consequently, the information that remains after pro- jection is overwhelmingly describable by direct effects of the two observed branch variables. For the categorical- additive family, F rises from 55.0% to 96.1%. This is the important direction of the effect. The branch projection has not made the microscopic triplet interaction disappear from the generative mechanism. It has erased much of the observer’s ability to distinguish the microscopic states on which that interaction depends. The coarse conditional distribution therefore looks sub- stantially more direct than the fine one. 3. Somatic projection: aggregation and the denominator trap The final ruler retains only a single somatic observ- able, Z = (B1 + B2)/2. The nine possible branch pairs (B1, B2) are thereby compressed into only five somatic values, Z ∈{0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0}. Relative to the original 64 synaptic configurations, this is an extreme contraction of the observable state space. The symmetric average performs a second kind of coarse-graining in addition to the within-branch satura- tion of Eq. (20). It removes branch identity and the spa- tial origin of the input. For example, (B1, B2) = (2, 0) gives Z = (2 + 0)/2 = 1, while (B1, B2) = (0, 2) gives exactly the same somatic value. The observer can there- fore no longer determine which branch generated a given contribution to the soma. The absolute information measures make clear how much has been discarded. Across the full cascade, Iavail falls from 0.156742 to 0.028607 to 0.023526 bits. The cat- egorical higher-order residual simultaneously falls from 0.0706 to 0.0011 to 0.0000 bits (Fig. 2). The direct- information fraction for the categorical-additive family is therefore 55.0% −→96.1% −→100.0%. (21) That is, the same underlying system moves from 55.0% direct at contact resolution, to 96.1% at branch resolu- tion, to 100.0% at somatic resolution. The last number requires careful interpretation. At the somatic ruler there is only one observed coordinate, Z. The categorical direct family of Eq. (1) allows an arbitrary one-coordinate function u(Z), so there can be no residual interaction among observed coordinates (i.e. there is only one coordinate left). Thus Ihigher = 0 and Idir = Iavail exactly. The 100% result does not mean that the six-contact mechanism in Eq. (18) has become micro- scopically additive. It means that, after the projection has removed the relevant internal distinctions, all of the information still accessible at the somatic ruler is rep- resentable as a direct dependence on the single retained variable. This is the denominator trap in its sharpest form. The fraction F = Idir/Iavail reaches unity while the denomi- nator itself contracts by 85%. The complementary total- entropy ratio makes that contraction visible: R is only 13.6% at the contact scale, 4.3% at the branch scale, and 3.7% at the soma. A coarse model can therefore be perfect relative to the information remaining at its own resolution while retaining only a small fraction of the pre- dictive structure that existed before projection. High F and low R are not contradictory statements; they answer different questions. These numerical ratios are not in- tended as a model of the recordings in Ref.11; the cascade is a constructive example showing how projection can in- crease apparent directness while the predictive informa- tion retained by the coarse representation decreases. The use of the categorical-additive family (P wi·1[B = i]) is also important here. If the numerical labels 0, 1, and 2 assigned to a branch state were forced to en- ter a straight-line predictor, an apparent residual could arise simply because the chosen coordinate happened to be nonlinearly encoded.
