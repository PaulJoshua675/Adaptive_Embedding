Continuous language diffusion and flow. Diffusion-LM and self-conditioned embedding diffusion established continuous denoising approaches to language generation (Li et al., 2022; Strudel et al., 2022), while CDCD and Plaid developed categorical diffusion and likelihood-based training (Dieleman et al., 2022; Gulrajani and Hashimoto, 2023). Recent work explores flow maps (Lee et al., 2026), learned embeddings with information-based noise scheduling (Chen et al., 2026), architectural refinements (Yang et al., 2026), hyperspherical dynamics (Deschenaux and Gulcehre, 2026), and embedding-space flow matching (Hu et al., 2026). We study architectural design within fixed LangFlow and Plaid-style formulations. Latent representations and decoding. CoDAR uses contextual autoregressive decoding (Shen et al., 2026), while LDLM jointly learns an encoder, denoiser, and decoder with controlled gradient interac- tions (Meshchaninov et al., 2026). Cola-DLM introduces a text VAE and latent prior (Guo et al., 2026), and AURORA-LM restricts the noisy-input pathway while preserving high-capacity latents (Liang et al., 2026). Analysis of ELF links token recovery to decoder margins and denoising trajectories (Du and Ma, 2026). Our changes operate within the existing codebook-based prediction interface. Normalization and conditioning. DiT and EDM2 emphasize adaptive conditioning and magnitude control in diffusion networks (Peebles and Xie, 2023; Karras et al., 2024). RMSNorm, nGPT, and query–key normalization provide complementary approaches to controlling representation and attention scales (Zhang and Sennrich, 2019; Loshchilov et al., 2025; Henry et al., 2020). Related work examines attention-entropy collapse (Zhai et al., 2023) and uses query–key RMSNorm in rectified-flow Transformers (Esser et al., 2024). We examine these normalization choices alongside noise-conditioning capacity in continuous language denoising. Self-conditioning. Analog Bits, self-conditioned embedding diffusion, and LangFlow use previous predictions to guide denoising (Chen et al., 2022; Strudel et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2026). AURORA- LM addresses training–inference mismatch through trajectory consistency (Liang et al., 2026). Our entropy-based gate regulates bootstrap inputs during training while retaining the baseline loss formulation and inference sampler. Discrete diffusion and evaluation. MDLM, LLaDA, and iLLaDA advance masked diffusion language modeling (Sahoo et al., 2024; Nie et al., 2025, 2026), while Duo connects uniform-state discrete diffusion to an underlying Gaussian process (Sahoo et al., 2025). Across diffusion families, likelihood rankings can differ from those based on generation quality and sampling cost (Sahoo et al., 2026); lower GenPPL can also accompany reduced distributional coverage (Franca and Tong, 2026). We therefore report GenPPL alongside mean per-sample unigram entropy, with explicit sampling budgets. 3. The Language Diffusion Transformer LangDiT is a Transformer denoiser for continuous language generation. Its design addresses three components of denoising: processing noisy embeddings, predicting discrete tokens, and incorporating previous predictions through self-conditioning. We develop the architecture within a LangFlow-style baseline (Chen et al., 2026), retaining its continuous representation, training and noise-schedule objective formulations, and inference sampler across architectural variants.
