Anatomical vs. informational directness The distinction exposes a conceptual trap that recurs throughout computational neuroscience and connection- ist network models: anatomical directness and informa- tional directness answer different questions. Anatomical directness asks whether cell j forms a direct connection onto cell i. Informational directness asks whether the dependence of the output on the variables resolved by the observer can be represented without irreducible joint terms. The distinction can be stated formally. Let Aij = 1 de- note the existence of an anatomical connection from neu- ron j to neuron i, so that at contact resolution Aij = 1 whenever nij(ε) > 0. Informational directness at ruler ε, by contrast, is the condition that the resolved conditional law lies within the direct family of Eq. (1), equivalently that the higher-order residual vanishes. There is there- fore no implication Aij = 1̸ ⇒Ihigher(ε) = 0. (26) The left-hand side of Eq. (26) is a statement about adja- cency. The right-hand side is a statement about factor- ization of the conditional response. A monosynaptic anatomical connection therefore need not correspond to an additive computational interaction at finer resolution. Multiple contacts from the same axon can occupy different dendritic locations, participate in different local nonlinear regimes, and interact with dif- ferent subsets of the surrounding input12,13. When those variables are resolved, the response can contain higher- order structure. When they are collapsed through the somatic projection of Eq. (24), that structure can be hidden inside one effective edge. Anatomical directness consequently does not imply informational additivity. A minimal counterfactual makes the distinction ex- plicit. Let xj ∈{0, 1} denote activity arriving through the anatomical connection j →i, and let n ∈{0, 1} de- note a resolved local dendritic context generated by other inputs. Consider logit P(Y = 1 | xj, n) = b + αxj + βn + γxjn. (27) The anatomy is identical for every value of γ: in all cases Aij = 1. What changes is the informational structure of the response. If γ = 0, Eq. (27) reduces to b + αxj + βn and therefore belongs to the direct family of Eq. (1). The anatomical edge contributes the same log-odds increment irrespective of the local context. If γ̸ = 0, the same anatomical edge behaves differently. Its context-dependent contribution is ∆j(n) = logit P(Y = 1 | xj = 1, n) −logit P(Y = 1 | xj = 0, n) = α + γn. (28) For n = 0, the increment in Eq. (28) is α; for n = 1, it is α + γ. Thus the effect assigned to neuron j changes even though the anatomical edge itself has not. When all four configurations of (xj, n) occur with nonzero proba- bility and γ̸ = 0, the interaction term xjn cannot be represented as a sum uj(xj) + un(n). The true condi- tional law therefore lies outside the direct family, and the conditional-KL representation of Ihigher in Eq. (8) gives a positive higher-order residual. The counterfactual isolates the point cleanly. Setting γ = 0 and γ̸ = 0 does not add or remove the anatom- ical connection j →i. It changes only whether the in- fluence carried by that connection is separable from the local dendritic context. The same anatomical graph can therefore support an informationally direct regime and an informationally synergistic regime. The converse point is equally important. The existence of a contact field does not prove that every biological con- nection exhibits large higher-order information in every operating regime. If local integration is approximately separable over the distribution of states being sampled, a direct model may remain accurate even at finer resolu- tion. The claim is instead one of non-identifiability: ob- serving an accurate scalar dependency at neuronal scale cannot determine whether the microscopic realization be- neath it was itself scalar and additive or whether richer contact-level interactions were removed by the projec- tion. This is why one edge and one computational degree of freedom become equivalent only after choosing the coarse ruler. At contact resolution, a single neuron-to-neuron edge can unfold into the contact field of Eq. (22), dis- tributed across spatially distinct local domains. At neu- ronal resolution, that field is compressed back into the effective weight of Eq. (24). The topology of the compu- tational description therefore changes with the level at which nodes and edges are defined. 5.
