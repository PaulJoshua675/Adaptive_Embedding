Many constructs in the social sciences are bet- ter understood when measured along a continuum rather than as discrete categories (Licht et al., 2025). For example, concepts such as ideological extrem- ity, toxicity severity, and moral emotions vary in de- gree rather than kind. To incorporate these nuances and gradations, a growing number of researchers prompt LLMs to generate scalar scores on numer- ical scales, treating model outputs as continuous or ordinal measures of underlying latent constructs (Le Mens and Gallego, 2025; Koh et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024). Yet, how consistent their outputs are across dif- ferent rating scales or how sensitive those outputs are to changes in scale design has received limited academic attention. Existing studies have relied on different prompts and rating scale designs that vary in both scale format and range, even when measur- ing the same underlying construct. For example, some studies evaluate toxicity using a continuous scale between 0 and 1 (Li et al., 2024; Koh et al., 2024), whereas other studies adopt Likert-type ordi- nal scales ranging from 1 to 5 (Movva et al., 2024).
