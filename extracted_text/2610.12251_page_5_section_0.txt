Every head moves cyclically, from the last slot or cell to the first. We write MK,S,L for M6 K,S,L, whose window has 64 cells and whose records have 26 bits. A pass is S steps: the slot head of the active bank comes back to the slot it started from, so a pass evaluates the records of the bank once each, in order, each on the values the records before it left. The empty record, of β zeros, reads the sink with weight 0 and writes the sink, so it only moves the slot head on. An image is the sequence of the bits of a list of records, and a bank holds an image ready when it holds it from slot 0, followed by empty slots, with its slot head at slot 0. A bank that holds an image of G ≤S records ready evaluates them in G steps and then idles for S −G. The bank index is read at the start of a step, so a record that sets HO passes control to the next bank from the next step on, at that bank’s current slot. Theorem 3.2 (The machine as a ternary threshold network). Let α ≥4, K ≥2 and S, L ≥1, and put W = 2α and N = Sβ. There is a clocked ternary threshold netlist Nα K,S,L of depth 19, every weight ±1, on W −4 + K + K(N + S + β) + 2KL state bits: the stored window cells 4, . . . , W −1, the bank index in one-hot form, the bits of each bank with its slot and bit heads in one-hot form, and the cells of each tape with its head in one-hot form. For every state x of Mα K,S,L, the next state that Nα K,S,L computes from the bits of x is the encoding of the next state of x. Proof. The netlist has thirty-two blocks.
