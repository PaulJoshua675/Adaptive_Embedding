A manual inspection shows that (1) no more than two documents share a Do- cID, and (2) Most collisions occur between near duplicate documents, indicating good separation of dissimilar documents. 7 Conclusions We propose a computable DocID design whose bit- level factorization allows new DocIDs to be scored, while model fusion and multiple-tree search en- able better zero-shot retrieval. We find that existing numeric DocID designs perform well on seen doc- uments but degrade substantially on unseen docu- ments, while our approach maintains comparable performance on seen documents and improves on unseen documents by a large margin over previous methods, and remains close to methods that up- date model weights. This suggests that our DocID design for incorporating new documents without retraining is promising, especially given the high computational cost of retraining generative retriev- ers. In future work, we plan to apply factorization to hierarchical DocIDs (RQ-VAE or H-KMEANS) to investigate whether it can further improve recall at high ranks.
