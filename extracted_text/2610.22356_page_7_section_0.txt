Reference-frame composition. Verified single-step relations sharing end- points form a training chain with stepwise turn targets. Dual-Kernel Attention conditions token relations on 3D position and relative pose, but multi- step reference-frame reasoning additionally re- quires composing relative transformations across observations. Standard single-step supervision guides spatial judgments within a given refer- ence frame, but does not explicitly supervise how that frame changes across successive steps. Reference-Frame Composition (RC) instead de- rives and supervises the reference-frame update at every step. Figure 3 illustrates one such chain and its stepwise targets. Chain Construction. RC first converts each ver- ified relation according to the direction conven- tion of its source. Compatible relations are then connected along a sequence of landmarks or views to form a path C = (v0, . . . , vK). The head- ing obtained at one step provides the reference for the next, turning the path into a sequence of reference-frame updates. The target at each step is recomputed from the source annotations; Appendix A.4 details the construction and verification. Stepwise Supervision. Each retained relation is converted under its source coordinate convention into a canonical clockwise planar turn increment δk ∈[0, 360◦). RC describes the resulting reference-frame composition as h0 = 0, hk = (hk−1 + δk) mod 360◦. (6) Here hk is the reference-frame heading after transition k, for 1 ≤k ≤K. RC uses the turn increments and, when available, their cumulative headings as stepwise targets. We apply RC to VSI-590K and the view-change subset of SPAR-7M to construct four types of supervision (Yang et al., 2025c, Zhang et al., 2025b): exact turn angles and discrete turning actions from VSI-590K, and yaw changes between consecutive views and cumulative headings from SPAR-7M.
