Next, we construct a candidate visual event set containing 5 visual events by selecting those adjacent to the indices of the selected synchronized visual events. This strategy naturally incorporates neighboring visual events, which are prone to misalignment with the audio events—thereby creating challenging hard negative samples for subsequent reward assessment. Collectively, the filtered audio events paired with their corresponding constructed visual candidate sets form the final synchronization queries Q⋆. As a result, we obtain an audiovisual event unit set and an audio-to-visual synchronization query set for each video. These well-defined reference structures enable reliable event-level reward calculation and support the subsequent GRPO training pipeline. A.2.2 Details about the Rollout Process SFT initializes the audiovisual captioning format of the model and enables preliminary event alignment capabilities. For GRPO, we first conduct the rollout process to identify qualified videos and yield high-quality event-level supervision signals. We perform rollout on the videos selected in Sec. A.2.1, all equipped with the well-constructed reference structures (i.e., sets of audiovisual event units and audio-to-visual synchronization queries). Each selected video is then inferred 8 times (i.e., group size) using the SFT model. Leveraging the constructed reference structures, we compute two types of event-level rewards for each gener- ated caption during the rollout process: the intra-event attribution reward and the inter-event synchronization reward. Specifically, the intra-event attribution reward focuses on evaluating the proportion of events that are correctly attributed to the appropriate characters, while the inter-event synchronization reward quantifies the alignment between audio events and the constructed candidate visual events by calculating the F1-score. To ensure the quality of supervision signals for subsequent preference training, we implement a two- stage filtering mechanism at the query level and the video level. At the query level, invalid audio- to-visual synchronization queries are filtered by the inter-event synchronization reward. Specifically, we aggregate reward values for each individual query across the 8 generated captions, and retain only queries whose average synchronization reward falls within the interval (0,0.75), following the filtering protocol of HumanOmniV2 [49]. At the video level, filtering is performed based on the sum of intra-event attribution reward and inter-event synchronization reward. We first eliminate videos with fewer than 5 valid synchronization queries, as such cases lack sufficient synchronized semantic information to support reliable training. We then compute the standard deviation of the combined reward scores across the 8 generated captions per video, and finally retain the top 50% of qualified videos, yielding 3.2K valid video samples for downstream GRPO training. We conduct two rounds of GRPO training, with one training epoch set for each round. In the first round, we only leverage Global Event Completeness and Inter-Event Synchronization for model optimization. Accordingly, the rollout video selection relies solely on the standard deviation of the inter-event synchronization reward. In the second round, we incorporate all three designed reward components into the joint training objective. Correspondingly, the rollout selection strategy is updated to adopt the standard deviation of the sum of inter-event synchronization reward and intra-event attribution reward as the screening criterion.
