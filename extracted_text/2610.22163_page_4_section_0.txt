Hence, we evaluated the response generation step only with translated posts. 4.2.1 Translation The translation stage showed high quality in translating Mandarin posts to English, answering the first part of RQ1. The Microsoft Azure AI Translation API failed to translate only 2 of the 382 Mandarin posts; these remained untranslated. The translations of all other posts retained their original meanings and all essential information. 4.2.2 Question Extraction The question number extractor can accurately fetch question numbers for all categories of questions, answering the first part of RQ2. The extractor was deemed correct if it outputs the correct question number when the original post (folders, subject, and content) contained sufficient information to identify a specific question. If no specific question could be determined, no question number was output. As indicated in the first row of Table 1, the extractor achieved accuracy rates of 97.78%, 90.74%, 100%, and 97.46% for logistics, conceptual, environment setup, and programming questions, respectively. The extractor also performs comparably well on Mandarin and translated posts compared to the original English posts. The second and third rows of Table 1 show the percentages of overlap between the QE results for English and Mandarin/translated posts. The percent- ages of overlaps between English and Mandarin posts are 98.89%, 72.22%, 100%, and 97.97% for the four categories, respectively. The percentages of overlaps between English and translated posts are 95.56%, 74.07%, 100%, and 97.46%. We suspect the high similarity between QE results for the Mandarin and translated posts is due to the fact that question numbers often appear in abbreviations like "P2 Q10", which remain unchanged in both Mandarin and translated posts. Most failed cases occurred in the conceptual category when the original posts did not contained a question number, yet the QE results for the Mandarin and translated posts still attempted to find one. 4.2.3 RAG To assess the RAG and Response Generation stages, we evaluated the top 15% most viewed question posts and randomly selected 10% from the remaining posts from each category. This test set include 21 logistics, 14 conceptual, 9 environment setup, and 47 programming questions, totaling 91 posts. This approach ensured coverage of both the questions of highest interest to students and less common queries that might present unique challenges. We computed two metrics from human evaluation to assess the RAG’s performance: relevance and helpfulness. Relevance measured whether the retrieved chunk was pertinent to the question post, while helpfulness determined if the chunk provided an answer or significant contextual information to the question. RAG cannot replace QE. Firstly, it cannot differentiate between posts that do and do not contain question numbers. Secondly, it does not perform as well as our question number extractor. Among the 91 evaluated posts, 6 logistics, 6 conceptual, and 24 programming posts contain a question number. When RAG searched all course materials including quizzes and exams, it only correctly retrieved 3 (50%), 4 (66.67%), and 20 (83.33%) of the questions within the top 10 searches, respectively. Furthermore, of the 20 correctly retrieved programming questions, only 14 (70%) are the top 10 RAG results. This confirms that QE is crucial in our pipeline to accommodate the special case of student queries that refer questions by numbers but not content, answering the second part of RQ2. Lastly, including quizzes and exams in RAG searches tends to overwhelm the results for conceptual questions with similar quiz and exam questions, averaging 7.5 out of 10, which obscures the retrieval of relevant lecture materials. Therefore, we exclude quizzes and exams in our RAG search, reserving it only at the QE stage. With QE, the RAG results are highly relevant and helpful, which answers RQ3. Among the 10 RAG retrievals, the average number of them being relevant are 6.1, 7.7, 7.3, and 6.6 for logistics, conceptual, environment setup, and programming questions, respectively (first row of Table 2). Additionally, 80.95%, 71.43%, 88.89%, and 89.36% of the four categories of question posts contained at least one RAG retrieval that was helpful to students’ questions. RAG retrievals also performed well with translated posts but less so with Mandarin posts. As shown in the third and fourth rows of Table 2, out of the 10 retrieved documents for all 382 posts, the average numbers of overlaps between RAG results for English and Mandarin/translated posts are 7.0, 5.7, 7.5, 6.8 and 8.5, 7.0, 8.9, 8.2 for the four categories, respectively. This indicates that translation before the RAG stage helps pick up relevant information, addressing second part of RQ1 by showing that translation is necessary to our AI tutor system where input and output are all in another language.
