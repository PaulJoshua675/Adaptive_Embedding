In our evaluation, we include two variants corresponding to different optimization targets: sksurv GBS (Cox), which minimizes the negative log partial likelihood of the Cox proportional hazards model, and sksurv GBS (C-index), which directly optimizes a smoothed concordance index. SurvivalBoost (Alberge et al., 2024): A modern, highly optimized gradient boosting framework specifically tailored for survival analysis, optimizing a proper scoring rule. B.1.3 DEEP NEURAL NETWORKS These methods leverage multi-layer architectures to capture complex, non-linear relationships be- tween covariates and survival times, often dropping the proportional hazards assumption. DeepHit (Lee et al., 2018): A discrete-time survival network that optimizes a loss that combines a likelihood and a ranking metric over an MLP, usable in survival analysis and competing events. DeepSurv (Katzman et al., 2018): It uses a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to replace the linear combination of covariates in the standard Cox model, allowing it to learn complex, non-linear inter- actions while still optimizing the Cox partial likelihood. DSM (Deep Survival Machines) (Nagpal et al., 2021): DSM uses a neural network to model the survival function as a mixture of parametric distributions (such as Weibull or Log-Normal), providing well-calibrated survival estimates. SumoNet (Rindt et al., 2022): SurvivalMonotonic-net utilizes a partially monotonic neural network to directly optimize the right-censored log-likelihood. TabSurv (Kirpichenko et al., 2026): A specialized deep learning framework that adapts modern tabular neural networks to time-to-event prediction. Because this framework supports multiple representation backbones, we evaluate several architectural variants that appear throughout our re- sults: the default TabSurv, TabSurv-MLP (using a standard Multi-Layer Perceptron), TabSurv- RealMLP (utilizing the more robust RealMLP tabular backbone), as well as TabSurv-WAS and TabSurv-WSA (which incorporate specialized attention and sparse embedding mechanisms de- signed specifically for tabular features). B.1.4 TABULAR FOUNDATION MODELS This category represents the most modern class of baselines: tabular foundation models trained on synthetic tabular data. They generalize to new datasets in a zero-shot or in-context manner. SurvPFN: (B¨ohm et al., 2026) SurvPFN is a survival-specific Prior-Data Fitted Network (PFN) based on the architecture of TabPFN v2, pretrained on synthetic censored survival tasks (sampling from different distributions such as Weibull). SurvivalPFN (Qi et al., 2026): SurvivalPFN performs amortized Bayesian survival inference by us- ing the observed covariates, event times, and censoring indicators as in-context examples to directly predict posterior event-time distributions for new individuals based on the CausalPFN architecture. Survival-IC (Seletkov et al., 2026): Survival In-Context (SIC) is a prior-fitted foundation model pretrained on synthetic survival datasets to approximate Bayesian posterior predictive inference and based on the architecture of TabICL. TFM4Survival (Kim et al., 2026): TFM4Survival adapts pretrained tabular foundation models such as TabICL to censored time-to-event data through a sampling-based formulation, allowing existing TFMs to perform survival prediction without survival-specific pretraining. 20
