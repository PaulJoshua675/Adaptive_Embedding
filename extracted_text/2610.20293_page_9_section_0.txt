Broadcast, Singleton, and CASI use the same target model and training configuration and are each trained for 500 steps with five independent seeds. Final checkpoints are evaluated on the same 1,000 held-out requests by auction-replaying the generated five-action set; the primary endpoint is mean full-set replay value. The experiment allocates 10% of production traffic to treat- ment, covering approximately 108 observations; the primary metric is advertiser value (Advv). Rel- ative to the production control, we observe a 3.51% lift in Advv and a 5.08% increase in search-ad spend. At the retrieval level, complex-intent coverage rises from 30.86% to 32.58%, while first- screen coverage rises from 16.32% to 16.99%. We treat these coverage changes as mechanism- consistent diagnostics rather than as a causal decomposition of the online business gain. This ran- domized comparison evaluates the deployed CASI-trained policy against production control; it does not isolate CASI from alternative credit constructions online. Historical served-value audit. We separately audit replay against historical served value V0; this is observational rather than counterfactual (Appendix H). Among the 3,540 requests with successful replay and matched historical V0, 1,438 are auction-positive; on this subset, replay has Spearman ρ = 0.346 with V0. Holding each generated set fixed, the real-context auction-positive rate is 40.6%, compared with 32.7% under context shuffling, a real-minus-shuffled difference of +7.9 pp (95% CI [+6.0, +9.8]). Among the 700 requests positive under both contexts, the difference in conditional value ranking is +0.023 (95% CI [−0.061, +0.103]), providing no clear evidence of improved ranking under the real context. Thus request context materially affects auction activation, while this observational audit does not establish replay as a counterfactual measure of production outcomes. 5
