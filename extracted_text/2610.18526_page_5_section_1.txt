5.1 Diagnosis We read cross-lingual disagreement at the three points defined in Section 3.2, moving from each language in isolation to the languages competing in a shared index. Table 1 reports the first reading, Table 2 the second and third. Languages are strong in isolation. When each language is retrieved only from its own documents, every model performs well, with only minor varia- tion across languages. On both collections, mono- lingual nDCG@20 ranges from 88 to 97 and re- call from 96 to 100 across every language, for all models but the compact Gemma-300M; cross- language rank agreement is moderate and roughly uniform (50–66). Gemma-300M is the lone ex- ception (nDCG 70/76, recall 87/92, agreement 0.35/0.46): as the smallest model and a general- purpose rather than retrieval-specialized embedder, it produces weaker and less consistent rankings even before competition. The difference that later separates a failing language from a succeeding one is therefore not a difference in how well the model represents it in isolation, where every language is retrieved well, but something that emerges under competition. Mixing demotes rather than disorders. Placing all languages in one index changes retrieval sharply, but not by scrambling them. Comparing each lan- guage’s monolingual ranking with its ranking in the shared index (Table 2), the internal order is largely preserved, between 56 and 76 on the paral- lel collections, while the correct foreign-language documents fall far down the list: their rank drops from the top to between 50 and 240 depending on the model; these documents reappear at larger re- trieval cutoffs, confirming they are demoted rather than lost (Appendix E). The relevant documents are still ordered coherently relative to one another; they are simply buried beneath other material. Cross-lingual retrieval works before competi- tion. Table 1 shows each language retrieves well within its own documents, but not that it retrieves foreign-language documents well in isolation. We therefore test each query language against one for- eign document-language pool at a time, before mixing all languages together. Retrieval is near- perfect in this bilingual setting: Recall@20 aver- ages 97.8 (mE5-large), 92.8 (Qwen3-0.6B), and 97.6 (Qwen3-8B) across every language pair. The same queries and documents fail only once all lan- guages compete in the shared index (Table 2). A representational deficit would show up in the bilin- gual pools too; it does not, so the failure is in- terference under competition, not an inability to represent foreign documents. 5.2 What Replaces the Lost Documents We showed that mixing demotes correct foreign- language documents rather than disordering them.
