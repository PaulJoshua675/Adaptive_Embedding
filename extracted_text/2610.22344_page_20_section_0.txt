Figure 5 compares the learner waiting fractions of GRPO and WIRL under the same training setup. The waiting fraction is the time spent waiting for training data divided by the sum of waiting and training time. NatureBench Lite We use the ten-task NatureBench Lite subset listed by Frontis-MA1 [7], with the S and M definitions from NatureBench [12]. We follow the official NatureBench evaluation protocol, with a four-hour runtime budget per task that excludes time spent scoring submissions. Following NatureBench, we compute the normalized relative gap for each evaluation instance by subtracting the paper-reported SOTA value from the agent’s primary metric value, adjusting the sign so that improvements are positive, and dividing by the absolute SOTA value. Instances without a valid submission receive a gap of −1. The task-level score 𝑔is the mean gap across instances. Match-SOTA and Surpass-SOTA report the percentages of tasks with 𝑔≥0 and 𝑔> 0.1, respectively. We report a single evaluation of MLExpert over the ten tasks, with Surpass-SOTA on five tasks and Match-SOTA on seven tasks, corresponding to 50.0% and 70.0%, respectively.
