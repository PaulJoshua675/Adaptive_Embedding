Transition Reliability: Agents are differentiated by distinct prompt engineering techniques, identity embeddings, and asymmetric data access (e.g., access to different document corpora). These differences cause their state transitions (𝑠𝑡→𝑠𝑡+1) to vary in quality and relevance. A more capable agent is more likely to generate a state that attracts high downstream bids, thereby raising its expected future revenue. Because of these variances, even among agents attempting to fulfill the exact same role in a task, true valuations naturally diverge (𝑣1 ≠𝑣2). This structural gap allows the most efficient agent to shade its bid below its own true valuation while still outbidding the rest of the market, thereby securing a positive profit margin and incentivizing participation in the market. 3.4. Auction Mechanisms under Imperfect Competition We study heterogeneous markets under the same three auctions discussed in (Chang et al., 2020): • 2nd-Price (Vickrey): The downstream buyer pays 𝑏(2), but the upstream seller receives 𝑏(1). The marketplace platform must act as a market maker, subsidizing the deficit (𝑏(1) −𝑏(2)). • Credit-Conserving Vickrey (CCV): The buyer pays 𝑏(2), and the upstream seller receives 𝑏(2). This avoids subsidies, but fails to pass the full winning valuation upstream when 𝑏(1) > 𝑏(2). • 1st-Price (Bucket Brigade): The winner pays their exact bid (𝑃(𝑡) out = 𝑏(𝑡) (1)), and the seller receives the highest downstream bid (𝑃(𝑡) in = 𝛾𝑏(𝑡+1) (1) ). Credit conservation is structurally maintained without external subsidies. However, to secure a profit, agents must learn to strategically shade their bids below their true valuations. Because standard Vickrey auctions require subsidies and CCV passes minimal value to upstream agents, we prioritize the simpler 1st-Price auction; we justify this choice in Section 5.1. 3.5. If the agent bids its true valuation, 𝑏𝑖(𝑠𝑡) = 𝑣𝑖(𝑠𝑡), its ex-post utility is zero. Consequently, a rational bidder must shade its bid below its private valuation to secure a positive expected surplus (Krishna, 2009). We parameterize the bidding policy via a state-dependent multiplicative shading factor, 𝜉(𝑠𝑡) ∈[0, 1), yielding the shaded bid 𝑏𝑖(𝑠𝑡) = 𝜉(𝑠𝑡)𝑣𝑖(𝑠𝑡). The ex-post surplus upon winning is thus (1 −𝜉(𝑠𝑡))𝑣𝑖(𝑠𝑡), highlighting the fundamental trade-off of 1st-price auctions: lowering 𝜉(𝑠𝑡) widens the conditional profit margin but strictly decreases the probability of winning.
