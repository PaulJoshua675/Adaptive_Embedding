Under the same assumptions, the equivalent conditions of Thm. 3 hold if and only if there is a constant κ > 0 such that p(r) = κr2. (E.11) For this unique family, tan ϑ = (ηκ/a)r∥g(w)∥is dimensionless and gauge-invariant. The proof is given in App. G.5. The radial qualifier is essential. For a general direction-dependent scalar preconditioner ep : V \ {0} →(0, ∞), gauge equivariance requires and is implied by ep(cw) = c2ep(w), whose complete family of solutions is ep(w) = ∥w∥2φ(w/∥w∥) for an arbitrary positive function φ on the unit sphere, as derived in App. G.5. Cor. 2 establishes uniqueness only within isotropic radial rules, where φ must be constant. Ordinary Euclidean gradient descent has p ≡1 and obeys tan ϑ(cw) = c−2 tan ϑ(w). The Euclidean gradient step on the quotient depends quadratically on an arbitrary parameter rescaling. This quadratic dependence is the exact form of the effective learning rate that a normalizer induces on the normalizer’s upstream block, where the angular progress of plain gradient descent scales as η/r2 and the parameter norm silently retunes the step (Hoffer et al., 2018; Arora et al., 2019). Cor. 2 identifies p(r) = κr2 as the unique isotropic radial rule that removes this dependence instead of tracking or annealing the resulting effective learning rate. Together, Prop. 3, Thm. 3, and Cor. 2 make the optimizer rule operational at block level. All five indices and the two additional diagnostics can be computed inexpensively on small batches at intervals during training. Attention entropy regime. The calibrated attention regime uses dimensionless, row-centered logit variance vi = O(1). This paragraph gives the entropy expansion and the limitation of using logit variance to infer saturation. For row logits zij with row-centered variance vi := (1/n) P j(zij −¯zi)2, a second-order expansion of softmax entropy gives H(pi) = log n −1 2vi + O(max j |zij −¯zi|3), (E.15) showing that attention is nearly uniform for vi ≪1 in this local regime. The calibrated dimensionless regime is vi = O(1). Large variance lies outside the Taylor regime and often accompanies saturation. Inferring low entropy from variance additionally requires an assumption on the logit shape. Entropy and maximum probability measure actual saturation directly. The temperature in Eq. 3 targets the calibrated variance regime, and UVIattn measures deviation from that regime.
