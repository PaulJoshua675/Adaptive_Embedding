Grid-Bench uses eight families of executable operations and exact reference states to evaluate how accurately language models predict the complete final state of a discrete spatial world. Across six models, success on individual operations gives way to much lower exact recovery over longer histories, with substantial differences across models and spatial conditions. When complete recovery fails, the returned states range from nearly correct grids to extensively incorrect configurations. Some retain many correct updates beyond what copying the initial state would recover, even at the longest histories. Reliable prediction of spatial change remains challenging even with explicit rules. The executable rules can supply training examples with exact targets, while cell-level diagnostics can show whether models improve at predicting required changes, preserving unchanged values, or both. Limitations Grid-Bench evaluates predictions in fully specified symbolic worlds with prescribed operations. Its task families and generation settings define the spatial conditions studied here. Transfer to other world sizes, operation distributions, or environments with uncertain observations remains an empirical question. Error extent and update–preservation partitions describe the final values models return. Connecting these outcomes to internal representations or execution strategies requires evidence about intermediate computa- tion.
