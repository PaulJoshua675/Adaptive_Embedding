B.7 Human Evaluation Finally, to assess whether the improvements ob- served under automatic evaluation are reflected in human judgments, we conduct a blinded human evaluation on En→Zh translation. We randomly se- lect 32 test examples, with 16 each from IdiomKB and RAGtrans, and collect translations from four systems: Direct, CSC, SRAT-A, and SRAT. We recruit 20 native Chinese speakers with advanced English proficiency as annotators. They indepen- dently evaluate the translations with system identi- ties hidden and system outputs randomly assigned to columns. Following the MQM criteria in Ap- pendix F, annotators assign error penalties across five dimensions: Accuracy, Fluency, Style, Termi- nology, and Non-translation. Scores are reported as negative penalties, with values closer to zero indicating better quality. Table 14 shows that human judgments sup- port the overall ranking observed under automatic evaluation: SRAT (−1.94) outperforms SRAT-A (−2.77), followed by CSC (−3.56) and Direct (−6.32). Compared with Direct, SRAT reduces accuracy and terminology penalties by 53.2% and 85.1%, respectively, while also improving fluency and style. Relative to SRAT-A, SRAT achieves better scores across all four dimensions, with the largest absolute improvement in accuracy, from −1.15 to −0.81. Non-translation penalties are neg- ligible across systems. These results provide ad- ditional evidence that selective retrieval improves translation quality beyond an always-retrieve strat- egy, with benefits extending beyond terminology to overall accuracy and expression. Last, we provide more details on how the human evaluation was conducted. All 20 annotators were native Chinese speakers enrolled in master’s or PhD programs at a U.S. university, with advanced English proficiency. They were recruited through a shared link to an online annotation form. Par- ticipation was entirely voluntary and unpaid, and annotators provided informed consent through the form both to participate and to have their annota- tions collected and used for the research reported in this paper. Annotators received the same MQM evaluation instructions reproduced in Appendix F. The annotation protocol was determined exempt from further ethics review by the relevant institu- tional ethics review board.
