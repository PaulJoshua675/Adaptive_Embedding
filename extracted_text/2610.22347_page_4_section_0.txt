Few-step autoregressive generation. Distilled AR video generators (Yin et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025a) reduce the denoising process to K steps, enabling real-time streaming. Each chunk contains N spatiotemporal latent tokens indexed by i. At step k ∈{1, . . . , K}, the generator takes the current noisy latent and the clean history as input and predicts a clean estimate ˆx(k) 0 . For k < K, this estimate is re-noised to the next noise level for further denoising, and the final estimate ˆx(K) 0 is returned as the generated chunk. These generators can be trained with distribution matching distillation (Yin et al., 2024b;a), which aligns their output distribution with a target distribution represented by a pretrained teacher. In particular, Self Forcing (Huang et al., 2025) trains on autoregressive rollouts conditioned on self- generated history and randomly selects a denoising exit step at which to evaluate the distribution- matching objective. Intermediate clean estimates thus receive supervision as generated samples, although the standard inference procedure runs every token through all K steps. 3.2 TOKEN-WISE COMMITMENT Figure 2 shows that tokens approach the full-step output at different rates: some intermediate es- timates already closely match their final values, while others continue to change. This motivates committing converged tokens while continuing to refine the rest. Speculative Forcing treats each intermediate token estimate as a draft and uses a verifier to predict whether further denoising would substantially change it (Figure 3). Accepted drafts are committed as final outputs and removed from further denoising, while the remaining tokens continue to be refined. Commitment and continued denoising. Every token starts active, and the first step produces a draft for every token. At each step k < K, the verifier evaluates the active tokens. An accepted token i has its output fixed to ˆx(k) 0 (i) and is excluded from all subsequent denoising forwards. Its layer- wise keys and values computed at commitment remain cached, allowing active tokens to continue attending to it. Rejected tokens are re-noised to the next noise level and continue denoising. Any tokens still active at step K are committed at that step. Let k∗(i) denote the commit step of token i. The completed chunk combines estimates accepted at potentially different steps, ct(i) = ˆx(k∗(i)) 0 (i). The base generator’s clean pass over ct then refreshes the KV cache for subsequent chunks. Commitment reduces the number of denoising token eval- uations from KN to PN i=1 k∗(i), with mean denoising depth ¯k = 1 N PN i=1 k∗(i).
