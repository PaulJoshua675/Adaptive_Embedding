Every tested model converges within a mere 5% of the pool. The sufficient budget. Choose a resolution τ. Growing the distractor sample in geometric steps, we predict mAP@100M with the estimator at each size and stop once doubling from n/2 to n moves the prediction by no more than τ; that sample size is the sufficient budget. Taking τ at the metric’s own query-sampling noise h=1.96 stdq(APq)/√nq (0.013–0.019) and running against the 100M ground truth across the ten web-scale cells (ILIAS and INSTRE, five backbones each, 108 distractors), the estimate reaches its sufficient budget within 1% of the pool, and at 5% it matches the true 100M mAP to a mean absolute error of 0.005. The 5% budget is more than sufficient to predict the 100M metric, and the information that determines it is present in a much smaller sample. Per-positive calibration (Fig. 8). Fig. 8 plots the estimated per-positive hardness ˆxq,j (from a 5% sample) against the true xq,j (100M). Where the sample resolves the positive (count ≥5) the estimate sits on the diagonal; in the deep tail (x < 1/S) the EVT extrapolation is noisier per positive (log- correlation ≈0.4) but unbiased in the median (median ˆx/x between 0.9 and 1.3 across backbones). Because mAP averages over ∼6000 positives, this per-positive tail noise cancels and the metric is recovered to 0.005. Ranking resolution. Across the 20 disjoint draws, 9/10 of the ten backbone pairs are ordered correctly in every draw; the smallest true gap resolved in all draws is 0.013. The only pair that ever swaps is PE-core vs. SigLIP2 (true gap 0.010, below the prediction error), which are statistically tied at 100M. The method thus resolves rankings down to a margin of ≈0.013 mAP: pairs wider than that are preserved across disjoint samples; pairs within the error are not, and should be reported as ties. 19
