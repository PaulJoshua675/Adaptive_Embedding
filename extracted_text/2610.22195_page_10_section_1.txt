We presented HTN-Orchestrate, a two-layer orchestration framework that separates global task planning from local multi-agent execution. Its upper layer maintains a hierar- chical task network that decomposes incrementally as pre- requisite findings become available and can be rebuilt when the initial plan proves insufficient, while its lower layer coordinates multiple workers within each executable sub- task. On five benchmarks spanning web research, scientific reasoning, and research workflows, HTN-Orchestrate con- sistently outperforms ReAct, Agent Team, and DeepSeek Harness. Ablation studies on BrowseComp suggest that result-informed planning, local multi-agent orchestration, and task-network rebuilding each contribute to this advan- tage, with execution traces revealing interdependence be- tween the two layers. These findings show that separating control across two layers while feeding results back through a shared, revisable task structure allows each layer to inform the other and strengthens both. Future work includes scal- ing the task-network depth for problems that require deeper decomposition hierarchies and evaluating the framework across additional backbone models.
