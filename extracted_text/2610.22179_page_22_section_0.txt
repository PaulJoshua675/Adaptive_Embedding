The source and coding models can have similar central mass but very different tails. The power-law source places substantially more probability on large magnitudes than the Gaussian model, whose tail decays exponentially in the squared magnitude. Consequently, large exponent symbols can have very small model probabilities qt, making their ideal costs −log2 qt extremely large. These terms dominate the power-law-to-Gaussian cross entropy, producing the large entry in Table 2; it is a mismatch penalty, not high source entropy. LCT limits each exponent code to at most 16 bits, including the escape literal, and stores overflowing tails as raw bytes. Its storage cost is therefore less sensitive to this mismatch. This does not violate the entropy bound: the comparison is with a mismatched ideal coder, not an optimal coder for the source.
