3.1 Two-Process Homeostatic and Circadian Model Homeostatic sleep pressure S(t) ∈[0, 1] represents the neurochemical accumulation and clearance of endogenous somnogens (naturally occurring biochemical compounds synthesized within the central nervous system that promote sleepiness — primarily extracellular adenosine, inflammatory cytokines, and prostaglandin D2). Neurochemical assays (in vivo microdialysis and electrochemical biosensor measurements of interstitial brain fluid) demonstrate that extracellular adenosine accumulates in the basal forebrain and cerebral cortex during wakefulness as a consequence of sustained metabolic consumption of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) by active neurons and astrocytes (star-shaped non-neuronal glial cells that regulate synaptic transmission, clear neurotransmitters, and release adenosine via gliotransmission) [29, 2]. During sleep, astrocytic reuptake and enzymatic breakdown by adenosine deaminase rapidly reduce extracellular adenosine concentrations [29]. During wakefulness (M(t) = 0), S(t) asymptotically approaches an upper saturation limit (Smax = 1.0) with a rise time constant τr = 18.2 h. During sleep (M(t) > 0), S(t) decays exponentially toward zero with a clearance time constant τd = 4.2 h. These empirical time constants were derived by Daan, Beersma, and Borbély from human slow-wave activity (SWA) saturation curves across extended 40-hour sleep deprivation and subsequent recovery protocols [10, 1].
