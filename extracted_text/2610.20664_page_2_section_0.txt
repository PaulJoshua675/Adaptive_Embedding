Even a query preferred by the teacher might yield little useful evidence, while a less favored one could retrieve the evidence needed to answer the question. Yet standard OPD still scores the stu- dent’s query using teacher–student token likelihoods conditioned only on the pre-retrieval context, so the resulting score may not reflect the query’s realized utility. It can therefore penalize a useful query simply because the teacher preferred a different one. Figure 1 illustrates a case: the student correctly identifies the “Towada Art Center” and issues a Japanese query about free admission. Stan- dard OPD still scores it at −1.54, favoring the teacher’s wording even though the teacher’s query retrieves no useful evidence. The student’s query, by contrast, retrieves the gold evidence, yet the supervision ignores it. We refer to this failure mode as evidence-blind supervision (EBS). To understand this issue, we regroup the OPD objective by interaction turns, revealing two implicit priors that govern supervision allocation and update direction (see Appendix A.1 for the derivation): JOPD = Eτ∼πS T X t=1 nt N |{z} budget prior · 1 nt X i∈turnt log πT πS | {z } direction prior (1) where nt is the token count of turn t, N = P t nt is the trajectory length, and the log-ratio between teacher πT and student πS is evaluated at each student-sampled token. Under this formulation, (i) the budget prior nt/N allocates each turn’s share of supervision by raw token volume, so a verbose synthesis turn can outweigh a concise search turn even when the latter contributes more to finding the answer. Our empirical analysis in Section 4.1 confirms this effect: the first search turn accounts for 51.1% of the total information criticality, yet receives only 18.0% of OPD’s token budget. Meanwhile, (ii) the direction prior log πT πS favors queries closer to the teacher’s preference, regardless of whether they retrieve useful evidence. Empirically, we find that roughly 43.7% of query supervision is inconsistent with post-retrieval information utility. Together, these limitations naturally motivate a key question: How can we seamlessly integrate information available from post-retrieval evidence into the OPD process without distorting or destabilizing training dynamics? Building upon these, we introduce information gain (IG) as a lightweight post-retrieval signal to mitigate the two priors in EBS. Therefore, we propose Information-Gain Calibrated On-Policy Distillation (IG-OPD), a novel OPD framework for evidence-aware supervision. Specifically, IG-OPD employs Information-Guided Turn-Credit Calibration (ITC) to adjust the budget prior by information criticality, and Information-Guided Query-Preference Calibration (IQC) to reweight the direction prior by information utility. Extensive experiments in a live web environment demonstrate that IG-OPD improves average accuracy by +3.4 and +1.7 for 2B and 4B students, respectively. It also boosts the gold-evidence hit rate by 14.7% relatively, with the 4B student surpassing its 8B teacher on MMSearch and BrowseComp-VL.
