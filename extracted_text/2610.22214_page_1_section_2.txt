Multiple patterning lithography (MPL) has been widely adopted in advanced nodes to overcome the resolution limits of optical lithography [1]. By assigning closely spaced features to different masks, MPL avoids patterning conflicts. However, as feature sizes shrink, the number of required masks increases, leading to higher fabrication costs. To address this issue, directed self-assembly (DSA) has emerged as a promising and cost-effective alternative [2, 3]. When combined with MPL (DSA-MP), features that would other- wise conflict can share the same mask if they satisfy DSA grouping rules, effectively reducing conflicts. The underlying mechanisms and rules of DSA are detailed in Section 2.1. DSA-MP holds great promise for via patterning at sub-10 nm nodes, but the associated layout decomposition problem is substan- tially harder than conventional MPL. Beyond assigning each via to a mask, the solver for DSA-MP must also decide which conflict- ing vias to group together. This problem is already NP-hard [4], and the difficulty further escalates in advanced nodes, where the conflict-to-via ratio has grown from around 1:2 [5–8] to 2:1 or even 3:1, reflecting much denser conflict relationships. This increase in conflict density makes prior methods difficult to scale. Exact approaches such as integer linear programming (ILP) guarantee optimality but incur prohibitive runtime. Reformulat- ing the problem as an exact cover matrix leads to the exact cover matrix-based ILP (EILP) [6, 9], which reduces problem size but re- mains computationally expensive. In contrast, heuristic methods like DLXplus [6], while faster, often sacrifice solution quality. This leads to a fundamental dilemma: how can we achieve the high so- lution quality of ILP while avoiding its prohibitive computational cost? The key to breaking out of this dilemma lies in the ILP solving process itself. While a full ILP run takes hours, the solver typically reaches a high-quality feasible solution and accumulates rich in- termediate solving records during the early stage of the ILP solve. We observe that reassigning only a small fraction of vias in this early solution is sufficient to match the optimal objective value. If we can identify this critical subset, the solver can re-optimize only those vias on a drastically reduced search space, saving enormous computational effort. This insight calls for a shift in perspective: instead of treating the ILP solver merely as an end-to-end optimizer that runs for hours to deliver a final answer, we exploit the features accumulated during its early solving phase to predict which vias require mask reassignment to reach the optimum. arXiv:2610.22214v1 [cs.AI] 15 Oct 2026
