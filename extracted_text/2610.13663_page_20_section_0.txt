Coverage-map ablation The ablation experiment excluding the coverage map was performed for all three analyst models (Tables 14, 16, and 18). For Claude Opus 4.6, removing the map reduces the mean maximum to 91.02 ± 0.25 %, which is 1.14 percentage points below the detailed prompt (92.15 %) and nearly matches the level of the simple prompt (91.13 %). All ten runs nevertheless retain accuracy above 90 %, indicating that the coverage map has little effect on the result for the strong model. For Perplexity Sonar, removing the map reduces the mean maximum to 82.34 ± 0.70 % (Δ = 9.42 percentage points), and no run reaches the threshold. For Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507, the mean maximum falls to 80.18 ± 0.45 % (Δ = 5.70 percentage points), effectively returning to the baseline level of 79.88 %: the coverage map proves to be the key component that enabled this model to improve over the baseline argmax decoder (the class label is assigned by the neuron with the highest spike rate). 7. Discussion The experiments show that prompt detail can partially compensate for the limitations of the analyst model, although the degree of compensation depends on the LLM used. Claude Opus 4.6 achieves accuracy above 90 % with both prompt variants. With the simple prompt, Perplexity Sonar remains at the baseline level in all ten complete runs, but with the detailed prompt it consistently exceeds 90 % and reaches the level of Claude Opus 4.6. For Qwen3-30B-A3B- Instruct-2507, the detailed prompt also produces a substantial gain: the best result increases from 79.88 % to 89.16 %. At the same time, the higher variability of the maxima (85.88 ± 2.42 %) and the absence of any run with a maximum accuracy above 90 % show that the instruction structure does not fully compensate for the limitations of this model. When search cost is taken into account (Table 4), the advantage of Claude Opus 4.6 also persists in economic terms: despite the highest cost per run ($98 versus $16 for Sonar), it reaches its best decoder after only 𝑘min = 2 to 𝑘max = 43 evaluated candidates (Table 3), which makes the cost of reaching the best result comparable to that of Sonar ($0.37 versus $0.21), whereas Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507, deployed on a rented GPU, costs less than both proprietary models ($5.90 per run at the managed rate for the same model class, with the Analyst alone contributing $2.00) but still fails to reach the target threshold over the full run. In every task, the agent finds a technique tied to the physics of the problem: class-conditional spike-rate statistics for the CCN, the soliton structure of the solution for the Schrödinger equation, and a phase-separated representation of the pulse–perturbation interaction for the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky equation. The second PINN problem (a soliton with a Gaussian perturbation) is particularly informative: its analytical solution is unknown, so the gain cannot be reduced to substituting a ready-made formula, and the best configuration (Claude Opus 4.6) improves the conservation of the integrals of motion by more than an order of magnitude. In the KS problem, the improvement appears in a different physical diagnostic, namely mass drift. All five experiments are nevertheless handled by the same framework without changes to its internal structure. 8. Limitations Despite the results reported above, the present study has several limitations that should be taken into account when interpreting the findings and when comparing our system with alternative approaches. • Restricted search space. The agent modifies a fixed catalogue of editable blocks (encoders, neuron models, connectivity schemes for SNNs; loss weights, activation functions and sampling strategies for PINNs). Structural transformations that fall outside this catalogue – for example, adding auxiliary networks or replacing the entire training paradigm – cannot be discovered by the current agent and would require an extended action space. • Flat experiment log without a hypothesis graph. The Recorder stores runs as a chronological JSON log; it does not maintain a directed acyclic graph of hypotheses and their dependencies. As a consequence, the Analyst re- derives relations between experiments from the log at every step, which limits the depth of multi-step reasoning that can be sustained over long sessions.
