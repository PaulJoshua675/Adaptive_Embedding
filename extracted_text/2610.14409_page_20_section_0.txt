We compare block granularity (27 nodes) with weight-matrix granularity (74 nodes) on the CIFAR-10 replication checkpoint; the two graphs compute an identical forward pass, and all measurements here are in float64. With a generous relaxation budget (Kh = 3, Tfree = 60, Tnudge = 30), cos(∆θEP, ∆θBP) is 1.0000 to four decimals at both granularities and at every γ from 3 × 10−7 to 10−4. At the budget of our EP training run (Table 6), both give 0.9999 at γ = 10−4 and 1.0000 at γ = 3 × 10−5 and 10−5, with update norms within 0.2% of each other; both are about 2% shorter than the backpropagation gradient, a consequence of the budget rather than the partition. The finer partition does raise the correspondence error, as the previous paragraph predicts: the relative velocity deviation is linear in γ at both, with a coefficient 13.7 times larger at the finer one (2.04 × 10−4 against 1.49 × 10−5 at γ = 10−4). That places the finer partition’s bias at the TF32 floor of Figure 2 rather than below it, so in that arithmetic its operating γ would need to be smaller. At inference both partitions converge in a single sweep in error coordinates (Kh = 1 at dt = 1, App. E), which costs 7–8% more at the finer one on CPU. These are single-checkpoint measurements; we did not train at weight-matrix granularity, so we cannot say whether its training dynamics differ over many steps. D.4 THE ADOPTED GRAPH For the runs of the main text we use block granularity, so that whole modules of the backbone serve as fk unmodified. The graph has N = 27 nodes: twelve encoder blocks chained from the input, the middle block, twelve decoder blocks each taking [previous, skip] as parents with the skip popped from the encoder stack in the architecture’s own order, the output projection, and the ViT head producing o. The time-conditioning pathway inherited from the diffusion backbone is not a node: V (x) is time-independent, so it receives a fixed constant, which each block sees as a trained per-channel bias.2 E ERROR-PARAMETERISED RELAXATION Rather than relaxing the node states hk directly, we follow Goemaere et al. (2026) and relax the prediction errors ek = fk(hpa(k))−hk, recovering states by the topological scan hk = fk(hpa(k))− ek. This is an invertible change of variables on the same energy, so the two parameterisations share their stationary points. Their curvature, however, is very different, and at the step sizes of a practical simulation only one of them converges in a usable number of sweeps. The error parameterisation is a simulation device rather than dynamics a substrate would run: ∂E/∂e is a reverse-mode derivative through the scan, and a single sweep from e = 0 at dt = 1 is one backward pass (Goemaere et al., 2026, Thm. C.9). What it locates, however, is the fixed point of EPCN, which does not depend on how it is found: the local state dynamics equation 36 converge to the same point (below), and every result in this paper is a property of that point.
