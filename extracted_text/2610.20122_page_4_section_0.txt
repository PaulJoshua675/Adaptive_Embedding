XViewRank therefore learns from both view-specific score evidence and query-level view overlap, instead of applying one fixed rule to every query and view combination. 2.3 Learning to Rank with Lightweight Signals Supervised ranking methods learn document order from relevance labels [2, 3, 28]. XViewRank follows this direction with retrieval-side features only: it does not encode passage text, but learns a fusion layer from signals that are cheap to compute once BM25 runs are available. Unlike general-purpose supervised fusion models over broad feature sets, XViewRank deliberately restricts the represen- tation to view-specific sparse scores and view-overlap context, so the learned behavior remains tied to the multi-view retrieval problem. 3 Method Figure 1 gives an overview of XViewRank. The method has three stages: view- specific retrieval, feature construction, and learned fusion. 3.1 Multi-View Candidate Generation For each query q, we construct three query views. The first view is the origi- nal query qori. The second view qhy is a hypothesis-style rewrite generated by an LLM. The third view qst is a step-back rewrite that expresses the informa- tion need at a more abstract level. We use these two generated views because they represent complementary reformulation directions: the hypothesis view adds concrete topical evidence that may appear in relevant passages, while the step- back view abstracts the information need to recover broader lexical matches. Let v ∈{ori, hy, st} denote a view. Each view is searched independently with BM25 over the same passage index, producing a ranked list Dv q. The candidate pool is the de-duplicated union of the documents returned by the three views: Dq = Dori q ∪Dhy q ∪Dst q . (1) This design keeps the retrieval evidence from different views separate before fusion. A candidate may receive score evidence from all three views, only from the original query, or only from a generated view. 3.2 Overlap-Conditioned Score Features For a candidate document d ∈Dq, XViewRank represents evidence from two levels: document-level retrieval strength and query-level view relationship.
