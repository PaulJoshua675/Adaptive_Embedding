4 Approximations The optimal routing rule derived in Section 3, particularly equations 5 and 6, depends on the conditional correctness probabilities qp and tp defined in (4). In many practical tasks, these population quantities are unavailable without expensive human labels or exhaustive ground-truth verifiers. In this section, we derive a principled approximation to the optimal routing score in (5) that eliminates this dependence on ground-truth correctness. We first present the resulting routing score and its practical interpretation, and then derive it from the population-optimal rule. Our approximation, which we call corrected disagreement, takes a simple form. Recall that hσ = (p, yw,1, . . . , yw,σ(p)) contains the prompt and its sampled weak-model responses, and that Ys and Y ′ s are independent responses drawn from the strong model for the same prompt. We define the corrected-disagreement routing score as SCD(hσ) = min i≤σ(p) Pr(Ys̸ = yw,i | P = p) | {z } weak–strong disagreement − Pr(Ys̸ = Y ′ s | P = p) | {z } strong-model self-disagreement . (7) The first term measures the disagreement of the strong model with the collection of sampled weak responses. It corresponds to selecting the weak response that the strong model is least likely to disagree with. The second term corrects for disagreement arising from uncertainty within the strong model itself. Importantly, both terms can be learned from model-generated data without ground-truth labels. We discuss how to learn these quantities in practice in Section 5. We next show SCD arises as a principled approximation to the population-optimal routing rule. 4.1 Deriving Corrected Disagreement The population-optimal routing gain in (5) is ∆(hσ) = tp −maxi≤σ(p) qp(yw,i), where qp and tp are defined in (4). Let sp(y) := Pr(Ys = y | P = p) denote the strong model’s predictive distribution. Then ∆(hσ) (a) = X y∈Y qp(y)sp(y) −max i≤σ(p) qp(yw,i) (b) ≈ X y∈Y sp(y)2 −max i≤σ(p) sp(yw,i) (c) = min i≤σ(p) Pr(Ys̸ = yw,i | P = p) −Pr(Ys̸ = Y ′ s | P = p) = SCD(hσ). (8) Here, (a) uses the conditional independence Ys ⊥⊥Y | P to write tp = P y qp(y)sp(y). In (b), we replace the unknown ground-truth distribution qp by the strong model’s predictive distribution sp. Finally, (c) follows from Pr(Ys̸ = y | P = p) = 1 −sp(y) and Pr(Ys̸ = Y ′ s | P = p) = 1 −P y sp(y)2. Thus, corrected disagreement approximates the population-optimal routing gain using only comparisons between model-generated responses. Approximation quality. The only approximation in (8) occurs in step (b), where the unknown ground-truth distribution qp is replaced by the strong model’s predictive distribution sp.
