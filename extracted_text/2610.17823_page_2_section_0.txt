The sparsity is provided by entmax [29], which assigns non-zero probability only to items with sufficiently large logits and effectively acts as a differentiable form of hard negative selection [28, 42]. By incorporating item prior directly into the vari- ational principle, we make this selection popularity-aware: popular items are more likely to enter the competition, but only when they are contextually plausible. Thus, the prior influences that items receive a gradient signal, and the model is trained to discriminate the target item from a dynamically selected set of negatives that are both contextually relevant and popular. We evaluate PR-entmax across five datasets and two widely- used sequential recommendation architectures (SASRec [16] and GRU4Rec [13]), demonstrating that it consistently mitigates pop- ularity bias while preserving or improving ranking quality. The proposed approach modifies only the training objective and requires no architectural changes. At inference, the prior term is removed, and items are ranked by the learned contextual relevance alone. These properties make it straightforward to integrate our method into existing sequential recommendation pipelines. Our contributions can be summarized as follows: • We introduce PR-entmax, a sparse prior-regularized objec- tive that makes popularity correction context-dependent. The proposed objective allows item popularity to affect the active support of the loss, i.e., the set of items receiving non-zero probabilities and gradients during training. • We derive the proposed objective from a generalized varia- tional principle that unifies several existing training objec- tives as special cases, providing a principled foundation for context-dependent debiasing. • Through experiments across five datasets and two sequen- tial architectures, we show that the method advances the relevance–debiasing trade-off. Additionally, we analyze how the sparsity parameter controls this trade-off, identifying regimes with strong performance. 2
