Language models equipped with retrieval and tool use have enabled autonomous agents to investi- gate questions using external evidence (Lewis et al., 2020; Yao et al., 2023). In autonomous research, an agent searches for sources, follows emerging leads, and synthesizes findings into an answer or a document ranking. Interleaving retrieval and rea- soning allows intermediate findings to shape subse- quent searches (Trivedi et al., 2023), requiring the agent to coordinate research planning with repeated source inspection. The capability of such an agent depends on how it allocates a limited research budget. Each search exposes candidate sources that must be inspected before their contents can inform further decisions. Time spent assessing these sources affects how many leads the agent can pursue and how much support it can gather. Efficient evidence process- ing therefore offers an opportunity to expand the evidence available for reasoning and support more capable research. Yet retrieval coverage does not ensure evidence use.
