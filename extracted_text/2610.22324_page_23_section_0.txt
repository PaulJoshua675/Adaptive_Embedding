Human study Each of our four annotators first selects the ACL Rolling Review (ARR) subareas they are comfortable reviewing (e.g. LLM agents, Human-Centered NLP and Human-AI Interaction, Ethics, Bias, and Fairness). Independently, every paper in our test set is tagged with its ARR subarea by prompting an LLM classifier with the target paper’s title and abstract. We then filter the test set down to the papers whose tag falls within at least one annotator’s chosen subareas, and assign each of these papers to an annotator by matching the paper’s tag to that annotator’s selected subareas, so every rated portfolio falls within its annotator’s stated area of expertise. Table 13 reports the resulting per-area breakdown. Each cell reports the three systems’ means as Qwen3-8B / Claude Opus 4.8 / Ours. All three metrics range from 0 to 3. n is the number of source papers tagged with that area (out of 60), and its share of the evaluation set. Areas with fewer than 5 papers are pooled into Other (Computational Social Science and Cultural Analytics, Dialogue and Interactive Systems, Generation, NLP Applications, NLP and Code Models, Question Answering, Sentiment Analysis, Stylistic Analysis, and Argument Mining). We observe that ours has the high- est or tied-highest unique ideas in five of the six areas (Claude Opus 4.8 leads on Resources and Evaluation), but Claude Opus 4.8 has more promising ideas in Human-Centered NLP, Resources and Evaluation, and Ethics, Bias, and Fairness.
