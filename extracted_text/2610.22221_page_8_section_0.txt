The first control asks how much of any Q correlation is simply material recognition. Mapping the true material to its per-category median Q gives ρ = 0.430 [0.400, 0.457], and a material classifier trained on CLIP features, at 45.6% accuracy, mapped the same way gives ρ = 0.279 [0.246, 0.311]. Any pooled correlation near 0.28 is therefore indistinguishable from category recognition, which is precisely why we rest the argument on LOMO rather than on the pooled figure. The second asks whether the trainable head is doing the work rather than the teacher. An identically shaped head given direct supervision on log(Q+1), with no audio involved, reaches only ρ = 0.275 [0.224, 0.293], below the 0.300 [0.267, 0.330] reached by a linear probe on the same features. Additional parameters buy nothing here: with roughly 14,000 noisy scalar targets, a regression head has little to learn from, whereas the teacher supplies a dense 1024-dimensional target whose geometry already encodes damping. The third asks whether any structured target would do. Holding the seed fixed, distilling from correctly matched audio reaches ρ = 0.466 (the first, and highest, of the three seeds, whose mean is the 0.440 of Table 3) against 0.298 with no distillation. Distilling from a fully shuffled teacher (10.6% material agreement) still reaches 0.359, and positional indexing, used by an earlier version of our pipeline (13.3% material agreement), reaches 0.360. Roughly a third of the raw gain therefore survives destroying the correspondence entirely, while the larger part, +0.107, requires the teacher to correspond to the frame it is paired with. Reported gains from cross-modal distillation should be read against a shuffled-teacher control for this reason. 7 Discussion Three findings survive the controls. First, damping structure is present in audio and largely absent from single-frame vision, and separating the components of Q shows that audio carries the damping term β better than pitch, which is what licenses reading the transferred structure as physical. Second, a frozen visual encoder can acquire part of that structure from a frozen audio teacher without any Q label entering training, and the gain holds on materials withheld from the projection head as well as the probe (0.234 to 0.338, 15 of 17 materials), where a category-level strategy earns nothing. Third, roughly half of the improvement needs no audio at all and follows from four frames instead of one, so part of what looks like a limit of vision is a limit of single-frame inputs. The effect is modest in absolute terms. Most of the teacher’s signal does not cross, and on the pooled measure the distilled encoder barely exceeds what perfect category knowledge would supply. That comparison is itself the methodological result we would most like carried forward: category identity alone reaches ρ = 0.43 on this task, so a pooled number near that value says little about physical understanding. Establishing physical transfer needs an evaluation under which category recognition earns nothing, namely true LOMO, together with a shuffled-teacher control (Section 6.5). The residual is a concrete target rather than a closed question. Damping depends on internal structure a camera often cannot observe, and the natural next step is β itself, the component audio predicts best, nearly independent of f0, and the one on which the distilled student already improves most. 8 Limitations Four caveats bound these results. First, evaluation is confined to Greatest Hits (977 videos, 17 materials) and to one visual domain, so whether the projection head learns a general appearance-to- damping mapping or associations tied to controlled drumstick footage is unresolved. Second, the R2 ≥0.8 filter keeps 63% of annotated impacts at strongly material-dependent rates (Section 3.2), and the five largest per-material gains of Section 6.4 fall on materials retaining a third to just over a half of their impacts, so those correlations cover a selected minority of each category. The discarded impacts cannot be assessed with Q at all, since there the target is undefined. Third, the four-frame model is evaluated on Q alone (the f0/β probe in Appendix A uses a single-frame student), so although probing shows that audio carries β better than f0 (Section 5.2), we do not establish transfer of physical structure in general, and properties that impact sound encodes weakly or not at all, such as friction, may behave differently. Finally, we measure transferred structure by probing accuracy alone, so whether better Q decodability yields better physical decisions remains to be shown on a task whose outcome is not a function of Q. 8
