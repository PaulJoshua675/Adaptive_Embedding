The bars indicate the mean and standard error over 50 episodes To find out whether a more difficult task favors communication from the outset, we evolved three runs directly in the large arena (Table 5). Two communicated and one did not, the same proportion as in the small arena. The difference lay in the value of each strategy. The run with a referential signal, which emits in 99% of the steps over the food and in 2.5% of the steps off the food, scored 0.551 and outperformed the run without a signal by 0.165, about 15 seconds over the food per robot (p = 0.0003). The run with a beacon scored 0.229 and fell short of the one that did not communicate (p = 0.0004). In the small arena, all three strategies were tied, while in the large arena they were ordered from the referential to the beacon. Discussion 5.1 Communication follows the value of information The most direct result is that of the compass. When the robots were given the direction of the food, the signal disappeared completely, even though everything else was identical. Without a compass, however, a signal used by the other robots appeared in two out of three runs.
