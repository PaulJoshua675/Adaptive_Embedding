Stage II: Instruction Tuning. The instruction corpus contains 182,815 examples spanning com- plementary task families. We include the released ChatTS ift and sft training sets (Xie et al., 2025) for open-ended univariate and multivariate question answering. Using our TimeSeriesExam fork, we further generate the TSExam perception set, 20,640 multiple-choice questions covering pattern recognition, anomaly detection, similarity, noise, and causality, together with an 11,000- example numerical corpus of open-ended questions whose answers are exact statistics or dynamical quantities computed from the realized series. The largest component is an in-house 100,000-example reasoning corpus designed to develop broad reasoning capabilities over time series. It spans numer- ical, deductive, inductive, abductive, causal, etiological, temporal-relation, forecasting, and event- prediction tasks, with examples generated from procedural rules, dynamical systems, and domain- specific simulators. Because instruction-tuned models are sensitive to the task interface, we align the mixture of reasoning tasks and their question–answer formats with the capabilities evaluated by TSRBench (Yu et al., 2026a); the underlying time series, questions, and answers are generated independently and no benchmark instances are used for training. For tasks requiring explicit deriva- tion, supervision additionally includes deterministic rationales constructed from the simulator state, while the remaining tasks use direct multiple-choice supervision. Gold-by-Construction Supervision and Benchmark Separation. For all in-house synthetic data, supervision is derived from known generative parameters or exact computations on the realized signal. Captions describe the sampled components, multiple-choice labels follow directly from the generating mechanism, numerical targets are exact signal reductions, and reasoning rationales are rendered from structured intermediate facts. This design avoids using an external language model to infer labels for the synthetic data. Importantly, no TSRBench or TimeSeriesExam evaluation exam- ples are included in training. The TSExam perception set, structural captions, and numerical corpus are generated with our fork of the public generator and contain freshly sampled series whose lengths differ from those in the benchmark. Similarly, although the reasoning corpus covers the reasoning task families and question–answer formats evaluated by TSRBench, all training instances are gener- ated independently and no benchmark examples are used for training. We additionally verified the reasoning corpus against the benchmark for exact series overlap, shared sports-game instances, and exact question–answer matches. ChatTS evaluation data are likewise excluded; only the released training splits are used. TSRBench (Yu et al., 2026a), our primary benchmark, com- prises 4,125 multiple-choice questions over 15 tasks spanning Perception, Reasoning, Prediction, and Decision-Making. TSExam (Cai et al., 2024) contains 746 multiple-choice questions on pat- tern recognition, noise understanding, anomaly detection, similarity, and causality, and ChatTS (Xie et al., 2025) measures both fine-grained categorical and numerical alignment and higher-level reasoning over univariate and multivariate series. We compare against two families of models. General-purpose vision-language models receive the series as line plots: Qwen3-VL-8B (Bai et al., 2025), our own backbone, isolates the effect of our design and training, while GPT-5 serves as a substantially larger proprietary reference rather than a size-matched competitor. We further com- pare against representative dedicated TSLMs: ChatTS-14B (Xie et al., 2025), TS-Reasoner-7B (Yu et al., 2026b), and TimeOmni-1-7B (Guan et al., 2026).
