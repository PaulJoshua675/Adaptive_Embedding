If the controllers receive privileged information about the target, communication can become redundant, and the only way to obtain communication is to enforce it with bonuses or rules, making it difficult to determine whether it emerged through evolution. In our earlier transfer study, the controllers received the relative bearing to the food and a distance-based term as inputs [8]. This may explain why communication there had to be supported by additional mechanisms. However, the value of the information does not guarantee that communication will emerge. One in three runs, in both the small and large arenas, solved the task without a signal. This is consistent with the argument of Scott-Phillips et al. [20]. Sending a signal is pointless if no one responds, and responding is pointless if no one sends a signal, so a population without communication tends to remain that way. The runs that did communicate also evolved different forms, such as a referential signal or a beacon, which is reminiscent of the historical contingency described by Wischmann et al. [6]. 5.2 When the task is more difficult, what the signal says matters We expected a larger arena to make communication more frequent. This was not the case. What changed was the relative value of the strategies. In the small arena, the referential signal, the beacon, and the solitary search showed similar performance. In the large arena, the referential signal clearly outperformed the solitary search, and the beacon performed even worse than not communicating at all. The robustness test showed the same order with champions that had evolved in the small arena. A signal indicating the location of food retains its value when the search becomes difficult, whereas a beacon that only indicates the location of the partner can lead the receiver astray. The difficulty of the task did not create communication, but it did separate useful communication from unhelpful communication. This complements our earlier transfer study, which suggested that a communication protocol transfers well only if the ecological and navigational conditions under which it evolved are preserved [8]. Here, the referential signal evolved in the small arena kept its value in a larger one, whereas solitary search did not. 5.3 Innate scaffolding in a social task The expectation that innate reflexes would facilitate evolution was not met. All three EASA variants performed worse than GRU and were also less robust in the large arena. The diagnosis points to two mechanisms.
