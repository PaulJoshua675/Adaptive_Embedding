D. Repeated PINN runs and statistical analysis To assess whether the observed improvements are robust to random initialization, we repeated the training procedure for the GNLSE problem with a Gaussian perturbation. We compared the baseline model with two agent- designed configurations: one selected for its low equation residual and the other for its accurate preservation of the physical invariant. Each configuration was successfully trained ten times. Because the random initializations were not matched across configurations, the resulting samples were treated as independent rather than paired. Table 11 summarizes the results. Both agent-designed configurations reduce the invariant error by more than two orders of magnitude on average relative to the baseline. The residual-selected configuration has a slightly lower mean error and coefficient of variation, although the two distributions overlap substantially. In the original search, the invariant-selected configuration achieved an error of 6.61⋅10−4, lower than in any of its ten subsequent retrainings. By contrast, the original error of the residual- selected configuration, 1.40 ⋅10−3, is close to its repeated-run mean. This contrast indicates a selection effect: the best result found during a search can be more favorable than the performance typically obtained when the same configuration is retrained. E. The aggregate coverage map of decoder method classes from the complete 250- iteration log for the ⟨Perplexity Sonar, detailed prompt⟩configuration on MNIST is presented in Table 12. Each valid decoder is assigned to one principal class according to the substance of its decision rule, while decoders that explicitly aggregate several predictions are grouped in the “Ensembles” row. The map shows that the analyst spent most of the budget on hybrid ensemble schemes and discriminant analysis, whereas the correlation, margin-based, and argmax classes remained uncovered in this run. Best decoders and coverage-map ablation. In the best configuration (Claude Opus 4.6, detailed prompt), the 92.83 % result was obtained by a hybrid method: class-averaged representations serve as centroids, and the decision is based on Mahalanobis distance with class-specific covariance. The pattern is the same for Perplexity Sonar with the detailed prompt: the best decoders belong to LDA/QDA and prototype method classes. These classes use the entire output spike-rate vector, whereas the baseline argmax uses only one of its components. The ablation confirms the role of the coverage map: when it is removed (while the catalog of method classes and Chain-of-Thought are retained), Perplexity Sonar does not reach 90 % during a run, and the best accuracy falls from 92.70 % to 83.18 %; the analyst converges on a narrow set of similar ensemble variations.
