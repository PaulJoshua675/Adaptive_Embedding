Preprint 3.5 LEARNING OBJECTIVES Geometry-state learning. We train the scene tokenizer and decoder for geometry reconstruction and novel-view querying, keeping the GFM and its dense head frozen. The objective is Lrep = Llog D + λgradLgrad, (12) where Llog D measures mean absolute log-depth error over valid pixels, and Lgrad supervises local depth gradients. The learned tokenizer and decoder are frozen during world-model training. World-model training. We train the world model in two stages: single-step initialization followed by autoregressive rollout training. Target-state construction. To construct future supervision in a consistent scene coordinate frame, target GeoStates are constructed by feeding growing observation prefixes into the frozen VGGT-Ω. For the target at t + h, the prefix terminates at t + h, while all prefixes share the same first input view to maintain a common coordinate anchor across time. Only the outputs corresponding to the synchronized observations at t+h are retained and passed to the Scene Tokenizer to construct S∗ t+h. Since VGGT-Ωpredictions may vary in scale across forward passes, we normalize the metric spatial quantities in gp,v (Eq. 4) using a calibration derived from the observed context before fusing them with the GFM features. This gives target GeoStates a consistent context-derived scale without target- frame alignment. Further details are provided in Appendix A.3. Single-step initialization. We first train Fθ for one-step 4 →1 prediction with joint state and geo- metric supervision: Linit = λstateLstate + λdepthLlog D( bDt+1, Dt+1), (13) where Lstate combines Smooth L1 and cosine losses between the predicted state bSt+1 and the en- coded target S∗ t+1. The predicted state is also decoded as bDt+1 = Gψ(bSt+1, Qt+1) using the ground-truth future FRONT camera Qt+1 and supervised against LiDAR depth Dt+1. Thus, state supervision directly guides prediction toward the encoded target, while depth supervision constrains the geometry decoded from the predicted state. Autoregressive rollout training. We then unroll the world model for H = 7 future steps and supervise each predicted state through its decoded geometry. At each step, bDt+h = Gψ(bSt+h, Qt+h) is decoded using the ground-truth future FRONT camera Qt+h: Lroll = 1 H H X h=1 Llog D( bDt+h, Dt+h), (14) Unlike single-step initialization, we omit direct state matching during rollout, allowing autoregres- sive states to be optimized for their decoded geometry rather than exact agreement with GFM fea- tures. We examine the role of state supervision in both training stages in Section 4.5. 4 EXPERIMENTS 4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP Datasets. We pretrain the Scene Tokenizer and ray-conditioned decoder on approximately 10k multi-view scenes from DL3DV (Ling et al., 2024), TartanAir (Wang et al., 2020), RealEstate10K (Zhou et al., 2018), CO3D (Reizenstein et al., 2021), and WildRGB-D (Xia et al., 2024), using ground-truth depth where available and VGGT-Ω(Wang et al., 2026) pseudo-depth otherwise. We then adapt both modules on a subset of the Waymo Open Dataset (Sun et al., 2020) selected for static scene content. World Model training uses approximately 140k windows from 800 segments, each containing four context and seven future states at 0.2 s intervals, with FRONT, FRONT LEFT, and FRONT RIGHT context views. We evaluate on 100 Waymo windows held out from model selection, 113 eligible KITTI (Geiger et al., 2012) drives, and all 150 scenes in the nuScenes (Caesar et al., 2020) validation split.
