Convergence guarantees for discrete diffusion models have been established via uniformization (Chen and Ying, 2024), discrete-time analysis (Zhang et al., 2025) and stochastic integrals (Ren et al., 2025), together with guarantees for new samplers (Liang et al., 2026a). For masked diffusion, Liang et al. (2026b) bounded the KL-divergence of one-token unmasking by the integrated score error. Li and Cai (2025) showed that diffusion language models can provably accelerate autoregressive sampling, and Zhao and Cai (2026) further showed that parallel samplers with data-independent unmasking schedules adapt to the intrinsic dependence of the data. Bernoulli factories. Given a coin with an unknown success probability p, a Bernoulli factory produces a coin with success probability f(p) (Keane and O’Brien, 1994). General constructions were given by reverse-time martingales ( Latuszy´nski et al., 2011), and nearly optimal factories are known for linear functions (Huber, 2016). Recently, Chen et al. (2026) used a Poissonized Bernoulli factory with clipped estimates for high-accuracy sampling from diffusion models and log-concave distributions, where the estimates come from a path integral. We adopt this idea to correct parallel decoding in MDMs, with estimates built from score queries instead. 3 Preliminaries The goal of MDM is to sample a sequence X ∈VL, where V is the vocabulary, and L is the sequence length, from the target distribution p∗. We focus on the inference process of MDM, which starts from the masked state and progressively replaces the masked tokens with tokens in V. In detail, for a partially masked sequence y ∈(V ∪{m})L, let M(y) = {l : yl = m} and U(y) = {l : yl ∈V} denote the masked and unmasked positions, respectively. For the block B ⊂M(y) and the revealed tokens xB ∈VB, we define xB ⊕y as (xB ⊕y)i = ( xi i ∈B yi i̸ ∈B. Let T be the number of unmasking steps, and {nt}t∈[T] be the unmasking schedule such that P t∈[T] nt = L. MDM starts with Y 0 = (m, . . . , m), gradually performs unmasking, obtains {Y t}t∈[T], and outputs X = Y T ∈VL. In step t ∈[T], MDM chooses a set of nt tokens Bt ⊆M(Y t−1) and samples XBt. For any i ∈Bt, the token Xi is sampled from the distribution Xi ∼pθ,i(·|Y t−1), which approximates the ground-truth conditional distribution with a neural network parameterized by θ called the score function: pθ,i(x|y) ≈p∗ i (x|y) = P(Xi = x|Y = y). We then reveal the masked tokens of Y t−1 at Bt with XBt and obtain Y t, i.e., Y t = XBt ⊕Y t−1. Correlation within block induces error.
