When importance comes from few samples, as with EWC at 𝑛= 10 or 50, the estimates are noisy and heavy-tailed, the penalty concentrates on the parameters that happened to receive large values, and shrinkage spreads protection back out. Its benefit then disappears as samples accumulate, and too much shrinkage hurts. When the importance measure is precise but miscalibrated for retention, as MAS appears to be on P-MNIST, a small fixed weight helps at every sample size. Fixed shrinkage mixes the importance- weighted penalty with a uniform one within each group, 𝜆Í 𝑖[(1−𝛽)Ω𝑖+ 𝛽¯Ω𝐺(𝑖)](𝜃𝑖−𝜃∗ 𝑖)2, where ¯Ω𝐺(𝑖) is the mean importance of the group that contains parameter 𝑖. It therefore adds an L2 floor that limits the cumulative drift of parameters that every task rates as unimportant. That reading fits the finding that MAS approximates the square root of the Fisher information [5], a profile that is already flattened relative to EWC, but the floor is a hypothesis we did not test directly.
