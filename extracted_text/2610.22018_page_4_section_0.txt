5 Discussion and Limitations This study supports a concrete claim: Arabic calligraphy recognition remains a brittle fine-grained cultural vision task for current VLMs. Aggregate accuracy hides the main failure mode: models often behave like default-label emitters with partial discrimination layered on top. Several models overuse Thuluth as the default label, while others collapse onto Naskh, Diwani, or Nasta’liq depend- ing on model family and prompt. These errors matter because calligraphic styles encode cultural, historical, and religious contexts, and automated tools should not collapse less-visible traditions into more familiar labels. Community relevance. The failure mode matters for education, museums, manuscript archives, social media, and community-facing search: collapsing Diwani, Ruq’ah, or Nasta’liq into Thuluth can distort access to Islamic visual heritage. The study has limitations: it covers six broad labels rather than regional schools, mixed-style com- positions, dating, handwriting, OCR, or localization; source and label are partly confounded for non- DuwatBench supplements; Prompt C confounds label order and response format; and API-served models can change over time. Methodologically, the benchmark is useful because it separates two effects that are often blurred in cultural-vision evaluations. The rebalanced manifest reduces the chance that high Thuluth prediction is simply learned from label frequency, while the prompt interventions show whether the same images become more legible when models are given style descriptions or an explicit Diwani prior. The negative result is therefore informative: even when a prompt names the right visual features, many systems still substitute a familiar-looking script for the correct one. Implications. For deployment, prompt descriptions are not enough: systems that classify or re- trieve Arabic calligraphy should report uncertainty, validate performance per style, and avoid treat- ing high aggregate accuracy as evidence that all traditions are equally legible. 6
