Mechanism ladder: how to spend S Table 4 is the paper’s central experiment: the same per-track (ˆz, S) offered to every mechanism of Sec. 3.7 on identical inputs, per-mode threshold tuning. Ordering: veto ≪fixed < switched < σ-pure < continuous ≈aniso ≈σ-blend. Veto fails: the Mahalanobis gate collapses associa- tion (71.26 at χ2 4(0.95)=9.49, switches doubled), recovers monotonically but never fully (−0.5 at τM=100 vs. gate- free 77.04), and stacked on the adaptive expansion is the worst rung (70.82): a gate can only remove matches, and it removes the ones the widened tolerance exists to keep. Switching recovers a fifth: the binary velocity switch peaks at 77.24, recovering about a fifth of the 0.93 fixed→adaptive gap, its best variant the most conservative (aggressive vari- ants: 77.08, 76.86). Enrichment adds nothing: anisotropic expansion is byte-identical to the scalar on 41/45 sequences, the sigma-point blend is statistically indistinguishable, and pure expected overlap (β=0) loses ≈0.5 even after retuning, since an expectation penalizes uncertainty where expansion rewards it. Together: the scalar max(σx/w, σy/h) is empirically a sufficient statistic for association tolerance, and the covari- ance must be spent as tolerance, not veto. Scope. The adaptive gain over fixed expansion (+1.03 mean per-sequence delta, 95% confidence interval (CI) [+0.08, +2.13]; pooled +0.93) concentrates in basketball (+2.71); the identity-switch improvement is broad-based (22W/7L, p=0.008).
