A. Experimental Setup A.1. Datasets and Tasks Argoverse 2 (AV2) contains 199,908 training, 24,988 vali- dation, and 24,984 test scenarios [26]. Each scenario pro- vides five seconds of history and requires six seconds of pre- diction at 10 Hz. Models predict the future positions of the scored actors. In marginal forecasting, each actor receives an independently ranked set of trajectories; in joint forecast- ing, each mode is a coherent scene-level world containing one trajectory per scored actor. All AV2 experiments use K = 6 modes, while the K = 1 appendix metrics evaluate the highest-confidence mode. The Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD) contains 486,995 training, 44,097 validation, and 44,920 test sce- narios [3]. It provides one second of history and an eight- second prediction horizon at 10 Hz, and designates up to eight agents per scenario for evaluation. We use the marginal prediction setting with K = 6 trajectories per des- ignated agent. The official metrics are computed separately for vehicles, pedestrians, and cyclists at evaluation cutoffs of 3, 5, and 8 seconds and then averaged. We additionally retain the per-cutoff validation metrics to test whether re- finement gains change with horizon length. A.2. Metrics Let ya,t and ˆyk a,t denote the ground-truth and mode-k po- sitions of actor a at future step t. Average displacement error (ADE) averages ∥ˆyk a,t −ya,t∥2 over the prediction horizon, whereas final displacement error (FDE) evaluates it at the final step. For AV2 marginal prediction, the mode with the smallest FDE is selected; minFDE and minADE report its FDE and ADE, respectively. MR is the fraction of actors for which every mode has an FDE greater than 2 m. Brier-minFDE additionally accounts for confidence as minFDE + (1 −sk∗)2, where sk∗is the normalized confi- dence of the selected mode. Unless otherwise stated, AV2 metrics use K = 6 and displacement errors are measured in meters. For AV2 joint prediction, ADE and FDE are first aver- aged over the scored actors within each predicted world, and the world with the smallest average FDE is selected. We report its avgMinADE and avgMinFDE. ActorMR is the fraction of actors in the selected world whose final error exceeds 2 m, and avgBrier-minFDE adds the same confi- dence penalty to the selected world’s avgMinFDE. For WOMD, minADE and minFDE take the minimum displacement error over the K predictions at each eval- uation cutoff. A prediction is a match when its end- point lies within the official lateral and longitudinal thresh- olds after rotating the error into the ground-truth agent frame. The base threshold pairs are (1.0, 2.0), (1.8, 3.6), and (3.0, 6.0) meters at 3, 5, and 8 seconds, respectively, and are scaled between 0.5 and 1.0 according to the agent’s initial speed. MR is the fraction of agents for which no mode matches. Overlap rate (OR) measures how often the highest-confidence prediction overlaps another object visi- ble at the prediction time at any step up to the cutoff. Mean average precision (mAP) evaluates confidence ranking after grouping ground-truth trajectories into motion buckets. Matching predictions are true positives and misses are false positives, with at most one true positive per agent; precision–recall curves are computed per bucket and then averaged. Soft mAP differs only in its treatment of addi- tional matching modes: after the highest-confidence match is counted as the true positive, further matches are ignored instead of counted as false positives. The main benchmark table reports the official mAP v2 and Soft mAP v2 values averaged over the three agent types and the 3, 5, and 8- second cutoffs; minFDE@8s is the corresponding all-object result at the 8-second cutoff. The horizon analysis reports minADE and minFDE at each cutoff separately using the same trained model. A.3. Training and Evaluation Protocol FutureLift receives frozen proposal coordinates and logits from each backbone and does not access backbone queries, exported embeddings, or intermediate decoder features. Be- cause this interface consists only of trajectories and scores, no backbone-specific architectural adaptation is required, although FutureLift is trained separately for each setting. For marginal experiments, FutureLift replaces the native refinement stage of QCNet or DONUT. For joint experi- ments, it refines the final proposal sets produced by FJMP, Forecast-MAE, or DONUT. We run the trained compari- son refiners from their official repositories using the au- thors’ released hyperparameters; accordingly, SmartRefine also receives the backbone trajectory features required by its official implementation. The daggered SRefiner re- sults are instead reproduced from the authors’ AV2 evalu- ation with matched FJMP and Forecast-MAE backbones. While the SRefiner paper defines its initial feature as an en- coding of proposal geometry and agent pose, its released AV2 implementation loads both proposal coordinates and a precomputed 134-dimensional per-mode tensor named embeds final; this tensor is projected to initialize re- finement. Thus, these SRefiner rows neither use our bit- identical proposal files nor satisfy FutureLift’s proposal- only input contract. Except for the explicitly daggered SRe- finer rows, refiners compared within a backbone block use the same frozen proposal files, split, preprocessing, K, eval- uator version, and random seeds. Future and scene features have dimension d = 128, and the default refiner uses two heterogeneous attention layers with eight heads.
