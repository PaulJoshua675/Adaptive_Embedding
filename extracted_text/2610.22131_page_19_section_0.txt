Intern-Decision: Efficient Multimodal Decision Model C. Known-Distribution Test Construction and Cases Mechanism coverage and reference construction The pilot consists of six categories with four families each. Every family has two settings and every setting has two option-order variants. The resulting 96 records cover the following mechanisms: 1. Direct randomness and support: uniform die faces with impossible options; weighted candy inventories; deterministic single-color pens; rare winning lottery tickets. 2. Composed events and mixtures: sums of two dice; numbers of independent hits; independent backup generators; delivery outcomes under an unobserved route mixture. 3. History, conditioning, and hidden state: independent coin flips after a streak; socks after known removals; die outcomes given a restricted sensor observation; a two-state Markov process with noisy sensor readings. 4. Daily evidence and observation bias: defect screening with a stated base rate; conditionally independent repeated tests; correlated copies of one test; waiting times observed at a uniformly sampled time. 5. Selective disclosure and puzzles: informed-host Monty Hall; uninformed-host Monty Hall conditioned on a safe reveal; prize-dependent switch offers; two-child problems with different child-selection protocols. 6. Sequential and combinatorial processes: distinct coupons collected after finite draws; birthday collisions on a toy calendar; gambler’s ruin; first-reward times with a guaranteed stopping limit. The everyday descriptions define synthetic mechanisms with explicit parameters, not empirical frequencies inferred from real-world populations. Their oracles use exact rational arithmetic: finite counting, Bayes’ rule and total probability, hidden-state forward updates, coupon-count dynamic programming, birthday no-collision products, gambler’s-ruin formulas, and truncated geometric probabilities. An input/reference pair carries a stable ID and input hash. Validation verifies the option keys and probability support, exact normalization, and unchanged references when the option insertion order is reversed. Reference metadata records the family, setting, exact fractions, and derivation separately from the model-visible input. Support and conditioning: two further actual cases Fair die with impossible options. The state specifies a fair six-faced die labeled 1 through 6, rolled once without landing on an edge or leaving the table. The question is “What number will the die show?” The ordered criteria contain labels 1, ..., 8. The reference map is 𝑞= {1:1/6, 2:1/6, 3:1/6, 4:1/6, 5:1/6, 6:1/6, 7:0, 8:0}. This tests equal mass across possible outcomes and zero mass on impossible ones. Returning one face with probability one fails the distribution test. Even a perfect prediction has expected Brier 5/6, owing to outcome randomness, but zero excess expected Brier. Sampling after removal. The state says a drawer initially contained six red and four blue socks; exactly three red socks were removed without replacement, with no other changes. One remaining sock is drawn uniformly. The question is “What color is the selected sock?” The reversed-order record lists blue then red; its exact reference is blue: 4/7, red: 3/7. The model must update the inventory before predicting the draw. Reversing criteria changes neither event probabilities nor the labels attached to them.
