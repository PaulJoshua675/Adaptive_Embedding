B. Ablation of TLM Intervention Schedules To address the question when should the agentic reasoning agent intervene and correct the RL policy?, we conducted an ablation study evaluating six distinct correction strategies. The experimental methodology compared baseline RL per- formance against six TLM schemes, each tuned for fairness and consistency. The strategies included: Constant Correction, where the TLM continuously overrides RL decisions; Periodic Correction, which applies guidance at fixed intervals (every 10 steps) with a sparse, consistent intervention rate (correction probability ≈0.1); Step-Based Correction, which intervenes only during the initial 1000 steps before transitioning to full autonomy; Linear Decay, which reduces the correction proba- bility linearly from 1.0 to 0.0 over 3000 steps; Exponential De- cay, applying a decay rate of 0.95 every 100 steps; and Factor- Based Correction, which maintains a constant intervention probability of λ = 0.5. All strategies were evaluated over five episodes of 300 simulation seconds using a pre-trained DDPG agent with vectorized normalization in a realistic O-RAN con- trol environment. TLM-based corrections were applied using deterministic action predictions from our O-RAN–fine-tuned L3 model to ensure reproducibility. Our results, presented in Fig. 7, show that the periodic correction strategy achieved the best overall trade-off. It delivered the highest performance gain per correction, 11.86, the lowest coefficient of variation, 15.2%, and a median latency of 465.6 ms.
