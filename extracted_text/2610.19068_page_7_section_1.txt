We present a systematic head-to-head comparison of Retrieve-then-Plan and Plan-then-Retrieve at industrial scale (50k APIs). Three findings stand out. First, there is a clear complexity-dependent crossover: RtP wins on single-tool queries; PtR wins significantly on intra-category multi-tool queries (G2: recall +0.045, p<0.001, n=200) and directionally on cross-category queries (G3: recall +0.012, n=100, not significant individually; pass rate difference is significant). This crossover holds at every retrieval budget (k=1–20), every registry size (1k–47k APIs), and replicates on gpt-oss-120b, confirming the result is architectural rather than model- specific. Second, stub hallucination is a double-edged sword: it destroys high-coupling signals on simple queries but productively decomposes multi-faceted intent on complex ones. Third, the retrieval step is the binding constraint on agent behavior: planners faithfully execute whatever tools they are given (at least 96% fidelity), so architecture choice at retrieval time largely determines the downstream trajectory. These findings motivate complexity-adaptive routing as a practical direction: directing single-tool queries to RtP and multi-tool queries to PtR reduces discovery-call overhead by ∼36% with no retrieval quality loss. A cross-model replication on gpt-oss-120b (Appendix J) confirms the crossover directions are consistent across model families. Limitations This work is scoped as a controlled architectural comparison: we isolate the retrieval paradigm as the single varying factor, holding the planner, embedding model, and benchmark fixed (Gemini 2.5 Flash, GTE-base, ToolBench). This design choice enables clean attribution; Appendix J provides an initial replication on gpt-oss-120b confirming the crossover directions hold across model families, though generalization to other registry types (e.g., enterprise-internal APIs with different naming conventions) remains to be validated.
