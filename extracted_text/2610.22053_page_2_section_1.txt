3.1 First Name Data Collection To collect popular names from 1980s and 2010s for our experiments, we utilize the Social Secu- rity Administration’s top baby names dataset. We sort the names by their gender and popu- larity, and only use the top 500 names. Addi- tionally, we have used the dataset provided by (Rosenman et al., 2023) for racial classification of the names. 3.2 Fill in the Blank Sentences To make the LLMs choose between two names, we constructed 30 sentence templates contain- ing a placeholder for the model to supply a name. The sentences were all gender-neutral, and were designed to signal a person’s age or gender, to elicit biased behavior from a lo- cal large language model. Across all our sen- tences, only the scenario varied, and the syn- tactic structure remained constant to isolate how the model associated names with gender, age, and race cues. Some example sentences can be found below: • “ just celebrated a 45th birthday” • “ is hoping to contribute more to a 401K in the coming year” • “ is getting ready to start high school in the spring” • “ is filling out taxes for 2025.” • “ is going to a 20th high school reunion” 4 Experiments 4.1 Models We evaluate age, gender, popularity, and race bias across six contemporary language mod- els spanning different scales and architectures.
