B.3.1. C.2 SUBSTITUTING THE STALENESS PROBE SoftCache assumes nothing of the probe ρi of Eq. (6) beyond ρi ≥0 and the reset of Ai at a refresh, so either dynamic cache can host it. We use SeaCache’s filtered probe (Chung et al., 2026) throughout. Here we swap in TeaCache’s polynomial-rescaled probe (Liu et al., 2025a) for both the refresh decision and the headroom, hold the rest of SoftCache fixed (J=8, β=1, tail re-spacing, the τ grid), and score both configurations at matched wall-clock budget against both caches on DPG-50 (Tab. C.2.1). The policy transfers. On TeaCache’s probe, SoftCache improves on TeaCache at every budget and degrades it at none: by 2.92 and 1.32 dB of PSNR at the two lower budgets, and by LPIPS at the two higher ones, where PSNR is level at 0.29 and 0.25 dB. The host sets the ceiling. The same policy on TeaCache’s probe lands 3.6 to 4.2 dB below plain SeaCache, while on SeaCache’s probe it is at or above SeaCache and 3.9 to 10.0 dB above TeaCache. We therefore read this experiment as evidence that jump scheduling and residual motion are portable, not as a comparison between the two caches. It uses one seed and 50 prompts on FLUX, and substitutes the gate and the headroom together, so it does not separate which use of Ai is responsible. C.3 CAP SWEEP AND THE BLIND-PICK PROTOCOL The cap ladder of Tab. C.4.2 fixes J=8 on DPG-50. Smaller caps (J=3, J=5) are perceptually worse from 5× on. Larger caps are a wash through 5×, gaining PSNR and giving it back on LPIPS and DreamSim, and win only at 7×, where they lose high-frequency texture (Sec. C.4). J=8 is therefore the smallest cap that gives up nothing through 5×. The 7× band is handled by β instead (Sec. 5.4), which costs no texture. Two blind checks below back this, both on DPG-50 only.
