Our framework contains two complementary mod- ules: (1) Cross-Contextual Bridging for representation-level semantic recomposition (Sec. 3.2), and (2) Adaptive Identity Preservation (AIP) for spatially-aware struc- tural alignment (Sec. 3.3). AIP consists of two subcomponents: Identity Anchoring (IA), which establishes scale- and context-aware correspondence, and Identity-Aware Self-Attention (IASA), which refines context-dependent feature integration. During the denoising process over diffusion timesteps T, we switch from explicit structural anchoring (IA) to attention-based refinement (IASA) at timestep t within the unet. more reliably induce coherent semantic shifts. Notably, our observation is that the ⟨EOS⟩embedding can capture not only a single attribute [9, 20] but also multiple semantic factors, simultaneously affecting actions and environments through one EOS-level edit (Fig. 2b3). These edits occur without modifying the word-level tokens, indicating that semantic adjustments at the ⟨EOS⟩level can override or complement persistent token-level semantics. These observations suggest that the ⟨EOS⟩embedding provides a context-preserving handle that can encode rich, multi-attribute semantics through a residual operation. Moti- vated by this, we design Cross-Contextual Bridging, which transfers phrase-level appearance information into each scene’s ⟨EOS⟩embedding, allowing subject- specific appearance cues to be integrated while maintaining the scene context. 3.2 Cross-Contextual Bridging To avoid treating a story prompt as a single monolithic description, we con- struct reference subprompts from structured storyboard inputs (e.g., Consis- tory+ benchmark [17]). Each input is decomposed into four semantic compo- nents: style, subject, appearance, and scene.
