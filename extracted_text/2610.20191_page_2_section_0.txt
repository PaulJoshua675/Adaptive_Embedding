A representative method TEMPT [7] pre-trains on pooled multi-center prescription data and then relies solely on prompt fine-tuning to adapt to the target hospital distribution. Despite recent progress, existing studies still suffer from two major gaps. Gap 1: Entangled cross-hospital knowledge. Ex- isting multi-center MR methods often overlook that prescrib- ing decisions reflect both generalizable medical knowledge and hospital-specific resources, clinical protocols, and prescribing preferences. As shown in Fig. 1 (a), medication distribu- tions may vary across hospitals for the same disease [7]. Fig. 1 (b) further illustrates that transferring entangled cross- hospital knowledge in ICU settings with coexisting conditions may cause negative transfer and impair recommendation per- formance. Gap 2: Insufficient safety awareness. Existing transfer-based methods prioritize representation alignment but often overlook clinical safety constraints. Unlike general CDR, MR requires multi-label predictions that satisfy treatment needs, DDI constraints, and local formulary availability [2], [8], [9]. As illustrated in Fig. 1 (c), ignoring these constraints may yield inappropriate or unsafe medication combinations. To address these gaps, we propose HEM-Med, a hierar- chical agentic memory framework for multi-center MR. For Gap 1, a disentangled memory module separates transferable clinical experience from hospital-specific prescribing patterns into cross-hospital experience memory and hospital-specific residual memory. For Gap 2, a safety-aware medication set generation module combines dual-level retrieval with DDI- aware set decoding to generate clinically relevant, safer, and locally adaptive recommendations. The contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose HEM-Med, a hierarchical agentic memory framework for multi-center MR that explicitly disen- tangles cross-hospital experience from hospital-specific prescribing preferences. • We develop a safety-aware module for ICU medication set generation, integrating dual-level retrieval and DDI- aware decoding to account for clinical relevance, local feasibility, and DDI risks. • Experiments on the eICU dataset show that HEM-Med improves recommendation accuracy while maintaining competitive medication safety.
