Red and green arrows show the position of eosinf from its corresponding eosorg. Subspace Construction. To compute alignment with appearance and scene subspaces, we first collect all appearance-and scene-related phrases from the Consistory+ benchmark. To verify whether a meaningful subspace can be con- structed, we examine the cumulative explained variance from the PCA of each set. As illustrated in Fig. 12, capturing 90% of the cumulative variance requires only 81 dimensions for appearance and 221 for scene, out of the total 768 di- mensions in the CLIP text embedding space. Additional Analysis on eosinf. While eosinf exhibits better alignment with semantic subspaces (Tab. 3), such alignment does not inherently guarantee se- mantic preservation—unintended deviations from the original meaning may de- grade generation quality. To investigate this, we compute the pairwise cosine similarity between eosorg and eosinf across samples (Fig. 14), under the hy- pothesis that significant semantic shifts would result in low similarity. However, the similarity remains consistently high (µ = 0.9260), suggesting that Cross- Contextual Bridging maintains core semantic content. We further support this with a t-SNE visualization (Fig. 15), where eosinf embeddings consistently clus- ter near their eosorg counterparts. Together with qualitative examples (Fig. 13) showing improved attention localization, these results suggest that the infused ⟨EOS⟩embedding effectively combines essential semantics while remaining close to the original semantic configuration. Text Encoder Robustness. SDXL employs two types of CLIP text encoders: one with a 768 dimensional output and another with 1024 dimensions. To exam- ine whether the larger encoder also exhibits similar linear behavior, we measure the pairwise cosine similarity between eosorg and eosinf using the 1024 dimen- sional encoder. As shown in bottom of Fig. 14, the similarity remains consistently high (µ = 0.9407), indicating that the Cross-Contextual Bridging mechanism can be used across different text encoder configurations.
