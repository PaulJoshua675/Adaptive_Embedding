Third, partial shrinkage beats both extremes. Full shrinkage (𝛽= 1), which anchors every unit of a hidden layer equally, was worse than tag only at every 𝜆, by 0.9 to 1.6 points, and worse than random links by 2.3 to 4.3 points (Holm 𝑝≤0.001 in all six comparisons). Per-unit importance carries real information, and partial shrinkage keeps it while damping its extremes. Fourth, every activity rule did worse than random links. All 24 comparisons with random links of the same lifetime and 𝜆were negative, by 0.4 to 2.7 points, and 21 were Holm-significant. The diagnostics explain part of the gap. Raw co-firing concentrated links on a few units. At 𝐿= 25 it touched only 49% of the units in a task, with a Gini coefficient of 0.66 against 100% and 0.01 for random links, and it shrank less (CV 0.497 against 0.413 at 𝜆= 0.003). Correlation links covered almost every unit at 𝐿= 200 (coverage 0.96) and still trailed random links by 1.5 points. At matched dispersion they also trailed graph-free shrinkage, for example 80.6% for correlation links with CV 0.445 against 81.7% for 𝛽= 0.1 with CV 0.450. For the rules that link similar units, one explanation is that averaging importance among units that already behave alike pulls each tag toward a local mean rather than the layer mean.
