As in SFT, we train only on assistant turns, including tool calls, since also training on tool responses hurts results after SFT (§5.2). 3.1 TRAINING Midtraining starts from a converged base model, so we choose the training setup to add new capabil- ities without degrading what the base model already knows. We train at 256K context, long enough to hold almost every midtraining document, many of which are far longer than the 8K pretraining context (Figure 2). Our first N-3.5 Lightning experiments start from the 8K pretraining checkpoint, N-3.5 Lightning-8K. For the N-3.5 Lightning scaling run and all N-3.5 Super runs, we start from the 1M long-context checkpoint, which lets us go straight from midtraining to SFT, keeping evaluations simple. In a mature pipeline, midtraining would come before the final long-context stage (§5.1). We set the replay share to 20% (§5.3). The learning rate warms up briefly and then stays constant, at 5e-5 for N-3.5 Lightning, which favors agentic gains, and 1e-5 for the larger N-3.5 Super, which favors preserving general capabilities (§5.3). Our evaluation protocol is therefore holistic. It runs the full post-training pipeline and also tracks base-model post-trainability metrics and pretraining general-capability benchmarks. • Short and full SFT. We use an agentic-focused SFT blend (Appendix B.1) at two budgets, 33B tokens (short SFT) and 100B tokens (full SFT), and compare each midtrained model with its starting checkpoint after identical SFT. Full SFT was close to saturation in our runs and supports the main claims, while short SFT gives a quicker signal. • Fixed evaluations. We evaluate on SWE-bench Verified, Multilingual, and Pro, Deep- SWE, Terminal-Bench (version 2.0, or 2.1 where noted), and a BrowseComp subset. Un- less stated otherwise, SWE-bench runs in OpenHands and Terminal-Bench in Terminus-2, with pass@1 averaged over 3 and 8 rollouts per task (Appendix C.1 gives the setup and references). We also report pass@k, since a higher pass@k has been shown to indicate more room for RL to improve (Yue et al., 2025). • Base-model post-trainability metrics. LoCoDiff (Mentat AI Team (AbanteAI), 2025), RepoBench (Liu et al., 2024), and bits-per-byte on held-out code repositories and on agent traces (Appendix C.2) measure capabilities that midtraining targets, such as file-state track- ing and cross-file modeling. For traces we score the decisive turns of DeepSWE trajectories (Yu et al., 2026), which correlated best with post-training results. LoCoDiff is averaged over 3 greedy runs on all 198 cases, and the other metrics are single runs. They improve with midtraining (Table 4), but did not track post-training results closely enough to guide recipe decisions. • Regression checks. During midtraining we also track the pretraining general-capability benchmarks from NVIDIA (2026b) in four groups, general knowledge, math, code, and commonsense understanding (Appendix C.1), to catch regressions (§5.3). 4.2 BLEND VALIDATION ON N-3.5 LIGHTNING Our first experiment midtrains the N-3.5 Lightning-8K base model for 100B tokens, once on the full blend and once without agentic traces, and compares both with the base model after identical SFT (Table 2). Gains seen after short SFT carry over to full SFT, so short SFT can serve as a quick check. Midtraining improves SWE-bench Verified and Multilingual pass@1 by 0.8 and 8.2 points after short SFT, and both gains remain after full SFT (3.8 and 3.7 points). Terminal-Bench is noisier and improves only in full-SFT pass@1. Agentic traces give the majority of the gain after SFT. The no-traces model, trained on natural data alone, gains less than one point on SWE-bench Verified after full SFT, while the full blend improves on it by 3.0, 2.5, and 6.6 points on SWE-bench Verified, Multilingual, and Terminal-Bench.
