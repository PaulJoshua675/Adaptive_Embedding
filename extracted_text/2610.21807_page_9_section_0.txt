Page 9 of 20 Naganawa Discover Networks (2026) 2:41 4.3 A semantic cluster map of the noun vocabulary Applying the selection rule of § 3.5 to the 800 most frequent nouns yields twelve semantic clusters covering 302 nouns and 50.3% of noun-token frequency among the candidates. The solution is markedly more balanced than an unconstrained coverage- maximizing choice would be: the largest cluster holds 40 nouns (13% of classified nouns, 14% of total noun-token frequency), and no cluster mixes obviously unrelated registers Table 1. Two caveats apply to reading these as substantive constructs. Cluster 12 (calendar and dates) is best understood as a temporal lexical artifact rather than a content theme, and Cluster 5 mixes job-seeking vocabulary with question-answering framing terms (“question,” “answer,” “reply”); both are retained because the selection rule was fixed in advance, but neither is interpreted substantively below. The 498 unclassified nouns (49.7% of noun-token frequency) are treated as background rather than forced into clus­ ters — including “logistics” itself, the corpus’s own search keyword and second most fre­ quent noun, which co-occurs with essentially everything in a keyword-filtered corpus and therefore has no locally dense neighborhood Fig. 4. Appendix Table 8 reports four validation checks (§ 3.5). All clusters show higher mean pairwise cosine similarity than random noun sets of the same size drawn from the 800 candidates, indicating that the clusters are not arbitrary partitions. Across eight neigh­ boring parameter settings, the adjusted Rand index (ARI) averages 0.82, with six settings reproducing the reported partition almost exactly (ARI ≥ 0.97). The main sensitivity is to PCA dimensionality rather than HDBSCAN parameters: reducing it to 18 produces three coarse clusters (ARI = 0.03), whereas increasing it to 25 produces ten partially aligned clusters (ARI = 0.55). Removing reply and thanks posts has little effect on the semantic space, although it affects the clustering output. These posts account for only 3.3% of noun tokens, and 85% of the ten nearest neighbours for each classified noun are preserved (median 90%), compared with 2.6% under random pairing. Cluster coher­ ence also remains essentially unchanged. However, HDBSCAN produces only two clus­ ters when rerun on the reduced corpus. Together with the parameter-stability results, this suggests that the brittleness lies in the density-based clustering step rather than in the underlying semantic space. Thus, the semantic neighbourhoods are robust to corpus composition, whereas the exact twelve-cluster partition depends on the embedding and parameter settings. We therefore treat the reported partition as one explicit and repro­ ducible solution rather than a uniquely determined structure.
