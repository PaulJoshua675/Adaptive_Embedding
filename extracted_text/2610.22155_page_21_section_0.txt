Preprint Prompt 1: Failure-mode annotation codebook (continued) Require evidence of a discrepancy between the available current state and the model’s repre- sentation or behavior. Generic coordinate errors, repeated actions on an unchanged screen, and incorrect predictions of future states do not qualify by themselves. 3. Misjudging whether action is needed (RT-Act). The model executes an action when it should wait, or explicitly waits when intervention is required. Examples include committing before a prerequisite is satisfied, waiting through an actionable opportunity, and unnecessarily repeating a completed action in a way that reverses progress. Require evidence about the current readiness state, the executed action or wait, and its con- sequence. Rejected actions, parser-induced fallback waits, and reasonable waits for pending processing do not establish intentional action-timing errors. EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS For each candidate event, identify the relevant observation and timestamp, proposed and executed be- havior, contribution to failure, and plausible alternative explanations. Preserve source references to the trajectory, execution feedback, and supporting frames. Task instructions establish objectives and known constraints; observations establish information avail- able to the model; execution records establish which actions were applied. Final outcomes may cor- roborate consequences, but hidden evaluation state alone cannot establish that the model ignored an observable change. Assign each eligible episode–category judgment an evidence status: • Confirmed: direct evidence satisfies the operational criterion and supports a relevant contribu- tion to failure. • Probable: affirmative evidence supports the category, but an alternative explanation remains or the response window is inferred. • Insufficient: available evidence does not establish the category; this does not establish its ab- sence. Record out-of-scope episode–category pairs, successful episodes, and invalid runs separately. These labels characterize observable behavior, not verified internal cognitive mechanisms. AGGREGATION AND INTERPRETATION The main figure counts confirmed and probable judgments. Retain confirmed-only results as a sensitivity analysis. Count each episode at most once per category. For each model, divide RT-Deliberate assignments by its valid failed episodes pooled across TL and TH. Divide RT-Perceive and RT-Act assignments by its valid failed PH episodes. Pool episode counts rather than averaging setting-level percentages. Other denotes valid failed episodes without any selected-category assignment at the reported evidence threshold. Divide this count by the model’s total valid failed episodes across TL, TH, and PH. It in- cludes residual errors and unresolved cases; invalid actions and misunderstanding game mechanics are illustrative examples, not separately quantified subcategories. Categories may overlap in PH, and the plotted denominators differ. Percentages therefore need not sum to 100%; Other is not computed by subtracting the three category percentages from 100%. Assign labels according to evidence without imposing target frequencies or coverage rates. Interpret the distributions as evidence-supported attributions under the selected settings, rather than an exhaustive causal decomposition of failures.
