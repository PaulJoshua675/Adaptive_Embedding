Another means to quantify the asymmetric roles of experts is through the so-termed routing criti- cality, which replaces the learned router with uniform random rout- ing and measures the resulting per- formance change. We apply this intervention one layer at a time and report the relative perplexity in- crease, PPLafter−PPLbase PPLbase . Larger values indicate greater dependence on the learned router. As shown in Fig.3a, uniform routing in the early and middle layers of the shared-expert MoE leads to a much smaller per- plexity increase than in standard MoEs. In other words, shared ex- perts in these layers capture such a large fraction of the learned repre- sentation that the layers are robust to random routing. Next, we examine how this asym- metry in expert roles shapes explo- ration. Exploration aims to discover alternative routes that produce diverse token representations. We therefore quantify exploration by measuring how much the learned representation changes under different routing decisions. Even before this analysis, the asymmetry between shared and routed experts suggests that exploration may be ineffective: if a highly exploratory routing policy, such as uniform routing, barely changes the output of a shared-expert layer, other routing is also unlikely to produce substantially different representations. We next confirm this intuition using two complementary variance metrics, one induced by experts and the other by routers. Shared experts suppress expert-induced variance.
