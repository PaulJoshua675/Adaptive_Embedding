We identify a fundamental mismatch in conventional multiclass MIL: a diagnosis reporting only the most severe lesion censors coexisting lower-severity findings, which standard training treats as negatives. DomSet-MIL addresses this mismatch by coupling DCL with SORR and top-q pooling, aligning dominant-label su- pervision with severity-ordered patch evidence. Across natural and controlled settings, it improves dominant-label prediction and latent or localized secondary-lesion recovery, while ablations support the complementary roles of censored supervision, residual routing, and sparse pooling. These results show that modeling the weak-label generation process enables richer pathological representations from slide-level annotations. Future work will consider partially ordered, data-dependent, and uncertain label mechanisms, as well as whether biomedical knowledge-guided prompts, such as those explored in MeDKCoOp [43], can support lesion-specific evidence learning.
