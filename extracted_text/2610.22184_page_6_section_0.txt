Simulation Experiments We evaluate on-body repositioning in simulation using a WBD controller trained on 13 of the 15 primitives (three training seeds), with a 180 mm sphere and a 180 mm cube withheld from training, validation, and normalization. We degrade each of the 15 objects’ WBD-EX policies into coarse command streams and compare the degraded expert alone against the WBD controller refining it. We add in- dependent uniform noise U(−α, α) to all 14 Unitree G1 arm commands. We report results at α ∈{2.5, 4} and guidance weights {1, 10, 20, 100, 350}, with clean experts as a reference (Fig. 4). WBD applies bounded corrections to the noisy commands, within ±2.5 of each noisy command in the experts’ pre-tanh action space (Appendix VII-F). Degrading an expert reproduces the failure we see with real operator commands: the stream still carries the goal direction while individual arm targets move far enough to break supporting contact mid-handoff. In both settings, the failure is a loss of supporting contact followed by a drop. We evaluate transfers among six body regions (Fig. 5): front, both underarms, both shoulders, and back. Each trial moves the object through a seeded random sequence of adjacent regions (Appendix VII-D). The WBD controller is trained across all regions and voxels around the body; see Appendix VII-C.
