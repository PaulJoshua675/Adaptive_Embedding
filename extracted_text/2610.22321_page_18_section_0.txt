Audio and video are first analyzed independently, followed by bidirectional event verification and final aggregation into three consistency scores and a diagnostic conclusion. Stages 3 and 4 then perform bidirectional event verification. For each audio event, audio-to-video verification searches its temporal window for supporting visual evidence and, when applicable, ex- amines temporal alignment and fine-grained physical attributes. The absence of a visible source is retained as evidence rather than directly treated as an inconsistency, allowing later reasoning over off-screen audio and other asymmetric relations. Conversely, video-to-audio verification first deter- mines whether a visual event should naturally produce sound. Naturally silent actions are preserved as reasonable silence, while sound-producing actions are checked for corresponding acoustic evi- dence, temporal alignment, and physical compatibility. Stage 5 aggregates the modality-specific analyses and the two directional verification records. The evaluator also re-examines the original audio-video clip instead of relying only on intermediate an- notations. It then produces scores for global narrative harmony, event-level temporal synchroniza- tion, and fine-grained attribute alignment, together with a diagnostic conclusion that summarizes the main consistent or inconsistent events. Harness-based process control. The three evaluation levels require several non-trivial judgments, so we do not rely on a single unconstrained model response to construct the supervision. Instead, the annotation harness introduces explicit process controls around the underlying expert models. First, audio and video are analyzed independently, preventing early cross-modal information from directly biasing event discovery. Second, each detected event is converted into explicit timestamps and evi- dence records, and later checks operate on these records rather than restarting from an unconstrained global judgment. Third, verification is conditional: existence is checked before temporal or attribute compatibility, and inapplicable cases such as reasonable silence are explicitly bypassed. Fourth, the same relation is checked in both audio-to-video and video-to-audio directions, providing a cross- check for missing and hallucinated events. Finally, the aggregation stage receives all intermediate evidence but also re-examines the original audio-video clip, forming a final feedback check against errors propagated from earlier stages. These controlled verification records are then transferred to AVC-Reward through both training stages. During SFT, the complete five-stage annotation trajectory, including single-modal analyses, bidirectional event verification, three scores, and the diagnostic conclusion, is used as the full super- vision target. The model is therefore trained to reproduce the same staged verification process rather than learning only a direct mapping from the input clip to final scores. During GRPO, the structure reward explicitly rewards the presence of all five reasoning stages, while score distillation aligns the resulting judgments with human-calibrated scores. Together, the two stages preserve the verifica- tion procedure established by the annotation harness while transferring it from the cascaded expert pipeline into a single reward model. Whether this learned procedure leads to correct judgments is further evaluated through human alignment, training ablations, and qualitative cases. 18
