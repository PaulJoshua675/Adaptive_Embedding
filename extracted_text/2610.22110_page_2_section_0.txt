There is also significant variant-internal diversity. Treating them as a single computational language obscures the systems that index place and community belonging. Masewalkopa is the endonym used by speakers of the Nawatl in Santa Ana de Tlacotenco, Milpa Alta, Mexico City (nhn). It is morphologically rich and predominantly head marking: words can encode subject, object, derivation, aspect, possession, predicate-nominal structure, and honorific morphology. Character- level similarity can therefore conceal a wrong possessor or a form from another writing tradition. During the first author’s three years of studying Masewalkopa, he encountered persistent discrepancies between locally taught forms and Classical Nawatl- centered digital resources. As a result, his objective shifted from maximizing a generic “Nawatl” score to producing forms that can be inspected by a single, identifiable community. 1.1 From a Multi-Variant Model to Single-Variant Specialization In the first author’s previous work [18], he assembled approximately 70,000 Spanish-Nawatl pairs across varieties and domains. The resulting system (found on huggingface2) checkpoint achieved 54.15 chrF++ on a split from that same mixed collection. However, when evaluated zero-shot on an external benchmark, the Ameri- casNLP 2021 Shared Task on Open Machine Translation for Indigenous Lan- guages [17], performance collapsed to 25.7 chrF++. This external evaluation dataset consisted of independent Spanish-Nawatl text that was completely unseen during training. Beyond simple domain mismatch, manual inspection revealed that the model generated conflicting lexical, orthographic, and inflectional forms from different regions within the same sentence. While each piece might be attested somewhere in Mexico, the combined output was incoherent as Masewalkopa. We use variant fragmentation for this interaction between data aggregation and model behavior. The multi-variant corpus expresses a single underlying meaning through divergent regional realizations, the subword model shares pieces regardless of regional provenance, and beam search can assemble a fluent-looking sequence from conflicting varietal conventions. Aggregate evaluation conceals the problem when references come from the same mixture. A high in-domain score can therefore coexist with low external validity. To resolve this, we warm-start from the first author’s 1.2-billion-parameter multi-variant mT5-large thesis checkpoint and adapt it exclusively with Mase- walkopa data. The broad-base model provides sequence modeling and cross-lingual alignment, while the small community corpus provides regional constraints.
