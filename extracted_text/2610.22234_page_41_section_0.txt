In four of the five failed trajectories, cross-modal state is transferred correctly and failure arises later from task incompletion or application/tool errors. The remaining trajectory exposes a failed handoff: the PDF source observed in the GUI is replaced by a different, unverified URL when code writes the spreadsheet. As the audit covers only a representative task subset, the observed behavioral frequencies may not reflect the overall trends across the full benchmark. More- over, the audited trajectories do not establish that the observed routing decisions are optimal. Even with these limitations, the examples directly show that OS-Helix performs task-grounded informa- tion handoffs and cross-modal verification and repair, using GUI and code as mutually enabling and checking mechanisms. This process-level evidence complements aggregate task scores, which capture outcomes rather than coordination behavior, and further supports our central claim that the model learns substantive cross-modal coordination.
