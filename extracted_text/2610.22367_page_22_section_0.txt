This completes locator construction; the circuit next forms the evaluator ω, which will be used together with σ to compute the required phase. In-place construction of the evaluator polynomial. The key equation (29) gives ω by multiplying the locator by the syndrome polynomial. The circuit first normalizes σ so that σ0 = 1. This leaves the locator roots unchanged, and the corresponding ω acquires the same nonzero factor, so the ratio ω/σ′ used to recover the error values is unchanged. Write ω<35 = (ω0, . . . , ω34) for the first 35 coefficients of ω: (σ; S1, . . . , S35; S36, . . . , S70) 7−→(σ; ω<35 = σS mod z35; S36, . . . , S70). (35) With this normalization, each output coefficient is ωi = Si+1 + i X j=1 σjSi−j+1, 0 ≤i < 35, (36) so it equals the syndrome symbol being overwritten plus a function of earlier symbols. The transformation is therefore reversible: the circuit can overwrite the first 35 syndrome symbols with the first 35 coefficients of ω and later restore them. This avoids a separate 420-qubit register for ω. The same map extends to all 70 syndrome symbols; by the key equation, the coefficients of σS from degree 35 through 69 vanish whenever the locator is correct, so the last 35 become zero and serve as workspace until the map is reversed. Phase-only error recovery. At this point the decoder could use σ and ω to recover every error value. An ordinary decoder does so by applying Forney’s formula (30) to construct the twelve-bit field element ej at each located error. The DQI task (24), however, needs only the single accumulated parity P j⟨cj, ej⟩2. Constructing all twelve bits of each ej only to retain one parity would waste most of this work, so the discovered circuit computes the required phase bit directly. Any parity of a field element can be expressed as a field trace after multiplication by a fixed constant. In particular, with Tr(x) = P11 r=0 x2r ∈F2, the twelve mask bits cj determine a unique element µj ∈F212 such that ⟨cj, ej⟩2 = Tr(µjej). (37)
