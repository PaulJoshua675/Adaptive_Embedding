REPRO: Reflective Evolutionary Prompt Optimizer for Automated LLM Red-Teaming 19 Property 2 (Thompson Sampling for Strategy Selection) Thompson Sampling [2] achieves sublinear regret, E[𝑅𝑇] = 𝑂( √︁ 𝐾𝑇log𝑇), in stationary stochastic 𝐾-armed bandit problems. REPRO adopts Thompson Sampling as the foundation of its strategy selection mechanism for this reason: in the idealized setting where the bound applies, Thompson Sampling is the principled choice over uniform random or 𝜖-greedy alternatives. REPRO’s strategy selection operates in a richer regime than the classical bound directly addresses. Rewards are non-stationary because the parent population shifts as optimization proceeds; selection is context-dependent through the hybrid weight mixing historical success with feedback-analysis relevance (Equation 7); the action space grows over time as the library accumulates LLM-synthesized strategies; and the mutation operator G adds stochasticity that the classical bandit model does not capture. REPRO’s hybrid-weight mechanism is designed precisely to handle these conditions: the analysis-relevance term provides forward-looking guidance when historical data is sparse, the UCB term preserves exploration pressure as the action space grows, and the exponential moving average on weight updates smooths the non-stationarity. The ablation in Section 4.4 confirms that the hybrid mechanism outperforms uniform random selection by the margin the Thompson Sampling bound would qualitatively predict, supporting the claim that the intuition behind the bound transfers to REPRO’s setting even where the formal assumptions are relaxed. Property 3 (Pareto Coverage under Operator Coverage) Let G be the LLM-guided mutation operator. If G eventually reaches every region of X with positive probability, then REPRO’s population approaches the Pareto-optimal set in Hausdorff distance up to the relaxation parameters of §3.4.1. Property 3.6.2 identifies the coverage property that would justify calling REPRO a Pareto- optimizer in the strong theoretical sense. A formal proof of operator coverage for LLM-guided mutation is beyond this paper’s scope and, to our knowledge, beyond the current state of the art for any LLM-based search algorithm. REPRO’s architecture addresses this gap through engineering rather than through proof: the diversity-driven adaptive mutation of §3.5 prevents the population from collapsing into a small region of prompt space, structural diversity seeds inject architecturally distinct candidates during restart phases, and the multi-level stagnation management system trig- gers escalating restarts when the operator fails to make progress. These mechanisms together approximate coverage in practice by ensuring that the optimizer does not remain stuck in any single region of prompt space. The empirical results of Section 4 provide the evidence that this approximation is effective at the scale of the benchmarks we evaluate. Taken together, the three properties describe a coherent theoretical stance for REPRO. Elitism provides a formal monotonicity guarantee on the framework’s output; Thompson Sampling provides principled exploration/exploitation balance in the strategy-selection sub-problem, with the hybrid- weight mechanism extending the classical setting to handle non-stationarity and context; and the diversity and restart mechanisms approximate the coverage property that would be required for strong Pareto convergence. The ablation study (Section 4.4) tests each of these as a working hypothesis against the running algorithm.
