Time-Series Representations. Time series admit multiple representations that expose different properties of the underlying signal. Beyond the raw sequential domain, classical approaches trans- form temporal observations into alternative coordinate systems, including frequency and time- frequency representations, recurrence-based representations, and delay embeddings that reconstruct trajectories in phase space (Takens, 2006). A related line of work represents time series as images, enabling spatial models to operate on temporal structure. Wang & Oates (2015) introduced Gramian Angular Fields and Markov Transition Fields for encoding temporal sequences as two-dimensional images, demonstrating their utility for classification and imputation. More recently, Naiman et al. (2024) showed that image transformations based on delay embeddings and short-time Fourier trans- forms can provide effective interfaces between time series and modern vision-based diffusion mod- els, supporting generation across both short and long sequences. Collectively, these works demon- strate that alternative representations can expose useful and distinct structure in temporal signals. Our work builds on this perspective but shifts the focus from selecting a representation for a partic- ular predictive or generative task to combining complementary representations for general-purpose time-series reasoning: we jointly expose the model to views designed to retain different information and induce different invariances. Time-Series Language Models. A growing body of work investigates how pretrained language models can be adapted to temporal data. Early approaches primarily focused on forecasting, either by serializing numerical observations as text (Gruver et al., 2023), prompting language models with textual descriptions of temporal sequences (Xue & Salim, 2023), or aligning and reprogramming time-series representations to exploit pretrained language-model representations (Zhou et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2024; Jin et al., 2024). More recent work has broadened this paradigm from task-specific prediction toward Time Series Language Models that accept time series as a modality and support natural-language understanding and reasoning.
