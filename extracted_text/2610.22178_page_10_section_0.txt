In two-arm tasks, the most frequent cycle is an arm swap, in which one arm reaches while the other retracts (65 against 1 of 1000 episodes without and with memory, top row of Fig. 7). Memory does not rescue all these episodes: where the policy without memory oscillates, the policy with memory succeeds on 45% of the seeds. 6.3 INFERENCE LATENCY Protocol. We measure the latency of one call, from the raw observation to the unnormalised actions of one chunk, including preprocessing and the transfers between host and device. This latency bounds how quickly a policy can react to a new observation and how often it can replan, whatever the number of actions it predicts. In practice, policies replan well before the end of a long chunk, typically in under a second, since executing more actions open loop makes them slower to react (Black et al., 2024; Chi et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2024b). Each model predicts one chunk from two 256×256 images (128×128 for ABot-M0.5), the robot state and a fixed prompt, at batch size one, with its released checkpoint and inference code. Each value in Fig. 6 is the median over 320 chunks (80 for ABot-M0.5) and three repetitions, each on its own GPU of a node reserved for the timing (Appendix D.4). Compilation. Most of the compared models are released without a compiled path, or with one that can be further optimised. Without compilation, all models are slow (Fig. 6, left): REKI, π0.5 with openpi (Physical Intelligence, 2025) and ABot-M0 (Yang et al., 2026) take 118 to 139 ms per chunk, InternVLA-A1.5 (Ma et al., 2026) 352 ms, and X-VLA (0.9B; Zheng et al., 2025) 51 ms. In most of these runs, the GPU is busy less than half of the time, so compiling the prediction, which removes most of the host time, makes it two to seven times faster (Fig. 6, right). We compile REKI entirely into CUDA graphs, including its memory (Appendix A.1). For a fair comparison, we also compile X-VLA, ABot-M0, InternVLA-A1.5 and ABot-M0.5 (Chen et al., 2026) without changing their computation, and report both their released path and ours. Results. With compilation, REKI is the fastest of the compared models (Fig. 6, right): it predicts a chunk in 21.8 ms, against 25.5 ms for X-VLA, 33.8 ms for π0.5 with openpi, 46.1 ms for ABot-M0 and 51.3 ms for InternVLA-A1.5. Its GPU time per chunk, 16.5 ms, is on par with that of X-VLA (16.1 ms) and below that of the other models, which need at least 25.3 ms. InternVLA-A1.5 uses the same VLM as REKI, and the difference lies in its action expert, which runs at each of the ten Euler steps: with 0.5B parameters in 24 layers against 0.2B in 10, its Euler steps take 34.9 ms against 8.2 ms for REKI (Appendix Table 10).
