INT8 deployment must preserve the quantized algorithm with a DPU-compatible calibration recipe [21] rather than training-heavy QAT [12]. We target the AMD Kria KR260 [1] (K26 Zynq Ultra- Scale+ SOM, B4096 DPU, 4 GB DDR4, measured 4.3 W idle to 8.7 W peak) with the DPUCZDX8G accelerator [2], and deploy FlashOcc-R50 [51] on Occ3D-nuScenes [7, 40]. FlashOcc keeps features in 2D BEV and recovers height with a channel-to-height plug-in, a zero-FLOP reshape of BEV channels into height bins. It is fixed-compute, one forward pass with input-independent FLOPs, where sparse, transformer, rendering, and efficiency-oriented designs re- duce average work instead [15, 18, 25, 33, 54]; a deadline bound requires fixed per-frame work. Two FlashOcc operations are not DPU primitives: the LSS view transform [16, 34] and BEV-neck bilinear resize. Send- ing them to ARM would put OS jitter back onto the criti- cal path, so both move to programmable logic (PL) as cus- tom HLS operators [11, 14]. The final softplus-plus-linear predictor over 40 000 BEV cells is fixed-cost but not small (22 GFLOP per frame); we measure it on the CPU and pro- totype it on the DPU in Sec. 4.3. 3.2. Static-index gather operators Both non-DPU operations are irregular only because of ge- ometry, and the geometry is fixed. The LSS projection ta- bles are functions of intrinsics, extrinsics, and preprocess- ing; bilinear coordinates and weights are functions of tensor shapes.
