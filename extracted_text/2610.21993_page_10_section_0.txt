The ConvNeXt-RS backbone is finetuned end-to-end throughout instead of being kept frozen. Inputs are resized to the resolution specified by the Copernicus-Bench benchmark protocol for each task. EuroSAT-S1 (native 64×64) and BigEarthNet-S1 (native 120×120) are upsampled to 224×224 using bilinear interpolation. Flood-S1 is already native at 224×224, so no resiz- ing is applied. DFC2020-S1 is kept at its native 256×256 resolution rather than being unified to 224×224 like the other three tasks. We retain each task’s benchmark-specified resolution, rather than unifying all four to a single value, to keep our evaluation as directly comparable to the published Copernicus-Bench baselines as our full-finetuning setting allows (Section 1). For DFC2020-S1 and Flood-S1, resizing (where applied) is performed jointly on the image and the segmentation mask, using paired image-mask transformations, with bilinear interpolation for the image and nearest-neighbor interpolation for the mask so that resizing does not blend adjacent class labels into fractional, non-existent intermediate values at boundaries. Because ConvNeXt- RS is a fully convolutional encoder with no fixed-input-size constraint from positional embed- dings, it accepts both resolutions directly, with the stride-16 feature map scaling accordingly (14×14 for 224×224 inputs vs. 16×16 for DFC2020-S1’s 256×256). We apply Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD) (Bao et al., 2022; Clark et al., 2020) with layer decay factor 0.75, scaling the learning rate geometrically from the classification head toward the stem. LLRD runs also exclude the backbone’s biases and normalization parameters from weight decay. For the UNet-128-pretrained encoder, LLRD improves mean accuracy on all four tasks and reduces across-seed variance, most markedly on DFC2020-S1 and BigEarthNet- S1 (Section 4.4.3); it does not improve a non-pretrained encoder (Table 5 and the 0% row of Ta- ble 11) and lowers its Flood-S1 mIoU by 0.8 pp, so we treat it as a finetuning recipe for pretrained encoders. All results are reported as mean ± sample standard deviation (n−1 denominator) over 5 fixed random seeds (42, 123, 456, 789, 1337), with deterministic seed initialization for PyTorch, CUDA, NumPy, Python’s random, data-loader workers, and the distributed data sampler. Our production results were obtained on V100 GPUs (4×V100 32 GB for pretraining, a single V100 for finetuning). After these GPUs were retired, the Flood-S1 ablation (Table 10), the LLRD decay-factor sweep (Table 9, all columns except the production λ=0.75), the label-efficiency experiments (Table 12, all rows except the 100% row, which reuses the production runs) and the frozen-encoder evaluation (Table 6) were run on NVIDIA RTX A6000 48 GB GPUs with the same precision settings (FP16 pretraining, FP32 finetuning).
