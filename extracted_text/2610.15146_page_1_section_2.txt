Scaling laws address a basic allocation question: given a fixed compute budget C, how should it be split between model size N and data D to minimize loss? Kaplan et al. [2020] established power-law scaling for Transformers, and Hoffmann et al. [2022] (Chinchilla) introduced the IsoFLOP methodology and revised the optimal allocation toward roughly equal scaling of N and D. How these laws transfer to spiking neural networks is unknown. SpikeGPT [Zhu et al., 2023] binarizes activations into spikes on an RWKV backbone [Peng et al., 2023] for energy-efficient neuromorphic inference, but was trained far below the regime where compute-optimal behavior is characterized, leaving open both how a spiking model should be scaled and what accuracy its sparsity costs. We conduct a Chinchilla-style IsoFLOP study across a grid of compute budgets, fitting parabo- las to recover compute-optimal (Nopt, Dopt) at each budget. Our contributions are: (1) the first compute-optimal IsoFLOP scaling law for a spiking language model, Nopt ∝C0.56, close to dense Transformers; (2) non-spiking RWKV and Transformer baselines at every budget, which separate a constant backbone cost (∼0.16 nats) from a spiking cost that falls to about 0.23 nats and then levels off; and (3) evidence that the apparent departure from the Chinchilla exponent is largely a parameter-counting artifact. 40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026).
