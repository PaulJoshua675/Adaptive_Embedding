While a 3D point can be projected into any camera view to obtain a 2D position, visibility in that cross-time view is not directly available from the dataset annotations. Defining both targets in the ttgt view keeps their physical definitions consistent, and we empirically find that they work well as auxiliary losses, consistent with the design in Li & Team (2026). Frame and Query Sampling. Since the Wan-VAE uses causal temporal convolutions, the latent at each timestamp doesn’t encode future RGB frames and thus can be safely head-truncated. Its encoding is first-frame anchored: the first latent corresponds to the first RGB frame, while each subsequent latent encodes four additional RGB frames. Thus, for T = 4N + 1, we slice the first T query frames and the first N + 1 latent frames. In practice, we randomly sample the query clip length from {21, 25, . . . , 49}. A too-short clip will fail to provide adequate context for motion learning and lead to a performance drop. During training, we sample at most 2048 queries per training clip, with tsrc independently sampled for each query. Temporally, we enforce ttgt = tcam for 40% of the queries, while the rest are randomly sampled. Spatially, 40% is sampled from the foreground and 10% is sampled from the foreground boundaries marked by Sobel-filtered depth maps.
