Neither context policy lowers task score in these selected comparisons. 3.3.6 How Much Task Performance Can Money and Time Buy? Final benchmark scores alone do not show how much performance is available under limited resources. We therefore examine monetary and time constraints using the same selected full-setting runs as the main evaluation on the offline subset of OSWorld-v2. Measuring Performance under Resource Limits. We retrospectively apply a common spending cap to every task. A trajectory contributes its recorded final partial score only if its full cost fits within the cap; otherwise, it contributes zero. We estimate spending by assuming that each attempt ends when its recorded trajectory finishes or its spending cap is reached, whichever comes first. Spending on unsuccessful attempts is also included. Figure 7(a) plots the resulting score against average expenditure per task, not the cap itself. Panel (b) applies the same scoring rule to a per-task time limit. Scores are averaged over all tasks. Notably, recorded wall-clock time depends on API bandwidth and rate limits, the number of concurrently active evaluation environments, provider-side queuing, and tool execution latency. These conditions were not controlled across models, limiting the comparability of the measurements. We therefore report the time curves as exploratory evidence and treat their trends as indicative rather than conclusive. Money and Time Favor Different Models. Under the assumed API prices, the highest-scoring model shifts from Qwen3.8-Flash to GLM-5.3-Flash, Gemini 3.8 Flash, and eventually GPT-6 Astra as the monetary budget increases. For example, Qwen3.8-Flash reaches 60.5 points at approximately $0.38 per task, whereas GPT-6 Astra reaches the highest score of 75.7 at approximately $15.26 per task. In the recorded-time comparison, however, GPT-6 Astra achieves higher scores at most time limits. From Benchmark Scores to Resource-Aware Model Selection. In practice, the relevant question is not only which model achieves the highest benchmark score, but which delivers the most task performance within a given budget or an acceptable completion time. These curves make this distinction explicit: models with lower final scores may be preferable under tighter resource constraints. 4 Real-Device Generalist CUA In Section 3, we study code execution and harness-to-model decision transfer on OSWorld-v2, where every task runs in a controlled benchmark environment.
