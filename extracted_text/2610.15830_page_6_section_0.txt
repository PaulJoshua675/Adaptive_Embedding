4.2 State-following feedback sustains long-lived regimes To test a present sustaining mechanism, a related tanh network receives the complete input while a designed rule chooses which target is updated. The reinforcing rule follows the current allocation index: it preferentially updates the better-represented target. A fixed-propensity control freezes the same rule’s probability at its initial value; an exploratory rule favors the worse-represented target. These interventions separate continued tracking of the learned state from an initial tendency to sample one target. Three tests answer different questions. First, a strong imposed history difference nearly disappears under later common updating. Second, without an explicit history manipulation, state-following feedback amplifies incidental initial differences: terminal |s| is 0.8683, versus 0.4207 under fixed propensity. Third, a separate mirrored-history experiment shows that state following and fixed propensity do not differ detectably in the signed effect of that history, but do differ in absolute specialization depth. The feedback result concerns amplification and support, not a uniquely stronger selection of the history-imposed sign. Appendix B reports the separate contrasts. Capacity changes the maintained specialization. A three-dimensional bottleneck yields almost exclusive readout advantage for one target; a two-dimensional bottleneck yields two regimes with less extreme advantages for opposite targets. Weak opposing update pulses are followed by recovery, whereas stronger pulses can switch regimes. Without externally favoring either target, most trajectories retain their initial regime throughout the 8,000-update comparison. We use “metastable” operationally for this finite-time residence, recovery, switching, and coexistence. Counts, longer observations, and hysteresis tests are reported in Appendix B; the evidence does not establish permanent bistability. Asymmetric steady states have classical precedent in Harris et al. (1997), while Kiselev (2026) provides a recent minimal bifurcation construction based on positive feedback. Developmental formation and maintenance have also long been distinguished (Crair et al., 1998). The contribution here is their intervention-based realization and comparison in learned shared representations. The experiments identify present conditions that sustain a specialization, rather than assigning its persistence to history alone. 5 What remains hidden when observable specialization is matched? We now turn from where the representation moves to how readily it moves. The experiment proceeds in three steps: match the endpoints produced by different formation routes; test their response to a common perturbation; then intervene on optimizer state to identify components that causally modify the short-horizon response difference. A final within-route control asks whether the result requires acquisition-driven and update-driven formation to be different categories. 5.1 Matched reconstruction measures, different brief responses We form a favored target in two ways. The acquisition-driven route observes that target precisely while weighting both reconstruction errors equally. The update-driven route balances precise observations across targets while assigning the favored target a loss weight of 0.80. Route strengths are calibrated on pilot seeds, then fixed for confirmation. Five selected endpoint measures—aligned s, target and non-target errors, total error, and low-precision s—meet pre-specified equivalence margins. The aligned-s difference is −0.0064 ([−0.0098, −0.0032]), within ±0.02. Mean integrated responses during 200 balanced-observation, equal-weight continuation updates are also equivalent, with either inherited or freshly initialized Adam state. All confirmatory seeds are retained under the pilot-fixed protocol. We then copy each formed learner into three brief interventions, each lasting ten updates and favoring the previously less accurately recovered target. The acquisition-only pulse observes that target precisely while keeping loss weights equal. The update-only pulse balances acquisition and gives that target weight 0.80. The combined pulse both observes it precisely and assigns it weight 0.85. For each pulse, a further pair of copies either retains the formed Adam state or starts with a fresh Adam state, keeping model weights unchanged at the branch point. We measure the decrease in the allocation index aligned to the originally favored target; crossing zero means that the other target has become more precisely recoverable. 6
