A Overview of our method Our method consists of two phases: planning and sampling, illustrated in Figure 1. During planning, we use contexts and queries to the target policies to estimate their feature means and second moments, without observing rewards. These estimates define a regularized design problem that selects a mixture of target policies to control the largest value-estimation uncertainty across them. The planning procedure also determines the regularization parameter and the sample budgets used in the next phase. During sampling, we deploy the selected mixture: for each fresh context, we sample a target policy and execute an action drawn from that policy. We use the resulting reward observations to fit a single linear reward model by ridge regression. Combining this model with target feature means estimated from a separate, fresh batch of contexts yields value estimates for all K policies. A related separation between planning and sampling appears in Zanette et al. [40], for the objective of learning a near-optimal policy. The distinction between context samples, policy queries, and reward observations is central to our approach. For each context x used to estimate feature moments, we sample an action ai ∼πi(· | x) from every target policy and compute ϕ(x, ai). This requires K policy queries, but only one context draw and no reward observations. Thus, policy-queries per context scale linearly with K, while the same contexts are shared across all target policies. Our primary objective is to reduce the number of reward observations, and we account for these resources separately. This distinction is also practically relevant. For example, contexts may represent patient measurements and target policies may be pretrained models recommending treatments. Querying these models on historical patient records can be inexpensive, whereas observing a reward requires administering a treatment and measuring its outcome.
