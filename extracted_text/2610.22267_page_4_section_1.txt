2.1 Recovering Mode Diversity in Diffusion Models. Prior methods recover the limited diversity of conditional diffusion models through repulsion, differing mainly in where they apply it but sharing a diversity signal that decomposes into a sum of pairwise interactions. One line intervenes during denoising: SPELL [14] adds sparse repellency terms to the reverse SDE, Particle Guidance (PG) [3] repels intermediate latents via a joint-particle potential, and DiversityPrompt [31] optimizes a token embedding online to steer generation toward low-density regions. All incur per-step overhead that scales with trajec- tory length. Contrastive Noise Optimization (CNO) [13] instead shifts repulsion to the initial noise, optimizing latents via InfoNCE in the Tweedie prediction space before sampling begins. Both, however, quantify diversity as a sum over sample pairs, so their repulsion never leaves the span of pairwise directions and responds only weakly to higher-order redundancy that collapses the batch onto a low-dimensional subspace. A separate line targets rare modes of the uncondi- tional or class-conditional distribution via classifier guidance [25, 29], self-guided sampling [30], or modified noise schedules [28]. Our goal instead is to recover sup- pressed modes of a text-conditional distribution under strong guidance for a fixed prompt. 2.2 Initial Noise Optimization in Diffusion Models. InitNO [8] steers noise toward valid regions via attention-based scores for bet- ter text adherence, while [2] learn a mapping to optimal noise configurations to characterize CFG behavior. Neither addresses diversity. CNO [13] establishes that optimizing the initial noise alone can recover suppressed modes, using a pairwise contrastive objective. We inherit this noise-space pipeline from CNO; what changes is the structure of the diversity signal, from a sum over pairs to log-determinant maximization, a set-level functional equal to the squared RKHS volume that penalizes redundancy at every order. This objective is jointly opti- mized with an anchor-based fidelity term under a single hyperparameter λ. The log-determinant as a diversity criterion originates in GDPP [4], which regularizes a GAN’s generator with a DPP kernel. We instead apply it as an inference-time objective on the Tweedie predictions of a frozen diffusion model, requiring no training and no DPP sampling.
