At deployment, the trained policy predicts action chunks directly, without critic-guided candidate averaging. For a fixed, bounded potential with appropriate terminal- state handling, potential-based shaping preserves the policies that maximize the original task return [20]. For any fixed policy, exact action and state values shift by the same term, −βΦ(s), leaving action advantages unchanged: AΦ = (Q −βΦ) −(V −βΦ) = A. Under these conditions, shaping preserves the policies that max- imize the original task return. This property concerns ex- act values; the effect of shaping on the learned critic and policy is assessed through subsequent policy progress. Refinement and routing. We assess each training program by measuring the policy’s improvement on the corresponding difficulty mode, using ν to deter- mine task success. We also compare how quickly the value critic converges with and without the compiled potential. If the potential provides no improvement in convergence speed over sparse task rewards alone, the vision–language critic C revisits the failure diagnosis, and Rhone revises the subgoal decomposition. For each subgoal, we select a learning procedure based on the policy’s measured success rate [38]. When this rate is near zero and a planner demonstration is available, we use behavior cloning on Dt to teach the policy an initial solution. When the success rate falls within the target band, we use Text2potential to shape the value-learning targets. For difficulty modes with success rates above the band, we periodically re-evaluate the policy to detect performance regressions. If none of the subgoals falls within the target band, we return the difficulty mode to the data engine for further environment generation and reduce its importance score when allocating subsequent generation budgets. Scheduling. Each round generates B targeted envi- ronments and their controls. After verification, we collect N policy rollouts in each valid environment. The vision–language critic examines a stratified sample of at most C failed rollouts per mode. Between generation rounds, the value critic receives KQ updates and the policy receives Kπ updates using the accumulated buffer. After validation, difficulty modes are compiled into training programs for policy learning and generation programs for subsequent rounds. A new generation round is triggered when fewer than eight difficulty modes remain within the success band or when learning progress plateaus for 2k policy-update steps. The loop terminates when no mode remains in the band or the generation budget is exhausted. 4. Experiments Experimental Setup. We evaluate CoDA on four manipulation task families and LIBERO-Pro [52] using π0.5 [8] as the primary policy backbone. Our main comparison includes the released π0.5 checkpoint, super- vised fine-tuning on seed demonstrations, and policies trained with domain randomization, uniform environ- ment variants [29], or success-rate-guided generation [10]. Generation baselines use the same SDK and bud- gets. We ablate persistent memory and the separation of generation and training stores, comparing against single- store alternatives that include a skill library [34]. We report per-suite success rates and average success across held-out suites, including generalization to unseen object instances, layouts, tasks, and categories. Additional met- rics assess generation quality through verification pass rate and coverage entropy, and diagnosis quality through targeting precision, refutation rate, and agreement with two annotators on 100 sampled diagnoses. We report computational cost in LLM tokens, simulation hours, and GPU-hours per 1,000 accepted environments. Full experimental details are provided in Appendix B. Main results. Table 1 shows CoDA achieves the highest OOD success across all four task families. Average OOD success reaches 27.6%, compared with 18.1% for gen- eration guided by task success rates and 19.2% for our critic without memory, improvements of 9.5 and 8.4 per- centage points, respectively. Both substantially exceed uniform generation (8.5%) and domain randomization (7.9%), but retaining and reusing experience through our memory yields the strongest performance.
