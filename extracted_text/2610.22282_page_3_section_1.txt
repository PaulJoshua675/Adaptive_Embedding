Reward alignment methods for visual generators differ mainly in where the learning signal enters the generator. We organize prior work along this axis, from the sampler trajectory to the attention computation. Sampler-space policy gradients. DDPO and DPOK first treated stochastic denoising transitions as policy actions (Black et al., 2024; Fan et al., 2023). Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO carried this view to flow matching and video by converting the deterministic sampler into an SDE and estimating group-relative advantages (Liu et al., 2025a; Xue et al., 2025b). Follow-up work lowers the cost of these stochastic trajectories. Temporal windows, shared-prefix branches, and one-step updates reduce the number or cost of stochastic transitions (Li et al., 2025a;b; He et al., 2026). TempFlow-GRPO concentrates stochasticity at designated branch points for temporal credit assignment (He et al., 2025), and TAGRPO aligns trajectories that start from identical initial noise (Wang et al., 2026). Dense temporal and spatial credit or regulated clipping further improve how a terminal reward shapes each transition (Deng et al., 2026; Shen et al., 2026a; Yang et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2025a). These methods keep the action inside the sampler, so exploration still perturbs the reverse trajectory. Objectives without sampler actions. A second family avoids sampler actions. Direct reward fine- tuning backpropagates through differentiable image or video rewards (Clark et al., 2023; Prabhudesai et al., 2024). For black-box rewards, DiffusionNFT, DGPO, and advantage-weighted matching replace reverse-trajectory likelihoods with forward-process or preference objectives (Zheng et al., 2026; Luo et al., 2026; Xue et al., 2025a). Adjoint matching, ELBO surrogates, path-space value gradients, and velocity matching pursue the same goal through native regression targets (Bergmeister et al., 2026; Tang et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026c; Choi et al., 2026). For few-step generators, a hybrid scheme combines distribution matching, GRPO on stochastic intermediate transitions, and reward backpropagation through the final step (Huang et al., 2026). Offline preference optimization removes online rollouts altogether, ranging from Diffusion-DPO to multidimensional and temporally dense video preferences (Wallace et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024; 2025b; Wu et al., 2025b). These objectives leave inference untouched, but they give up an explicit sampled action. In contrast, RPO keeps a score-function policy with a recorded action and places that action inside attention. Discrete actions and attention routing. Discrete policies already exist in visual generation. MaskGRPO, Mask-GRPO, and MaskFocus optimize token corruption, unmasking, or mask sched- ules in natively discrete image generators (Ma et al., 2026; Luo et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025a). Structured attention sparsity is also well studied, although mainly for efficiency. Q/K-derived block gates and training-free compression expose attention redundancy (Gao et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2024; Xi et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025). VSA, trainable dynamic routing, and hierarchical sparse attention learn such sparsity inside image and video generators (Zhang et al., 2025b; Zhao et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2025; Lv et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026a). Reinforcement learning has also selected sparse-attention levels or recomputation schedules for acceleration (Zhao et al., 2026; Aliev et al., 2026). KVPO comes closest to alignment. It explores through stochastic historical-key routing in autoregressive video and scores branches with a velocity-energy surrogate (Zhang et al., 2026). Other work optimizes attention allocation for multimodal reasoning or routes preference credit through diffusion attention (Li et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026b). Dropout-GRPO samples Bernoulli masks to diversify deterministic latent reasoning (Jung, 2026).
