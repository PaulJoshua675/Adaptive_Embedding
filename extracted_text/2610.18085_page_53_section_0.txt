Bum Jun Kim I.5 Summary Case studies on public checkpoints complement the paired training comparisons. The case studies show that pair imbalance also arises in ordinary training, to a degree that varies across models and pairs. In Pythia 410M, for example, the query and key matrices start with nearly equal norms, but during pre-training their norms drift apart by a factor of up to about 6 in deep layers, which corresponds to a log-norm ratio of 1.86. The case studies also test canonical restart on a released model. We fine-tune GPT-2 774M with fresh AdamW moments, starting from the released checkpoint and from an exact Q–K edit of this checkpoint that maps WQ 7→WQ/8 and WK 7→8WK. Canonicalizing both checkpoints at restart makes the two runs identical, with the same predictions and token NLLs at every evaluation. Without canonicalization, the edited run predicts a different top-1 token than the canonicalized runs at 7.3% of held-out positions after 200 updates. Periodic Soft SAGGS reduces this fraction to 1.1% but not to zero. Canonical restart also works when the moments are kept rather than reset: In a GPT-2 124M case whose edit transforms the moments along with the weights, canonicalizing both weights and moments fully restores the trajectory of the unedited run. I.6 Protocols The four figures in this appendix use supplementary measurements on public checkpoints, independently of the primary training runs. Training case studies use a single fixed seed, FP32, deterministic PyTorch operations, eager attention, and no dropout, TensorFloat-32, or gradient clipping. Within each comparison, all branches use the same fixed training batches, except for the explicitly marked alternative-sampling reference. The training runs use one GPU; the checkpoint-norm survey reads weight tensors on CPU. The case studies are descriptive, single-seed measurements, not additional multi-seed PPL comparisons. Evaluation subsets contain teacher-forced next-token targets, not sampled generations or full-validation PPL measurements. Public checkpoint survey (Figure 5). Figure 5(a) includes GPT-2 124M, 355M, 774M and 1.5B; Pythia 6.9B; Qwen2.5 3B and 7B; Mistral 7B v0.1; Hermes-3-Llama-3.1-8B; a ViT-B/16 CIFAR-10 classifier; and the DistilBERT SST-2 classifier. All layers and available exact matrix pairs are retained, with biases excluded from norms. Fused GPT-2 and Pythia projections are split into Q, K, and V. Pythia stores these projections grouped by head. SwiGLU uses only its up–down value path. GELU MLPs are excluded. Figure 5(a) removes the shape offset (1/2) log(nA/nB), and therefore measures equal-RMS rather than equal-Frobenius balance for GQA. The measured Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct row is omitted because its log-ratios differ from the base by at most 1.9 × 10−4. Figure 5(b) uses public Pythia 160M, 410M, 1B and 1.4B checkpoints from step 0 through step 143000: 20 each for 160M and 410M and 11 each for 1B and 1.4B. Each training step represents 2,097,152 tokens. Pythia 2.8B was excluded because its early checkpoints contained the same already-trained weights as the endpoint, preventing an initialization comparison. Q–K starts within 0.003 of balance in all four plotted models. V–O starts near log(0.316L), consistent with the initialization offset. The model sizes differ in architecture dimensions; no monotone size trend or performance consequence is inferred from these observations. Fine-tuning and Lion protocols. Figures 6 and 7 use the released GPT-2 774M (openai-community/ gpt2-large) and 355M (openai-community/gpt2-medium) checkpoints, respectively. Both use batch size four, context length 128, and fixed random windows from a prefix of the WikiText-103 raw training split containing just over 600,000 tokens, with end-of-text separators. The evaluation set comprises the first 128 non-heading validation paragraphs with at least 129 GPT-2 tokens, truncated to 129 tokens each, yielding 16,384 targets. The 774M run starts each branch with fresh AdamW moments, learning rate 2 × 10−5, (β1, β2) = (0.9, 0.95), ϵ = 10−8, decay 0.1, and 200 updates at constant learning rate without warmup. Q–K reciprocal edits co-transform their biases and leave the attention scaling operation unchanged. Canonicalization and Soft operate on Q–K and V–O. Q, K, and V biases follow their weight scales, while attention output biases are untouched. Soft uses α = 0.5 at handoff and every ten steps without moment transformation. The alternative-sampling branch uses a different seed to draw new training windows. In Figure 6(a) and Figure 6(b), this branch is compared to the canonicalized branch, just like the other curves, and is a single reference, not a variability estimate. The measured handoff maximum logit difference is 8.8 × 10−5 on the first 16 evaluation sequences, with zero top-1 disagreements over all evaluation targets. The two canonicalized branches have identical predictions and token NLLs at all 12 evaluation steps.
