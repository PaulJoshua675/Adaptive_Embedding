AI Consilium: a Benchmark of LLM Clinical Multidisciplinary Second Opinions A PREPRINT judgment: 0 indicates a case that is simple for the rater’s specialty and for which the need for a consensus is questionable; 3 indicates a very complex case for which convening a consensus is highly appropriate. Score Assign this score when 0 The case is simple for the rater’s specialty, with one obvious problem, a standard diagnostic pathway, limited need for additional analysis, and low risk of error. 1 The case is moderately complex, with several relevant data points and simple alternatives, but the expected decision is largely standard. 2 The case is complex, with involvement of several systems, ambiguous data, important clinical dynamics, or several plausible differential diagnoses. 3 The case is very complex, with a rare or atypical presentation, high risk, incomplete or conflicting data, and a clear need for multidisciplinary input. Overall quality of all consensus stages, column AB This is a global expert judgment of whether the consensus report is usable as a coherent clinical product. Score Assign this score when 0 The consensus report as a whole is unusable: there are many major errors, and fabricated data lead to an incorrect conclusion or unsafe management. 1 Some useful fragments are present, but overall quality is low: substantial errors occur across several stages, and the result requires major revision. 2 The consensus report is generally acceptable, but there are noticeable deficiencies in facts, reasoning, conclusion, management, or consistency between stages. 3 All stages are consistent, factually accurate, logically coherent, and clinically useful; the result can be accepted without major revision. Writing comments The comment should not restate the entire case. Its purpose is to make clear why low scores were assigned and where the main deficiency is located. • A comment should be provided when any score of 0 or 1 is assigned. • For fabricated facts, specify the exact fact and explain why it is not present in the source data. • If the assessment is generally favorable, one concise sentence about the main limitation is sufficient. If there are no major problems, the field may be left blank. • Avoid non-specific comments such as “many errors,” “acceptable but could be better,” or “poor conclusion” unless the problem is specified.
