A Further Model Evaluation The main experiments compare RELAGRAM and DeepACO on solution quality. Here, we look more closely at how the models work and provide a matched comparison with a single-pass control. Both models learn edge scores, but iteration happens in different places. RELAGRAM repeatedly refines a latent state using the same network before decoding the final heatmap. DeepACO instead uses an anisotropic GNN to produce a heuristic for Ant Colony Optimization, where completed solutions update the pheromone state and guide subsequent search. DeepACO performs better overall, although RELAGRAM is competitive in two Combined256 settings: 17.11% versus 17.38% on CVRP-500 and 24.32% versus 23.31% on CVRP-1000. We compare RELAGRAM with a direct model that maps the input straight to one heatmap. Both use the same 0.526M-parameter transformer and are matched in optimizer updates, unique training instances, and sampled routes. p-values are from two-sided paired t-tests over 256 shared evaluation instances (α = 0.05). The recursive/direct greedy gaps are 18.886/18.467% at 10k updates (p = 0.226) and 17.857/17.596% at 20k (p = 0.406). Neither difference is statistically significant, so we find no evidence that RELAGRAM outperforms this control under greedy decoding at these checkpoints. RELAGRAM nevertheless provides an additional test-time scaling axis: greater depth can improve solution quality without retraining, particularly after transfer to larger instances. B Inference-Time Scaling Details We timed a single instance on the same 10 GB A100 slice. One recursive step takes 2.60, 36.32, and 131.40 ms for n = 100, 500, 1000; one greedy decode takes 36.39, 169.04, and 331.91 ms. The recursive step is cheaper at every size, although its cost rises on the larger transferred instances. Aug8 is stronger than extra greedy depth: its gaps are 9.77%, 17.61%, and 24.36%, versus 13.35%, 21.81%, and 28.45% at the best tested depths. These settings are not latency-matched: depth adds model passes but decodes one route, while Aug8 decodes eight transformed instances. We therefore treat depth as a new test-time computation axis.
