Bum Jun Kim, Makoto Kawano, Yusuke Iwasawa, Yutaka Matsuo C.5 Typed objectives and units of physical data Uncertainty weighting (Kendall et al., 2018), gradient normalization (Chen et al., 2018), and gradient-statistics annealing for PINNs (Wang et al., 2021) adapt loss weights during training, and practitioner guides for PINNs also recommend nondimensionalizing the problem before training (Wang et al., 2023). NDA instead types the objective. A weight λi factorizes into a dimensionless preference αi and an inverse-unit converter 1/Si with u(Si) = u(Li). Thm. 2 shows that every invariant linear rule whose weights depend on the characteristic scales carries this inverse-unit factor, with the remaining scale dependence confined to dimensionless π groups, and Cor. 1 shows that the preferences are constant within this class when the loss units vary independently. Deriving Si from observable characteristic scales makes the objective invariant to the data’s unit system without adaptive weighting. Adaptive methods can then adjust dimensionless preferences among the normalized terms. This typing connects to the broader use of units in scientific machine learning, where dimensional analysis constrains learned models of physical systems (Villar et al., 2023), Buckingham–π style reductions are classical (Buckingham, 1914), and recent work learns the dimensionless groups from data (Bakarji et al., 2022). Outside machine learning, units-of-measure type systems check the dimensional consistency of a program at compile time and infer unit-polymorphic types (Kennedy, 2009). All of these lines attach externally declared physical units to inputs, outputs, and program variables. NDA instead assigns units to internal tensors as transformation laws under internal rescalings, a gauge structure of the computation graph that applies when no physical unit exists. The gauge terminology follows geometric deep learning, where equivariance is stated relative to a choice of local frame rather than to a single global symmetry (Cohen et al., 2019). C.6 Positioning summary The related methods address complementary parts of scale management. Parameterization methods such as µP and unit scaling prescribe initialization, multipliers, and update scales (Yang and Hu, 2021; Yang et al., 2024; Blake et al., 2023). Normalized architectures control activation magnitude (Loshchilov et al., 2025; Karras et al., 2024). Weight-space symmetry analyses describe function-preserving reparameterizations (Neyshabur et al., 2015; Dinh et al., 2017; Kunin et al., 2021). Modular norms and duality maps organize optimizer updates (Large et al., 2024; Bernstein and Newhouse, 2025). Loss-balancing and quantization methods address scale in objectives and finite-precision computation, respectively (Kendall et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2018; Dettmers et al., 2022; Xiao et al., 2023). Units-of-measure type systems and units-equivariant learning provide the closest conceptual parallels for externally declared units (Kennedy, 2009; Villar et al., 2023). Our NDA framework unifies these perspectives in one graph-level description. Units are transformation laws under declared internal rescalings. This definition lets NDA apply to tensors without physical units. Forward consistency determines reverse-mode units (Prop. 1), while UVI diagnostics and the parameter-gauge audit make the declarations testable on trained networks. Through NDA, we also connect training-time scale control to PTQ. Our contribution is this unified treatment and the resulting diagnostics and constructions.
