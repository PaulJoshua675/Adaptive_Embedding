AI Consilium: a Benchmark of LLM Clinical Multidisciplinary Second Opinions A PREPRINT 3.2 NEJM Clinical Case Dataset, Selection Rationale, and Prespecified Split We used the nejm_test configuration of NEJM–MedQA, which mirrors the supplementary data of Savage et al. [Savage et al., 2024, Link, 2024]. The source dataset includes 300 extended clinicopathological cases selected from 310 recent Case Records after excluding ten without a definitive diagnosis or within the applicable context limit [Brinkmann et al., 2024, Savage et al., 2024]. We used 300 DOI-unique cases published in 2015–2023. Case descriptions contained a median of 1,036 words (IQR, 852–1,197; range, 231–2,167). We retained the clinical presentation and normalized metadata but removed source-study instructions, examples, prior model responses, and grades. The separate Answer field was withheld during generation and used only for evaluation. Role assignment and both response conditions received the same DOI, title, and case description; the consilium received an identifier-stripped case, the fixed role opinions, and the review history. We chose this corpus because its long, diagnostically resolved narratives support open-ended assessment of reasoning, management, and safety, not only diagnostic agreement. The published diagnosis was used for diagnostic concordance but not as exhaustive ground truth for management. Because Case Records favor unusual and difficult cases, results should not be interpreted as performance in routine practice [Kanjee et al., 2023, Savage et al., 2024, McDuff et al., 2025, Shao and Zhang, 2025]. Before evaluation, we reused a prespecified fixed DOI manifest defining 110 cases for physician annotation and Judge calibration, the 190-case held-out evaluation cohort was its exact set complement, with source-dataset order preserved. Here, “held-out” means that the 190 evaluation cases were not used for physician annotation or Judge calibration in this study. 3.3 Role Assignment And Independent Role-Specific Second Opinions For each case, the configured role-assignment procedure selected between one and five clinical roles, designated one as primary, and provided a brief case-specific rationale for each role and for the panel as a whole. Exact duplicate role names were rejected case-insensitively, and the primary role was required to match one of the selected roles. The primary-role designation was retained as metadata and did not alter subsequent generation: every selected role produced an opinion. Role-specific prompts received the role name but not its selection rationale or primary-role status. Every selected role received the same case input and produced its opinion without seeing the opinions produced for other roles. Consequently, each output could be read and evaluated independently as a second opinion from that prompted clinical perspective. The role-specific outputs were fixed before the consilium and were not regenerated during discussion. In the free-form condition, the model produced a self-contained clinical opinion and was instructed to state missing information and assumptions. In the structured condition, the required fields were: subjective and objective information, clinical syndromes and involved mechanisms, differential diagnosis with reasoning, diagnostic and treatment tactics and final clinical opinion. The structured condition combined a prespecified response schema with more detailed clinical-reasoning instructions. Comparisons with the free-form condition therefore reflected the overall difference between the two prompting approaches, rather than the isolated effect of response format. 3.4 Interactive Multidisciplinary Consilium Protocol We use consilium to denote structured multidisciplinary review of a clinical case. The reference workflow adapted the five-stage logic of MedAgents from multiple-choice medical question answering to generation of a self-contained clinical second opinion [Tang et al., 2024]. It was an adaptation rather than a literal reproduction: the output was an open-ended clinical report, roles addressed both diagnosis and management, and all role-specific opinions were retained. The interactive protocol first synthesized the case and fixed role-specific opinions into a shared report. In each round, every role independently reviewed the current report and returned either a yes vote or a no vote with a clinically relevant critique and proposed revision. From the second round onward, reviewers also received the reviews and moderator decision from the immediately preceding round. If every role voted yes, the controller recorded consensus. Otherwise, the moderator considered the objections, checked them against the case, and either revised the report or recorded that no substantive change was warranted. The cycle ended when all roles accepted the report, the moderator reported no substantive change, a revised report exactly repeated a previously seen report state, or five review rounds were completed. When dissent persisted through the fifth review and the moderator made a substantive revision, that revision was retained at the maximum-round stop without a sixth role vote.
