Autoregressive models based on the Transformer ar- chitecture (Vaswani et al., 2017), are the dominant paradigm in Natural Language Processing (NLP), especially for Natural Language Generation (NLG) tasks. The decoder of a Transformer generates se- quences from left to right (L2R), conditioning each token on previously generated outputs. This left-to- right ordering is intuitive, aligns well with human processing patterns, and is computationally effi- cient. However, a rigid L2R generation order also has limitations; for e.g., when used for sequence transduction tasks such as tagging, where the de- coder produces a sequence of tags, early errors can influence later predictions (error propagation), a common occurrence in autoregressive architectures (Bengio et al., 2015; Liu et al., 2016). Second, the strict L2R order makes it impossible for the model to exploit dependencies that do not naturally fol- low a left-to-right progression. For instance, in the task of Part-of-Speech (POS) tagging, there may exist syntactic dependencies between tags that are not necessarily left-to-right in nature; for e.g. in the sentence She saw her brother, tagging her cor- rectly as a possessive pronoun rather than an object pronoun depends on the tag of the following word brother, unavailable to a left-to-right model at the time of prediction. In this paper, we investigate the effects of remov- ing the fixed L2R ordering in the Encoder-Decoder Transformer on sequence labelling tasks. Our main hypothesis is that removing the L2R assumption allows the model to choose more flexible and dy- namic orderings that may help to alleviate error propagation or to exploit syntactic dependencies that are not left-to-right (H1). We also investigate whether such a model shows an advantage over bidirectional encoder-only models like BERT (De- vlin et al., 2019a) which are proficient in word-level classification tasks like tagging, but cannot lever- age previously generated tags that a (dynamically ordered) encoder-decoder model can (H2).
