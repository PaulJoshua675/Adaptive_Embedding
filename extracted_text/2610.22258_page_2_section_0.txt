Figure 1 illustrates that a deep learning model trained on standard image denoising datasets performs poorly on texture images, both quantitatively and qualitatively. Fine-tuning the model on a texture dataset enhances the performance, highlighting the need for texture-aware denoising strategies. In this paper, our objective is to carry out a systematic study of the generalizability of deep learning techniques for denois- ing texture images. As argued in [6], SSIM is inadequate for objective assessment of the quality of texture. Therefore, we employ alternative measures such as STSIM1 [6], STSIM2 [6], MS-SSIM [7], CW-SSIM [8] and DISTS [9] in addition to PSNR and SSIM. RELATED WORKS Classical image denoising methods include Gaussian smoothing, bilateral filtering, non-local means [10], wavelet thresholding, and BM3D [11]. However, these approaches have been surpassed by SOTA deep learning techniques. Jain and Seung [12] first demonstrated the effectiveness of Convo- lutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for denoising, showing that CNNs could match or even surpass the performance of the then traditional models. Subsequently, with the introduction of SRCNN [13] and ARCNN [14] for super-resolution and JPEG artifact reduction, respectively, CNN-based methods have achieved impressive performance and have since become mainstream in the field of image restoration. Denoising Autoencoders (DAEs) [15] introduced learning noise distributions to recover clean images via mapping inputs to meaningful intermediate representations. This was followed by CNN-based models such as DnCNN [16], which leveraged deep residual learning [17] to effectively remove Gaussian noise. Originally proposed to address degradation of accuracy with the depth of the network, it was also found to improve performance in image classification and object detection. CNN-based denoising methods process images either at full resolution, preserving spatial details but lacking global contextual, or at reduced resolutions, capturing semantics but losing spatial accuracy. To address this trade-off, Multi-Scale Image Restoration Network (MIRNet) [3] employs parallel multi-scale feature extraction with attention-based aggregation, while Multi-Stage Progressive Image Restoration Network (MPRNet) [18] integrates contextual features extracted from an encoder-decoder network with a high-resolution branch that preserves local details. This ensures both spatial precision and strong contextual representation. Most image-denoising algorithms are developed and evalu- ated on synthetic data, typically generated under the assump- tion of additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN). However, AWGN does not accurately represent real-world camera noise. CycleISP [20] addresses this by modeling the camera imaging pipeline in both forward and reverse directions, to generate realistic image pairs in both RAW and sRGB spaces. Improving performance often increases computational cost. Nonlinear Activation Free Network (NAFNet) [21] mitigates this by removing or replacing nonlinear activations with multiplications, achieving improved performance with reduced complexity. To overcome CNN limitations such as restricted recep- tive fields and static filters, transformer-based models have been proposed. Restoration Transformer (Restormer) [22] leverages global context for SOTA restoration performance. But transformers with global attention are computationally expensive. To reduce the high computational cost of global attention, Cross Aggregation Transformer (CAT) [23] intro- duced rectangle-window self-attention.
