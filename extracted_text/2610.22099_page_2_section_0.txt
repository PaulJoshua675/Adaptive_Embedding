A fixed power-law schedule specifies the expected visible fraction, while a shared shift of the policy logits matches this target without changing the policy’s relative preferences. Thus, the expected amount of context grows according to the schedule even as individual commitments remain reversible. Our training recipe first provides dense, reference-derived supervision of visibility decisions, then optimizes final-answer correctness through group-relative policy optimization (Shao et al., 2024). Only the auxiliary controller is updated in either stage; training requires neither gradients through the dLLM nor its sequence likelihood. The same visibility-control formulation applies to full-canvas and semi-autoregressive decoding (section 3). We summarize our contributions as follows: • We introduce TIPS, an approximately 600K-parameter policy that learns both unmasking and remasking while keeping the dLLM backbone frozen. • We instantiate TIPS with full-canvas and blockwise decoding on LLaDA-8B-Instruct. At most decoder NFE budgets, we surpass previous state-of-the-art methods in accuracy on GSM8K and MATH-500 by up to 13.25 percentage points, otherwise remaining competitive. • We investigate how TIPS uses commitment and revision through ablations and other supplementary experiments. These analyses highlight the importance of remasking, suggesting its benefits are not only token replacement, but also temporarily withdrawing harmful context. We further characterize the scope and limits of TIPS through transfer experiments. 2
