At inference the errors ek are relaxed for Kh sweeps at dt = 1, and Figure 3b shows the deviation is independent of Kh: values in {1, 2, 4, 8, 14} agree to within 1%. This follows from three choices acting together. The error parameterisation makes the relaxation Hessian a pertur- bation of the identity, ∂2E/∂e2 = I + γM (Appendix E); gradient descent with dt = 1 on identity curvature is deadbeat, the iteration map I −dt H = −γM annihilating the error in a single sweep up to O(γ); and feedforward initialisation3 starts the system at ek = 0, the exact minimum of the γ = 0 energy, so only the O(γ) displacement the injection causes remains. The first sweep therefore lands within O(γ2) of the equilibrium errors. We read the velocity as ∇xEPCN at fixed e. With the errors held fixed, only the injection term depends on x, so vPCN = −γ−1∇xEPCN = −α ∇xhN(x, e): the γ cancels, and e enters as the argument of an O(1) function rather than being divided by γ. An O(γ2) error in e therefore moves the velocity by only O(γ2), against the O(γ) bias, so the residual deviation in Figure 2 is that bias rather than under-relaxation, as the flatness in Kh confirms. At equilibrium this readout equals the −γ−1J⊤ 1 e∗ 1 of Section 3.1 by the envelope identity; away from equilibrium the two differ. The flatness from Kh = 1 is specific to dt = 1; smaller steps contract the error by a constant factor each sweep, reaching the same fixed point over a few sweeps. Con- vergence of the dt = 1 iteration requires the eigenvalues of γM to remain below unity, a bound never approached in the measured range. Note though that this is a constraint of the explicit Euler discretisation rather than of the model: continuous-time hardware has no step size, and relaxes by true gradient flow at any γ. All inference measurements in the main text therefore use Kh = 1 at dt = 1 from feedforward initialisation. Samples.
