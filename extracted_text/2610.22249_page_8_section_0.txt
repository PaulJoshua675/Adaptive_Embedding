In a victory speech by a swimmer long troubled by injury, the finish is what a new champion would naturally re- call, and the injury motivates setting it aside. In commentary on Messi’s World Cup title, no one suggests that the title amounts merely to winning; the target raises this view only to reject it, although winning the final is precisely what the title is. Yet the LLM judge favors the original in ev- ery judgment for both answers except one tie. For Messi, it concedes that the sentence is “slightly odd logically” and still prefers it because it “reads smoothly and passionately.” FsfairX and Skywork also score the original above the reordered ver- sion in both cases. The evaluators reward the con- struction’s rhetorical effect, not whether the con- text raises the rejected alternative. 5 Discussion and Conclusion Frequency and appropriateness are separate prop- erties of model usage. Models produce the con- struction far more often than people do, and many of these uses are correct but not called for. Eval- uators do not supply the missing judgment: sev- eral reward models prefer the construction after general-quality matching, and both judges prefer it within unlicensed uses. In the available check- points, its density also rises through SFT and DPO. Suppressing the form would remove its appropriate uses along with the rest; reducing tics of this kind requires evaluation and training signals that regis- ter whether an expression is called for. The diag- nostic is not specific to Mandarin: screening for defects and then testing contextual licensing and issue advancement can be applied to English it’s not X, it’s Y and to other expressions that are legit- imate in some contexts and overused in others. A controlled follow-up can test this attribution by holding SFT initialization, source composi- tion, update budget, and prompts fixed, interven- ing on construction-bearing preference pairs with matched edits, and repeating training across seeds. Measuring activation, repetition, and local func- tion would then separate changes in how often the construction is selected from changes in what it contributes. Limitations Data coverage. Our analysis covers five com- mercial models, a limited set of task domains, and 307 annotated uses. Broader models, domains, and human data would strengthen the estimates.
