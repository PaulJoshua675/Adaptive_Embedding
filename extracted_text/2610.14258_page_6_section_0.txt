Proposition 3.2 implies that EB pulls the most important parameters strongly toward the mean while leaving the least important ones nearly unchanged. That allocation would be right if the noise level of an estimate carried no information about its true value, which is what the normal model assumes. For Fisher estimates the noise level grows with the true value, so the model is misspecified exactly where it matters, and the rule moves protection away from the parameters with the largest estimated importance. A fixed weight instead shrinks every deviation by the same fraction, raising small values as well as lowering large ones. When noise is multiplicative, as here, a standard remedy is to shrink on the log scale.
