We propose TRACE, a topology-aware objective that ex- tends skeleton supervision from a fixed target to guidance for adaptive reconnection. TRACE uses a topology-preserving skeleton to identify neighboring same-class branches that should remain connected. Based on the current prediction, it identifies the easiest valid local reconnection and jointly supervises the skeleton point with the bottleneck limiting this reconnection. To support this formulation, we further in- troduce a Topology-Aware Skeleton Carrier (TASC), which preserves both foreground and class-specific topology while defining local connectivity requirements. We identify the reconnection bottleneck through an exact local search us- ing centered logit-margin scores derived directly from stan- dard semantic logits. The proposed objective avoids costly prediction-side skeletonization and requires no architectural modifications, enabling efficient plug-and-play integration into existing segmentation pipelines. Our contributions are as follows: • We introduce TRACE, a prediction-adaptive connectivity objective that uses a skeleton to define required connec- tions and supervises the easiest reconnection bottleneck. • We present TASC, a class-labeled skeleton that preserves both foreground and class-specific topology while identi- fying same-class branches that should remain connected. • Across six 2D and 3D thin-structure benchmarks, TRACE improves connectivity and topology while maintaining strong region-overlap and boundary accuracy. • We further demonstrate that TRACE is computationally efficient, generalizes across segmentation backbones, and complements existing topology-aware methods.
