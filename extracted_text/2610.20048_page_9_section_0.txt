The advantage is therefore distributional rather than driven by outliers, though the 25% of documents on which the cross-encoder remains better indicates the two signals are complementary rather than redundant. 6.3 Qualitative analysis 6.3.1 Recovering a coherent concept family. Looking at a specific document titled “Structural separation and access in telecommuni- cations markets“ annotated with five subjects: Access regulation, Spin-off company, Telecommunications, Telecommunications policy, and Theory. The trained cross-encoder identifies none of them. It re- turns Bottleneck, Competition, Local area network, Market structure, Regulation, Segregation, and Structural break. All of these subjects taken individually are adequate for a document about regulated net- work access, but none of them is the one chosen by subject indexers. It proposes a Regulation where the gold label is the narrower Access regulation, and Local area network, where the topic is telecommuni- cations infrastructure generally. TAGHN identifies four of the five (Access regulation, Telecommunications, Telecommunications pol- icy, Theory), missing only the Spin-off company. The cross-encoder scores each subject against the document independently and thus cannot identify that Regulation has a narrower term that fits better, whereas message passing over the BT/NT edges lets the parent’s score be informed by its children’s document match. While both the models make errors, the errors are not too far off from the correct labels. For example, TAGHN also yields the Bottleneck and Telecom- munications network. However, these false positives are near-misses within the correct region of the thesaurus. 6.3.2 Edge gates at a rare leaf.
