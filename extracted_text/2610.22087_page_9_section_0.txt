Discussion Content-based retrieval with off-the-shelf pathology FMs identified the correct diagnosis among the top five candidates in up to 81.3% of cases, without any task-specific training or manual annotation. This was shown on salivary gland tumors, a rare and diagnostically challenging group, demonstrating that current FMs allow pathologists to find rare entities in institutional archives. While Top-1 recall varied across FMs (50–63%), it narrowed at Top-5 (73–81%), without any statistically significant difference between models at any depth. Macro-averaged F1 showed a similarly modest spread, from 68.8% for the smallest FM, Atlas2-S, to 76.6% for the largest, Atlas2. Chamfer scores between correct and incorrect retrievals overlapped considerably reflecting the overlapping morphological features characteristic of salivary gland tumors. Atlas2 achieved significantly better ranking performance than its distilled counterparts, Atlas2-B and Atlas2-S, but showed no significant difference relative to UNI2h. Discriminatory power rose with the dimensionality of the representations as absolute similarity fell, suggesting that larger representations encode finer tissue detail that better separates correct from incorrect matches.
