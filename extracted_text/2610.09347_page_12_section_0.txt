The explanation is that shielding is governed by the size of the set of representations the learner maps onto the target, not by capacity. For an affine learner that set is a two parameter family, so every g affinely related to the target already attains optimal fitness and there is no pressure to land on the target exactly. Capacity and preimage size coincide on continuous regression problems, which is why the distinction has not been visible before. A measurement caveat applies to the continuous half. On Boolean targets the innate measure is sign based and so invariant to rescaling of g. On continuous targets it is not: R2 of g against the target penalises scale differences that an affine learner removes for free, so the continuous numbers measure strict innateness, meaning g equalling the target, not the structure g captures. The Boolean measurements are the primary evidence here for that reason. 12
