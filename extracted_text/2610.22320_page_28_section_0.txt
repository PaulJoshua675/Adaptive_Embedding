Although the large pre-trained model (Flux-Kontext) better preserves the subject identity, it still struggles to reflect the given text (e.g., computer) and produces rigidly similar pose. influence on generation. We therefore apply only the AIP module to FLUX, which is model-agnostic by design. Recent large pretrained image editing model FLUX-Kontext exhibits strong identity-preservation capabilities and can be directly applied to visual story- telling. However, we observe that it remains anchored to the reference, pro- ducing copy-and-paste-style synthesis that fails to follow scene-specific prompts (Fig. 26, Tab. 12). As shown in Tab. 12, while it achieves strong identity scores (e.g., CLIP-I, DreamSim), its prompt alignment (CLIP-T) and overall qual- ity remain limited, indicating that identity preservation alone is insufficient for storytelling—scene-specific adaptation is essential. We further compare with CharaConsist [34], a recent FLUX-based storytelling method. Since its evalua- tion benchmark is not publicly available, we compare on Consistory+ for a fair setting. EOS-Story achieves competitive or superior performance across both identity and prompt-alignment metrics, demonstrating that our scene-adaptive design transfers effectively to FLUX.
