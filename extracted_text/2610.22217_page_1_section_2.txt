Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across a broad range of multimodal tasks (Bai et al., 2025a; Wang et al., 2025b). To further enhance their down- stream multimodal capabilities, recent studies have increasingly adopted Reinforcement Learning (RL) (Yu et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026a; Liu et al., 2026a) to cultivate specialized visual understand- ing skills through task-specific rewards, including fine-grained visual perception (Sun et al., 2025; Xiong et al., 2026), visual grounding (Zhang et al., 2026), and complex multimodal reasoning (Xia et al., 2025; Peng et al., 2025). These approaches have demonstrated the effectiveness of RL in improving specific aspects of visual understanding. However, learning and integrating complemen- tary visual understanding capabilities across heterogeneous multimodal tasks remains a fundamental challenge in multimodal post-training (Wei et al., 2026). A straightforward approach to enhancing VLM capabilities is to jointly optimize a shared policy over diverse multimodal training data (Ma et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2026; Cai et al., 2026). However, heterogeneous training objectives can induce conflicts between capability-specific gradients, making it difficult for a single policy to simultaneously acquire specialized capabilities (Yu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021). To mitigate such conflicts, recent studies instead optimize capability-specific experts independently and subsequently consolidate their acquired knowledge through knowledge distillation.
