Models. We use 11 open-weight models, 6 from the leaderboard of tiny open-weight models of Ar- tificial Analysis (Appendix J): G9v3-3B, Granite-4.2-3B, LFM2.5-2.6B-Base, MiniCPM5-2B-Base, Nanbeige4-3B-Base and Phi-4-mini-instruct. The others are Gemma-4-E2B, OLMo-3-1025-7B, Qwen3.5-9B, Qwen3.5-9B-Base, and, for scale, Qwen3.8-27B. To mix base and post-trained mod- els, we chose at random between the base and the post-trained checkpoint of each model; of Qwen3.5-9B we use both, as a direct comparison of a base model with its post-trained version. Label sets. Unless stated otherwise, every new relation draws its labels from the animal label set, the 179 animal names that the label-set algorithm keeps. All models use this set. The label-set comparison asks whether our results depend on the labels being animals. It reruns every relation with labels from another WordNet noun category, such as artifacts (noun.artifact), and names that category instead of “animal” in the new-relation templates. We run it on MiniCPM5-2B-Base, our smallest model, once per category. Each category’s label set is similarly constructed by the label-set algorithm; since only one tokenizer is involved, every name must be a single token under it. A category takes part only if its label set has at least 86 names, enough for the relation with the most answers. (Each distinct pretrained relation answer needs a distinct label for the new relation.) Of the other 24 WordNet categories, thirteen qualify (Table 7). Subject frequency. For the subject-frequency control, we count how often each subject occurs in Dolma v1.7 (Soldaini et al., 2024), an open corpus built for pretraining language models.
