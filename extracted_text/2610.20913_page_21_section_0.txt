Hence candidate multiplicity disappears before gradients return to the shared trajectory graph. Fanout-aware execution and communication overlap. Within each event, candidate workers batch candidate–head rows that share the same grouped KV. Native fused GQA avoids explicit candidate-wise KV replication; where beneficial, an event-level KV tile remains resident while multiple candidate tiles are processed and their KV gradients are accumulated locally. Communication is overlapped across independent event and optimization work: projected KV for one event may transfer while another event’s behavior memory executes, completed event-KV gradients may return while other candidate tiles continue, and closed dense-gradient or sparse-row reductions may proceed during the remainder of backward. In the measured model training workload, this overlap reduces exposed communication from approximately 18% to 10% of step wall time. 21
