Real-world queries often require knowledge beyond a model’s pretrained parametric memory, such as time-sensitive facts and long-tailed entities (Lewis et al., 2020; Mallen et al., 2023; Jiang et al., 2024). To address this, search agents equip Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) with external search tools to iteratively reason over retrieved evidence. However, acquiring these capabilities typically requires large-scale agentic reinforcement learning (RL) on heavyweight models (e.g., MMSearch- R1 (Wu et al., 2025), OpenSearch-VL (Chen et al., 2026), and HyperEyes (Li et al., 2026b)), making the agents expensive to train and deploy. Thus, on-policy distillation (OPD) offers a natural route to transfer such RL-acquired capabilities to compact students by supervising the student’s own rollouts with dense teacher signals derived from a reverse-KL objective. Previous work on OPD focused mainly on single-turn settings such as mathematics and coding, emphasizing stabilizing training under high-variance rewards (Hao et al., 2026; Xie et al., 2026; Jin et al., 2026) or combining OPD with outcome-based RL (Tan et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2025). Recent work has extended OPD to multi- turn settings, such as embodied reasoning and web navigation, focusing primarily on mitigating error accumulation across interaction turns (Wang et al., 2026b; Li et al., 2026a; Zhou et al., 2026a). Despite their success, these approaches typically derive their supervision of each student action us- ing the preceding context alone, without accounting for the subsequent environmental feedback.
