Precondition. Beyond the standard requirement (that F be symmetric in features i, k with xi = xk, bi = bk), our method additionally requires: (S1) i and k have identical parents and identical children in the causal graph — which also implies they cannot be linked by an edge, since i →k would place i in k’s parent set but not its own; and (S2) the mechanism is exchangeable (ci ≡ck as functions, or si ≡sk if using surrogates, not necessarily identical implementation), with every shared child’s mechanism symmetric in i, k. Proof. By (S1), i and k share the same parent set. Together with xi = xk, bi = bk, and ci ≡ck (S2) applied to that shared parent set, an induction over the topological order gives γ(p) i = γ(p) k at every path point p: the base case holds for root features by xi = xk, bi = bk directly, and the inductive step holds because i and k receive identical inputs from their (identical, by (S1)) parents and apply the same mechanism (S2). Consequently ∂γi/∂α = ∂γk/∂α at every path point, so the direct terms of Ri and Rk agree, since F is symmetric in i, k by hypothesis. For the indirect terms, (S1) gives i, k identical children, and (S2) makes every shared child’s mechanism symmetric in i, k, so ∂γj/∂γi = ∂γj/∂γk for every shared child j; combined with F’s symmetry in i, k, the indirect sums in Equation 3 agree term-by-term. Hence Ri = Rk. □ If i, k occupy different positions in the graph, our method attributes them asymmetrically by design, since it is meant to reflect their differing causal roles. Symmetry at any truncation depth. The same preconditions (S1)+(S2) suffice for R(H) i = R(H) k (Equation 11) at every H, not only H = 1. Two extensions of the argument above are needed. First, since γ(p) i ≡γ(p) k holds identically as a function along the path, not merely pointwise, their derivatives agree at every order too, so dγi dα = dγk dα in addition to ∂γi ∂α = ∂γk ∂α . Second, (Jm)ji = (Jm)jk for every m ≥1 and every j: decomposing a length-m path from i by its first step, (Jm)ji = P l ∈children(i)(Jm−1)jlJli, and since i, k have identical children (S1) and Jli = Jlk for every shared child l (established above), this equals P l ∈children(k)(Jm−1)jlJlk = (Jm)jk — one identity valid for every m simultaneously, not an induction on m. Combining both facts, every term of R(H) i — direct, every middle layer, and the boundary layer — matches the corresponding term of R(H) k , so R(H) i = R(H) k for every H. Linearity Statement. If F = aF1 + bF2, the attributions for F equal the same linear combination of the attributions for F1 and F2: RF i = aRF1 i + bRF2 i . Precondition. F1 and F2 share the same causal graph and the same mechanism’s input-output relation (ci as a function, or si if using surrogates, not its specific implementation), differing only in how they are combined into F. Proof. Under this precondition, the path points γ(p) and path derivatives ∂γi/∂α, ∂γj/∂γi are identical for F1 and F2 (they depend only on the shared graph and mechanisms). By linearity of the partial derivative, ∂F ∂γi = a ∂F1 ∂γi + b ∂F2 ∂γi (23) at every path point. Substituting into Equation 3 — both the direct term and every indirect term, which use only these shared path derivatives — gives RF i = aRF1 i + bRF2 i for every feature i. □ This axiom is mainly of practical interest for ensembles: since the graph and mechanisms are properties of the data rather than of any individual ensemble member, an ensemble F = aF1 + bF2 trained on one dataset satisfies the precondition automatically, so linearity holds without needing to verify anything further. It would only fail if F1, F2 were deliberately given different graphs or different mechanisms — a scenario with little practical relevance. Dummy Statement.
