Neural Dimensional Analysis C.3 Rescaling symmetries and normalized architectures A classical line studies weight rescalings that leave a network’s function invariant. For positive-homogeneous activations, paired rescalings of incoming and outgoing weights form a symmetry group. Path-norm methods and path-normalized variants of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) optimize in the induced quotient (Neyshabur et al., 2015), and the same reparameterizations show that sharpness measures are not invariant (Dinh et al., 2017), imprint conservation laws on gradient flow (Kunin et al., 2021), and define the orbits used by teleportation methods (Zhao et al., 2022). Weight normalization exploits the same freedom constructively by splitting each weight vector into a direction and an explicit magnitude (Salimans and Kingma, 2016), which is the radial coordinate that becomes irrelevant under the downstream-nondimensionalization hypothesis of Prop. 6. A normalizer creates this freedom by making the upstream weight block scale-invariant. The scale-invariant weight block’s norm then acts only through an effective learning rate (van Laarhoven, 2017; Hoffer et al., 2018; Arora et al., 2019), which App. E.3 recovers as the p ≡1 case of Prop. 3, and weight decay on such a block is equivalent to a learning-rate schedule (Li and Arora, 2020). NDA follows how activation rescalings propagate through the graph. In an untyped graph, a rescaling can change the computed function. The typing rules identify the interfaces that absorb or propagate the rescaling. The two views meet at interfaces, because a weight-space rescaling of adjacent layers induces an activation rescaling between those layers. The same normalized gauge also appears at the architecture level. nGPT constrains hidden states and weight rows to the unit hypersphere, with large reported convergence speedups (Loshchilov et al., 2025), following earlier work that placed learned latent representations on the unit hypersphere (Xu and Durrett, 2018), and the magnitude-preserving diffusion layers of Karras et al. (2024) hold activations and weights on fixed-norm shells throughout the backbone, an independent cross-domain instance of computing only in a normalized coordinate. GCT shares the normalized-state mechanics but differs in derivation and content. GCT follows from a typing principle that also produces UVI diagnostics, UCT corrective modules, and a treatment of losses and optimizers, and the architecture adds an orthogonalizing projection P ⊥motivated by the residual-drift analysis, a learnable dimensionless step initialized at 1/ √ 2L, and dimensionless gates. For unitful outputs such as regression and PINN predictions, GCT retains an explicit scale state to restore the output magnitude and units. The component ablations evaluate proposal canonicalization and the orthogonal projection (Tables 1 and 6). C.4 Quantization and activation-scale management Quantization research documents substantial variation in the scale of internal language-model activations. Integer inference represents tensors as scales times integer grids (Jacob et al., 2018; Nagel et al., 2021), connecting integer inference to the factorization in Sec. 4. Relevant phenomena include outlier features (Dettmers et al., 2022), massive activations (Sun et al., 2024), and attention sinks (Xiao et al., 2024). These observations motivate examining both cross-token scale variation and within-vector concentration. Methods for low-bit inference include mixed-precision decomposition (Dettmers et al., 2022), error-compensated weight rounding (Frantar et al., 2023), activation-scale migration (Xiao et al., 2023), activation-aware weight quantization (Lin et al., 2024), and rotations that reduce coordinate outliers (Ashkboos et al., 2024). Logit soft-capping provides a complementary architectural control at attention and output interfaces (Team, 2024). These methods address different aspects of the scale and precision problem. An architectural approach is taken by Bondarenko et al. (2023), who associate activation outliers with attention heads implementing a no-op and use clipped softmax and gated attention to improve int8 quantization. NDA controls cross-token scale variation by constructing a canonical residual stream. Within-vector outliers remain a separate factor, κamp, in Prop. 5. Thm. 7 shows that the cross-token penalty persists in a worst-case functional bound after radial rejection. The experiments compare this architectural control with per-token and per-channel stream scales and SmoothQuant migration. Normalized streams reduce the need for fine-grained scale management in these settings while retaining the ordinary per-tensor quantization cost.
