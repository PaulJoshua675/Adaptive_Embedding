REPRO: Reflective Evolutionary Prompt Optimizer for Automated LLM Red-Teaming 3 1.1 Research Questions To structure the empirical evaluation, we investigate three research questions: • RQ1: How effective and efficient is REPRO as a black-box red-teaming method? (§4.2) • RQ2: Can REPRO discover a single shared attack that covers all behaviors of a benchmark it is optimized against? (§4.3) • RQ3: What is the contribution of each algorithmic component? (§4.4) 1.2 Contributions The primary contributions of this work are: (1) The REPRO framework. We design and implement REPRO, a closed-loop reflective evolutionary optimizer that, to our knowledge, is the first prompt optimizer explicitly designed for the adversarial-objective regime. REPRO operationalizes model-agnostic black- box security testing of LLM-based software systems without requiring logit access or per- model manual engineering, and is backed by a literature-grounded attack library spanning 14 strategy families. (2) Semantic crossover. We introduce the first LLM-guided multi-parent recombination opera- tor in adversarial prompt optimization, enabling the synthesis of novel attacks that combine complementary techniques from diverse strategy families rather than evolving each attack in isolation. (3) Structured failure analysis. We introduce a purpose-built feedback routine that classifies target-model refusals into a taxonomy of defense types, routes each type to a ranked list of counter-strategies, and maintains per-behavior state about which counter-strategies have been tried and with what outcomes. This replaces the free-form self-reflection used in prior iterative red-teaming methods with a stateful, domain-specialized signal that directly drives mutation guidance and strategy selection. The component ablation (Section 4.4) does not isolate a measurable ASR benefit for this component at the iteration budget tested. We therefore present structured failure analysis as a design contribution whose empirical contribution to the reported results remains unquantified, and we discuss the interpretation in Sections 4.4 and 5.2. (4) Bi-level formulation with multi-objective selection. We formulate adversarial prompt optimization as a bi-level problem in which population evolution (upper level) and adap- tive strategy selection (lower level) are coupled through a shared fitness signal, and we specialize the upper level to Pareto optimization over attack success, cross-environment transfer (XFER), detectability, and prompt length. This characterization connects REPRO to established results in evolutionary computation and bandit theory, and grounds the design decisions behind elitism, hybrid strategy scoring, and Pareto selection. The current empirical evaluation exercises the ASR objective under a single deployment environment; the multi-environment and multi-objective evaluation is supported by the framework but left to future work. No Pareto front, hypervolume, detectability measurement, or length measurement is reported in this paper. This contribution should therefore be read as a formulation together with an implemented mechanism, not as an evaluated capability. (5) Empirical demonstration under realistic constraints. We show that REPRO achieves 100% ASR across AdvBench, JailbreakBench, and HarmBench on 36 target models from 11 families while operating in a pure black-box setting, using only a compressed open-weight reflective model on a single consumer GPU, and requiring substantially fewer target queries per behavior than prior gray-box work.
