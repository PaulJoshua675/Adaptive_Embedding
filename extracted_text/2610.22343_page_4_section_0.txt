GeoState Learning constructs view-decoupled scene states with a scene tokenizer and query-conditioned geometry decoder. GeoState World Modeling predicts future states directly in this representation space. GeoState world modeling. With viewpoint variation removed from the predictive state, temporal modeling can focus on scene dynamics rather than camera-induced changes in the representation. Given four historical GeoStates {St−3, St−2, St−1, St}, a temporal world model autoregressively predicts future states using a sliding four-state context. Each predicted state bSt+h is then queried under a future camera Qt+h to obtain bDt+h = Gψ(bSt+h, Qt+h). For each patch p in source view v, we project and fuse the GFM features {F (ℓ) p,v}ℓ∈{4,11,17,23} with per-level linear projections and an MLP to obtain efeat p,v ∈R512. To facilitate cross-view feature aggregation and reduce scale inconsistency across GFM forwards, following Surflo (Gu´edon et al., 2026), we augment the GFM patch features with explicit geometric cues derived from Xv, Cv, and eQv. For each patch, we obtain its 3D location and confidence, source-camera pose, and viewing ray, and encode them as epoint p,v , epose v , and eray p,v, re- spectively, after median-normalizing their explicit spatial scale.
