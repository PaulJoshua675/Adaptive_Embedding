A benchmark may be easy for the wrong reason. Annotation conventions, source identity, repeated templates, and collection artifacts can all predict labels without measuring the intended construct [Gururangan et al., 2018, Geirhos et al., 2020]. This problem is especially consequential for agent- safety evaluation. A tool guard may be deployed to approve or block a consequential action, while its benchmark record also contains scaffolding added by the evaluation pipeline: provenance tags, tool names, trajectory length, source subsets, and repeated interaction structure. Simple lexical and length features separated honest and attack trajectories in the public LinuxArena sample, motivating a search for analogous rules elsewhere. Such a rule may track real risk, collection differences, or evidence also used by a capable guard; rule–guard agreement cannot distinguish these explanations. To systematically uncover potential shortcuts, we introduce shortcut auditing as a three-level inference ladder for agent-safety guard evaluations.
