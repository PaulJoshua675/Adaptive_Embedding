Recent advances in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have extended visual understanding toward increasingly complex spatial reasoning, while progress in 3D generation enables the synthesis and editing of assets with detailed geometry and appearance (Xu et al., 2025; Deng et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026). Together, these developments bring models closer to understanding, creating, and manipulating 3D content through natural language. However, generated assets can still contain geometric artifacts, missing components, and inconsistencies with the requested attributes or structure (Wu et al., 2024; Duggal et al., 2025). Such errors limit their usefulness: An object may look convincing while failing the geometric or functional requirement for which it was created. To improve these systems, existing research has pursued broader pretraining, targeted post-training, and test-time scaling. These approaches strengthen perceptual representations, align outputs with instructions or preferences, and allocate additional computation to candidate search (Xu et al., 2025; Deng et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2025).
