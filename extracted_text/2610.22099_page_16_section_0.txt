B.2.5 Occupancy control and finalization deferral We revisit an earlier RLVR-only comparison of single-layer policies with one 256-token response canvas, T = 32 decoder calls, and three training seeds. Policies are trained with no occupancy constraint, a soft schedule loss, or fixed power-law calibration, while retaining reversible actions and deterministic finalization. The soft schedule loss imposes a penalty that is multiplied against the binary terminal correctness reward. Specifically, if, by the finalization pass, the state has masked fraction m ∈[0, 1], then the assigned terminal correctness reward is multiplied by (1 −m). For example, if the response is correct but only 50% of the canvas was unmasked by the finalization pass, the reward becomes 0.5 for that trajectory. Figure 5 shows that the uncalibrated trajectory levels off before the final call. After the last learned transition, 57.11% of positions remain masked, versus 48.01% with the soft loss and 6.89% with fixed calibration (Table 7). The subsequent jump is imposed by deterministic completion, not learned visibility decisions: this is finalization deferral. We find the soft schedule loss, while non-constraining, insufficient for alleviating the finalization deferral phenomenon. Fixed calibration reduces this terminal workload and is accompanied by higher accuracy, from 45.90% to 50.83%. 16
