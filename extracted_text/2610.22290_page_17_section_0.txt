B.5 Strategy descriptors and evaluation diversity metrics The search descriptor b(y) combines an LLM- assigned approach label with structural SQL fea- tures, while evaluation relies on a separate, deter- ministic AST-based representation. This separation avoids evaluating diversity using the same labels that guide the search. The evaluation representa- tion captures features such as joins, subqueries, aggregation, window functions, set operations, and ordering with a limit, distinguishing different so- lution structures while ignoring superficial differ- ences such as table aliases. B.6 Retention and coverage change Retention. For each question, we identify the strategies among the base model’s correct samples and compute the fraction also represented among a post-trained model’s correct samples. Each strat- egy counts once regardless of its frequency, and the fractions are averaged equally over questions. We report retention on 825 BIRD and 562 Spider questions where the base model produces at least two correct strategies. Coverage change. For each common strategy, we compare the percentage of questions on which the post-trained model produces at least one correct query of that type with the corresponding percent- age for the base model. BIRD coverage is com- puted over the 1,131 questions on which the base model produces a correct query. A decrease of 22 percentage points therefore means that the strat- egy appears on 22 points fewer questions. C Additional Results C.1 Archive growth during quality-diversity search MAP-Elites increases the number of occupied be- havior cells from 1,132 after seed sampling to 2,708 after eight mutation rounds (Table 6). Thus, targeted search finds verified approaches beyond those obtained by initial sampling alone. C.2 Comparing elite and IID archives The IID comparison archive is generated by sam- pling directly from the same 1,000 questions, with- out targeted mutations. Both archives use 72,000 generation attempts and supply 1,500 verified re- play examples. This isolates the benefit of targeted search from simply increasing the number of sam- pled candidates. Among 547 questions represented by at least two solutions in both archives, pairs sam- pled from the elite archive exhibit different strate- gies (based on AST similarity) more often than those from the IID archive (66.2% versus 45.5%).
