Given the result of m-th iteration v(m) and its corresponding Jacobian J(m), the result of (m + 1)-th iteration is obtained by a parallel scan. (c) In backpropagation, only one scan is needed. The transposed Jacobian matrix at the final trajectory is used to compute the adjoint vector y from upstream gradient g. that is equivalent to BPTT when evaluated on the converged trajectory, avoiding backpropagation through iterations. To enhance modeling capacity without sacrificing incremen- tal step-by-step execution, FidLIF causally generates input- dependent neuronal parameters from recent inputs. Conse- quently, FidLIF supports full-sequence parallel training and step-by-step incremental inference using the same recurrent neuron formulation. Our contributions are as follows: • We introduce FidLIF, a novel framework that retains high-fidelity hard-reset dynamics of LIF neurons while enabling full-sequence parallel training and incremental step-by-step inference within the same neuron. FidLIF also supports input-dependent neuronal parameters for enhanced expressivity. • We reformulate both forward and backward propagation as associative scans: membrane potential and spike tra- jectories are computed through iterative parallel scans, while exact BPTT temporal credit assignment is obtained through a single reverse scan, achieving O(K log T) and O(log T) temporal depth. • We provide a Triton-based implementation for the pro- posed framework and comprehensively evaluate its per- formance across sequential image, event-vision, and speech classification tasks. Our results demonstrate that FidLIF delivers significant speedups and memory savings on long sequences, while achieving competitive perfor- mance on versatile downstream tasks.
