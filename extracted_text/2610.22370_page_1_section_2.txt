Diffusion models [1, 2] have become a dominant paradigm for high-quality image [3–5] and video synthesis [6–8], owing to their strong fidelity and scalability. However, their iterative denoising process incurs substantial computational cost, as a large number of tokens are repeatedly processed at every inference step [9]. To address this, token merging [10] has emerged as an effective training-free acceleration strategy that groups similar tokens, merges them into representative tokens, and uses the merged representations in subsequent layers [9–13]. For example, early token merging methods such as ToMeSD [10] first construct local merge candidate groups in a content-agnostic manner, and then form merge pairs simply based on feature similarity within each group. Consequently, content-relevant tokens can be selected as merge sources, causing local details to be absorbed and leading to visual distortion [9]. This raises a central challenge: which tokens should be absorbed through merging, and which must be retained as anchors for subsequent computation.
