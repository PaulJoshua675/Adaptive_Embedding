2.1 Advertising Delivery Research on advertising delivery has largely focused on automating individual tasks. Automated bidding has progressed from optimizing bid functions under budget constraints [1] to using reinforcement learning [10, 11] or generative modeling [12] for sequential decision-making. RTBAgent incorporates expert knowledge and reflection on past experience into bidding decisions [13]. AdComB jointly optimizes budget allocation and bidding [5], while HiBid uses a hierarchical mechanism to connect cross-channel budgets with within-channel bidding [4]. AuctionNet evaluates automated bidding through traffic generation and auction interactions [14]. AdPilot supports end-to-end autonomous advertising delivery through a Reflector–Actor workflow informed by current states and historical memory. Post-training further improves its delivery decisions [2]. AdCoEvo organizes operational responsibilities into roles with separate policies. Period-end diagnosis guides account planning and strategy adjustment in the next period, while settled feedback supports role-policy updates during continuous delivery. 2.2 Multi-Agent Collaboration The core advantage of multi-agent systems is decomposing complex processes into roles with clear responsi- bilities. Roles address distinct objectives and contexts, then make collective decisions through interaction. Studies have applied complementary roles and task decomposition to complex problem solving and workflow automation, supporting multi-step tasks that are difficult for a single agent to complete independently [15–19]. Current research on multi-agent systems organizes role collaboration through standardized workflows, dynamic task allocation, and runtime supervision [17, 20–23]. It also trains models using joint execution trajectories, role evaluations, or interaction rewards to improve collaborative decision-making capabilities [24, 25]. Our work focuses on the collaboration and continual evolution of multi-agent systems in advertising delivery tasks. 2.3 Agentic RL Agentic RL uses feedback from environmental interactions to optimize multi-step decision-making and tool-use policies [26–28]. For example, ReTool uses tool-call outcomes to improve when and how tools are called [29], while AgentFlow uses multi-turn trajectories to improve planning policies [30]. MARTI uses multi-agent interaction trajectories for distributed training updates [31]. We continually update role policies through alternating reinforcement learning, focusing on mutual adaptation among roles during continuous delivery. Training signals reflect each role’s scope of influence and feedback timing.
