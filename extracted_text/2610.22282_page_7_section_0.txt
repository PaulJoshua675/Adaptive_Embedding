RPO improves desired property across all five reported metrics. 5 EXPERIMENTS Our experiments test whether reward learned through temporary routing transfers to the dense generator used at inference. After the setup, we evaluate text-to-image generation (Section 5.2), compare learning speed with sampler-space RL (Section 5.3), and measure VBench transfer and training cost on video (Section 5.4). 5.1 SETUP Image setting. We train Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium (Esser et al., 2024) on prompts from the PickScore SFW split (Kirstain et al., 2023). Each rollout generates a 512 × 512 image with a deterministic 10-step flow sampler and no classifier-free guidance. The training reward is HPSv3 (Ma et al., 2025) scaled by 0.1. After training, we evaluate the dense model on DrawBench prompts (Sa- haria et al., 2022). An aesthetic score and DeQA-Score (You et al., 2025) measure image quality, while ImageReward (Xu et al., 2023), PickScore, and UnifiedReward (Wang et al., 2025b) measure preference. During training, we evaluate the dense model every 5 steps on 64 fixed validation prompts with a 28-step sampler. Video setting. We train Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (Wan Team et al., 2025), a text-to-video flow-matching generator, on a curated creative prompt distribution. Each rollout renders 13 frames at 704 × 1280 with a 10-step sampler and classifier-free guidance scale 5. The reward is HPSv3 scaled by 0.1 and is computed on every fourth frame. Baselines and training. On SD3.5, we compare against Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025a). It trains the same LoRA adapters with the same HPSv3 reward and the same 10-step training sampler, but it explores by sampling SDE trajectories. On Wan2.2, we compare against Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO (Xue et al., 2025b). Both baselines train the same generator with the same HPSv3 reward and also explore through SDE trajectories. DanceGRPO uses groups of G = 8 and updates the full model. On both generators, RPO instead collects 64 matched pairs (G = 2) per training step.
