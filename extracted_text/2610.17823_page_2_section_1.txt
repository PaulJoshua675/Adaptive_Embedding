Popularity bias mitigation. Approaches to mitigating popularity bias are commonly grouped into pre-processing, in-processing, and post-processing methods [22]. Pre-processing methods modify the training data distribution through re-weighting, re-sampling, or balancing strategies, reducing the dominance of head items before model training [9, 10, 36]. Post-processing methods instead modify the final recommendation lists to improve long-tail exposure, diver- sity, or calibration without changing the trained model [21, 23, 47]. In-processing methods, which our work belongs to, modify the training procedure itself: examples include regularization terms that penalize exposure imbalance [1, 6, 47] and corrections based on item priors [7, 27]. While these directions can reduce the domi- nance of popular items, they often introduce a relevance-debiasing trade-off: aggressive promotion of less popular items may reduce ranking quality when popularity is correlated with user preference. Debiasing through the training objective. Most relevant to our work are methods that mitigate popularity bias by modifying the loss function. Inverse propensity weighting [34] re-weights training examples to counteract exposure imbalance. Logit adjustment [27] shifts logits by the log-prior of each item or class, training the model to estimate a de-popularized score. It has been studied in long-tailed classification and imbalanced learning [7, 27]. In recom- mendation, related corrections also appear in sampled softmax and log-Q correction, where item sampling probabilities are accounted for during training [17, 43]. Margin-based methods [7, 45] introduce popularity-dependent margins. Other recent approaches include causal interventions based on popularity [46] and popularity-aware negative sampling that control which negative items are used dur- ing training [32]. These methods typically apply their corrections within a dense softmax, where every item contributes to the loss. Our approach instead operates over a sparse mapping, where the correction interacts with a context-dependent subset of competing items. Sparse probability mappings. A separate line of work studies sparse probability mappings as alternatives to softmax. Sparsemax and entmax replace dense softmax distributions with sparse dis- tributions, assigning exactly zero probability to part of the output space [24, 29]. These mappings can be derived from the variational principle and Fenchel–Young loss frameworks [4, 5, 25, 26]. Ent- max and related sparse mappings have been applied as an alter- native to softmax in attention mechanisms and output distribu- tions [25, 26, 29, 33]. In recommender systems, sparse mappings have been used mainly inside model architectures, for example, in sparse attention mechanisms [44] or for combining multiple intent representations [8]. To our knowledge, however, their use as out- put probability mappings for training sequential recommenders remains underexplored. 3
