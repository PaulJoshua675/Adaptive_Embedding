To achieve identity consistency while allowing subjects to adapt to diverse scenes, we propose EOS-Story (Fig. 3), a framework that models scene-specific variance in two complementary spaces. (1) Cross-Contextual Bridging (CCB) addresses semantic variance by transferring phrase-level ap- pearance cues into the scene context through the ⟨EOS⟩embedding, mitigat- ing information dilution in long and complex prompts. (2) Adaptive Identity Preservation (AIP) addresses structural variance by aligning identity features to each scene’s subject scale (Identity Anchoring), followed by Identity-Aware Self-Attention refinement. 3.1 EOS Embedding as a Semantic Handle In this section, we explore the generative functional role of the ⟨EOS⟩embedding. We first test whether the ⟨EOS⟩em- bedding can serve as a global seman- tic control point. As shown in Fig. 2a, adding an attribute embedding (e.g., “black”) at different token positions of a base prompt (“A photo of a dog”) produces notably different outcomes. Directly adding the attribute embed- ding to the target word token (“dog”) fails to alter the output, and even a broad injection across all non-EOS to- kens produces only minor changes. In contrast, injecting the attribute only into the ⟨EOS⟩embedding induces a clear semantic shift, suggesting that the ⟨EOS⟩embedding serves as an ef- fective handle for embedding-level se- mantic editing during generation.
