A Additional Method Details A.1 Autoregressive Reference and Decision Paths Problem setup. Given ordered images I = (I1, . . . , Im) and a spatial question q, the goal is to predict a typed answer y ∈Yq: one of the supplied options, a number with a declared unit, or a location in normalized image coordinates. In an autoregressive formulation, a vision-language model πθ generates an answer string w = (w1, . . . , wL) autoregressively, πθ(w | I, q) = L Y l=1 πθ(wl | I, q, w<l), (8) and a task-specific parser then maps w to y. Answer generation therefore adds sequential decoding steps whose number depends on the output length L. In the classification formulation, the state at stage t is st = (I, q, h<t), where h<t contains the preceding selections and visual refinements. A deterministic controller constructs Ct, advances the state after selection, and decodes the terminal path to the required answer. For a fixed observation and question, reachable states form a finite tree of depth at most three, with path probability P(a1:S | I, q) = S Y t=1 pθ(at | I, q, h<t, Ct). (9) Ordinary decoding follows the greedy path in Equation (1); multi-point decoding expands several spatial paths. Training supervises all annotated transitions using Equation (7). Each textual decision supports up to 32 candidates, with padded slots masked before normalization. A.2 Input Sequence and Candidate Representations Sequence layout. Each question–answer example (QA) is represented by an image prefix followed by the question, candidate descriptions, and a decision marker: [state, I1, . . . , Im, question, q, opt, c1, end, . . . , opt, cK, end, decide]. (10) The sequence markers reuse existing vocabulary entries, leaving the vocabulary and embedding matrix un- changed. Candidate representations. The frozen vision encoder (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) and merger embed each image into the language sequence, and the first 31 language layers contextualize the whole causal row. The state at an option-end marker then summarizes that option in the context of the image, the question, and the preceding options, while the state at the decision marker sees all current candidates. Changing an option therefore changes its representation rather than the interpretation of a fixed classifier slot, which lets the candidate set vary freely across questions. Training shuffles candidate order, while evaluation follows each benchmark’s ordering protocol. Multi-stage rows. Each QA occupies its own causal row. A later stage appends the selected option, an optional new image crop, and a new question and candidate block. During training, all supervised stages fit in one teacher-forced (Williams and Zipser, 1989) causal sequence. Each decision marker precedes the later choices and crops, so one forward pass supervises every stage without showing a future crop to an earlier decision. At inference, the next stage is constructed only after the current prediction. A.3 Candidate Scoring with Pretrained Query and Key Projections The shared scorer produces one class logit for each current candidate. The final full-attention layer’s pre- trained query projection acts on the decision state, and its key projection acts on each candidate state; comparing the resulting vectors gives the logits used in Equation (1).
