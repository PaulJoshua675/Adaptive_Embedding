Q3.5-2B-MLP was trained on the Bridge and RT-1 mixture of the OXE LeRobot dataset (O’Neill et al., 2024) for 50,000 optimization steps, with learning rate is 4 \ times 10^{-5} on action decoder, and 1 \ times 10^{-4} on LoRA. The learning rate is linearly warmed up for 5,000 steps and then cosine-decayed. Training uses AdamW with \b e ta _1=0. 9, \b e ta _2=0. 95, \ e psilon =10^{-8},and weight decay 10^{-8}. Training is conducted in bfloat16 on four H200 GPUs with a per-device batch size of 16 and two gradient-accumulation steps, yielding a global batch size of 128. We apply rank-128 LoRA to all linear layers, including the vision tower and multimodal projector, with a scaling factor of 256. Q3.5-2B-MLP achieves 59.4% in the Simpler-Bridge with WidowX robot. Observation Preprocessing. For a training sample at control step t, the base VLA Model receives the current observation x_ t and the language instruction, while the MaLoW receives all preceding observations x_0 , \ l d ots ,x_{t-1}. We do not impose a maximum history length. Bridge trajectories are recorded at 5 Hz, and timestamps are therefore expressed using a control period of 0.2 seconds. The current observation is processed through the same single-image preprocessing path as the Q3.5-2B-MLP, while all the historical observations are decoded directly from the episode video and be sent into the MaLoW. All the frames are resized to 224 \times 224, and passed to the Qwen3.5-2B preprocessor. This matches the preprocessing of the historical frames received from SimplerEnv during interactive evaluation. We divide the observation history into consecutive, non-overlapping two-frame blocks, matching the temporal patch size of the Qwen vision encoder. If the history contains an odd number of frames, its final frame is repeated to complete the last block. For example, the history at step t = 3 is represented as [x_0 ,x_1 ] and [x_2 ,x_2 ]. Each block is assigned a timestamp interval using the true Bridge control clock. A committed block j is labeled [2j\De lta t,(2j+2)\Delta t ], where \D e lta t=0. 2 seconds. Vision Encoder. We use a frozen copy of the vision tower and vision adaptor from the Q3.5-2B-MLP as the MaLoW’s vision encoder. Action-History Conditioning. In addition to visual history, every historical frame is paired with the action that produced it. Specifically, frame x_ i is paired with the preceding action a_{i-1}. The first frame x_ 0 is paired with a learned empty-action embedding representing a_{-1}. Historical actions undergo the same normalization transformations as the action targets. Each seven-dimensional action is mapped to the visual-token dimension by a two-layer MLP with a GELU activation. Thus, the input sequence for a two-frame temporal block is [V_ {2j},V _{2 j+1},T_j, E(a_{2j-1}),E(a_{2j})], where V_ i denotes the visual tokens for frame i, T_ j denotes the frozen token embeddings of the block’s timestamp interval, and E(\cdot ) is the action-history MLP. Aggregator. For every temporal block, the visual, timestamp, and action-history tokens are projected to a hidden dimension of 512 and processed by a four-layer Transformer encoder. The rest of the aggregator’s details are the same as the one used in section C.1. State-Space Model and the Converter. The state-space model and the converter share the same details as the one used in section C.1. LoRA Application Locations. Because Qwen3.5 interleaves full self-attention and linear-attention layers, the rank 8 generated LoRA is applied to gate_proj, up_proj, and down_proj in every decoder layer, to self_attn.v_proj in full-attention layers, and to linear_attn.in_proj_qkv in linear-attention layers. Training Details. MaLoW (Q3.5-2B-MLP) is trained on the Bridge portion of the OXE LeRobot dataset. Before training, we merge the Q3.5-2B-MLP LoRA adapter into Qwen3.5-2B and load the stage-1 action-decoder weights. The merged Qwen backbone is then frozen. The trainable components in the MaLoW (Q3.5-2B-MLP) are the MaLoW, including the aggregator, action-history encoder, temporal state-space model, and LoRA output heads, together with the action decoder.
