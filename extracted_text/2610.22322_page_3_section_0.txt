Here, the Smith wins with a bid of 0.8 and crafts a crafting table; the environment then returns a reward and the next state. 2 METHOD We aim to jointly learn specialized action policies and self-orchestration in decentralized agent swarms. Each agent uses its local context to decide how strongly to participate through bidding. Bids determine both which agent acts and how task rewards are allocated across participating agents for learning. We first describe this orchestration mechanism (§2.1), then explain how agents learn to act from the resulting credit (§2.2) and learn to bid through counterfactual comparisons (§2.3). 2.1 SELF-ORCHESTRATION THROUGH BIDDING We consider episodic tasks with verified rewards. An episode presents a task x and unfolds over turns t = 1, . . . , T, with one agent acting at each turn and a verifier assigning a task reward R. The swarm consists of N agents with separate model parameters. Each agent i uses a policy πi to generate both action and bidding. Following Qi et al. (2026), we adopt an auction mechanism for decentralized turn allocation. At each turn, agents independently submit bids bi reflecting their willingness to participate in the current decision. The agent with the highest bid is selected to act, and its bid is denoted by bt. This allows participation to depend on the agents’ local assessments without a central dispatcher. Tied bids are broken uniformly at random. If all agents bid zero, no agent acts and the turn is skipped. Bids also connect participation with credit assignment. Specifically, the selected bid bt determines the fraction of accumulated value at turn t that is passed backward to the preceding acting agent. Thus, a larger bid increases an agent’s opportunity to act while committing a larger share of the resulting value to earlier participation.
