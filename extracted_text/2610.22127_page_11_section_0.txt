A Relevant Model Architecture Details A.1 Crosscoder Crosscoder is an encoder-decoder-based architec- ture that explains the activations from both fine- tuned (personalized) and base (non-personalized) LLMs by learning a shared dictionary of inter- pretable concepts (latents). Formally, for the in- put token xt, let the activations from a particular layer l of the personalized and non-personalized MoEs be h(l,ft) xt ∈Rd and h(l,base) xt ∈Rd respec- tively (d is the model dimension), the encoder of the crosscoder maps those activations to a higher di- mensional (D >> d)2 latent space, where each di- mension j ∈{1, . . . , D} explains distinct features encoded in those activations h(l,ft) xt , and h(l,base) xt as: aj(xt) = ReLU(e(ft) j h(l,ft) xt +e(base) j h(l,base) xt +b(enc) j ) (10) where aj is the activation for latent j, e(ft) j , e(base) j ∈Rd are the the encoder weight vectors corresponding to the latent j, and b(enc) j is the corresponding encoder bias. The decoder of the crosscoder then reconstructs those activations h(l,ft) xt , and h(l,base) xt for the token xt using the sparse activation distribution of shared dictionary latents as: ˜h(l,ft) xt = X j aj(xt) d(ft) j + b(dec) ˜h(l,base) xt = X j aj(xt) d(base) j + b(dec) (11) where d(ft) j , d(base) j ∈Rd are the the decoder weight vectors corresponding to the latent j, and 2D = α×d, where α is the expansion factor, and we keep it as 32 for our experiments. 11
