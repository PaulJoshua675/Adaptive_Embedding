B.2.3 Visibility-consistent confidence features At an already visible position, the policy controls whether the current token remains in context, rather than whether the decoder’s newly preferred token should be inserted. A global-top-1 confidence can consequently describe the wrong token. When analyzing our trajectories, the global argmax differed from the visible token in 73,930 of 3,407,617 recorded visible-position events, or 2.17%. This observation motivates anchoring the first feature to the current visible token and excluding that token when constructing the strongest alternative. While not necessarily an ablation, we believe that ensuring the policy inputs remain consistent with the current decoding state is important for the semantic correctness of our method. B.2.4 Confidence-feature dimensionality study We additionally report an earlier study of feature width K ∈{1, 2, 10, 50}. This experiment uses a one-layer full-canvas policy trained with RLVR at Ttrain = 32. Inputs are raw full-vocabulary probabilities. The first coordinate measures support for the active token, using the greedy proposal at masked positions and the current token at visible positions; larger widths append the strongest alternative probabilities. The K = 1 and K = 2 variants therefore isolate whether to expose the strongest competitor. Figure 4 reports all 14 recorded inference budgets. For each fixed trained policy, we average five seeds. Adding the strongest alternative improves the curve broadly: K = 2 exceeds K = 1 at every budget above one call, including gains of 3.56 points at T = 16, 2.14 points at T = 32, and 2.92 points at T = 64.
