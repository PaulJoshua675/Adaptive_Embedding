This paper targets goal drift in LLM-based agent systems and builds a steering agent that decides when to intervene with a low-cost fast path and how with a deliberative slow path. Joint reinforcement learning over the two paths unlocks steering intelligence that improves both single-agent and multi-agent systems, generalizes to out-of-domain tasks, transfers to unseen executors, and supports controllable intervention frequency. As LLM-based agents and multi-agent systems tackle increasingly complex and long-horizon tasks, the steering agent provides an effective on- the-fly supervision tool to improve their performance and efficiency.
