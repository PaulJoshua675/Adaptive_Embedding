2.1 LLM-based Agent Systems LLM-based single- and multi-agent systems are increasingly applied to complex, long-horizon tasks, including information-seeking research (Manus, 2025; Team et al., 2025), software engineering (OpenAI, 2025; DeepSeek- AI, 2026), and scientific discovery (Zhu et al., 2026; Qu et al., 2026). However, as increasingly complex tasks require longer execution horizons, these systems may deviate from their original task objectives, a failure mode known as goal drift (Arike et al., 2025; Cemri et al., 2025; Laban et al., 2026). To better equip LLMs for long-horizon interaction and multi-agent collaboration, recent work has explored agentic post-training, particularly reinforcement learning (RL) (Chen et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2025; Liao et al., 2025). Long- horizon agentic RL optimizes LLMs end-to-end with tools on complex tasks requiring extended interaction (Gao et al., 2025; Team et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2026). Multi-agent RL jointly optimizes collaborating agents under shared task- level objectives, encouraging individual agents to contribute to overall task success (Liao et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2026).
