We assess appropriateness through linguistic criteria and do not measure how readers perceive the construction. Whether un- needed uses cause annoyance or affect comprehen- sion requires a separate user study. Languages and constructions. We study a sin- gle Mandarin construction. Although the decision tree is designed to transfer to English it’s not X, it’s Y and other recurring expressions, we have not ap- plied it beyond bushi X, ershi Y. Ethical Considerations This section discusses the ethical considerations and broader impact of this work. Potential Risks. The decision tree describes when a corrective construction is called for, and this description could be used to make model- written text resemble human writing more closely and thus harder to identify. Conversely, the fre- quency measures could be misread as evidence of machine authorship and applied to judge human writers, who also use the construction. Our anal- yses concern aggregate usage across models and corpora; they are not designed or validated for at- tributing individual texts to humans or machines. Intellectual Property. The benchmarks, open- weight checkpoints, reward models, and prefer- ence mixtures used in this work are publicly re- leased for research, and we use them in accordance with their respective licenses. Commercial models are accessed through their oﬀicial APIs under the providers’ terms of service. All materials are used solely for scientific research. Data Privacy. The human reference is drawn from publicly available datasets, and personally identifiable information was removed before anal- ysis. Model answers are generated from pub- lic benchmark prompts. The human annotators are students with a background in linguistics who participated voluntarily; no personal information about them is collected beyond their annotations. Intended Use. The diagnostic framework is in- tended for analyzing contextual appropriateness in model-generated text and for examining how eval- uators and training stages shape such usage. It is not intended for authorship detection or for evalu- ating individual writers. Documentation of Artifacts. We will release our code and the annotation guidelines for the de- cision tree. Both are documented with respect to their scope: the Mandarin construction bushi X, er- shi Y, the task domains covered, and the procedures described in Appendices A–D. AI Assistants in Research or Writing. We use Cursor for code development assistance and a large language model for refining and polishing the lan- guage of the paper. The use of language models as annotators and judges is part of the method and is described in Section 3 and Appendices A.1 and B.
