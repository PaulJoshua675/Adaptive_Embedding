2.3.1 Errors in the Observation To account for errors in the measured PSA, we model the observation at visit t as yt = log (Bt/B0) + δt, |δt| ≤ε, (2.7) where δt denotes the error at visit t, whose magnitude is bounded by ε, and the exact observation (2.6) corresponds to δt = 0. Both the policy and the forced-treatment rule act on the perturbed observation, and hence an error may also delay the restart of treatment required by the forced-treatment rule, which we refer to as the forced restart. Since the measured PSA is affected by analytical and within-subject biological variation [35], we model the errors as random, and refer to them as random perturbations. Specifically, every δt is drawn independently from the uniform distribution on [−ε, ε]. We then learn policies that account for such errors by solving (2.1) under the observation (2.7). 2.3.2 Robustness to Shifts in the Patient Dynamics To account for shifts in the patient dynamics, we allow the transition kernel of each patient to change in every month within an ambiguity set Pc of transition kernels. Specifically, an adversary policy σ selects a kernel from Pc at visit t, possibly at random, based on the observations up to visit t, and we define J(π, σ; D) as the expected cumulative reward of π under the kernels selected by σ. We consider the robust counterpart of (2.1) given by the zero-sum game max π∈Π min σ J(π, σ; D). (2.8) Since the kernel is selected anew at every visit based on the realized history, (2.8) follows the dynamic formu- lation of robust MDPs [29,30], and hence accounts for shifts in the dynamics that occur during treatment. To construct Pc in adaptive therapy, we consider shifts in the competition and growth parameters α and β, whose estimates are subject to statistical error and may differ across patients (Section 2.2). Following Remark 1 of [29], we draw K pairs of multipliers m(k) = (m(k) α , m(k) β ) of (α, β), k = 1, . . . , K, once from a nominal distribution Qnom concentrated around one (see Appendix B), and keep them fixed. For a patient with context c, the k-th generating kernel F (k) c is given by the transition (2.5) with parameters (m(k) α α, m(k) β β), and Pc consists of the mixtures of the K generating kernels whose weights lie in Wρ =  (1 −ρ) 1 K 1 + ρ q : q ∈∆K , ρ ∈[0, 1], (2.9) where ∆K denotes the probability simplex. The radius ρ can be interpreted as the expected fraction of months in which the patient dynamics are selected by the adversary. Indeed, since the expected cumulative reward is linear in the mixture weights at every visit, it suffices for the adversary to place the weight ρ on a single generating kernel at every visit. Equivalently, at every visit the kernel is drawn uniformly from the K generating kernels with probability 1 −ρ, and is selected by the adversary with probability ρ. Clearly, for ρ = 0, problem (2.8) reduces to (2.1) with the parameters redrawn uniformly from the generating kernels in every month, while for ρ = 1 the adversary selects the kernel at every visit.
