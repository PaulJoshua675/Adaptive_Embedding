The numerator accumulates the weights of all proposals equal to v, including repeated occurrences, while the denominator normalizes the weights across the pool. By the strong law of large numbers, the numerator converges almost surely to P1(v)eβR(v), while the denominator converges almost surely to Zβ > 0. Their ratio therefore converges to bQβ,K(v) −→P1(v)eβR(v) Zβ = Qβ(v) almost surely. Since the candidate space U is finite, this convergence holds simultaneously for every completion. Consequently, 1 2 X v∈U | bQβ,K(v) −Qβ(v)| −→0 almost surely, which establishes convergence in total variation. This guarantee is asymptotic and provides no finite-K error bound. It also does not imply that higher example reward corresponds to greater correctness. A.3 Extension to Unknown Lengths With unknown target lengths, each complete candidate consists of a length allocation L and an assignment u ∈V TL to its expanded target positions. Let L denote the finite support of the length allocator πψ(· | ez). For every L ∈L, Theorem 3.1 ensures that the conditional infill distributions P(· | zL) and P(· | zL \ EL) are normalized over V TL. Since the length allocator is also normalized, both joint distributions in Equation (7) are normalized. Assuming their conditional infill probabilities are positive, the joint distributions satisfy the assumptions of Theorem 3.2 on their common support. Moreover, the shared allocator cancels from the likelihood ratio, giving R(L, u) = log P(u | zL) P(u | zL \ EL). Thus, reward guidance and importance resampling extend directly to joint length-content candidates. Although the allocator cancels from each candidate’s reward, guidance can still change the distribution over selected lengths. To see this, define the length-specific normalizing constant Zβ(L) := Eu∼P(·|zL)eβR(L,u). The overall normalizer and the marginal guided length distribution then satisfy Zβ = X L∈L πψ(L | ez) Zβ(L), Qβ(L) = X u∈V TL Qβ(L, u) = πψ(L | ez) Zβ(L) Zβ . Hence, length allocations with larger Zβ(L) receive greater relative probability under guidance, even though the allocator itself is unchanged. The argument does not require πψ to match the data length distribution or to factorize across target sites; oracle lengths correspond to a single supported allocation. 21
