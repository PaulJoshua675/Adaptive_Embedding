We check this behavior by computing input-gradient magnitudes with respect to the three score features and grouping queries by average view overlap. Table 8 shows that total score sensitivity is higher for low- and medium-overlap queries than for high-overlap queries, while generated-view scores account for most of the gradient mass across bins. This supports the intended role of overlap features as query-level context for score combination. Figure 2 examines the relationship without relying on discrete bins. On DL19, average pairwise overlap is negatively associated with the per-query nDCG@10 gain over RRF (Spearman ρ = −0.3190, permutation p = 0.0353). DL20 has the same direction but a weaker, non-significant association (ρ = −0.1878, p = 0.1758). The continuous analysis therefore supports the low-agreement mechanism on DL19 while showing that its strength varies across test years. Table 9 quantifies this pattern by computing the mean of the three pairwise top-100 Jaccard overlaps, forming tertiles separately within DL19 and DL20, and then pooling corresponding bins.
