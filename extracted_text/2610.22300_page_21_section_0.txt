E TOWN RELATIONS The six Town relations share one pool of 300 towns, 15 from each of 20 regions, each a real place with an English Wikipedia article. A fixed rule gives each town its answer; a town the rule cannot resolve has no answer in that relation and is left out of it. Five relations read Wikidata. We match each town to a Wikidata item by its English Wikipedia title, falling back to its name. For 50 towns the match fails: 38 find no item and 12 find items with conflicting coordinates. These 50 towns have no answer in any of the five. Where Wikidata gives a town several populations or elevations, we take the preferred one, else the one with the latest point-in-time qualifier; a town whose values leave no single choice has no answer. Four relations cut a numeric attribute into five bands. The bands are contiguous and hold roughly equal numbers of towns, and towns with equal values share a band. A band with fewer than 20 towns would be merged into its smaller neighbor; none is. A band’s answer names its position (lowest, lower, middle, upper or highest) and the range of its towns’ values, as in “lowest population (150 to 39,653).” • Town →region: the region we chose the town from, such as “Borneo” or “Sahara.” 20 answers, 300 towns. New Guinea is named “Papua” and the South American rainforest “Amazonia,” since otherwise they would share their first token with New Zealand and South Africa. • Town →latitude band: latitude in degrees. 5 answers, 250 towns, 50 per band. The bands span −90.0◦to −20.7◦, −20.7◦to −3.3◦, −3.1◦to 25.9◦, 26.5◦to 50.1◦and 50.2◦to 78.0◦. • Town →longitude band: longitude in degrees, positive to the east. 5 answers, 250 towns, 50 per band. The bands span −97.3◦to −53.5◦, −52.9◦to 16.6◦, 16.9◦to 37.7◦, 39.6◦to 134.1◦and 134.2◦to 178.0◦. • Town →elevation band: elevation above sea level in meters, with values given in feet converted. 5 answers, 195 towns, 39 per band. Besides the 50 unmatched towns, 43 have no elevation and 12 have no single value. The bands span −19 to 9 m, 10 to 48 m, 50 to 252 m, 260 to 830 m and 914.4 to 3830 m. • Town →population band: number of inhabitants. 5 answers, 235 towns, with 48, 46, 47, 47 and 47 per band from lowest to highest. Besides the 50 unmatched towns, 11 have no population and 4 have no single value. The bands span 150 to 39,653, 42,200 to 133,024, 136,302 to 297,497, 307,990 to 741,651 and 755,732 to 20,937,757. • Town →country language: the official language of the town’s country, named in English, such as “Japanese.” We keep only countries with exactly one official language. For a coun- try with several, such as New Zealand (English, M¯aori and New Zealand Sign Language), any choice would be arbitrary and could score a correct answer as wrong. This leaves 107 towns without an answer, besides the 50 unmatched towns and 9 whose item names no country. The remaining 134 towns have 17 languages. We drop the six with fewer than five towns (§4), which keeps 11 answers and 123 towns: Danish, English, French, Greenlandic, Indonesian, Japanese, Malay, Polish, Portuguese, Putonghua and Swedish. Under every model’s tokenizer, the answers of each relation have distinct first tokens, so no two answers merge. F LABEL SETS The label-set algorithm. A name of the category is kept only if (i) its primary sense lies under the category’s root synset (animal.n.01 for animals), so the new-relation prompt’s request for “one animal” is true of it; (ii) no other kept name is its hyponym, so each prompt has one correct label; (iii) it is common in English, with wordfreq zipf frequency at least 2.32; (iv) its first token differs from every other kept name’s under the tokenizer of every model the label set is used with, since we score only first tokens; and (v) the cosine between its first token’s unembedding row and any other kept name’s is at most 0.446 under MiniCPM5-2B-Base.
