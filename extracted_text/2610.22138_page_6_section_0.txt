Nine additional benign peers remove only about a seventh of the damage, and the difference is not statistically significant. 4.2 Most infection is not voiced Judged by the transcript alone, attacked groups look much healthier than they are. Under three ad- versaries, benign agents publicly endorse the claim in 19.7% of exposed turns, while their matched private probes give the false answer in 51.6%: 2.6 times as many turns carry a false private answer as endorse it publicly (Fig. 3). Conversely, only 34.3% of turns with a false private answer publicly endorse the claim; the rest ignore it, hedge, or even reject it (Table 3). Silence explains only part of this: in 28.6% of these turns, the agent responds to the claim but hedges on or rejects it. A low public- endorsement rate therefore does not imply intact belief: transcript-level monitoring understates how far false belief has spread. The gap is also not a by-product of free-form chat, in which agents need not state an answer: agents that privately hold the true answer reject the claim in 61.9% of their turns, whereas agents that privately hold the false answer endorse it in only 34.3%. Is hidden infection an artifact of probe timing? Because the private answer is probed after the round (§3.2), an agent persuaded only by a later speaker would also count as hidden infection. This does not drive the effect. Of the turns with a false answer after the round, 87.7% already had it before the round. For turns with no intervening message between the utterance and the probe (first speakers paired with the probe before the round, last speak- ers with the probe after it), the share of false-answer turns that hedge on or ignore the claim remains at 54–56%, compared with 57.8% across all turns (Appendix B). Agents that already hold the false answer when they speak generally do not voice it. 4.3 Infected agents report the false answer as their own Does a false private answer indicate a change in belief? Our probe asks agents to answer based on their knowledge and the discussion, so an infected answer might merely summarize what the group said. It also shows agents’ own earlier messages, so an agent that conceded in public might simply stay consistent with itself in private.
