New text KV entries are written from right to left in the current-chunk region, keeping the filled portion adjacent to the local text ring. Each text query attends to the visible KV, including its own entry. At a chunk boundary, the Summary query attends only to the completed chunk’s text KV and its own KV. In the example, 𝑇23 completes chunk 11 (Figure 5b), and 𝑆11 attends to 𝑇22, 𝑇23, and itself (Figure 5c). Updating the text and Summary rings. Once text and Summary attention finish, the completed chunk’s text KV is copied into the local text ring, and its Summary KV and auxiliary compression state are stored in the Summary Ring Buffer. If the rings are not yet full, the new entries occupy unused slots. Otherwise, they replace the oldest local chunk and its Summary record; the old Summary KV moves into remote history, with its auxiliary state retained for compression. The shared pointer advances to the next chunk slot. In Figure 5d, chunk 11 replaces chunk 7: 𝑆7 enters remote history, 𝑆11 takes its Summary ring slot, and the pointer advances to the slot holding 𝑆8. Compressing remote Summaries. When 𝑘𝑚uncompressed Summaries accumulate in remote history, they are compressed into a single Meta KV entry. This entry overwrites the group’s first slot, leaving the remaining 𝑘𝑚−1 slots available for reuse. In Figure 5e, 𝑆4, 𝑆5, 𝑆6, 𝑆7 are compressed into 𝑀1, stored immediately after 𝑀0.
