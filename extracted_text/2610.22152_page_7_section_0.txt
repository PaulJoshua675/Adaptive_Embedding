The partial recovery of true reward (2 of 4 possible units, Table 3) reflects this precisely: the constrained agent’s policy no longer contains the exploit but has not yet converged to an efficient delivery route for every dirt cell within the fixed step budget, a distinction we return to below. 6.1 Limitations This study has three limitations. First, the agent is trained and evaluated on a single fixed grid layout – dirt, bin, and agent-start positions generated once and reused across all episodes – rather than a distribution of randomly sampled layouts. This is a standard simplification for interpretable gridworld demonstrations, and it isolates the proxy/true gaming phenomenon from a separate, harder generalization problem. This also means that our results should be read as establishing that the gaming behavior and the constrained fix both occur on this layout; we make no claim here about whether the fix transfers to unseen layouts without retraining, and treat this as the most immediate direction for follow-up work. Second, the constrained agent’s final policy, while free of concealment and abandonment behaviors entirely, had not converged to optimal navigation within the episode budget: two of the four dirt units remained unresolved, consistent with residual action oscillation near grid boundaries rather than any renewed incentive to game the proxy. This is a training efficiency gap, separate from and not evidence against the specification-gaming question this work targets – the policy’s qualitative behavior (no concealment, no abandonment) is the outcome our method is designed to produce, and it was produced; full navigational optimality was not a target of the constrained fix itself. Third, the exact form of Proposition 1 depends on a structural property specific to this environment: every unit of dirt is resolved through exactly one of three mutually exclusive outcomes (covered, delivered, or abandoned), with no possibility of double-counting because the agent can carry at most one unit at a time. An environment without this clean partition – for instance, one where a single action could simultaneously advance and undermine the true objective, or where the agent could act on multiple objective-relevant units concurrently – would require a different argument, and the identity as stated here should not be assumed to generalize automatically to such settings. We view identifying the general conditions under which an exact (rather than approximate) proxy/true/cost identity exists as an open question raised but not answered by this work. 7 Conclusion and Future Work We constructed CleaningGridWorld, a gridworld with a deliberately constructed, exploitable gap between a proxy reward and a true objective, and showed that a standard deep Q-network trained on the proxy alone converges to a policy that games it completely, reaching near-maximal proxy reward while the true reward remains at zero. We then introduced a Lagrangian-penalized constrained RL fix, with a cost signal targeting the specific gaming mechanisms the baseline agent discovered, and showed that it eliminates those behaviors entirely from the final policy while recovering substantial true-objective performance.
