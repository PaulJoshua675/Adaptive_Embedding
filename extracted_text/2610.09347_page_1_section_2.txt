Genetic programming (GP) was introduced as a way of evolving computer programs to solve problems [1]. The defining arrangement is that the program is the answer: it is executed on the task, and its fitness is the error of its own output. Two application areas have carried most of the work. In symbolic regression the evolved program is the model. The arrangement runs from early successes in recovering physical laws from data [2], through a modern line of work combining expression search with neural guidance, physics-inspired decomposition, or reinforcement learning [3, 4, 5], to benchmarking against standard machine learning methods at scale [6]. In classification the evolved program produces quantities from which a decision is made, in fault diagnosis [7], on high-dimensional data [8], and in multiclass problems [9]. A substantial line of work departs from the defining arrangement. The evolved program stops being the answer. It becomes an input to a separate, deterministic learner that is refitted for every candidate, and fitness measures the learner’s output instead of the program’s.
