REPA (Yu et al., 2025b) aligns intermediate SiT (Ma et al., 2024) features with pretrained representations, where MAE is the weakest of the tested targets, and DDT (Wang et al., 2026) adopts this alignment. REG (Wu et al., 2025) denoises a pretrained class token together with the image latents, VA-VAE (Yao et al., 2025) aligns VAE latents with a pretrained vision encoder, and REPA-E (Leng et al., 2025) tunes the VAE and the diffusion model end to end with the REPA loss. Discrete tokenization with pretrained representations. VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) learns discrete image tokens through pixel reconstruction, and later tokenizers simplify the quantizer (Yu et al., 2024a; Mentzer et al., 2024). Wang et al. (2024) train tokenizers to reconstruct the features from image understanding encoders, and in autoregressive generation all but the MAE teacher outperform pixel-trained VQGAN tokens. Recent tokenizers keep pixel reconstruction as the central objective and add pretrained encoder features through feature alignment (Yu et al., 2026), semantic regularization (Xiong et al., 2025), self-distillation (Du et al., 2026), or feature reconstruction from several encoder depths (Jia et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026b; Zheng et al., 2025b, 2026a). TA-Tok (Han et al., 2025) trains feature-reconstructed tokens for unified multimodal models, but decodes images through pixel-trained VQ tokens or latents. In FeaTok, only pretrained visual features (Def. 1) shape the tokens, which reconstruct a multi-layer target and include a quantized global token. 5
