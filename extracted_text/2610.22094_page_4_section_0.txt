Semantic-Conflict-Aware Contrastive Learn- ing Semantic-conflict candidates. An off-diagonal pair be- comes a semantic-conflict candidate when its negative tar- get may conflict with partial semantic compatibility. Be- cause pair-level clinical adjudication is unavailable, SCCL treats similarity-flagged pairs only as candidates: it removes them from softmax normalization without relabeling them as positives. Per-modality similarity. Given the batch’s global em- beddings, SCCL computes intra-modal cosine-similarity matrices Av ij = cos(vi, vj), At ij = cos(ti, tj). (3) Let Ω= {(i, j) : i̸ = j} be the off-diagonal index set. Adaptive thresholds via EMA. At each training step, SCCL maintains modality-specific EMA estimates of the batch mean and standard deviation of the off-diagonal sim- ilarities, using momentum m: µv ←mµv+(1−m) Av Ω, σv ←mσv+(1−m) std(Av Ω), (4) and analogously for µt, σt. The modality-specific thresh- olds are τ v = µv + k σv, τ t = µt + k σt, (5) where k determines how far into the upper tail an entry must fall. Separate EMA statistics allow the thresholds to follow each modality’s evolving similarity scale. The four EMA states are initialized from the first mini-batch. During the first Tw steps, they continue to update while masking is disabled (M = 0). Statistics and masks are stop-gradient and device-local, without cross-device gathering. Valida- tion freezes the EMA states and counters. Thresholds are not clipped; values above the cosine ceiling simply select no candidate. Candidate-selection rules. SCCL uses two candidate- selection rules: the inclusive (OR) rule accepts evidence from either modality, whereas the consensus (AND) rule re- quires agreement between the two modality-specific cues: M OR ij = 1  Av ij > τ v ∨At ij > τ t , (6) M AND ij = 1  Av ij > τ v ∧At ij > τ t , (7) with the diagonal forced to 0. Under identical thresholds, M AND ij ≤M OR ij , so CAND ⊆COR. OR therefore favors candidate coverage, whereas AND is more conservative. Without candidate-level ground truth, this is an expected coverage–precision trade-off rather than measured recall or precision. Hard masking. Dual-Rule Teacher Distillation Rule-specific teachers. Training with M OR and M AND yields an inclusive teacher θOR and a consensus teacher θAND, respectively. We train one teacher under each rule and use both as sources for distillation. Dual-rule distillation. We train a single student θs to match the ensemble of the two frozen teachers. The student is initialized independently rather than from either teacher or an average of their parameters; teacher knowledge is transferred only through logits computed on the fly for each batch, with no logits precomputed or stored.
