On the 32 fitted patients, nominal RL attains 48.43 months, compared with 25.34 months for AT50, and harms none of the fitted patients. A few remarks are in order. First, the improvement is attained without administering more drug. Nominal RL treats in 30.3% of the months and AT50 in 28.7%, while nominal RL divides its treated months into 12.9 courses on average, compared with 6.2 for AT50. Hence nominal RL alternates between treatment and breaks roughly twice as often as AT50, with correspondingly shorter courses and breaks. Second, the improvement holds across patients with different initial resistance (Figure 1), a factor that correlates with the time to radiographic progression in both the adaptive therapy cohort and the standard-of-care cohort of [52]. When we split the virtual test population into tertiles of the initial resistant fraction R0/B0, nominal RL attains 60.81 months in the lowest tertile and 35.57 months in the highest, compared with 37.28 and 19.93 months for AT50 (Table 6). AT50 mainly harms patients with few resistant cells, with a harm rate of 39.9% in the lowest tertile compared with 10.8% in the highest, while the harm rate of nominal RL is at most 0.4% in every tertile. Third, the definition of progression in Section 2.2 is essential for an informative comparison. If we declare progression only when the tumor grows under treatment as in [5], the growth of the tumor during a break is never penalized, and the rule that withholds treatment whenever permitted attains 53.79 months, the same value as all three seeds of nominal RL trained under this definition, compared with 52.60 months for AT50 (Appendix D). Consequently, such a definition does not allow us to assess the benefit of adapting the treatment to PSA. In summary, by learning from data we obtain a policy that substantially improves on AT50 for every level of initial resistance without administering more drug. In addition, since the virtual test patients are drawn independently of the training patients, nominal RL generalizes to new patients from the training population, in line with Theorem 2.1. 3.3 Retaining the Benefit of Adaptive Therapy under Errors in PSA Nominal RL acts on PSA as if it were exact. In clinical practice, however, PSA is subject to measurement errors. We therefore evaluate nominal RL under the random perturbations of Section 2.3.1 with ε = 0.2, which is comparable to the reference change value of about 19% for total PSA, i.e., the smallest relative change between two consecutive measurements that can be distinguished from analytical and within-subject biological variation [35]. We also refer to the policy learned from (2.1) under the same perturbations as random-perturbation RL, and compare it with nominal RL and the two clinical rules in Figure 2. We observe that errors in PSA with ε = 0.2 substantially reduce the benefit of the policies that do not account for them. Under random perturbations, nominal RL drops from 50.35 to 32.96 months, and its harm rate increases from 0.2% to 14.4%. Similarly, AT50 drops from 29.58 to 16.46 months, only marginally above the 15.81 months of continuous treatment, and its harm rate increases from 28.4% to 43.8%. In contrast, random-perturbation RL retains the benefit of adaptive therapy under such errors.
