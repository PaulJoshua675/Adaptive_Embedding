SNNs provide a sparse, event-driven alternative to conventional ANNs, making them particularly attractive for neuromorphic and energy-efficient learning sys- tems. Modern SNNs can be trained either through ANN-to-SNN conversion or di- rectly with surrogate-gradient methods, enabling competitive performance across a range of vision and learning tasks [6,16,25,28]. Continual learning in SNNs has recently attracted increasing attention, motivated by the need for adaptive neuromorphic systems that can learn over time under memory and energy con- straints [15]. Existing approaches mostly focus on regularization-based mecha- nisms to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, including adaptive synaptic plasticity, Bayesian learning, and sparse selective activation [17,26,27]. In contrast, replay- based continual learning for SNNs remains under-explored [18]. Recent work on compressed latent replay for SNNs further confirms the relevance of rehearsal- based strategies in spiking models, while highlighting the need for memory- efficient replay mechanisms tailored to temporal spiking representations [4]. In parallel, recent continual learning methods for conventional ANNs have begun to exploit XAI to identify and preserve task-relevant information from previ- ous experiences [1,5,7,22,23]. Among these, EPR [23] is particularly relevant to our work: it uses saliency maps to select discriminative image patches and stores only those patches in the replay buffer. This reduces memory require- ments while maintaining competitive continual learning performance.
