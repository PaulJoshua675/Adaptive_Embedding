To estimate the robustness across different backbone models, we evaluate LeanSearch v2 (reasoning mode) and LEANGRAPHSEARCH (reasoning mode) with Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Sonnet 5 as backbone models, then average each metric across the two models. 4.2 RESULTS Table 1 shows that LEANGRAPHSEARCH consistently improves retrieval performance at every mea- sured cutoff. The gains over LeanSearch v2 increase at larger cutoffs, with nDCG@10 increasing from 0.616 to 0.629, Recall@50 by 7.8 percentage points, and Recall@100 by 12.0 percentage points. In Table 2, LEANGRAPHSEARCH improves group recall at four of the five cutoffs, with the exception of Recall@10. Improvements in complete-route coverage are more consistent: Cov- ered@k increases at every cutoff, outperforming LeanSearch v2 by 3.7 percentage points at k = 20 and 2.9 percentage points at k = 50. Overall, these results suggest that graph-based retrieval improves both individual declaration re- trieval and the recovery of premise sets that support complete proof routes. In addition to the quan- titative results, our qualitative analysis reveals the following findings: Graph-aware expansion reveals underlying mathematical objects. On MathlibQR, semantic retrievers frequently identify constructors, projections, or characteristic theorems whose descriptions closely match the query, but miss the associated parent declarations or deeper dependent declarations because their names and signatures are less lexically similar to the query. Graph-aware expansion connects these semantic matches to underlying declarations through dependency relationships. Graph augmentation improves the composition of the retrieved context. On MathlibMPR, LEANGRAPHSEARCH returns fewer filtered declarations on average than LeanSearch v2, yet re- trieves more gold declarations within the top five positions, improving group recall and complete- route coverage. Route-level examples further illustrate this effect. These results suggest that graph augmentation concentrates relevant premises in its retrieved context instead of merely expanding it.
