Twenty of the 27 selected values of 𝜆also sat at an edge of the three-point bracket, so the bracket was too narrow to locate every optimum (Appendix Table 6). 5.4 Study 4, variance for EWC and bias for MAS For EWC on P-MNIST the number of samples mattered enormously (Figure 3a and d, Table 4). Without shrinkage, ACC rose from 61.7% with 𝑛= 10 to 83.2% with 𝑛= 1000, by 10.7 ± 0.5 points per tenfold increase in 𝑛. The gain from 𝛽= 0.25 fell in step, from 19.6 points at 𝑛= 10 to 6.2, 0.1, and −0.4 points at 𝑛= 50, 200, and 1000, a slope of −10.0 ± 0.4 points per decade (Holm 𝑝< 10−4). With 𝛽= 0.5 the gain became a significant loss of 2.4 points at 𝑛= 1000. The raw importance estimates tell the same story. Their CV fell from 4.47 to 1.41 as 𝑛grew, and the EB weight fell from 0.49 to 0.10. At 𝑛= 10, EB recovered 15.9 of the 19.6 points that 𝛽= 0.25 gained, but it lost 1.2 to 1.3 points at 𝑛≥200. This is the signature of variance. Shrinkage substitutes for samples, and once the samples suffice it removes signal. For MAS on P-MNIST, more samples helped the unshrunk arm only slightly (0.5 ± 0.2 points per decade), and 𝛽= 0.25 helped at every 𝑛, by 2.1, 0.9, 1.9, and 1.1 points. Its gain had a slope of −0.33±0.17 points per decade (95% CI −0.71 to 0.05, unadjusted 𝑝= 0.08), and its gain of 1.06±0.18 points at 𝑛= 1000 was Holm-significant. With 𝛽= 0.5 the gain was smaller and declined with 𝑛 (slope −0.5 ± 0.1 points per decade, Holm-adjusted 𝑝< 0.05), so part of the benefit at small 𝑛is a variance effect. The raw importance of MAS was far less dispersed than that of EWC, with a CV of 1.00 at 𝑛= 10 and 0.78 at 𝑛= 1000, and the EB weight fell from 0.24 at 𝑛= 10 to 0.006 at 𝑛= 1000. EB did not help, and it hurt at small 𝑛. At 𝑛= 10 its mean weight matched the fixed weight of 0.25, yet EB lost 1.5 points where 𝛽= 0.25 gained 2.1, so where shrinkage falls matters as much as how much there is.
