The categorical family is fully direct for either scalar map by construction. Appendix B: NUMERICAL DETAILS 1. Exact information projections All discrete examples are evaluated by exact state enu- meration; no Monte Carlo entropy estimator is used. For an observable z, states with identical z are aggregated to obtain P(z) and P(Y = 1 | z). The direct model is fitted by minimizing the exactly weighted Bernoulli cross-entropy. Binary coordinates enter linearly. Multi- level coordinates use one-hot indicators for all but one reference level, implementing the univariate functions ui in Eq. (1). Linear-moment results instead use the numer- ical observable itself. Optimization uses L-BFGS-B with an analytic gradient. The resulting bands show why no projection-independent monotonicity claim is available. 3. Held-out capacity calculations All continuous inputs are independent and uniform on [−1, 1]. Linear readouts are ridge fits with an unpenal- ized intercept and predictions clipped to [0, 1]. Figure 3 uses 100 independent trials for the three contact models and 40 independent trials for each random-feature width. The random-feature model uses fixed sigmoid features within a trial and trains only its ridge readout. For Fig. 4, each nonlinear subunit receives four con- tacts and contains weighted pair products plus one triple product followed by a hyperbolic tangent. The feature set at n subunits is a strict subset of the feature set at every larger sampled n. Effective rank is computed from the eigenvalues of the held-out feature covariance. Scaling exponents are ordinary least-squares fits in log space to normalized mean curves. Confidence intervals for means are mean ±1.96 standard errors; exponent intervals are percentile bootstraps over the 60 trials. No morphology, cable equation or active-conductance parameter is fitted or implied.
