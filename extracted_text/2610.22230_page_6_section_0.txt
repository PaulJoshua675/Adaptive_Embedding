We developed a theoretical performance projection model derived from hardware-level simulations of the memory sub- system, scale-up fabric, and NIC data paths1. Guided by these projections, we co-designed a suite of RCCL optimizations across the RDMA transport, GPU kernel, and protocol layers for the MI350X. These refinements narrowed the communication gap from 2× slower than NCCL to within 10–15% of NCCL performance, enabling production-scale DLRM training. Shampoo Optimizer and rocSOLVER Shampoo [35, 36] is a second-order optimizer widely de- ployed due to its faster convergence compared to first-order methods like Adam [37]. Shampoo maintains Kronecker- factored preconditioner matrices, requiring periodic compu- tation of matrix fourth roots to update the preconditioners. This matrix root computation relies on eigen decomposition provided by the rocSOLVER library. For large models, these steps dominate Shampoo’s overhead, making rocSOLVER’s performance critical to overall training throughput. The de facto algorithm to compute the eigen decomposition of a symmetric input matrix A has three steps: (1) reduce A into tridiagonal form, yielding an orthogonal U and a tridiagonal T such that A = UTU T ; (2) compute eigenvalues Λ and eigenvectors V of T; and (3) backtransform eigenvectors Q = UV so that A = QΛQT . In rocSOLVER, reduction to tridiagonal form is accomplished with block Householder transformations [38] operating on panels of the input matrix; updates to the rest of the matrix are applied once per panel as a pair of GEMM operations. Step (2), based on Cuppen’s Divide-and-Conquer (D.C.) [39], is the most complex step and the focus of our optimizations. Cuppen’s D.C. splits T into sub-matrices that are processed independently and then merged in pairs. Typically, D.C. operates on unreduced sub-blocks of T (i.e., blocks separated by zero off-diagonal elements), but this can lead to load unbalancing: sub-optimal CU allocation often follows because the size of sub- blocks is input-dependent.
