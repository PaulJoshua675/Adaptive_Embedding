In this work, we systematically investigate the fundamental structural vulnerability of tra- ditional graph collaborative filtering models when confronted with strict cold-start scenarios. Specifically, we identify that the topological isolation of unobserved items severs the message- passing mechanism, precipitating a catastrophic collapse in representation learning. To over- come this inherent limitation, we propose DualGCN, an innovative dual-branch graph knowledge distillation framework. By seamlessly integrating high-dimensional semantic priors extracted from pre-trained language models, we fundamentally reshape the collaborative latent space. The proposed framework operates through a decoupled two-stage training paradigm. Ini- tially, the teacher branch constructs a robust, topology-smoothed collaborative reference frame anchored by semantic features. Subsequently, the student branch leverages a residual mapping network and a cosine distillation objective to achieve highly reliable cross-modal alignment. Furthermore, to unify the global retrieval space, we introduce a dynamic norm reconstruction mechanism that rigorously calibrates physical scales during inference, effectively circumventing magnitude-induced representation bias. Extensive empirical evaluations across three real-world benchmark datasets comprehen- sively validate the superiority of DualGCN. The proposed architecture not only establishes a new state-of-the-art for strict cold-start recommendation but also strictly preserves the represen- tation capacity for conventional warm-start items without incurring substantial computational overhead. Moving forward, we intend to extend this semantic anchoring paradigm to dynamic graph environments and explore the integration of richer multi-modal signals to further enhance out-of-distribution generalization capabilities. 24
