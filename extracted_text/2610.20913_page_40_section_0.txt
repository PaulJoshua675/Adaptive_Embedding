TransX-GenRet and TransX-GenRank also benefit from initialization with the shared TransX behavioral representation and show successful preference optimization offline (Figure 10). Their end-to-end quality, however, is more sensitive to output-space construction, preference supervision, and autoregressive decoding: GenRet depends strongly on Semantic-ID design and valid-ID handling, while GenRank is more exposed to sequential error propagation and slate-preference coverage. We therefore treat these variants as exploratory extensions rather than production-validated results; detailed diagnostics are deferred to Appendix E. 8.3 Online Deployment Results We evaluated TransX-Base, TransX-Retrieval, and TransX-Ranking in three separate production A/B experiments, each against the incumbent model on its respective product surface (e.g., distinct landing pages or email and notification channels within the same recommendation product). At the time of writing, TransX-GenRet and TransX-GenRank remain under offline iteration while we address the performance tradeoffs identified in the previous section. We therefore restrict the online evaluation to the three production-ready variants. For the retrieval experiment, we additionally report Candidate CTR, defined as the number of clicks on exposed candidates attributed to the evaluated retrieval source divided by the num- ber of such candidate exposures; Top-20 unique contribution is the mean, over eligible requests, of the number of top-20 exposed items contributed exclusively by that retrieval source.
