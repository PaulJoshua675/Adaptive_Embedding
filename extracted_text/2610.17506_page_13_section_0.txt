Performance was measured as percent correct on test signals. 4.2.1 Dyadic Advantage Extends to Larger Ensembles First, we looked at the overall effect of group size on performance. Figure 4 shows a monotonic increase in performance with more ESNs interacting, averaged over all tested reservoir sizes. Compared to an individual ESN, ensembles of 16 ESNs exhibited an improvement of almost 20 percentage points. Performance appears to begin to asymptote at 16 ESNs, although it is unclear how many ESNs can be added while performance gains continue to accrue. Figure 4 also shows that the rate of increase in performance differs for ensembles composed of different reservoir sizes. Ensembles composed of 10-unit reservoirs exhibit the strongest effect of group size. Single 10-unit reservoirs show some of the lowest performance, but ensembles of 16 show equivalent performance as larger networks. This result suggests that larger ensembles of small reservoirs can compete with the performance of larger networks for less total computational cost. 4.2.2 Larger Ensembles of Smaller Networks Are More Efficient Figure 5a compares ensembles that are matched for total computational cost at each point along the x-axis, where computational cost is doubled at each step. For each given ensemble size, including individual ESNs, a non-monotonic performance curve was found such that increasing ESN size yielded better performance up to a point, and further increases caused declines in performance due to well-known limits of reservoir size (Jirak et al., 2023). 13
