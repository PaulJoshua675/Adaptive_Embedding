The highest-CU systems are less grounded than Claude and the SFT-only models. Thinking helps retain grounding Proposed (think) has lower utility than Proposed (no think) (0.51 vs. 0.64). Primary reason for this is a lower win rate (0.34 vs. 0.50), but we see that thinking gets more grounding (0.81 vs. 0.61; Figure 2). The grounding gap appears only after RL, since SFT-only think and no-think models are equally grounded (0.91 vs. 0.90). Thinking thus seems to help the policy retain grounding as RL pushes for utility. We hy- pothesize two reasons why thinking may not raise utility. First, the reward scores only the final ideas, and the impact signal is a judged comparison against a gold idea that cannot be verified at intermediate steps. Thus the steps might not have enough causal influence on the reward (Sprague et al., 2025). Second, design- ing thinking traces suitable for creative ideation is an open-problem. In our work, we tried one approach that was hindsight generation. While we instruct the teacher model to propose negatives, verify etc. there may be instances where there is no ‘search’ for candidates beyond what the model generated originally; we also tried distilling forward-thinking traces but it showed the same trend (Appendix E). We leave the problem of de- signing synthetic or appropriate thinking traces for creative/open-ended problems to future work. Table 2: GiantsBench results on 2,374 test papers.
