Model Merging integrates task-specific specialists into a unified model without joint retraining, and has been widely studied in LLMs [14, 23, 37, 41]. Existing methods mainly address interfer- ence among task-specific updates, or task vectors, through conflict mitigation [6, 8, 12, 20, 22, 35, 40, 42, 45] or coefficient determina- tion [6, 43]. The former reduces conflicts by pruning redundant or conflicting parameters, such as TIES-merging [40], DARE [45], CABS [44], and LwPTV [47], or by projecting task vectors into orthogonal subspaces, such as TATR [35]. These methods motivate our pruning design, but their criteria are not tailored to recommen- dation objectives and overlook objective-specific contribution and cross-objective relevance. The latter determines how to combine task vectors. AdaMerging [43] adaptively learns merging weights, while PCB-merging [6] considers both intra-task and inter-task bal- ancing, which inspires our pruning design. However, these methods typically yield a single fixed merged model, resulting in a “one-size- fits-all” solution [1] that is unsuitable for CMOR, where different preference vectors require different trade-offs among accuracy, di- versity, and fairness. Recent Pareto merging methods learn multiple solutions via low-rank structures [5], surrogate evaluation [19], or MoE-based coefficient generation [36]. Following Tang et al. [36], we adopt a preference-conditioned coefficient generator as deter- miner and tailor it with our pruning criterion to CMOR. Parameter Generation enables controllability by dynamically gen- erating model weights to meet specific task demands. This approach was pioneered in controllable multi-task learning [24, 27], which demonstrated that hypernetworks could map preference vectors to task-specific parameters. Subsequent advances such as PHN [24] and PHN-HVI [11] have shown that a single hypernetwork can effectively approximate the entire Pareto front. In recommendation, CMR [4] adapts hypernetwork by generating sensitive re-ranking parameters for instant preference adjustment, while Hyperban- dit and its variant further extend this idea to time-varying user preferences in streaming recommendation [31, 33]. Beyond hy- pernetworks, Paragon [34] employs diffusion models to synthe- size objective-conditioned adapters, matching the performance of retraining at lower cost.
