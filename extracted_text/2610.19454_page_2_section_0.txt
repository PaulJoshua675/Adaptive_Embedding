As operations grow, limited manpower and responsiveness make this approach difficult to scale and often prevent optimal performance [2]. Operators’ accumulated experience is also hard to turn into reusable operational capabilities for later delivery. Prior work has automated individual tasks, such as bidding [1, 3] and budget allocation [4–6], but overall advertising operations still rely on human coordination. More recently, large language model agents have begun to take on decisions across the entire advertising delivery process. AdPilot uses a Reflector–Actor workflow: it first generates a diagnosis from current metrics and historical trends, then makes delivery decisions based on that diagnosis, and improves agent capabilities through a series of post-training steps [2]. Training policies for the entire delivery process requires linking business feedback to specific decisions. Budget planning and ad-unit adjustments continue to affect subsequent delivery, and their effects need to be evaluated through cumulative returns; post-delivery diagnosis takes effect through planning and adjustment in the next period. Organizing multi-agent collaboration by operational responsibility therefore also requires constructing training signals according to each decision’s scope of influence and feedback timing, and scheduling role updates after feedback settlement, enabling models to co-evolve during continuous delivery. Meanwhile, the advertising delivery market is not static: a continually changing auction environment may create a mismatch between historical strategies and current conditions, requiring timely updates to delivery strategies [3, 7]. Beyond autonomous execution, we therefore need to study how multi-agent systems use continuous business feedback to adjust strategies and jointly adapt to market changes through collaboration. To automate the entire advertising delivery process and improve adaptability to market changes, we propose AdCoEvo, a co-evolving multi-agent advertising delivery system. As shown on the right of Figure 1, the system connects decisions across different timescales and operational granularities by assigning separate responsibilities for account-level budget planning, ad-unit delivery strategy adjustment, and end-of-period diagnosis. Diagnostic results guide account planning and strategy adjustment in the next period. After offline training, the system updates one role per period in rotation using settled feedback, while keeping other roles’ parameters fixed. The updated policy takes effect in the next period. Through joint execution, it changes the delivery states and training feedback encountered by other roles, which adapt their policies in later rotations. We call this mutual adaptation across periods co-evolution. Experiments show that AdCoEvo improves advertiser value (ADV) and reduces cost per acquisition (CPA) in both environments.1 Compared with a multi-agent system with fixed parameters, co-evolution can yield greater delivery returns.
