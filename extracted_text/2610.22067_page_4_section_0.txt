Scope measures the proportion of the context covered by the evidence set, Scope(E) = S(E) L . (1) Evidence dispersion. Dispersion measures the extent to which the evidence spans the document, relative to the amount of non-evidence text. We define the evidence span as the distance from the beginning of the first interval to the end of the last interval, H(E) = bN −a1 + 1. (Note that H(E) ≥S(E).) The number of non-evidence tokens lying between the first and last evidence intervals and the total number of non-evidence tokens in the context are, respectively, Ginternal(E) = H(E) −S(E), and Gtotal(E) = L −S(E). (2) We define evidence dispersion as the fraction of all non-evidence text that lies inside the evidence span, Dispersion(E) = Ginternal Gtotal . (3) Dispersion lies in [0, 1]: values near zero indicate clustered evidence, while values near one indicate that most non-evidence text lies between the evidence intervals. It is zero for a single interval and one when the evidence span covers the full context (a1 = 0, bN = L −1). Reasoning depth. Reasoning depth measures the sequential dependencies involved in deriving an answer from evidence. We represent a derivation as a directed acyclic graph, in which nodes denote atomic operations, including evidence lookups and inference steps, and edges denote direct depen- dencies between operations. Depth is the number of nodes on the longest dependency path ending in an answer-producing operation. This representation accommodates both sequential inference and the synthesis of evidence obtained in parallel; it does not assume that reasoning forms a single chain. For example, the question “Which film was released first, Bichunmoo or Musa?” needs the release year of each film. The two years are retrieved independently at depth one, and comparing them is one further step at depth two (Figure 7 in Appendix E). Additional parallel lookups do not increase depth unless further sequential operations are needed to produce the answer. Under this convention, an answer read directly from the evidence has depth one. Depth does not count the total number of operations or the difficulty of individual steps. 4 BENCHMARK CONSTRUCTION We construct LURCH by standardising instances from existing long-context benchmarks, using ref- erence or automatically annotated evidence, and generating validated reasoning graphs (Figure 2). 4.1 DATA COLLECTION Filtering, validation and subsampling reduce 323,040 instances from 26 candidate sources to 6,550 instances from 17 sources. We retain English-language instances with naturally occurring con- texts of 32k–256k tokens, prioritising tasks involving multi-hop reasoning, comparison, aggrega- tion, or evidence integration rather than single-fact retrieval or span extraction. We also exclude questions answered correctly by a screening model without the context, reducing the likelihood that they can be solved from parametric knowledge alone.
