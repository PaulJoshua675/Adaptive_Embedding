Occupancy prediction, a dense 3D map of free and occupied space [38, 40, 47], is becoming that safety-bearing layer for vulnerable road users (VRUs: pedestrians, cyclists, motorcyclists). Yet occupancy meth- ods, from lift-splat models [17, 34, 51] to sparse and set- prediction models [28, 43], are judged mainly by mIoU and sometimes mean FPS. Mean throughput is the wrong safety currency: on a late frame, the pedestrian is seen too late. A safety case uses worst-case execution time (WCET), a per-frame bound checked against the deadline, and ISO 26262 [20] asks for that evidence rather than an aver- age. GPUs cannot provide it: datacenter GPUs and automo- tive edge SoCs optimize the mean, while dynamic schedul- ing, driver and OS jitter, thermal throttling, and contention move the tail [29].
