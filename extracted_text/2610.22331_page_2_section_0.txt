This knowledge, not obser- vations or model confidence, provides the natural signal for credit assignment in long-horizon search. We propose Knowledge-Grouped Policy Op- timization (KnowGPO), a RL framework that aligns trajectories by the task-relevant knowledge they have acquired rather than by their surface ob- servations. For each query, we decompose the in- formation required to derive the correct answer into a set of atomic knowledge conditions. At ev- ery step of a trajectory, we represent its state by the subset of conditions it has satisfied, called its knowledge state. We further define incremental knowledge gain, the increase in satisfied condi- tions between consecutive steps, as a process re- ward that scores each action’s immediate contri- bution. While knowledge-state grouping ensures only semantically comparable steps are contrasted, incremental gain provides the dense signal within each group, enabling fine-grained credit assign- ment without a learned critic. Trajectories sharing the same knowledge state are grouped for within- group comparison, yielding step-level advantages that combine with episode-level outcome advan- tages through a single mixing coefficient. The result is fine-grained credit assignment for long- horizon deep search without observation match- ing, without dependence on the model’s own confi- dence, and without a learned reward model. We train Qwen3-8B with KnowGPO on three challenging deep search benchmarks: Xbench, BrowseComp-ZH, and GAIA. KnowGPO im- proves over outcome-only GRPO by 2.0% to 5.9% and consistently outperforms recent process- supervision baselines including GiGPO and IGPO, with the resulting 8B agent matching or surpassing open-source systems of up to 32B parameters. In summary, our contributions are threefold: • We propose KnowGPO, a reinforcement learn- ing framework that performs credit assign- ment in a query-specific knowledge state space rather than over surface observations, enabling within-group comparison across se- mantically equivalent trajectories. • We introduce incremental knowledge gain as a process reward grounded in the environment, providing dense step-level supervision with- out observation matching, learned critics, or reliance on model-internal probabilities. • We demonstrate that KnowGPO outper- forms outcome-based and recent process- supervision baselines by 3.8% to 4.2% across three deep search benchmarks, with an 8B agent matching or surpassing open-source sys- tems of up to 32B parameters. 2
