Time Series Language Models (TSLMs) seek to extend the reasoning and interaction capabilities of large language models to sequential data, enabling models to answer questions, infer temporal rela- tionships, forecast future behavior, and support decisions over time series (Xie et al., 2025; Langer et al., 2026; Guan et al., 2026; Yu et al., 2026a). A central challenge in this emerging paradigm is how temporal signals should be represented to the language model. Existing approaches serialize numerical values into text, learn dedicated time-series embeddings, or project features from spe- cialized temporal encoders into the language-model representation space (Xie et al., 2025; Langer et al., 2026; Guan et al., 2026). As summarized in Table 1, these approaches typically commit to a single time-series representation, with absolute measurement scale either retained directly or pro- vided separately as auxiliary metadata. Yet these representational choices impose fundamentally different inductive biases: representations that retain absolute amplitudes and coordinate informa- tion support quantitative readout, whereas normalized representations can suppress such informa- tion while emphasizing relative structure and temporal dynamics.
