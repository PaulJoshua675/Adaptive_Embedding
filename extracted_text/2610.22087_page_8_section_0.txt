8 Next, we examined per-class behavior on the same cohort, using ROIs selected by a pathologist (Fig. 3). Macro-averaged recall at Top-1/3/5 was 62.5%/76.7%/81.3% for Atlas2, 50.2%/66.6%/72.9% for UNI2h, 52.3%/71.5%/77.6% for Atlas2-B, and 54.4%/65.2%/73.7% for Atlas2-S, without any statistically significant difference between the models at any depth. Performance was not dependent on class size with malignant melanoma and carcinoid tumor, with only two cases each, retrieved correctly by every model at Top-1. Conversely, basal cell adenocarcinoma, also with only two cases, was not retrieved correctly by any model at Top-1 (Fig. 3a). Restricted to classes with at least ten cases, precision and recall were balanced except for adenocarcinoma, which was under-retrieved rather than over-called. F1 was also where the models diverged most: squamous cell carcinoma was essentially model-independent, whereas adenocarcinoma ranged from 0.36 (Atlas2-S) to 0.62 (Atlas2) and mucoepidermoid carcinoma from 0.62 (UNI2h) to 0.78 (Atlas2). Across these five classes, macro-averaged F1 was 76.6% for Altas2, 71.8% for UNI2h, 74.0% for Atlas2-B and 69.0% for Atlas2-S without any statistically significant difference (Fig. 3b). Four of the seven classes with at least five cases achieved above-chance Top-1 and Top-3 retrieval across all four models (p < 0.05), while adenocarcinoma, malignant myoepithelioma, and infiltrating duct carcinoma only tended to exceed chance (Fig. 3c). (b) Precision, recall and F1 of the Top-1 retrieval for the five classes with at least ten cases. (c) Top-1/3/5 recall, averaged across the four foundation models, compared with chance-level retrieval, for the seven classes with at least five cases.
