Therefore, graded rewards preserve the true quality ordering more faithfully by assigning larger advantage weights to samples that satisfy more sub-questions, thereby better aligning optimization with improvements in true sample quality. By contrast, discrete rewards collapse partially correct samples into the same value, failing to distinguish imperfect outputs of differing true visual quality and potentially destabilizing generative policy optimization, especially under complex compositional requirements. 3.2 Graded Verification Enhances Multimodal Reasoning and Critique Capabilities Drawbacks of Binary Outcome Supervision. Binary verifiers supervise model training solely through the final True/False outcome, implicitly assuming that a correct verdict reflects a correct reasoning process. This assumption is often reasonable in domains such as mathematical problem solving, where the solution space is large and arriving at the correct answer typically requires coherent multi-step reasoning. However, it becomes much more fragile in binary multimodal critique tasks. Without explicit supervision over the judgment process, a verifier may exploit shallow visual patterns or shortcut cues to produce seemingly correct verdicts without genuinely understanding the underlying multimodal reasoning process (Wang et al., 2026c; Shao et al., 2025), thereby leading to reward hacking Experiment Setup. We collect textual prompts from ShareGPT-4o-Image (Chen et al., 2025) and control prompt length to construct samples with varying levels of complexity. To enhance visual diversity, we randomly sample from multiple generative models, including FLUX.1-dev (Labs et al., 2025), Qwen-Image (Wu et al., 2025), and Z-Image (Team, 2025b), to generate the corresponding images. We then perform supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to learn the sub-question decomposition paradigm distilled from Seed1.8 (Seed, 2026). Using the SFT-trained model, we filter out samples whose predictions exactly match the ground truth, and further apply a GPT-5 (OpenAI, 2025a) Bo10 filtering strategy to remove samples with accuracy below 60% while controlling the proportion of fully correct samples at 30%. The remaining high-quality samples are used for Decoupled Reinforcement Learning with DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) on Qwen3-VL-8B (Team, 2025a). The model is trained for 80 steps on 16 NVIDIA A800-80G GPUs and evaluated on ViVerBench (Zhang et al., 2025b). Decoupled Reinforcement Learning.
