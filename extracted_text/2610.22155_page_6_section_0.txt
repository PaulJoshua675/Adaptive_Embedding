Preprint 5.2 CONTEXT MANAGEMENT To minimize harness-induced overhead when evaluating models’ real-time capabilities, we follow Qwen-CUA (Lu et al., 2026a) in removing older history in blocks rather than at every step. This keeps the history prefix unchanged between folds, allowing models to reuse its KV cache across consecutive steps and reducing repeated prefill computation caused by frequent history truncation. All models follow the same history retention policy. Implementation details are provided in Ap- pendix A.3. 5.3 EPISODE EXECUTION Suppose the model receives an observation at environment time ti and commits an action after a delay ∆ti determined by the clock protocol. Under Wall Clock, ∆ti equals the actual inference latency; under Paused Clock, ∆ti = 0; under Token Clock, ∆ti is determined by the generation tokens consumed before action commitment. The action timestamp is tact i = ti + ∆ti. The environment advances from ti to tact i before the action is applied. If generation terminates without a valid action, the harness substitutes wait at the timestamp determined by the same clock protocol. GUI execution latency does not advance environment time. An episode terminates when the environment reaches a task-defined terminal condition (e.g., game over, successful completion of a web transaction, or expiration of a task-specific countdown) or the benchmark-defined time horizon (e.g., five minutes of environment time). The scorer evaluates the terminal environment state against task-specific success criteria, such as completing the first level of a game or securing a ticket. 6 EXPERIMENTS 6.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP Models. We evaluate 12 frontier models spanning (1) standard asynchronous models, includ- ing GPT-6-Astra (OpenAI, 2026b), GPT-5.6 Sol (OpenAI, 2026), Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic, 2026), Claude Opus 5 (Anthropic, 2026a), Gemini 3.8 Flash (Google, 2026b), Deepseek V4.1 Flash (Deepseek, 2026) and Kimi-K3 (Team et al., 2026); (2) real-time streaming models, which are optimized for low-latency interaction, including GPT-Realtime-2 (OpenAI, 2025), Gemini 3.1 Flash live (Gemini, 2026) and Gemini 3.8 Live (Google, 2026); and (3) decision models, which directly re- turn structured decisions rather than generate free-form text, including OpenAI Decisions (OpenAI, 2026a) and Jev 1.13 (TypeSafe, 2026). Evaluation settings. To evaluate model performance in real-world deployment settings, we first evaluate all 12 models under Wall Clock. We report the pass rate for each run, defined as the fraction of tasks successfully completed, along with passˆk and pass@k for k ∈{1, 2, 3}. Following τ- bench Yao et al. (2025), passˆk measures the probability of succeeding in all k attempts at a task, whereas pass@k measures the probability of succeeding in at least one of k attempts. To further distinguish task performance under actual inference latency, without inference delays, and under a standardized temporal cost of token generation, we further compare Wall Clock, Paused Clock, and Token Clock on six representative models: GPT-Realtime-2, Gemini 3.1 Flash live, GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Opus 5, Gemini 3.8 Flash, and Kimi-K3. We exclude decision models from this analysis because they do not report output token counts, which are required for Token Clock evaluation. For each clock protocol, we test both low and high reasoning effort to examine the tradeoff between decision quality and timeliness. Crossing the three clock protocols with the two reasoning levels yields six settings: WL and WH for Wall Clock, PL and PH for Paused Clock, and TL and TH for Token Clock, where L and H denote low and high reasoning effort, respectively. Temporal configuration. We set the observation rate to f = 1 FPS, following the frame-sampling recipes primarily used in current training and evaluation of real-time models (Xia et al., 2026; Qian et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025; Team, 2026).
