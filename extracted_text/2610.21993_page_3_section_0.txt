We structure our contributions around three questions: whether sparse convolutional masked pre- training, previously developed and validated only on natural images, can be adapted to produce transferable representations for S1GRD SAR imagery under constrained compute; how much pretraining data and downstream labeled data this benefit actually requires; and whether the ob- served gains are genuine, rather than an artifact of geographic overlap between the pretraining corpus and the downstream evaluation sets. Adapting sparse masked pretraining to S1GRD SAR imagery. • ConvNeXt-RS encoder: a domain-adapted ConvNeXt encoder for SAR dense prediction, reducing stem stride from 4× to 2× to produce 14×14 feature maps (stride 16×) instead of the standard 7×7 (stride 32×), providing 4× more spatial tokens at identical parameter count (Section 3.1). • Lightweight reconstruction decoders: documentation of FP16 mixed precision pretrain- ing instability with the standard UNet-768 decoder on S1GRD data, and evaluation of UNet-128 and ConvNeXt-512 alternatives that stabilize pretraining, with ConvNeXt-512 reducing pretraining VRAM by 7.5–8.5% per GPU at matched accuracy (Section 3.2). • LLRD finetuning recipe: a finding that layer-wise learning rate decay (LLRD) improves downstream task performance and reduces across-seed variance when finetuning the UNet- 128-pretrained SparK encoder, but does not help a non-pretrained one (Section 3.4). Evaluated across four Copernicus-Bench S1GRD tasks (Wang et al., 2025b) spanning classi- fication, multi-label classification, semantic segmentation, and change detection, the resulting 27.8M-parameter encoder improves over a non-pretrained baseline by +4.7 pp, +5.0 pp, +5.8 pp, and +1.4 pp on EuroSAT-S1, BigEarthNet-S1, DFC2020-S1, and Flood-S1 respectively (Sec- tion 4.3). Despite using a 27.8M-parameter encoder, roughly 5× smaller than Copernicus-FM ViT’s 139M (frozen) parameters, our finetuned model beats it on three of the four tasks, and it likewise beats SoftCon (Wang et al., 2024) on three of four tasks and both CROMA (Fuller et al., 2023) and DOFA (Xiong et al., 2024) on all four. The comparatively modest Flood-S1 gain is examined further in a dedicated ceiling ablation (Section 4.4.4), which points to a boundary con- dition for this approach: single-image masked pretraining appears to transfer less effectively to temporal change detection than to single-scene interpretation tasks. Characterizing how much pretraining data and downstream supervision this benefit actually requires. • Pretraining data scaling study: a systematic evaluation of pretraining data efficiency across eight data fractions (0–100% of SSL4EO-S12 v1.1 (Blumenstiel et al., 2025)) with formal statistical analysis, revealing task dependent saturation behavior in SAR amplitude representation learning (Section 4.5). • Representation analysis: Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) (Kornblith et al., 2019) analysis across all data fractions, characterizing how encoder representations evolve with pretraining data quantity (Section 4.7). • Label efficiency analysis: a demonstration that SparK-SAR pretraining provides statis- tically significant accuracy gains over random initialization at every label fraction tested (1–50%), with pretrained models matching full-label random-initialization performance using 10–50% of labeled training data across all four tasks (Section 4.6). 3
