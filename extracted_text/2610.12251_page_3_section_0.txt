The appendices give the serialization format, the sizes of the constructions, the records of the organism, the restoration bound, the measurements under noise and the verification. 2 Ternary threshold networks This section fixes the networks of the paper: ternary threshold units, the layered maps they form, and netlists, which describe such a map unit by unit and which levelization turns into layers. Throughout, Σ = {0, 1, . . . , 255} is the set of bytes, Σ∗the set of finite byte strings, |u| the length of u ∈Σ∗and u∥v concatenation. The Heaviside function is H: R →{0, 1}, with H(z) = 1 if z ≥0 and H(z) = 0 otherwise, applied coordinatewise to vectors. 2.1 Units and layered maps Definition 2.1 (Ternary threshold unit). Let k ≥0. A ternary threshold unit with k inputs is a map u: {0, 1}k →{0, 1} of the form u(x) = H(⟨w, x⟩+ θ), w ∈{−1, 0, 1}k, θ ∈Z. The integer ⟨w, x⟩+ θ is the pre-activation. The effective fan-in of u is |{j : wj̸ = 0}|. Example 2.2. AND(x, y) = H(x + y −2), OR(x, y) = H(x + y −1), NAND(x, y) = H(1 −x −y), NOT(x) = H(−x), and the identity x = H(x−1) on {0, 1}. More generally H(P i∈P xi −P i∈N xi − |P|) is the conjunction of the literals xi (i ∈P) and ¬xi (i ∈N), and H(P i∈P xi−P i∈N xi+|N|−1) is their disjunction, so a negated literal costs no unit. Ternary threshold units therefore form a functionally complete basis. A function that is not linearly separable, such as parity, needs depth at least two [16]; the exclusive or is XOR(x, y) = AND(OR(x, y), NAND(x, y)), of depth two. Definition 2.3 (Layered ternary threshold map). Let n0, . . . , nd be positive integers with n0 = nd = n, and for ℓ= 1, . . . , d let Wℓ∈{−1, 0, 1}nℓ×nℓ−1 and θℓ∈Znℓ. The layered ternary threshold map (LTTM) with these parameters is F = Φd ◦· · · ◦Φ1 : {0, 1}n →{0, 1}n, where Φℓ(y) = H(Wℓy + θℓ). Its depth is d, its size is M = Pd ℓ=1 nℓ, the number of its rows, and its dynamics is xt+1 = F(xt) on {0, 1}n. Every LTTM is a finite automaton with 2n states, and every orbit is eventually periodic; on threshold networks as finite automata see [17, 18]. 2.2 Netlists and levelization A network is easier to design unit by unit than layer by layer.
