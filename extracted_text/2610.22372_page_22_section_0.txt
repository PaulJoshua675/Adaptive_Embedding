E A Worked Example of ComprePref Underlying task: Put the white mug on the left plate. Explicit preference: Pick by the handle. Input prompt: Put the white mug on the left plate. The mug just came out of the microwave—the body is too hot to touch. Implied behavior: Grasp the handle while completing the same placement task. What stays fixed: The task goal, scene, and preference evaluator. SR evaluates the original placement goal; SA uses the original handle-grasp preference label with task-success gating. What changes: The language supplied to the policy conveys the preference through context instead of a separate explicit preference field. A second example replaces the explicit preference “speed up” with “Guests will use them immediately” for the task of putting alphabet soup and a cream cheese box in a basket. The motion preference is still evaluated from the trajectory, not from a still image. These contextual prompts assess grounding of a specified benchmark preference; they do not establish recovery of an individual user’s unobserved preference history. F Adding a New Preference Adding a standard preference requires one human reference demonstration and a natural-language description of its satisfaction metric for the preference-conditioned generation pipeline, followed by approximately three to five validation trials for each applicable task. For a preference applied to roughly five tasks, the observed manual effort is about 20 minutes on average; demonstration generation then proceeds automatically. This estimate covers preferences expressible through the current scene representation, skill library, and automatic programmable metrics. Preferences requiring a new skill or non-observable feedback incur additional work. For example, to add a preference for grasping a mug by its handle, a user provides one reference demonstration and a natural-language description of the satisfaction metric: the robot should grasp the handle rather than the mug body. The original mug-placement goal remains unchanged. The user then runs approximately three to five validation trials for each applicable task to check whether the generated trajectories follow the preference and whether the metric correctly evaluates grasp location. After validation, the pipeline automatically generates additional demonstrations. G Benchmark Release and Maintenance The benchmark has five evaluation suites. The 312 tasks include both preference-conditioned and no-preference tasks across SinglePref (100), PrefScale (100), PrefConflict (48), and PrefCompose (64); ComprePref reuses tasks with contextual prompts and does not add new mission-preference combinations to this count. Demonstrations are generated trajectories used as training/reference data; evaluation rollouts are executions of the evaluated policy and are not additional benchmark tasks. The release documentation covers installation, dataset/configuration descriptions, and examples for generation, training, and inference. The configuration follows the five-suite organization described here, and the Croissant metadata includes the fields rai:personalSensitiveInformation and rai:hasSyntheticData. We will maintain the code, datasets, and leaderboard, improve the documentation based on community feedback, and expand rollout coverage and generation diversity. 22
