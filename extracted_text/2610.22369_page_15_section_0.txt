Preprint generates 1024×1024 images, HunyuanVideo 640×480 videos of 65 frames and Wan2.1 832×480 videos of 81 frames. Videos are saved as MP4 at 8 fps on HunyuanVideo and 16 fps on Wan2.1. The 720p HunyuanVideo comparison of Fig. D.2.6 generates 1280 × 720 videos of 129 frames. FLUX and HunyuanVideo run on one H100 NVL and Wan2.1 on one RTX A5000, so Wan2.1 latencies are not comparable with the other two backbones. Every SoftCache result uses the cap J = 8, first-order residual motion (Eq. (10)), the tail re-spacing of Eq. (11) and the two jump guards of Sec. A.3, with τ as the only parameter that sets the speed. The scale β is 1 below 4× and 0.5 above it (Sec. 5.4). All methods, SoftCache and every baseline, are run by us on the hardware and settings above, and every number in the paper comes from these runs. Tab. DPCache (Cui et al., 2026) releases code for FLUX and for Wan2.1-I2V-14B, so we adapt its of- ficial implementation to HunyuanVideo and Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B and recalibrate its cost tensor on 10 VBench prompts disjoint from the evaluation prompts. On FLUX its released cost tensor was calibrated with fp16 Taylor states, so we recalibrate it at bf16 with its own protocol on 10 of the 200 DrawBench prompts. There, DPCache at k = 5 is excluded as degenerate (8.64×, 8.46 dB PSNR), and TeaCache and TaylorSeer are not run on video, where each additional method costs a full VBench sweep per backbone and speed. Every method is scored against the uncached sample of the same prompt and seed. SoftCache, SeaCache and TeaCache run in our sampler and share one reference on every backbone. DPCache and TaylorSeer run their own drivers: DPCache’s reference is byte-identical to ours, and TaylorSeer’s differs from it only by driver-level numerics.
