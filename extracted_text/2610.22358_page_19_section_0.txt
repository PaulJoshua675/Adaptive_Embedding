Preprint C SAMPLING AND COMPUTATIONAL EFFICIENCY Ensemble size determines the precision of forecast summaries, while the sampler and its execution determine their cost. We first describe sampling error and ensemble construction, then report controlled latency and memory comparisons. C.1 FINITE-ENSEMBLE APPROXIMATION A finite ensemble approximates a model’s predictive distribution through sampled paths. Forecast summaries and scores can vary with the returned ensemble due to Monte Carlo approximation. We first separate approximation error from model error, then describe ensemble construction; Section D.1.1 defines the scores of the empirical forecast. C.1.1 FORECAST QUANTITIES AND MODEL ERROR Condition on the available forecasting information I and on the fitted model. For m ≥1 iid paths from Pθ(· | I) and a fixed functional g of finite variance, write µθ = EPθ[g(X) | I]. The sample average is unbiased for µθ, and independence gives bµg = 1 m m X i=1 g(X(i)), EZ[(bµg −µθ)2 | I] = VarPθ(g(X) | I) m . (8) Thus ordinary iid Monte Carlo standard error scales as m−1/2. This error remains for nondegenerate queries even when Pθ is the true conditional law. Direct quantile outputs avoid estimating their reported quantiles from finitely many paths, but retain model error. Efficient ensemble generation therefore matters for both inference cost and the accuracy of downstream estimates. Let the finite value µ⋆be the corresponding expectation under the true conditional law. The sample average’s squared error relative to this target decomposes as EZ[(bµg −µ⋆)2 | I] = (µθ −µ⋆)2 + VarPθ(g(X) | I) m . (9) The cross term vanishes because EZ[bµg −µθ | I] = 0. The first term is error in the model’s conditional functional expectation; the second is Monte Carlo approximation error. This expression concerns estimation of a conditional expectation, not prediction error against one random future outcome, which also contains outcome variability. For an event A with model probability pθ = Pθ(A | I), taking g(X) = 1{X ∈A} gives sampling variance pθ(1 −pθ)/m. For 0 < pθ < 1, the relative standard error is p (1 −pθ)/(mpθ), making rare-event probability estimates demanding at small ensemble sizes. Increasing m reduces this sampling term but does not remove model error. Using conditional marginals or analytical integration, when available for a particular query, need not incur the same sampling error. Dependent or stratified draws require a different variance analysis. The m−1/2 rate describes the approximation error of the iid sample average at a finite sample budget. C.1.2 ENSEMBLE CONSTRUCTION AND STRATIFICATION At inference, we approximate the forecast distribution with an ensemble of m complete future trajectories, from which we estimate quantiles and other forecast summaries. Ensemble size controls the number of paths, independently of the one- or two-evaluation budget per path. With few draws, these summaries can be sensitive to sampling variability.
