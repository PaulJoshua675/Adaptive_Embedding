Several architectures have marked the evolution of using ViTs [27] in LLIE. For example, UFormer [49] adapts the U-shaped [26] encoder-decoder paradigm, replacing the standard convolutions found in U-Net [26] with Transformer blocks while preserving level-wise skip connections. Retinexformer [50] incorporates illumination representations into the self-attention mechanism to model non-local interactions across regions with varying lighting conditions. More recent work, such as ISALux [51] or ModalFormer [52], successfully manage to integrate semantic and structure priors into the self-attention module aiming to compensate the light-deficiency induced detail loss with visual or feature-based information represented through different modalities. Despite their strong performance, Transformer-based methods face notable computational chal- lenges. The self-attention mechanism incurs substantial computational complexity due to its reliance on the spatial dimensions of the feature maps. To mitigate this limitation, several methods, such as Restormer [53], DepthLux [54], employ different strategies: computing the attention map channel wise considering the depth of the feature map as the number of channels, contracting the feature space before the self-attention module and then expanding it back to its original dimensions, or re- placing convolutions with their more efficient variation, depthwise separable convolutions. RGB-Thermal Fusion for LLIE. RGB-Thermal fusion has recently been explored to enhance ro- bustness in extreme low-light conditions by leveraging the illumination-invariant nature of thermal imagery. In particular, some studies [55] propose a thermal-aware ViT architecture, where thermal features are explicitly embedded into the transformer pipeline to guide global feature modeling un- der severe illumination degradation. Complementarily, Jha et al. focus on the interaction between illumination cues derived from the low-light RGB image and the corresponding thermal map, de- signing a mechanism that aligns and integrates these complementary representations for more stable enhancement [56]. These works demonstrate that modeling cross-modal dependencies, especially between illumination and thermal structure, can significantly improve detail recovery and perceptual consistency in challenging low-light scenarios. 2.3 Modality Fusion for Image Semantic Segmentation Multi-modal semantic segmentation has emerged as an effective strategy for improving scene under- standing and feature richness limitations under real-world conditions where RGB might be insuffi- cient [57].
