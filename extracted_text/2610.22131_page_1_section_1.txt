Driven by the rapid progress of large language models (LLMs), agent systems have evolved from passive text generation models into interactive systems capable of perceiving environments, using tools, and taking actions [19, 30, 32]. Unlike open-ended text generation, many agent operations are inherently closed-set decision problems, where the model selects from a predefined set of alternatives, such as tool invocation, model routing, and actions [20]. However, current agents often rely on autoregressive free-form generation even when the underlying task only requires structured decisions, introducing additional decoding overhead and complicating the interface between models and downstream controllers [19, 25, 30]. These observations motivate the development of dedicated decision models that can directly produce structured outcomes together with reliable confidence estimates for efficient agent execution. Recent work on structured decision models, referred to as System-1 decision models, explores this perspective by replacing unrestricted generation with explicit decision interfaces and probabilistic outputs. Systems such as Jev [27] provide typed interfaces for choice, score, and binary decisions, while several open implementations investigate alternative architectures and inference strategies for efficient structured decision making [1, 17, 22, 26]. These interfaces primarily target textual decisions, and implementations differ in whether they score multiple questions jointly or separately.
