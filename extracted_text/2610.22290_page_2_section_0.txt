As Figure 1 shows, methods with nearly identical pass@128 can differ substantially in the number of distinct correct strategies they produce. Preserving a diverse set of good solutions is the central concern of quality-diversity (QD) optimiza- tion. Rather than seeking a single optimum, QD algorithms such as novelty search (Lehman and Stanley, 2011) and MAP-Elites (Mouret and Clune, 2015) maintain an archive of high-performing solu- tions spread across a space of behavior descriptors. In the deep RL literature, such archives have been coupled with policy-gradient learners to broaden exploration, enabling the discovery of new behav- iors in policies trained from scratch (Khadka and Tumer, 2018; Pourchot and Sigaud, 2019; Ecof- fet et al., 2021). Post-training a language model, however, presents a different challenge, since the base model already exhibits a variety of correct strategies that RL can suppress. At the same time, language models offer a natural advantage for QD search, as the base model itself can propose meaningful variations through repeated sampling (Lehman et al., 2023; Romera-Paredes et al., 2024; Bradley et al., 2024; Samvelyan et al., 2024). We study whether quality-diversity archives can preserve solution diversity during GRPO training. Our method, ELITEREPLAY, first runs MAP-Elites with the frozen base model to build an elite archive: a set of verified solutions, with at most one solution per cell of a grid indexed by a behavior descriptor. It then trains with GRPO while adding a small replay loss on this archive. Measuring diversity requires distinguishing how solutions are obtained, not just whether they are correct. We therefore evaluate diversity at the level of solution strategies, using a structural description of how each solution solves the question, computed deterministically from the solution itself. This al- lows us to measure U@k, the number of unique correct strategies among k samples, alongside ef- fective diversity (Shypula et al., 2025), the prob- ability that two random samples are both correct and different, which captures improvements in reli- ability without sacrificing diversity. We evaluate ELITEREPLAY on text-to-SQL, a natural testbed where questions can admit multi- ple correct queries and natural-language ambigu- ity is common. As shown in Figure 1, ELITERE- PLAY achieves 74.8% pass@128 on BIRD (Li et al., 2023), comparable to MaxRL’s 73.7%, while producing 9.1 distinct correct strategies per question compared to just 6.2. On the out-of- distribution Spider (Yu et al., 2018), it achieves 93.2% pass@128, outperforming the strongest baseline by 6.3 percentage points while produc- ing up to 77% more distinct correct strategies. ELITEREPLAY also retains 62% and 60% of the base model’s correct strategies on BIRD and Spi- der, respectively, at least 23 percentage points more than any baseline. This preserved diversity also benefits ambiguous questions, with ELITEREPLAY covering both interpretations on 27.0% of Am- biQT (Bhaskar et al., 2023) questions, compared to 14.2% for the strongest baseline. Our contributions are: • We propose ELITEREPLAY, which anchors RL to an elite archive constructed through quality-diversity search using the base model (§2). • We evaluate RL post-training through strategy- level diversity, measuring unique correct strategies (U@k), effective diversity, and re- tention of the base model’s strategies (§3). • We show that ELITEREPLAY preserves more diverse correct strategies while maintain- ing strong pass@k, retains most base-model strategies, and better covers ambiguous ques- tions (§5). 2 ELITEREPLAY Our proposed approach improves accuracy with RL while preserving the base model’s distinct correct solutions in two stages (Figure 2). Before train- ing, QD search with the frozen base model builds an elite archive of verified solutions that span dis- tinct approaches (§2.1). During training, GRPO is combined with a replay loss on this archive (§2.2).
