A. Play and interaction in RL RL agents can acquire sophisticated behavior via open- ended interaction and planning. Model-based agents such as MuZero [6] learn a latent dynamics model and plan within it to master board games and Atari without access to the rules. In multi-agent settings, emergent autocurricula produce complex strategies purely from self-play and environment interaction, as in the hide-and-seek tool-use environment [7]. Comparable interaction-driven pipelines now reach elite per- formance on physical systems such as competitive table ten- nis [5]. These results establish that interaction and planning are powerful, but they depend on well-speciﬁed rewards, simulators, or game rules. DSP instead targets contact-rich manipulation, where reward engineering and designing safe exploration plans are difﬁcult, and obtains its behavior from human play rather than from reward-driven search. B. Curiosity and intrinsic motivation To reduce dependence on hand-designed rewards, intrinsic-motivation methods reward the agent for reaching novel or hard-to-predict states. Prediction-error curiosity uses the error of a learned forward as an intrinsic signal [3], [8], while Random Network Distillation measures novelty as the error of predicting a ﬁxed random network’s features [9]. While such signals drive broad exploration, they are still heuristics and they do not bias interactions toward safe, object-centric contact that dexterous manipulation requires. C. Learning from play Closest to our framing is robot learning from play: goal- free, human-generated interaction used as a task-agnostic source of supervision. Play-LMP learns latent plans from teleoperated play and reuses them for goal-conditioned con- trol [10], and subsequent work distills affordances from play to improve sample efﬁciency [4]. These approaches largely treat play as a ﬁxed dataset for imitation. In contrast, DSP uses a human play data seed to train a policy that serves as a reusable, autonomous data-collection mechanism. D. Dexterous manipulation Dexterous, multi-ﬁngered manipulation has been ap- proached both by model-free reinforcement learning and by imitation. RL methods learn agile in-hand skills such as general object reorientation [11], [12], [13], [14] and sim-to- real cube manipulation [15], [16], [17], but require carefully shaped task rewards and hundreds of millions of simulated interactions. DSP differs from existing work and does not use simulation data: it learns contact-rich dynamics purely from autonomous play, without task-speciﬁc demonstrations or rewards, and plans for arbitrary goals by scoring play- policy proposals with the learned world model under MPC.
