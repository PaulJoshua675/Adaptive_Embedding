Full signed effects are shown in Figure 2. 4.2 Answer encoding and lexical variation Label reversal changes the question’s answer mapping while preserving financial content. A/B averaging may reduce assignment preferences but cannot guarantee semantic invariance. The fully matched UD comparison tests another encoding; natural words provide a smaller additional check. A reversal of the sign or rank of a factor across these formats weakens any encoding-independent interpretation. Low requested-prefix mass further limits the meaning of scores conditional on those prefixes (Table 14). The wording results supply a direct check on the pilot’s single-pair limitation. The six pairs broaden language coverage but share one authoring process. Pair-specific results and leave-one- pair-out ranges are therefore robustness evidence conditional on these templates, not population estimates of lexical invariance. The complete ranges are in the machine-readable summary. Natural- word comparisons use matched subsets rather than comparing unequal scenario samples (Table 13). 4.3 Numerical recognition and commentary removal The isolated EPS task has a known correct relation, whereas the next-day abnormal-return forecast does not have observed outcomes. Strong diagnostic performance can coexist with a small EPS forecast contrast: recognizing a fact and using it to form a forecast are different behaviors. Con- versely, weak diagnostic performance prevents a small EPS contrast from being attributed solely to deliberate down-weighting of the surprise.
