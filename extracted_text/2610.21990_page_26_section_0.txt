(3.2) After the distribution over the low-light image has been computed, each original RGB image from the 2 datasets used: PST900 [8] and CART [25], is passed through the following pipeline. Retinex Decomposition. To better approximate illumination variations under low-light conditions, a Single-Scale Retinex (SSR) [68] approximation is employed to decouple each image into illumina- tion and reflectance components. Given the luminance channel Y of an input image I ∈RH×W×3, the decomposition assumes that the observed intensity at pixel x can be expressed as the product of illumination L(x) and reflectance R(x): Y (x) = L(x) · R(x). (3.3) An important point about the luminance channel Y is that even though it encodes brightness, it is composed of illumination (lighting) and reflectance elements (texture, albedo) and it is not equiva- lent with the illumination component assumed in the Retinex Theory [40]. For the purpose of this study, the illumination component is approximated using a Gaussian smooth- ing operator (Gσ) with kernel standard deviation σ = 15, reflecting the assumption that illumination varies slowly across the spatial domain. The reflectance component, which captures high-frequency structural details, is obtained via point-wise division, where ϵ is a small constant introduced for nu- merical stability: L = Gσ(Y ), R = Y L + ϵ. After decomposing the image into illumination and reflectance components, the illumination channel can be manipulated independently while preserving struc- tural details.
