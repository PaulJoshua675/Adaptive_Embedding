Setup. We fix the retriever to the raw, un-fine-tuned base-Qwen3- Embedding index and vary only how the query is built. For PCQR, a rewriter reads the full conversation and the full user profile and pro- duces a self-contained personalized query, which is then encoded with the same ad-hoc instruction template as our other Qwen3 rows; we use the minimal MQ4CS prompt listed in Appendix C at temperature 0. We sweep the rewriter across the open-weight Qwen3 model sizes of 0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, and 32B, the frontier model gpt-4o, and the human oracle rewrite. PCDR instead encodes the conversation and the full user profile directly, with no generative call. All rows are evaluated on the same 44 held-out turns with full-corpus retrieval and NDCG@3. To score all rewrites in a sin- gle pass over the corpus, the rewriter rows use exact search over int8-quantized document embeddings, whereas PCDR is evaluated with the full-precision index of Section 5. The quantization changes scores only slightly: the human oracle rewrite obtains 36.7 on the int8 index and 36.3 on the full-precision index, the latter being the upper bound reported in Table 3. Significance testing. For each rewriter we compare its per-turn NDCG@3 against PCDR with a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test over the 44 turns, and apply a Bonferroni correction for the six comparisons, giving a threshold of 𝛼= 0.05/6 ≈0.0083. In other words, on this data we can neither conclude that PCDR beats PCQR nor the re- verse: PCDR is statistically indistinguishable from strong rewriters and from the human oracle, while a rewriter of the same size as the PCDR encoder is clearly worse. The point estimates suggest the reformulate-then-retrieve pipeline needs a rewriter roughly an order of magnitude larger to match direct encoding, and pays an extra generative call per turn, but with 44 queries the test is underpowered and we do not claim this as a finding. Future work. A conclusive comparison needs far more personal- ized relevance judgments than the iKAT held-out split provides. A promising direction is to train PCDR on a larger synthesized per- sonalized dataset and then compare PCDR against PCQR across all iKAT test collections from 2023 to 2025 at once, where the combined query set would give the statistical power this appendix lacks.
