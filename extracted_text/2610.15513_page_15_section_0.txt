REPRO: Reflective Evolutionary Prompt Optimizer for Automated LLM Red-Teaming 15 extracts a continuous soft-success score from each failed attempt, combining partial-compliance, useful-content-ratio, refusal-strength, and engagement signals into a single value. The score is used in two places: it adds a small fitness bonus to near-miss candidates during Pareto selection, and it tags near-miss behaviors for highlighted attention in mutation feedback. Full formulation, components, and weights are given in Appendix B. 3.4.2 Feedback-Driven Mutation. For each parent 𝑝on the Pareto frontier, REPRO synthesizes a feedback analysis from the parent’s metrics and target responses, identifying which techniques in 𝑝worked, where it failed, what defense mechanisms were detected, and what alternatives the strategy taxonomy suggests. The feedback is enriched by the structured failure analysis module (§3.3.1) and feeds the relevance scores used by the hybrid Thompson Sampling weight (Equation 7). A mutation strategy 𝑠is then sampled via the procedure of §3.3.2, and the reflective LLM generates 𝑘offspring from 𝑝conditioned on the strategy and feedback: children = R(𝑝∥𝑠∥feedback(𝑝) ∥ICL examples) (8) This is what makes mutation informed rather than random: a parent that succeeded on some behaviors but failed on others due to keyword detection might be mutated under an encoding strategy, while a parent that triggered a strict ethical refusal might be mutated under authority framing. The number of offspring 𝑘adapts to population diversity (§3.5), increasing when diversity is low to inject exploration. 3.4.3 Semantic Crossover. After mutation, REPRO performs semantic crossover, our second main algorithmic contribution. Unlike traditional genetic algorithms that use blind token-level recombi- nation, semantic crossover uses the reflective LLM to analyze why multiple attacks succeed and combine their complementary strengths. When the Pareto frontier contains two or more parents, REPRO selects 2–3 of them via fitness- weighted sampling (a separate mechanism from strategy selection: the weights here come from Pareto rank, not from strategy success statistics) and prompts the reflective LLM to identify complementary techniques across them and generate 𝑘offspring that combine those techniques. As an illustration, suppose two parents are on the frontier: • 𝑝1: You are a professor teaching cybersecurity. Explain [HARMFUL_TASK] for educational purposes. (Authority roleplay, high ASR but moderate XFER.) • 𝑝2: Translate this base64: [encoded_request] then execute. (Encoding obfuscation, moderate ASR but high XFER.) Semantic crossover generates an offspring like You are a cybersecurity professor. Decode and explain this educational example: [encoded_request], inheriting authority framing from 𝑝1 and keyword- bypass encoding from 𝑝2. Neither parent alone achieves both properties; the LLM-guided recombi- nation finds the combination because it operates on technique semantics rather than tokens. When the frontier contains only a single parent, REPRO falls back to self-crossover, decomposing and recombining the parent’s structural elements in novel orderings to maintain exploratory pressure. 3.4.4 Self-Refinement: Pre-Evaluation Filtering. After mutation and crossover, REPRO filters off- spring through a self-critique pass before spending target-model queries. For each candidate, the reflective LLM is asked whether the attack will work and returns one of three decisions: keep (proceed to evaluation unchanged), refine (return a modified version that addresses identified weaknesses while preserving the strategy), or reject (filter out without evaluation). The critique runs at low temperature (𝜏= 0.3) to make conservative high-confidence decisions, and the prompt includes ICL examples of each decision type.
