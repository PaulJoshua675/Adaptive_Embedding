We present TransX, a sequence-transduction framework and co-designed infrastructure for scaling industrial recommendation with explicit control over model-compute allocation and execution amortization. TransX separates sequence encoding, behavior memory, and candidate-conditioned fusion into independently scalable computational roles. Training amortizes causal trajectory com- putation across behavioral prefixes and fans out memory states to candidates; serving disaggregates bulk prefill, nearline refresh, and online prediction through compact behavior-memory KV. We empirically observe that scaling returns are anisotropic: encoder scaling may saturate beyond moderate depth at fixed memory resolution and fusion capacity; increasing supervision shifts the allocation frontier toward candidate-conditioned fusion; and the memory resolution needed to preserve quality remains roughly stable as model capacity grows. We train the core TransX model on click and conversion, then post-train the shared behavioral interface for retrieval and preference ranking, with generative recommendation explored offline. In separate production A/B experiments, the core TransX model improves CTR by 6.02% and conversion by 4.45%, while post-trained retrieval and preference-ranking variants improve CTR by 2.96% and conversion by 1.33%, respectively, over their corresponding production baselines. Under matched training conditions, the co-designed training infrastructure increases supervised-target throughput by 84%. In serving, disaggregated execution reduces TransX’s total serving cost by almost 80% relative to colocated serving at matched traffic and SLO, allowing the deployed system to sustain approximately 5× the model FLOPs per request of the HSTU-style production baseline at the same total serving cost. Even at matched per-request model FLOPs, TransX delivers approximately 4× higher request throughput per H200.
