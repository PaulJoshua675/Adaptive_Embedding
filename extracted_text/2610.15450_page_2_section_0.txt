Preprint Figure 1: From a single FC estimate to cofluctuation based multi-view learning. Conventional FC aggregates all frames into a single connectivity estimate, whereas CoFlu-Brain uses different compositions of framewise cofluctuation observations to construct multiple FC views. Existing FC representation models typically operate on a single Pearson-FC matrix or graph for each scan (Kan et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2026a), leaving frame-resolved variation unused during representation learning. Framewise cofluctuation provides a finer-grained description of regional interactions by characterizing the joint standardized activity of regional pairs at individual frames (Zamani Esfahlani et al., 2020; Faskowitz et al., 2020). This perspec- tive makes it possible to construct multiple training views from different compositions of framewise observations. This gap raises a central question: can different compositions of framewise cofluctuation observations provide self-supervision for learning stable and transferable connectivity rep- resentations? We hypothesize that retained frame sets sampled from the same segment provide different views of subject-specific cofluctuation structure. Although the resulting FC views may differ numerically, their shared connectivity structure should remain repro- ducibly inferable. Moreover, a retained view should contain suﬀicient information to predict the representation of the corresponding non-overlapping dropped view. The former encour- ages consistency across retained views, whereas the latter captures predictive relationships between retained and dropped observations. Motivated by this gap, we propose CoFlu-Brain, a cofluctuation-based approach to self- supervised FC pretraining. For each fMRI segment, CoFlu-Brain repeatedly partitions the frames into retained and dropped sets, organizes their framewise cofluctuation observations into separate FC views, and uses the dropped-view representations as prediction targets. Its joint-embedding objective promotes consistent representations across retained views while requiring each retained representation to predict its corresponding dropped-view represen- tation in latent space. We evaluate the resulting representations through multi-dataset pretraining, frozen transfer, and systematic ablations. Our contributions are: 1. We propose a cofluctuation-based FC pretraining approach that constructs retained and dropped views from framewise observations and couples consistency across retained views with retained-to-dropped latent prediction. 2. We develop CoFlu-Brain, an FC foundation model pretrained on multi-cohort fMRI data, and demonstrate strong frozen-transfer performance across demographic, developmental, and clinical downstream tasks. 3. We conduct a systematic empirical analysis across nine downstream tasks, ex- amining the contributions of the learning objectives and the effects of data coverage, branch count, and temporal sampling, while assessing seed robustness for the pri- mary and temporal-sampling comparisons. 2
