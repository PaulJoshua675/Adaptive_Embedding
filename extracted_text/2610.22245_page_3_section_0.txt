To this end, our contributions are summarized as follows: Our contributions are summarized as follows: • Fundamentals of weak-to-strong routing. In Section 3, we characterize optimal routing within a broader framework that jointly accounts for weak-model test-time inference allocation, identifying the population quantities governing when to continue locally and when to seek stronger assistance. • Routing through model disagreement. In Section 4, we derive a novel, ground-truth-free approximation to the optimal routing signal based on two disagreement signals: how much the weak and strong models disagree, and how much the strong model disagrees with itself. Importantly, (i) the approximation gap shrinks as the strong model becomes more accurate, and (ii) both signals can be efficiently learned from scalable model-generated data, without ground-truth supervision. • Empirical evaluation in agentic and reasoning tasks. In Section 6, we evaluate disagreement- based routing across multi-step agentic environments, where step-level correctness is difficult to define or supervise, and controlled single-turn reasoning tasks 1. Across both regimes, our learned disagreement-based signals consistently outperform label-free baselines, validating that disagreement-based approximation provides a reliable, scalable learning signal when ground-truth supervision is unavailable. 2
