As shown in Table 2, single ZCPs show clear performance ceilings and strong instability across search spaces. On NAS-Bench- 101 [34], most gradient-based proxies fail to produce reliable rankings: Fisher [1] even gives a negative correlation (τ = −0.190), and SynFlow [27] achieves only 0.250. Even the strong ZCP-based method NEAR [10] reaches only 0.520, while FENAS improves the correlation to 0.658. This demonstrates that FENAS does not simply reuse raw proxy scores, but converts weak and noisy proxy signals into more reliable ranking predictions through meta-level integration. A similar pattern is observed on NAS-Bench-201 [7]. Although some ZCPs perform well on specific datasets, their rankings are not consistently reliable across tasks. For example, SynFlow [27] reaches a relatively high correlation on CIFAR-10 [13] (ρ = 0.77), but degrades on more complex datasets. In contrast, FENAS achieves consistently strong results, reaching ρ = 0.907 and τ = 0.731 on CIFAR-10, while maintaining high correlations of τ = 0.730 and τ = 0.716 on CIFAR-100 [13] and ImageNet16-120 [5], respectively. These results show that the improvement of FENAS comes from integrating complementary physical perspectives rather than relying on any single proxy.
