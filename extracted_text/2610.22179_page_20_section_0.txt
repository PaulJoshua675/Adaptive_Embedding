D.2 Exponent scaling and boundary effects Proof of Proposition 1: shift-corrected exponents. Let X take finite, nonzero BF16 values and a̸ = 0 be a finite constant. Define the ideal exponents E = ⌊log2 |X|⌋, Ea = ⌊log2 |aX|⌋, and the common shift k = ⌊log2 |a|⌋. Excluding rounding and finite-range effects, the claim is (Ea −k) −E ∈{0, 1}. (13) Write log2 |X| = E + U and log2 |a| = k + δ, where 0 ≤U, δ < 1. Then ⌊log2 |aX|⌋= E + k + ⌊U + δ⌋= E + k + J, J = 1{U ≥1 −δ}. (14) The indicator is zero when δ = 0, including values exactly on a power-of-two boundary. More generally, let pj = Pr(E = j) and qj = Pr(E = j, J = 1). The exact redistribution is Pr(Ea = m) = pm−k −qm−k + qm−k−1. (15) No independence or uniformity of the fractional log magnitude U is assumed: the mass crossing a boundary can depend on the original bin. Given E, there are at most two possible values of Ea; given Ea, there are at most two possible values of E. Hence both conditional entropies are at most one bit, and the chain rule gives H(Ea) −H(E) = H(Ea | E) −H(E | Ea), |H(Ea) −H(E)| ≤1. (16) For δ = 0, integer translation is a bijection, proving entropy invariance. Proof of the BF16 boundary rule.
