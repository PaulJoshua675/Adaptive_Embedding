E.7 Perturbation stability on the canonical quotient Perturbation stability. RMS normalization cancels a positive multiplier because the vector and the corre- sponding RMS are multiplied by the same factor. For x̸ = 0 and ϵx = 0, this cancellation gives C(cx) = C(x) for every c > 0. A perturbation that also changes direction can affect the result. Thm. 6 separates a perturbation into a radial component along the input and a tangential component perpendicular to the input. As long as the radial component does not reverse the input’s direction, normalization removes pure radial perturbations exactly, and the remaining error is bounded by the tangential component measured relative to the radially rescaled input. Factorization identifies the information available to an invariant map. The next result quantifies the effect of a perturbation after canonicalization, assuming that the perturbation’s radial component preserves orientation, meaning a > −1 in the notation below. Pure radial perturbations are removed exactly. For a mixed perturbation, the bound uses the tangential ratio ρ/(1+a), which measures directional error after accounting for radial rescaling.
