Problem formulation. At each decision step t we predict the chunk At ∈RH×da of the next H actions of an embodied system, conditioned on the current context ct with ct = (zt, ot, st), (1) which gathers a text prompt zt, camera observations ot and the current robot state st ∈Rds. More generally, a policy may condition on every context observed so far, c1:t. The camera observations ot = {o1 t, . . . , oV t } gather the V available views, static or embodied. The robot state st is expressed as absolute joint angles or an absolute end-effector pose, and the actions as absolute joint angles or delta end-effector poses and yield the next H states at a given control frequency. Action and state dimensions (da, ds) vary across embodiments and control modes. 3.1 DIFFUSION POLICY Following prior work (Chi et al., 2025; Black et al., 2024), the action expert generates the chunk by flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023). Write A = At for the chunk generated at step t and c = c1:t for the contexts observed up to it; the index t is fixed in this subsection and omitted. Given noise ϵ ∼N(0, I) and a flow time τ ∼p(τ), the interpolant Aτ = (1 −τ) A + τ ϵ moves from the clean chunk at τ = 0 to noise at τ = 1, and the expert vθ is trained to regress its constant velocity ϵ −A, ℓfm(θ; A, c) = Eτ,ϵ h vθ(Aτ, τ | c) −(ϵ −A) 2 2 i . (2) The squared error is averaged over the valid steps and action dimensions of the chunk, so that every embodiment has the same weight whatever its number of action dimensions. The action loss is the expectation of this term over the dataset D of (context, chunk) pairs, Lact(θ) = E(c,A)∼D[ℓfm(θ; A, c)] .
