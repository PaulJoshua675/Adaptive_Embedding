Ethics and data This is a retrospective, non-interventional secondary analysis of service records collected under the platform’s Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, which name research among the purposes of processing. The study read production records read-only, kept only highlight documents public under the platform’s own rule (public is the default), and wrote records without account identifiers or reader keys. Page text came from the platform’s stored copies or, for fresh pages, from the public web without credentials; model providers received public page text only, and the training service received page text and highlight positions keyed by ordinals. Record-level files are not released, and every reported result is an aggregate over pages with at least six readers. The operating company is not a research institution and has no institutional review board; no institutional review board reviewed this study, and no formal ethics approval was sought. Both authors are affiliated with the platform’s operator. AI assistance The study design, the extraction, training and analysis code, the statistics, the tables and a first draft of this paper were produced with an AI coding agent (Anthropic’s Claude) under the authors’ direction. OpenAI’s Codex and a separate Claude instance audited the design, the code and the write-ups. The authors take full responsibility for the content. Data and code availability Record-level data cannot be released. The ancillary files contain the pre-registration with its amendments, the results write-up, every aggregate artifact, and the code that builds the prompts, scores the rankings and builds the training targets. Every number in this paper regenerates from those artifacts (make-numbers.py), and check-claims.py checks the paper’s listed qualitative comparisons against them; retraining a model needs the record-level data, so the training and scoring code is there as specification.
