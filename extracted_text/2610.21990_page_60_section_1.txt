Furthermore, the VesperMUSE LLIE configuration demonstrates a distinct advantage in pitch-black environments. While the noise is still a very serious problem and some classes with objects covering a smaller surface get misclassified because of the low-level feature shift caused by low-light envi- ronments, the LLIE configuration manages to recover essential hidden structural details even when surrounded by heavy noise (the Survivor instance on the last column of Figure 4.6). CART Evaluation. The strong quantitative performance on CART [25] is supported by Vesper- MUSE’s visually comparison against methods like SFAF-MA [88], CMNext [85], SGFNet [81], CM-SSM [73]. A first observation would be that the proposed method manages to continuously maintain a prediction for larger objects and not predict other classes inside of them (this behaviour can be noticed for SFAF-MA [88] and CMNext [85]). Moreover, the last column of Figure 4.7 shows another advantage of VesperMUSE on three out of the 4 compared models. In environments where multiple classes intertwine, all methods besides VesperMUSE and CMNext [85] seem to get confused on which is the dominant class in the prob- lematic regions, as the Trees class is often erroneously conflated with Rocky Terrain or Bare Ground. By taking a look at the qualitative results for the VesperMUSE LLIE configuration, it can be easily concluded that the performance comes from predominant classes with very large objects like: Bare Ground, Rocky Terrain, Water, and that correctly segmenting smaller classes for the majority of the samples still remains a challenge. 60
