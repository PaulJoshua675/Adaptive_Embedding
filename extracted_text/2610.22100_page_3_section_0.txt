Method and setup Novelty. For a byte stream 𝑏, let 𝑚𝑡be the length of the longest prefix of 𝑏𝑡∶that also starts within the previous 128 bytes (capped at 16). Novelty is −𝑚𝑡: high when the upcoming bytes do not repeat recent context. Novelty depends on future bytes, so it cannot be used directly; we predict it causally. The novelty table averages −𝑚𝑡over each preceding two-byte context (𝑏𝑡−2, 𝑏𝑡−1) in a 4 MB sample of training text (falling back to one byte, then the global mean, for contexts seen fewer than 20 times). A patch starts at 𝑡when the table value exceeds a threshold set on training text for a target rate. Fitting takes seconds on a CPU; applying it is a lookup per byte. We fit two tables: nov, on GSM8K and MATH training solutions (as in the earlier dependence table), and novc, on 4 MB sampled from the raw pretraining corpus, with no math-specific selection. Baselines. Entropy: BLT’s global threshold on the next-byte entropy of a small byte language model (32 dimensions, one layer) trained on the corpus. Dependence: the boundary-dependence table of [Vera Zúñiga, 2026] (the loss increase when a patch start is removed, per two-byte context). Syntax + entropy: a hand-written rule that starts a patch after every math-syntax marker (=, \boxed{, ####, >>) and fills the rest of the budget by entropy. Models and data. BLT-style models (cross-attention pooling, windowed local decoder, 128-byte context) at three sizes: 1M (width 128, 4 global layers, 131M training bytes), 12M (320, 8 layers, 0.5B bytes) and 50M (576, 12 layers, 1.5B bytes), all trained from scratch on the same 1.9 GB corpus (OpenWebMath [Paster et al., 2024] shards plus GSM8K [Cobbe et al., 2021] and MATH [Hendrycks et al., 2021] training solutions repeated to 10% of bytes; SHA-256 pinned; documents overlapping the evaluation set removed). Each rule’s threshold targets 10% of training bytes (13% for novc), which gives 10.9–12.1% of evaluation bytes (entropy 11.2%, dependence 10.9%, novelty 11.9%, corpus-fitted novelty 11.4%). At 50M all runs use gradient clipping at norm 1.0, which we checked to be neutral for the baselines (entropy without clipping: final answers 59.0% vs 59.9% with it). Evaluation. On held-out GSM8K and MATH solutions: exact match of greedy decoding given the true prefix, with patch boundaries decided online from the bytes seen so far, for 1000 in-line computed results (the value after = in GSM8K’s calculator annotations) and 660 final answers (after ####), plus bits per byte.
