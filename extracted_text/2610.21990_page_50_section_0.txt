To enable a rigorous quantitative comparison with existing datasets, encompassing both real and synthetic low-light imagery, a diverse set of evaluation metrics is employed. This comprehensive analysis ensures a thorough assessment of the effects induced by light deficiency and the fidelity of the proposed synthetic generation process. The proposed metrics are as follows: Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR). The goal is to quantify the level of corruption introduced by noise during the low-light generation process, while remaining invariant to global intensity scaling be- tween the reference and degraded images. This is important because realistic low-light images are fundamentally characterized by photon-limited noise, and matching the real-world SNR distribution is critical for modeling the effect of low-light condition on the overall composition of the image.
