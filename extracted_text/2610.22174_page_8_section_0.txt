RQ1: Overall Effectiveness of Mosaic RQ1 asks whether training in the multi-session setting improves agent performance and whether Mosaic contributes beyond broadcasting. As shown in Table 1, single-session inference has limited effectiveness on BrowseComp. Agentic RL training in the single-session setting yields modest gains, even when combined with test-time scaling in the multi-session setting. This suggests that multi-session inference capabilities do not readily emerge through generalization alone. Among methods trained on multi-session trajectories, Broadcasting improves multi-session inference performance. Mosaic improves it further, outperforming all baselines in both the compact and subagent scenarios. Figure 3 (a) illustrates the transformative gains from multi-session inference, multi-session training, and Mosaic. Figure 4 shows the training dynamics of each method. Mosaic boosts trajectory reward faster than Broadcasting and reaches a higher level. The number of sessions increases over training for both methods, but its growth levels off more clearly with Mosaic. This may be partly due to the more targeted signal provided by its session-aware advantage, which discourages unnecessary increases in session count. Overall, Mosaic achieves better task performance at lower execution cost. As §3.2 applies marginal gain to session-level advantage shaping, we thus compare Mosaic with IGPO, which applies it at the turn level. From Table 1, Mosaic achieves better performance than IGPO.
