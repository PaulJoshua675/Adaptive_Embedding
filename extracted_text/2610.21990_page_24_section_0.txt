3.1 Framework Design and Motivation The framework behind VesperMUSE is based on three main components each having a dedicated role in the focus of this study: finding solutions for improving semantic segmentation under both low-light and well illuminated scenes. VesperMUSE can be run both in low-light or normal light configuration. If the latter is activated, then the framework starts from Stage 3. Stage 1: Low-Light Synthetic Adaptation. The first step is obtaining the synthetic low-light imag- ing which will be processed using the algorithm presented in Section 3.2.1. To link this framework as closely as possible to real world scenarios, the reasoning was physics-informed. Stage 2: Low-Light Image Enhancement. After the low-light maps are processed they serve as inputs to the thermal-guided LLIE module described in Section 3.3, along with the thermal imaging. The LLIE model is trained under a supervised approach trying to predict the original RGB map coming from the two focus datasets: PST900 [8] and CART [25]. The training and test splits are based on the ones used for the semantic segmentation stage. The result of the training process is the best checkpoint in terms of Peak-Signal-to-Noise-Ratio (PSNR), each dataset having its own selected checkpoint. Stage 3: Obtaining the Surface Normal maps. The RGB image (LLIE or not) serves as the in- put to the DepthAnything V2 [30] model, the resulting map being a monocular depth estimation. This representation is then used to produce surface normals by taking the gradients of it and thereby aiming to normalise the structural imagery. Stage 4: Fusion-based Semantic Segmentation. The three inputs corresponding to the same scene: RGB, thermal, and surface normal are then passed to the Fusion Vision Transformer which processes each of them using a dedicated pre-trained backbone and then fuses them in a modality- agnostic manner. After the features have been fused, a decoding process commences processing the output of the framework, the segmented map.
