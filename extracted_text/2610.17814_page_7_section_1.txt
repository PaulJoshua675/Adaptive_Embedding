Open-Web event admission begins with a complete item, not a suc- cessful extraction or retrieved target. Evaluating that item through- out the pipeline reveals a system property hidden by scored-only ranking: processing determines who can receive a decision score. Across six backbones, scored populations differ in both size and class composition. Precision perturbations show that this selec- tion can change even when the backbone, inputs, and aggregate coverage remain fixed. The consequences reach beyond the ranking comparison. Score availability bounds attainable retention, while the selected thresh- old governs decisions among scoreable inputs. Recovery and cali- bration sensitivity show why improving upstream access and im- proving complete-stream operation are separate achievements. Our evaluation connects them through matched ranking populations, a DEV feasibility check, and a decomposition of upstream and deci- sion losses. Overall coverage becomes a diagnostic quantity rather than a standalone quality score. 7
