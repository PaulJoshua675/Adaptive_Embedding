To date, automatic tuning of neural networks has been carried out mainly within the framework of an allocated single metric of the given competition: agent-based systems optimize the entire ML script, NAS approaches search over architectures according to accuracy, and PINN frameworks train networks to minimize the relative error. For tasks whose quality is defined by a multidimensional signal—by the conservation of a set of physical invariants or by additional structural requirements—frameworks of a different class are required. The results reported in this paper demonstrate the robustness and generalization ability of the proposed multi-agent framework, which consists of an analyst, a programmer, and a recorder, together with a sandboxed execution environment and an experiment log serving as external memory, on tasks of different nature without changes to its internal structure. The same unchanged framework was applied to five tasks with different types of information representation: accuracy increased from 79.88 % to 92.83 % on MNIST and from 90.83 % to 96.67 % on digits; in the single-soliton PINN problem, the relative amplitude error decreased by a factor of approximately 2491; in the more complex problem with a Gaussian perturbation and no known analytical solution, the conservation error of the integrals of motion decreased by a factor of approximately 325 in the selected run and by factors of 116 and 103, respectively, based on the mean errors over ten additional trainings of the residual-selected and invariant-selected configurations (Table 11); and in the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky problem, the boundary-value-problem residual decreased by a factor of 9.3 and mass drift by a factor of 14 (Tables 1 and 7). A separate result concerns the interaction between the analyst model and prompt structure. On MNIST, the detailed prompt enabled Perplexity Sonar to exceed 90 % accuracy in all ten complete runs and to reach a maximum of 92.70 %, close to the result of Claude Opus 4.6. For Qwen3-30B-A3B- Instruct-2507, maximum accuracy increased from the baseline level of 79.88 % to 89.16 %, but the target threshold was not reached. Prompt design can therefore substantially improve the performance of a less capable LLM, but only partially compensates for differences between models. Future work includes increasing the complexity of the PINN problem (the interaction of two solitons and other regimes without analytical solutions), jointly tuning several blocks (the decoder and plasticity rules for the SNN), and constructing models that account for the constraints of neuromorphic systems.
