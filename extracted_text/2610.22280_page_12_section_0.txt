LEASH: Uncertainty-Aware Weak-to-Strong Escalation and Oversight for Computer-Use Agents Proof sketch. Under this model, our score equals (up to an additive constant) twice the log-odds that the weak agent fails (Lemma 1 in Appendix A.1), which recalls the classical linear discriminant analysis identity (Fisher, 1936). Escalating the highest-scoring tasks thus escalates the tasks most likely to fail, and a Neyman–Pearson exchange argument (Neyman and Pearson, 1933) shows that no other rule at the same rate catches more failures; (ii) follows by the same argument applied to Δ. Implication. Proposition 1 (i) says that, at any escalation budget, no escalation rule based on the query representation 𝑧captures more of the weak agent’s failures than the initial gate of LEASH. For example, random escalation with budget 𝑝hands off only a fraction 𝑝of these failures, whereas our gate hands off strictly more. Moreover, (ii) shows that our gate spends the strong-agent budget where it pays off most (Jitkrittum et al., 2023), so catching more of the weak agent’s failures directly turns into higher overall task success. Meanwhile, the analysis covers only the initial gate; analyzing the runtime gate would require an optimal-stopping formulation, which we leave to future work.
