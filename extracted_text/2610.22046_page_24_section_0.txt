The squared-loss optimum over functions of the hidden prefix, where σ(h1:t) ⊆σ(st), is the projection E[V ∗ t | h1:t], with the same or greater irreducible error. The same equivalence holds for the discrete tPIT: it takes values in N0 ∪{∞}, so the argument runs through its conditional law. If tPIT = f(st) almost surely for a σ(st)-measurable f, then conditionally on st the variable tPIT equals the constant f(st) almost surely, so its conditional law is the point mass at f(st). Conversely, if that conditional law is the point mass at some c(st), then Pr(tPIT = c(st) | st) = 1 almost surely, and taking expectations gives tPIT = c(st) almost surely, and countability makes the unique-atom selector c measurable. Subtracting V π t does not change conditional variance, because V π t is σ(st)-measurable: Var(mt | st) = Var(V ∗ t | st). The condition 0 < V π t < 1 by itself does not imply that either variance is positive. For example, suppose every future reasoning prefix reachable from st has the same value p ∈(0, 1) until the final answer is sampled. Then a fresh continuation outcome remains random, while V ∗ t = p and mt = 0 are fixed by st. Positive conditional variance instead requires continuation paths of positive probability that produce different future maxima. In that case, the first term in the projection identity is the irreducible mean-squared error of every prefix-state predictor. A hidden-state predictor can have additional error because h1:t may discard information from st. Thus the proposition gives a conditional obstruction to exact recovery.
