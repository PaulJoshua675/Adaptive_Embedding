Errors were primarily limited to proper nouns and rare words, while key topic-related nouns were consistently retained. Furthermore, no translation errors that altered the topic itself were identified. Based on the above, we concluded that the machine translation system used in this study possesses sufficient accuracy for content analysis at the noun level. Furthermore, certain translation and part-of-speech tagging artifacts identified during verification—specifically fragments of contractions such as “don” and “isn,” invalid word fragments such as “gnation,” and for­ mulaic polite expressions such as “please” and “thank” that were incorrectly tagged as nouns—were excluded as stop words. 3.3 Preprocessing and noun extraction All analysis operates on the English translation. Tokenization and POS-tagging used NLTK 3.9.2, restricted to nouns (NN*), lemmatized (WordNet), and lowercased. Retained tokens are either alphabetic tokens of length > 2 or numeric tokens of ≥ 4 dig­ its (keeping year references such as “2024” while dropping tracking-number fragments); a standard English stopword list plus a hand-curated list removes generic non-content nouns (e.g., “thing,” “way”), contraction fragments and translation artifacts, and formu­ laic politeness tokens.
