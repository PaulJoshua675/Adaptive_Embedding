Initial sample sizes, batch sizes, and budgets vary across tasks (Table C2). Experimental protocol. We conduct retrospective sequential evaluation using measured phenotypes from these genetic perturbation datasets. Candidate identities and fixed representations are available at the start. Each method initially receives the phenotypes of 𝑛0 candidates, and the remaining outcomes are revealed as candidates are selected in subsequent rounds. We fix the hit threshold 𝜏before screening to target the top 5% of candidates in the predefined effect direction (Mehrjou et al., 2022). Selection aims to maximize final cumulative hits 𝐶𝑇within the budget, and recall Recall𝑇= 𝐶𝑇/Í 𝑥∈X 𝑧(𝑥) measures discovery coverage. Within each of five seeds (0–4), methods share the candidate pool, initial data, budget, and hit threshold (Appendix C.2). Baselines. We compare diverse baselines with publicly available implementations (Appendix C.3). Pertur- bation discovery baselines include PoH (Rubbi et al., 2026), OAE (Pacchiano et al., 2023), and DiscoBAX (Lyle et al., 2023). General acquisition functions include UCB (Srinivas et al., 2010), Expected Improvement (Jones et al., 1998), and Thompson Sampling (Thompson, 1933). Active learning baselines include BADGE (Ash et al., 2020), CoreSet (Sener and Savarese, 2018), and TopUncertain and KMeans-Embedding from GeneDisco (Mehrjou et al., 2022). Random serves as a control.
