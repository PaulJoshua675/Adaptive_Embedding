We then monotonically accumulate these incre- ments to obtain the cumulative knowledge state: si,t = si,t−1 ∨∆si,t, si,0 = 0K, (3) where ∨denotes element-wise OR. Monotonic ac- cumulation reflects the fact that acquired knowl- edge cannot be unacquired, and absorbs transient verifier noise across nearby steps. The knowl- edge state si,t defines a shared semantic space across episodes: two steps with the same s have, by construction, acquired the same set of task- relevant facts, regardless of which queries were issued, which pages were visited, or how reason- ing was phrased in between. This is the property that surface observations fail to provide and that KnowGPO exploits in the next subsection. 3.3 Knowledge-Grouped Policy Optimization Given the knowledge state representation, Know- GPO assigns credit at two levels. Episode-level advantages align the policy with the final task ob- jective, and step-level advantages, computed by grouping steps under matched knowledge states, attribute credit to individual search decisions. Episode-level advantage. For each query Q, we sample N episodes {τi}N i=1 from the current policy and compute the group-normalized advantage of episode i as AE i = R(τi) −µ σ + ε , (4) where µ and σ are the empirical mean and stan- dard deviation of {R(τi)}N i=1, and ε is a small con- stant for numerical stability. This is the standard outcome-based group advantage of GRPO. Step-level advantage via knowledge-grouped comparison. To attribute credit at the step-level, we exploit the fact that the knowledge state ˜s par- titions the space of intermediate steps into seman- tically equivalent classes. Let U denote the set of distinct knowledge states observed across all N episodes for query Q. For each ˜s ∈U, we form a knowledge group G(˜s) = {(i, t) | si,t = ˜s, ai,t̸ = answer} (5) containing every non-terminal step that starts from state ˜s. We then define the step-level reward as the incremental knowledge gain, ri,t = ∥si,t∥1 −∥si,t−1∥1, (6) which counts the number of new knowledge con- ditions satisfied by step (i, t). The step-level ad- vantage is the group-normalized incremental gain within G(˜s): AS i,t = ri,t −µ˜s σ˜s + ε , (7) where µ˜s and σ˜s are computed over G(˜s).
