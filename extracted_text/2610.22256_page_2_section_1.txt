2.1 ReLU activations and their gradients We review backpropagation through a ReLU2 activation followed by a linear projection; the ReLU derivation is given in Appendix C.1. The input is Z ∈RM×N and the weight is W ∈RD×N, where M is the number of activation rows (for example, tokens flattened across a batch), and N and D are the input and output feature dimensions, respectively. Let ReLU(z) = max(0, z) act elementwise, G = ∂L/∂Y ∈RM×D be the upstream gradient, and ⊙denote elementwise multiplication. For the forward pass H = ReLU(Z), ˜H = ReLU2(Z) = H2, Y = ˜HW ⊤, (1) the gradients are ∂L ∂W = G⊤˜H, ∂L ∂˜H = GW, ∂L ∂Z = 2 ∂L ∂˜H ⊙I[Z > 0] ⊙Z, (2) The activation-dependent information required for these gradients is contained in the positive values of Z and their positions. The same principle applies to differentiable pointwise functions of ReLU(Z), such as ReLU3(Z) or sin(ReLU(Z)), although these are less popular. 2.2 Entropy of sparse ReLU activations We wish to losslessly compress the activations H. Let L = MN be the number of logical entries, excluding padding, and let s = ∥H∥0 denote the number of nonzero values. We denote the zero fraction, or activation sparsity, by ρ = 1 −s/L.
