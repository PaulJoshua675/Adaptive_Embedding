An Audit Agent checks each interviewee turn against the user’s own evidence, namely the statis- tical profile and the historical ReDial conversation, for three types of issues. Fabrication covers spe- cific claims that are neither stated in nor plausibly inferable from this evidence, such as invented view- ing experiences or personal anecdotes. Caricature covers stereotyped persona performances and in- vented rationalizations: plausible-sounding justi- fications for preferences that the profile does not attest, or recognizable persona archetypes it does not support. History contradiction covers state- ments that conflict with the user’s liked and disliked movies or with their earlier ReDial conversation. In addition, deterministic structural checks verify the number of turns, the alternation of speaker roles, and the absence of empty turns. The auditor assigns each flag a severity (low, medium, or high), and a deterministic rule derives the dialogue-level decision. Any structural error or ≥3 high-severity flags trigger REGENERATE (up to two retries, with a corrective nudge appended to the interviewee prompt). At least one high-severity or ≥3 medium-severity flags trigger PARTIAL: the interview is retained, but its high- and medium- severity turns are excluded from enrichment. All other interviews are accepted (ACCEPT). For ex- ample, in one interview, the interviewee claimed to have enjoyed Blue Streak, although the user’s Re- Dial conversation indicated that they had not seen it. In another, the interviewee dismissed the action in Justice League as hard to care about, although the film is on the user’s liked list. The auditor flagged both turns, so they could be excluded before profile enrichment. Full auditor prompts, flag definitions, and representative examples are in Appendix B. 3.4 Profile Enrichment Stage 5 (Profile Enrichment). From audited interviews, we extract six implicit user attributes grounded in prior user-modeling lit- erature: aspect-level preferences (Radlinski et al., 2019), pickiness (Yoon et al., 2024), decision- making style (Scott and Bruce, 1995; Hayati et al., 2020), viewing motivation (Oliver and Raney, 2011), novelty preference (Tang and Liao, 2022), and domain expertise (Kostric et al., 2025).
