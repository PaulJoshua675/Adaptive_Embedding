RL baselines and key WIRL design choices. We compare WIRL with trajectory-level GRPO and PPO- style optimization under the same MLE environment and MLE-SFT initialization. GRPO uses group- relative advantages, whereas PPO-style optimization uses a clipped objective weighted by terminal trajectory rewards without an additional critic. We also evaluate WIRL variants that remove the trend reward or policy correction separately. As shown in Table 4, GRPO and PPO-style optimization achieve 56.1% and 60.6%, respectively, both below the 62.1% achieved by MLE-SFT, supporting WIRL’s overall design of learning from execution-window feedback. In contrast, the two WIRL variants reach 66.7% and 65.2%, respectively, while full WIRL further improves the medal rate to 72.7%, indicating that both the local trend reward and policy correction contribute to the final improvement. Further analysis in Appendix D.1 shows that asynchronous execution-window collection reduces the learner waiting fraction compared with trajectory-based GRPO. 5.4. To examine the long-horizon optimization behavior of MLExpert, we select MLSP 2013 Birds as a representative case. Figure 3(a) traces the optimization process from audio features and LightGBM through PANNs representations to AST fine-tuning with attention pooling and an MLP head, followed by training-schedule adjustments and snapshot averaging. As shown in Figure 3(b), MLExpert and MLExpert-SFT start with solutions of similar quality, but MLExpert improves ROC-AUC from 0.58 to 0.964, compared with 0.60 to 0.88 for MLExpert-SFT. This case shows how MLExpert continues to improve solutions through sustained experimentation and execution feedback. 5.5. Generalization to Other Optimization Tasks Agent scaffold optimization. Following ShinkaEvolve [24], we optimize a Python scaffold for a fixed GPT-4.1-nano solver on AIME 2025 [25] and evaluate it on AIME problems from years not used for optimization. Table 5 shows that the scaffold optimized by MLExpert achieves 43.7% held-out accuracy, compared with 23.3% for the scaffold optimized by the base model. These results show that post-training improves the ability to design effective agent workflows, with optimization gains extending to unseen problems.
