Lakshmi Rajendram Bashyam and Ralf Krestel 3.3 Retrieve-and-rerank Training an ML model for an XMLC task can be challenging. Fine- tuning the model on the entire label set can work well for head labels (labels that are frequently observed in the training dataset). But it can negatively impact tail labels (labels that are hardly ob- served in the train dataset) since they only contribute a very weak signal for the model. One of the ways to effectively capture tail labels is to design a system that does not depend on the label fre- quency. A retrieve-and-rerank pipeline is one of the most effective ways to handle XMLC tasks without under performing on the tail labels. The architecture of our system is divided into two stages. Stage-1 is the candidate generation stage of our pipeline. For a document (represented by the metadata elements title and abstract), the ob- jective of Stage-1 is to select a small set from thousands of sub- jects present in the thesaurus, which the downstream module then reranks. This is a recall-maximizing stage that does not blow up the candidate set size excessively. Formally, for each document we seek candidates𝐶such that the recall |𝐶∩𝐺|/|𝐺| (with 𝐺the gold labels) is as high as possible under a budget |𝐶| ≈a few hundred. The gold labels that are not in the candidate list cannot be recovered later in the pipeline; hence, this creates an upper ceiling for the recall. The precision is left to be improved in the second stage. Rather than scaling up a single retriever, we combine four com- plementary sources. A dense multi-vector BGE-M3[15][5] retriever (dense, sparse, and ColBERT[13] max-similarity) supplies semantic matches. A trained subject category classifier restricts attention to the relevant thesaurus branches. An NER-based mapper links domain entities extracted from the text to descriptors. Finally, a surface exact-match over labels and alternative labels safeguards rare tail terms, which often appear verbatim.
