Reinforcement learning (RL) has become an effective paradigm for specializing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) under capability-specific objectives (Liu et al., 2026b; Xia et al., 2025; Peng et al., 2026). By optimizing targeted reward signals, capability-specific RL enables models to focus on the visual understanding and reasoning behaviors required by a particular capability, thereby developing specialized expertise (Xiong et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026; Peng et al., 2025). To broaden capa- bility coverage, recent efforts have explored jointly training a shared policy across heterogeneous capabilities (Ma et al., 2025; Sarch et al., 2026). However, heterogeneous objectives can induce competing gradient signals over shared parameters, causing updates beneficial to one capability to distract the optimization direction of others (Yu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021). As a result, joint opti- mization may restrict specialized expertise, highlighting the difficulty of simultaneously developing and consolidating heterogeneous capabilities within a unified policy. Rather than jointly optimizing a shared policy, knowledge distillation provides an alternative route for consolidating specialized capabilities into a unified model. On-policy distillation aligns teacher supervision with student-generated trajectories, effectively mitigating distribution shift (Agarwal et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2024). Recent context-conditioned variants further enrich teacher supervision with privileged information or auxiliary context (Zhao et al., 2026; Ye et al., 2026; Yuan et al., 2026). For capability integration, Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation (MOPD) extends this paradigm to multiple independently optimized experts, distilling their complementary capabilities into a shared student along student-generated rollouts (Ma et al., 2026; Xiao et al., 2026). Nevertheless, these approaches primarily transfer expert knowledge through predictive distributions, leaving reusable reasoning patterns contained in expert trajectories largely implicit and potentially propagating noisy or over-specialized expert behaviors to the student. To externalize such implicit trajectory-level knowledge, recent studies have explored abstracting raw execution experiences into explicit skills that capture reusable solution patterns (Xin et al., 2026). Trace2Skill consolidates execution trajectories into transferable skills through hierarchical consolidation (Ni et al., 2026), while SkillRL and Skill1 construct evolving skill libraries to support skill retrieval and policy improvement (Xia et al., 2026; Shi et al., 2026).
