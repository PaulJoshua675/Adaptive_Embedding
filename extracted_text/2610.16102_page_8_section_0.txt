3.1 From–to as directed binding Polanyi’s relation is asymmetric: the “proximal” term is attended from, the “distal” term to (Polanyi 1966, 10), and reversing them changes what is known. Binding in VaCoAl has the same asymmetry, because the shift σ marks which argument is the filler; Bind(R, F) and Bind(F, R) are different vectors (2026c). The prosopographic graph carries the asymmetry at the level of data. Its edges run from mentor to student, and the 369 bidirectional assertions found among roughly 470,000 records had to be removed before the structure could be traversed as a DAG (2026g). Read through Polanyi, that purification step makes the direction of transmission consistent before anything is inferred from it. 3.2 Subsidiary and focal as two reads of one vector A composite H = P i Bind(Ri, Fi) carries every filler at once, and none of them is singled out until a query asks for it. Unbinding H against a role Rk brings Fk forward while the other terms recede into noise near the 0.5 similarity baseline. The programme already uses both kinds of read on the same vectors. The “calculus degree” of a scholar is a holistic read: the Hamming similarity of a whole composite to a bundled reference concept. Extracting a scholar’s FIELD component is an analytic read: one role is unbound and the rest is ignored (2026a; 2026d). Polanyi’s two kinds of awareness therefore become two kinds of read on one representation. The holistic read answers “how much is this like that?” without naming parts; the analytic read names one part and loses the whole. Polanyi observed that one cannot attend focally to a particular while keeping it in its subsidiary role. In the algebra, the two reads are different operations with different outputs, and the integrated similarity is not recoverable from any single unbound filler. 3.3 The Identikit as role–filler composition Polanyi used the police Identikit to show that communicating a face still depends on tacit matching: the witness can assemble a likeness from drawn features only because she can match each feature against the remembered face, and she cannot say how she does that (Polanyi 1966). Hyperdimensional computing gives the composition an exact form. A face record is a bundle of bound pairs, P i Bind(rolei, featurei), and each feature can be recovered by unbinding its role. The matching, in contrast, is a similarity search in which stored record and probe vote block by block. CASRAM’s face-recognition system divides the work between two machines (Shuhari System 2026). An edge PC with a GPU extracts a feature embedding from each face; an FPGA module carrying CASRAM, connected to it over PCIe, performs the 1:N match; and the interface overlays each identified face with an ID and a confidence. We do not claim that this product binds faces as role–filler pairs. What the product shows is that the tacit–explicit boundary can be moved. For Polanyi’s witness, the composition could be told and the matching could not. In this system it is the other way round. The matching is done by a memory whose decisions are majority votes with a readable confidence (2026a), while the encoding that produced the embedding is the part no one can state. The boundary has not disappeared; it has moved, and it now runs along a physical link between two pieces of hardware. That is the thesis of §4 in miniature: where the boundary falls depends on the substrate and its operations, not on the knowledge itself. 3.4 Knowing how without telling how The name VaCoAl, Vague Coincident Algorithm, already states the model of recognition: an answer is what many partial, individually unreliable votes coincide on (2026a).
