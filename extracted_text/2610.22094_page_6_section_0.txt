Table 1 compares SCCL-DT with published zero-shot chest X-ray classification methods across five benchmarks. The final distilled student achieves the highest mean AUC of 0.850, exceeding RadZero (0.837) and CARZero (0.836) by 0.013 and 0.014, respectively. Under this unified classification evaluation protocol, SCCL- DT achieves the highest AUC on Open-I, ChestX-ray14, and ChestXDet10, while ranking second on PadChest and CheXpert; on CheXpert, it trails the best result by only 0.001. The consistent performance across these heteroge- neous datasets provides evidence of cross-dataset robust- ness, while inference retains a single model. CARZero and RadZero provide the closest radiology- specific comparisons. SCCL-DT outperforms each on four of the five datasets: relative to CARZero, the gains are +0.015 on Open-I, +0.017 on PadChest, +0.024 on ChestX-ray14, and +0.015 on ChestXDet10, whereas the two methods are nearly tied on CheXpert (0.922 vs. 0.923). Relative to RadZero, SCCL-DT performs better on all datasets except PadChest and improves the mean AUC by 0.013. Accordingly, Table 1 provides a same-test-set, public-checkpoint classification comparison under a com- mon dataset and label scope. Ablation and Diagnostic Studies We report controlled PadChest studies under a unified evaluation protocol across AUC, MCC, F1, and accu- racy (ACC). The OR/AND/KD, threshold, and similarity- cue studies characterize prespecified SCCL configurations. Two-tier, count-matched Random, and Soft weighting in- stead serve as diagnostic controls rather than SCCL com- ponents: they examine whether a single compromise rule can replace the separate inclusive and consensus teachers, whether gains depend on structured candidate selection be- yond mask quantity alone, and whether hard exclusion is preferable to soft attenuation, respectively. These results are not used for hyperparameter tuning or default-configuration selection. The five-dataset mean AUC provides a cross- dataset consistency check. We retain ACC as a critical classification metric; because it remains near 0.991 across configurations, most between-configuration differences ap- pear in AUC, MCC, and F1.
