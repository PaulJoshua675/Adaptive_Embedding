Together, these methods remain tied to specific tasks or relations, making them insufficient for general audio-video consistency evaluation under the complexity of modern joint generation. 3 METHOD 3.1 EVALUATION OVERVIEW The complexities exposed by modern joint generation introduce three requirements for evaluation. Multi-event composition calls for event disentanglement, so that individual and overlapping events can be examined separately. Multi-layer audio mixture requires the evaluator to distinguish the roles of different audio events, as different audio sources can have different relations to the video. Asymmetric and diverse cross-modal relations further require establishing and verifying event- dependent cross-modal relations for individual events. Meeting these requirements requires an evaluator capable of jointly understanding audio-visual con- tent and reasoning over different events and their relations. MLLMs with audio-video understanding capabilities can jointly perform these tasks within a unified reasoning process, making them a suit- able foundation for audio-video consistency evaluation. Moreover, for more general and accurate evaluation, we argue that the judgments of the MLLM should be organized into an event-centric metric with three coarse-to-fine levels. Global narrative harmony first checks whether the audio and video are coherent across the full clip. Event-level temporal synchronization and fine-grained attribute alignment then move to the event level. They respectively assess the temporal alignment of each event and the consistency of audio-visual attributes within that event. Through event-level evaluation and detailed, independent verification of each event’s cross-modal relations, the metric addresses the higher evaluation demands above. With this evaluation design in place, Section 3.2 defines the event-centric protocol and three-level metric, Section 3.3 constructs the corresponding training supervision, and Section 3.4 learns AVC- Reward through SFT and GRPO. 3.2 EVENT-CENTRIC EVALUATION PROTOCOL Following Section 3.1, we define three-level consistency scores. Global narrative harmony (Smac) measures agreement in narrative content, entities, environment, and affective tone. Event-level temporal synchronization (Stemp) measures event presence and the alignment of onset, offset, and duration under the appropriate cross-modal relations. Fine-grained attribute alignment (Sattr) measures attribute compatibility within verified relations, including sound–material agreement, lip- shape agreement with speech, and plausible responses to off-screen sounds. All three scores lie in [0, 1], with higher values indicating greater consistency. To compute these scores, we guide the MLLM through a five-stage reasoning protocol that specifies the evidence and judgments required at each stage.
