METHOD We propose HEM-Med, a hierarchical agentic memory framework for multi-center MR. As shown in Fig. 2, it com- prises two components: (1) consensus–residual memory dis- entanglement, which separates historical medication–context experience into cross-hospital consensus and hospital-specific residual memories; and (2) safety-aware recommendation, which combines dual-level retrieval, experience-aware rerank- ing, DDI-aware set decoding, and safety review to generate locally feasible and clinically safe medication sets. A. Patient-Level Attribution Memory Raw ICU EHRs lack explicit medication links to diagnoses, procedures, or laboratory abnormalities. HEM-Med builds auditable L3 patient-level memory via constrained DeepSeek- assisted attribution. For each training ICU stay si and each observed medication mj ∈Mi, we construct a stay-medication query: qij = (hi, mj, Di, Pi, Oi) , (6) where hi denotes the hospital identifier, and Di, Pi, and Oi denote the observed diagnoses, procedures, and abnormal laboratory signals of the current stay, respectively. DeepSeek-v4-flash selects at most one observed context anchor for constrained attribution, without adding external indications or clinical facts: aij ∈{diag(d) | d ∈Di} ∪{proc(p) | p ∈Pi} ∪{lab(o) | o ∈Oi} ∪{∅}. (7) Insufficient context yields ∅. Attribution is a conservative empirical hypothesis, not a verified prescribing intention, indication, or causal treatment relation. For each validated stay-medication pair, the L3 record is defined as: e(L3) ij = (si, hi, mj, aij, rij, Sij) , (8) where rij denotes the conservative attribution hypothesis, and Sij stores training-set statistical support for subsequent aggregation. Training-only support statistics distinguish cross-hospital experience from hospital-specific residual patterns in an- chor–medication associations. All validated L3 training records are collected as: K(L3) = n e(L3) ij | si ∈Strain, mj ∈Mi o .
