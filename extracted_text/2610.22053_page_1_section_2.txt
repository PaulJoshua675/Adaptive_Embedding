Large Language Model usage has surged dra- matically, with nearly 50% of U.S. adults re- porting they have used LLMs for daily tasks at least once (Rainie, 2025). These systems often handle natural language prompts that include personal names. Since names can encode de- mographic cues such as age, gender, and race, even minor model biases may lead to discrim- inatory outcomes. Such biases may perpet- uate stereotypes, unfairly advantage certain groups, or generate harmful content. Identi- fying and measuring these biases is therefore essential: it enables more rigorous model eval- uation, supports the development of fairer sys- tems, and advances transparency in AI safety. Therefore, it remains integral to measure the biases LLMs can hold, and in this work, we address four specific biases: age, gender, pop- ularity, and race. Our experiments prompt lo- cal LLMs with fill-in-the-blank sentences given two names that may reveal a specific bias, such as age, gender, or race. We measure • Age bias and popularity bias by collecting the top 500 most popular names, and how many times they were assigned, from the 1980s and 2010s • Gender bias by categorizing the most pop- ular names from 1980s and 2010s as fe- male and male • Racial bias by using a racial classification dataset provided by Rosenman et al.
