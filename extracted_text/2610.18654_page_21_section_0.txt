The remote budget 𝐾includes eight sink tokens; a 1024-token local window is retained separately. Figure 12 shows rapid recovery toward dense performance as the remote budget increases on both Hybrid KSA and C-KSA with 𝑘𝑚= 128. On C-KSA, block-level selection reaches 64.71 at 𝐾= 512. At 𝐾= 4096, the selected remote tokens and the 1024-token local window together cover only about 3.9% of a 128K context, yet retain 82.83% of decode attention mass. The corresponding RULER score is 66.24, compared with 66.31 for dense attention. This concentration of attention on a small subset of tokens helps explain why sparse attention can preserve retrieval performance when relevant tokens are selected. At the same budget, Hybrid KSA retains 81.71% of decode attention mass. Local context and sinks alone already retain substantial attention mass but yield poor retrieval performance, highlighting the importance of selecting relevant remote content. Table 10 extends the comparison to Hybrid KSA and four C-KSA+Full backbones at 𝐾= 4096, reporting mean RULER performance across all six context lengths and the 128K score. Both selection methods remain within 0.51 percentage points of the dense references in mean RULER score across all five backbones. Accurate selection therefore recovers near-dense RULER performance without backbone updates in these settings, including on models already adapted to C-KSA. Full-parameter sparse continuation also showed no clear RULER improvement under the training recipe and budget we tested. Together with the recovery observed after module-only training in Section 3.3, these results support focusing sparse adaptation on the aggregation module and Indexer rather than requiring backbone updates. 21
