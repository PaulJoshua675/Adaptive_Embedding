B.3.3 Remasking is more frequent than token editing We distinguish a remasking event from a completed token edit. A remasking episode starts when a visible token becomes masked and ends when that position next becomes visible; it is an edit only if the returning token differs from the original. Table 18 reports remasking under four denominators, preventing trajectory-level incidence from being confused with a per-position rate. Every observed trajectory contains at least one remask, but the fraction of visible position-opportunities that are remasked is approximately 18% for the full-canvas policy and 31–38% for the block policy. Most remasking episodes do not change token identity. Across the measured budgets, 91.52–95.17% of full-canvas episodes and 92.91–98.16% of blockwise episodes return the original token. Every recorded episode resolves by the end of decoding, and the mean delay before re-reveal is 2.25–3.13 calls for full-canvas decoding and 1.20–1.41 calls for blockwise decoding (Table 19). Thus, remasking often temporarily withdraws context rather than replacing its content. Together with the remasking ablation, these observations motivate the hypothesis that hiding unreliable context can help other positions even when the hidden token ultimately returns unchanged. Another potential explanation is simply due to sampling noise, as each re-masking decision is sampled from a Bernoulli distribution informed by the policy network.
