A.7 CROSS-CAPABILITY GRADIENT INTERFERENCE IN MIX-RL We further investigate cross-capability interference by comparing the parameter-update alignment of Mix-RL against that of the independently trained capability experts {πθc}C c=1. Specifically, we evaluate capability-specific optimization directions uc(·) estimated directly from the RL rewards rc. For Mix-RL, let θmix,t denote the shared policy parameters at training checkpoint t, and ∆θmix,t = θmix,t+1 −θmix,t denote the realized parameter displacement between adjacent checkpoints. At each checkpoint t, we estimate the optimization directions for all C capabilities under the shared parameters θmix,t. The alignment between the shared update and each capability-specific direction is defined as Mc(t) = ⟨∆θmix,t, uc(θmix,t)⟩ ∥∆θmix,t∥2∥uc(θmix,t)∥2 . (21) Similarly, during the independent RL training of each capability expert πθc, let θc,t denote its in- termediate parameters at checkpoint t. We define the corresponding parameter displacement as ∆θc,t = θc,t+1 −θc,t and measure its alignment with its respective optimization direction: Sc(t) = ⟨∆θc,t, uc(θc,t)⟩ ∥∆θc,t∥2∥uc(θc,t)∥2 . (22) Here, positive alignment indicates that the realized update follows the corresponding capability- specific optimization direction, whereas negative alignment reflects an opposing update. To fur- ther characterize cross-capability interference, we compute pairwise cosine similarities among the capability-specific directions {uc(θmix,t)}C c=1 at each Mix-RL checkpoint, where negative values indicate conflicting local optimization directions. As shown in Figure 4a, the shared Mix-RL update exhibits divergent alignment across capabilities, advancing along the optimization directions of some capabilities while opposing those of others. The recurring negative pairwise similarities further confirm cross-capability gradient interference. In contrast, Figure 4b shows that independently trained capability experts maintain a positively aligned trajectory on average, despite occasional local fluctuations. Together, these observations suggest that shared-policy optimization introduces competing capability-specific update directions, potentially hindering specialized capability acquisition. 20
