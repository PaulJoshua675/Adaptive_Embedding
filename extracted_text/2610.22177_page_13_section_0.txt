Table 2 reports the reconstruction performance of each variant under the 8-view setting. Empirically, injecting full optical flow yields performance comparable to the baseline, with slight degradation observed in some metrics. In contrast, injecting residual flow consistently achieves the best performance across both backbones. A possible explanation is that full optical flow contains both camera- induced rigid motion and dynamic motion. But with the random set of views sampling for 3D vision models’ input, there is no guarantee of the sequence of the view nor the existance of the next view whose camera relate to the source view in the way expressed by rigid motion. In contrast, By explicitly removing the rigid component, residual flow isolates motion signals that deviate from the global motion due to the camera. These signals often correspond to independently moving objects or geometrically challenging regions, making them more readily helpful for 3D reconstruction. 4.4.2 Layer-wise Injection Analysis To determine the optimal depth for incorporating motion and orientation priors, we evaluate feature injection at three distinct stages of the reconstruc- tion backbone: Early (input image patch features), Middle (intermediate aggregator layers), and Late (aggregator output).
