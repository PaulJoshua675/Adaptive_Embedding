Role-aware attributes. Each node vi is described by five discrete features: operation label τ(vi), indegree deg−(vi), outdegree deg+(vi), forward depth ℓfwd(vi), and reverse depth ℓrev(vi). The forward depth is the maximum length of a directed path from an input node to vi, and the reverse depth is the maximum length of a directed path from vi to an output node. The operation label captures computational semantics, indegree and outdegree characterize local merging and branching roles, and (ℓfwd, ℓrev) locates the node along the directed input-to-output flow. This depth pair distinguishes nodes with the same forward depth but different remaining distances to the output side. These features make local in/out roles and input-to-output positions available before attention-based interaction. Existing predictors usually lack an explicit global direction-aware spectral prior [13–16, 19–21, 23–25], while standard Laplacian encodings capture symmetric connectivity but not the asymmetry between u →v and v →u. We therefore derive P from the Magnetic Laplacian [31, 32], whose complex phase records edge orientation. This supplies the global spectral stage of encoder-wide directional consistency. Construction. Given the directed adjacency matrix A, define the symmetrized support (As)u,v = 1 2(Au,v + Av,u), the orientation phase (Θ(q))u,v = 2πq(Au,v −Av,u), and the degree (Ds)u,u = Pn v=1(As)u,v. The Magnetic Laplacian is L(q) mag = Ds −As ⊙exp  iΘ(q) . (2) Since As is symmetric and Θ(q) is antisymmetric, L(q) mag is Hermitian and admits L(q) mag = UΛU∗, with U containing complex eigenvectors and Λ containing real eigenvalues. Two-channel spectral features. We select k low-frequency eigenvectors and retain their real and imaginary parts: Pmag = [γ1, . . . , γk] ∈Cn×k, and for each node u and spectral component r, Eu,r = [Re(Pmag(u, r))∥Im(Pmag(u, r))], giving E ∈Rn×k×2. The real and imaginary channels provide a real-valued representation of the complex spectral signal, preserving orientation-dependent phase information. Spectral aggregation. For each node, we embed the two-channel spectral components and aggregate its k spectral modes with self-attention over the spectral dimension, producing pu ∈Rd. Stacking all node-wise spectral embeddings gives P = [p1, . . . , pn]⊤∈Rn×d. Details of the spectral-mode aggregation are provided in Appendix A.7. The initial encoder states are then obtained by adding this global direction-aware spectral encoding to the node initialization: H(0) = X + P. (3) 4.3 Direction-Dual Structural Attention Standard self-attention [14, 33] evaluates pairwise interactions from node representations. The Magnetic spectral encoding in Sec. 4.2 supplies a node-level global direction-aware spectral prior. Direction-dual structural attention complements this representation with explicit pairwise relation sup- ports, providing encoder-wide directional consistency. It organizes pairwise interactions through six directional relation channels. Each channel specifies a distinct structural context for query–key interac- tions, covering local dependencies, directed reachability, or shared-successor and shared-predecessor structures.
