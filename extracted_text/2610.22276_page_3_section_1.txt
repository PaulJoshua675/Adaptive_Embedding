Jev-Omni extends this interface to multimodal inputs (akhi- laaa3, 2026), while KEV implements candidate scoring with learned query and key projections (Palmer, 2026). Yu and Yao (2026) and hr98w (2026) study direct decisions and shared-context execution; FBddcz and EmbodiedJev contributors (2026) applies decision interfaces to skill and action selection. Concurrent work, VideoJev, uses a shared candidate scorer for temporal grounding and video question answering, with cached and joint inference for queries about the same video (Zhu et al., 2026a). Visual Jev reads candidate- token probabilities from the existing language-model head and finds no consistent accuracy advantage from an added typed head in its matched control. MJev-Spatial instead scores contextual candidate content with the backbone’s final-layer query and key projections and trains all output types with the same candidate- classification objective. Our matched readout experiments show competitive quality and lower training cost for this design, with task-dependent tradeoffs (Section 4.3). The earlier Molmo2-ER-based MJev-Spatial prototype used LoRA and an added decision head (Fang et al., 2026; Hu et al., 2022). The present model uses Qwen3.5-4B and trains all retained language parameters. Backbone efficiency is also studied through pruning and quantization, joint low-rank and sparsity optimization, and adaptive execution (Zhou et al., 2026a,b, 2025b,c). Class-aware adaptation provides a complementary route to efficient vision-language classification. AWT enriches class descriptions and combines augmented views through entropy-based weighting and optimal transport (Zhu et al., 2024a). Self-TPT uses self-supervised prompt adaptation for new classes to avoid costly per-image optimization at inference (Zhu et al., 2026b). Spatial understanding with VLMs. Broader multimodal research covers visual reasoning (Qin et al., 2025), retrieval (Qin et al., 2024), and multimodal generation (Zhang et al., 2024; Qin et al., 2026; Zhong et al., 2025). Video understanding further requires temporal localization (Zhu et al., 2024b, 2026c) and efficient processing of long or streaming observations (Li et al., 2026; Zeng et al., 2026a). SpatialVLM and SpatialRGPT learn spatial relations and numerical answers from spatial supervision (Chen et al., 2024; Cheng et al., 2024), while Molmo, RoboPoint, and RoboRefer support pointing through coordinate text (Deitke et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2025a). Alternative interfaces can ground predictions more directly. MolmoPoint selects visual patches and refines locations through a sequence of specialized grounding tokens (Clark et al., 2026). MJev-Spatial uses one candidate-classification mechanism for options, numerical values, and points, with task-specific candidates supplying the output structure. Its multi-point extension expands conditional classification paths rather than generating a variable-length coordinate string. Representing continuous predictions with discrete classes is established in computer vision. DORN formulates monocular depth estimation as ordinal regression over logarithmically spaced depth intervals (Fu et al., 2018). Our length representation also uses logarithmically spaced coarse intervals, followed by linear refinement, but its intervals are candidate descriptions scored by a shared language backbone. Counts and angles retain their own ranges and discretization schemes. Visual search methods such as V∗refine image regions to recover details needed for answering (Wu and Xie, 2024). Video-o3 similarly acquires evidence iteratively in long videos, interleaving clue seeking with inspection of relevant segments (Zeng et al., 2026b).
