With two channels tied under a noisy input, EASA switched channels twice in 1,000 steps, compared to 500 switches in a simple maximum selection. This persistence was not enough to compensate for the interference of reflexes in a social task. 4.6 Evolved priorities of the basal ganglia Table 4 compares the evolved selection parameters in the three runs with the manually set values. Three changes appeared in all three runs. The weight of obstacle detection on avoidance decreased from 0.9 to between 0.53 and 0.79, weakening the reflex. Inhibition of the net channel by obstacles decreased from -0.3 to between -0.20 and 0.09. The hunger drive on exploration decreased from 0.6 to between -0.19 and 0.48. Other changes were not replicated. The coupling between net bidding and avoidance was positive in two runs and negative in the third, and dopamine shifted substantially in only one run. Overall, evolution reduced the weight of innate factors in all three runs. Noise from the motors and sensors had minimal effects, less than 15% across all runs. The large arena significantly impacted the strategies. The referential signal run lost only 5%, the beacon run lost 23%, and the non-communicating run lost 36%. The EASA variants lost between 23% and 45%. The compass network showed no reduction in fitness.
