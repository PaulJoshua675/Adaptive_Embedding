D Read-site selection The confidence, value, combined and pointer probes read hidden states at the read site S⋆= {23, 24}. We select the read site by the detection AUROC of a probe at each layer, using the final label Y only. The selection measure is the detection AUROC in the middle step bin, computed within each difficulty group and then averaged, with a probe of the confidence probe’s architecture trained on the hidden states of each layer. The middle bin reduces both the difficulty prior present in early steps and the answer commitment present in the final step. Figure 5A also shows the same measure for transcoder activations, obtained by applying the published per-layer transcoders to the residual stream before the layer normalization that precedes the MLP, with their activations projected to the width of the hidden states by a fixed random matrix. The transcoders were trained on the output of that normalization, so the transcoder curves do not describe the transcoders at their training site. No result in this paper uses the transcoder curves. We also record predictive V-information [Xu et al., 2020, Ethayarajh et al., 2022]; predictive V-information peaks at layer 28 at 4B and at layer 18 at 8B and was not used for the selection. We record a usable-information measure because raw mutual information cannot order deterministic layers [Pimentel et al., 2020, Xu et al., 2020]. Layer selection. The detection AUROC forms a broad upper-middle-layer plateau and peaks at layer 23 for both model scales, at 0.7264 [0.7079, 0.7445] for 4B and 0.7124 [0.6933, 0.7295] for 8B (Figure 5A); the interval at the peak overlaps those of most neighbouring layers. The read site {23, 24} is not distinguishable from layer 23 alone because the four bootstrap intervals, the two at layer 23 above and the two read-site intervals in Table 10, overlap. This selection uses one measurement per scale with no seed spread, and the two scales, selected separately, peak at the same layer. The upper-middle location is consistent with prior truth and correctness probes [Azaria and Mitchell, 2023, Marks and Tegmark, 2024, Burns et al., 2023]. Prior work also reports that the best probe layer tends to be deeper in larger models [O’Neill et al., 2025], so we repeat the selection at each scale. Conditional probing measures only the information added beyond a baseline such as the first layer [Hewitt et al., 2021], and adjacent residual-stream layers are expected to be redundant. The layer-count sweep tests 2, 3, 5 and 8 adjacent layers centred on the peak.
