This work demonstrates that modifying the Branch network input handling in PI-DeepONets can substan- tially reduce computational cost without sacrificing predictive accuracy. While the theoretical analysis cap- tures the qualitative scaling behaviour, the empirical results quantify substantial practical gains, particularly in higher-dimensional settings. A key outcome is that the ATenH architecture enables problems that are infeasible under the Vanilla for- mulation. In particular, while the Vanilla architecture failed on the 3D linear advection problem due to memory limitations, the ATenH architecture successfully completed training with a mean predictive error of 5.44%. This highlights the fact that implementation-level considerations can determine whether a problem is tractable in practice. 16
