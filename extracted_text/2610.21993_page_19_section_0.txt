Geographic Data Overlap Analysis SSL4EO-S12 v1.1 and the Copernicus-Bench evaluation sets both draw from the Sentinel-1 archive and share similar global geographic sampling strategies, raising the possibility of geo- graphic overlap between pretraining and evaluation data. To assess whether our reported gains are inflated by such overlap, we identify pretraining tiles with geographic proximity to test set samples using task-specific thresholds derived from the physical patch dimensions of each dataset pair, given a Sentinel-1 GSD of 10 m/pixel: 1,640 m for EuroSAT-S1, 1,920 m for BigEarthNet- S1, and 2,440 m for Flood-S1. DFC2020-S1 is not assessed due to the absence of coordinate metadata in the dataset release. Under these thresholds, 10.9% of EuroSAT-S1 test samples, 4.3% of BigEarthNet-S1 test samples, and 10.1% of Flood-S1 test samples have at least one geograph- ically proximate pretraining tile; from the pretraining side, only 1,406 of 243,968 SSL4EO-S12 v1.1 tiles (0.58%) are implicated across all three assessable tasks combined. To directly test whether this overlap inflates our reported gains, we re-pretrain SparK-SAR from scratch on a de-collided version of SSL4EO-S12 v1.1 with all 1,406 overlapping tiles re- moved (242,562 remaining), and re-finetune on all four downstream tasks (5 seeds each). The de-collided model was pretrained (FP16 mixed precision, global batch size 256) on a single NVIDIA A100 80 GB GPU and finetuned (FP32) on single NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs, whereas the original models were pretrained on 4×V100 32 GB GPUs (64 samples per GPU) and fine- tuned on V100 GPUs; the remainder of the recipe is unchanged, so hardware and per-device batch size are minor additional differences between the two columns of Table 13. Table 13 re- ports the results: all four tasks change by less than 0.1 percentage points relative to the original pretraining, within one standard deviation, confirming that the reported gains are not attributable to geographic leakage.
