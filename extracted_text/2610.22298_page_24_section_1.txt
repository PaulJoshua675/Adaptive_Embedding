This benchmark provides a multidimensional evaluation of LLM-generated clinical second opinions across factual grounding, problem prioritization, data use, reasoning coherence, differential diagnosis, final-conclusion quality, management, and safety. On the same 190 held-out cases, it distinguished a stable hierarchy among eight model pipelines, separated model capability from response-format effects, and quantified the contribution of the complete multidisciplinary synthesis. The structured final consilium report was more highly rated than the pre-consilium primary- role opinion across every shared criterion, while structured reports achieved higher overall means in seven of eight models, primarily through improved management planning. GPT-5.6-Sol led every evaluated component and reached consensus in the fewest review rounds. These results show that a clinically informative benchmark can capture differences that diagnostic accuracy alone would miss: whether a model can transform case information and multiple perspectives into a grounded, coherent, actionable, and safe second opinion. At the same time, structured reporting increased inference requirements, and additional review rounds were not associated with higher final-report scores. Clinical quality, procedural convergence, response structure, and inference cost should therefore be evaluated as complementary outcomes. The benchmark offers a comprehensive framework for comparing future models and synthesis strategies, while prospective studies remain necessary to establish effects on clinician decisions and patient care. 7 Limitations NEJM Case Records overrepresent unusual, difficult cases and do not reproduce routine prevalence, documentation, time pressure, or longitudinal uncertainty.
