Across this fixed benchmark, model identity produced the largest descriptive separation in overall performance. GPT- 5.6-Sol ranked first in structured role reasoning, role clinical opinion, free-form conclusions, and structured conclusions; Qwen3-Next-80B ranked last in all four components. GPT-5.6-Sol exceeded the next-highest model by 0.430 points for role reasoning, 0.404 for role clinical opinion, 0.165 for free-form conclusions, and 0.203 for structured conclusions. The best-to-worst model spread in final reports was 0.463 points for free-form and 0.572 for structured outputs, substantially larger than every within-model format difference (−0.045 to +0.064). The model ordering of final reports was identical in the two response formats, showing that the benchmark recovered a stable model hierarchy and that model choice was more strongly associated with final-report quality than the global response schema. Structured conclusions had higher overall means than their free-form counterparts in seven of the eight model pipelines. GPT-5.6-Sol showed the largest structured-minus-free-form difference (+0.064 points), followed by Gemma-4-31B (+0.038); Qwen3-Next-80B was the only pipeline with a lower structured mean (−0.045). Thus, the model-level comparison showed a broad, although modest, advantage for structured final reporting. To contextualize these quality differences, Table 7 presents final-report Judge scores alongside complete-pipeline token use for each model and response format on the same 190 held-out cases. For GPT-5.6-Sol, the mean score increased from 2.708 with free-form reporting to 2.772 with structured reporting, while mean token use increased from 65.2 thousand to 132.1 thousand per case.
