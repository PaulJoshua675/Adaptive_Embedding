Our formation and redirection results likewise resist a universal “acquisition sets rate, updating sets direction” rule. They supply a computational intervention contrast, not an identification of acquisition noise with a biological gate. 7.2 Current support and persistent learning state Performative prediction and recommendation feedback show that a model can alter its future data (Perdomo et al., 2020; Li & Wai, 2022; Chaney et al., 2018). Our state-following experiment uses a narrower, externally designed loop from measured allocation to subsequent target updates. A classical asymmetric-state model establishes the qualitative dynamical ingredients (Harris et al., 1997); a recent minimal bifurcation model provides a related construction (Kiselev, 2026). The useful comparison is between feedback that continues to track the learned state and an initial propensity that is frozen. Finite-time recovery, coexistence, and switching locate maintained regimes operationally (Kuehn, 2011; Hancock et al., 2025); they do not establish an adequate low-dimensional coordinate for those regimes. Underspecification shows that predictors matched on standard performance can differ on later tests (D’Amour et al., 2022). Representation-similarity methods compare another aspect of their endpoints (Raghu et al., 2017; Kornblith et al., 2019), while methods such as VAMPnets seek coordinates for slow dynamics (Mardt et al., 2018). Our matched-endpoint experiment adds a direct manipulation of a persistent state component. It asks not only whether responses differ, but whether replacing optimizer state removes or reverses that difference. Training history is also known to change later plasticity at larger scales. Early input degradation can induce critical-period effects in deep networks (Achille et al., 2019); early optimization trajectories influence later curvature and conditioning (Jastrzebski et al., 2020); and continual training can progressively reduce the capacity to learn new tasks (Dohare et al., 2024). A recent training-memory survey organizes optimizer state, data order, parameter path, and auxiliary state as distinct historical carriers (Sevetlidis & Pavlidis, 2026). Our result is narrower and more intervention-specific: in an endpoint-matched shared-representation system, it separates a large inherited-Adam transient from smaller delayed route differences and shows that the selected allocation endpoints approach one another under prolonged common training. The contrast with persistent deficits in larger networks makes architecture and training horizon concrete generalization variables rather than generic caveats. Optimizer memory itself has precedents. Resetting Adam moments can improve learning after objective changes in deep reinforcement learning (Asadi et al., 2023). In a recent preprint, Xu (2026) identifies the first moment as a causal carrier of subliminal transfer: its transplantation leaves immediate outputs unchanged while differences accumulate during later source-free training. We measure a different response in a different construction. Our ten-update pulse finds a large second-moment effect and a first-moment mean effect inside the specified equivalence margin. When Adam state is removed, route differences can still emerge later under fresh Adam or calibrated SGD, but they then shrink under continued common training. These delayed differences do not identify the same carrier or outcome as Xu’s accumulating transfer. Together the findings show why the optimizer component, intervention, measured response, and observation horizon must all be specified rather than assigning permanent roles to m and v. 7.3 What the intervention map makes usable Table 2 connects each observed phenomenon to a comparison a subsequent study can reuse.
