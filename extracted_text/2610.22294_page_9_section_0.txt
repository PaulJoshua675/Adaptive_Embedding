We first randomly draw a sequence of 16 demonstrations from the benchmark, followed by one target. For each D ∈{1, 2, 4, 8, 16}, we construct a prompt by retaining only the first D demonstrations while keeping the target unchanged. These five related prompts form a prompt family, allowing us to vary the demonstration count without changing the target or previously included demonstrations. We evaluate 64 families per benchmark across four tasks and four model interfaces, drawing K = 64 samples for each prompt. Figure 5 reports standard infilling accuracy and the gain from guidance. Increasing the demonstration count from D = 1 to D = 16 improves accuracy without additional guidance in all 16 model–task series (Figure 5, top). For LLaDA, unguided accuracy rises from 14.0% to 76.5% on SST-2, from 33.3% to 74.7% on List Functions–Easy, from 0.0% to 98.8% on SALT repetition, and from 64.1% to 94.9% on Movie Director. This shows that demonstrations provide useful conditioning information even before applying reward guidance. Consistent with this observation, the reference answer’s example reward generally increases with D, indicating stronger relative likelihood support from the demonstrations (Section B.2). Beyond these improvements from demonstration conditioning, example guidance yields further accuracy gains in several settings, particularly SST-2 at intermediate demonstration counts (Figure 5, bottom). At D = 4, guidance improves LLaDA’s accuracy by 14.4 percentage points on SST-2, 6.6 on List Functions–Easy, 3.6 on SALT repetition, and 2.9 on Movie Director, where unguided accuracy already exceeds 94%. These gains tend to shrink as standard infilling accuracy saturates, and depend on whether the reward favors correct samples. 4.2 Multi-Target Few-Shot Prompting We next examine how demonstration conditioning and example guidance behave when multiple targets are infilled jointly. For each prompt family, we randomly draw 16 demonstrations and 8 targets from List Functions–Easy, then interleave them into a shared sequence. For each configuration D ∈{2, 4, 8, 16} and J ∈{1, 2, 4, 8}, we construct a canvas by removing all but the first D demonstrations and first J targets from this sequence. The retained items preserve their relative order, allowing us to vary demonstration and target counts without independently reshuffling the canvas. For each resulting canvas, the DLM draws K = 16 joint samples using oracle target lengths, which we evaluate under standard infilling and additional example guidance. With multiple targets, we distinguish between completing every target correctly and maintaining accuracy at individual sites.
