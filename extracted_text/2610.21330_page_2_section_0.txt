We then benchmark 11 rewriting strategies across 4 categories. Structure strategies improve page organization or reformat webpages into more LLM-friendly layouts. Evidence strategies add quotations, sta- tistics, or source attributions to support existing claims. Style strate- gies modify wording without adding substantive content. These three categories follow existing GEO work [1, 25]. Motivated by political persuasion research [11, 16], we further introduce 2 Argu- mentation strategies, which strengthen a position by emphasizing its distinctions from plausible alternatives or explicitly addressing opposing arguments. Our results reveal three main patterns. First, target-unspecified rewriting rarely increases target exposure, whereas target-specified rewriting more often does so. This distinction is important because election webpages often discuss multiple parties. In the U.S. setting, 92% of retrieved webpages mention both major parties, so rewriting without an explicit target does not indicate which party’s exposure should increase. Second, Structure and Argumentation strategies show stronger gains than Evidence and Style strategies across gen- erative engines and elections. Consistent with this pattern, one strategy from each of these two categories shows the strongest overall gains across settings and exposure metrics. Third, for these two strongest strategies, rewriting gains remain broadly positive across the 15 evaluated U.S. personas, while persona conditioning shows no consistent tendency to increase or decrease these gains. In contrast, explicit partisan identities can create baseline exposure gaps of up to 11.6 percentage points, larger than the gain from rewriting a single webpage. We further consider whether similar shifts appear when partisanship is only implicit in the voter profile. In this case, baseline exposure shifts vary with how strongly each engine responds to the implicit partisan signal. To summarize, we make the following contributions: • We introduce VoteGEO-Bench, built on two elections with elec- tion queries, historical webpages, and voter personas (Section 3). • We provide motivating insights into the potential risks of Vote- GEO by analyzing source types and webpage visibility over time (Section 4). • We organize 11 rewriting strategies into 4 categories, including 2 new Argumentation strategies (Section 5). • We evaluate how target specification, rewriting strategies, and voter personas affect target exposure across engines and elec- tions (Section 6). 2 Preliminaries 2.1 VoteGEO Generative engines first retrieve a set of webpages relevant to the query, often through web search, and then use these webpages as context to generate a response. Generative engine optimization (GEO) aims to improve the visibility of web content in such re- sponses through content rewriting [1, 25]. Existing GEO research typically separates retrieval from generation and focuses on the generation stage by fixing the retrieved webpage pool and studying how rewriting one webpage changes its visibility in the generated response [1, 8, 24, 25, 29, 30]. We follow this problem setting and define a query cell as an election-related query issued to a particular engine on a particular day. Each query cell 𝑖has a query 𝑞𝑖and a corresponding webpage pool D𝑖. Let 𝑑𝑖∈D𝑖denote the webpage to be rewritten. The other webpages in D𝑖remain fixed, and GEO studies whether rewriting 𝑑𝑖improves its visibility in the generated response. Existing GEO settings typically consider webpages centered on a single target entity, such as a product description page or a brand landing page [1, 25]. In voting scenarios, however, a webpage may discuss multiple candidates or parties, so selecting a webpage does not uniquely determine whose exposure the rewrite should increase. VoteGEO therefore introduces a target 𝑡𝑖, defined as a candidate or party mentioned in 𝑑𝑖, and studies whether rewriting 𝑑𝑖can increase the exposure of 𝑡𝑖in the generated response. Under target-specified rewriting, the rewriting model is given both 𝑑𝑖and 𝑡𝑖, whereas under target-unspecified rewriting, it is given only 𝑑𝑖.
