Round scheduling and randomization. A new round is triggered when fewer than eight difficulty modes re- main within the success band, or when success on modes within the band changes by less than two percentage points over the preceding 2,000 policy-update steps. The three random seeds jointly vary environment generation, policy rollouts, and model training. C. Additional Experiments Complementary generation and training programs. Table 5 evaluates the contributions of the generation and training programs retained in memory. Retaining only scene generation programs raises OOD success from 19.2% to 22.8%, while retaining only policy training pro- grams raises it to 22.1%. Retaining both reaches 27.6%: the resulting 8.4-percentage-point improvement exceeds the sum of the individual gains, 3.6 and 2.9 points. The three baseline variants that reuse the same memory rep- resentation for both scene generation and policy train- ing perform worse than retaining either type of special- ized program alone. With both generation and training programs, the policy reaches 25% OOD success in five rounds, compared with fourteen rounds without memory. Transfer across task families. Table 6 evaluates gen- eration programs transferred to a different task family by rebinding their object roles, without relearning the programs. The baseline learns programs from scratch in the target family using the same LLM token budget as transfer. Averaged across all cross-family transfers, reused programs achieve 60.3% of the learning progress obtained with programs learned within the target family, compared with 35.8% for the from-scratch baseline. This advantage holds in every cross-family comparison, demonstrating that generation experience can be reused to support policy learning in new task families. Transfer across policy backbones. Table 7 evaluates whether memory collected with one policy backbone can support training another, with the memory held fixed and no additional environment generation. Transferred memory improves OOD success by an average of 18.9 percentage points over the SFT baseline without memory. In both transfer directions, performance remains within two percentage points of running the full framework with the target backbone. These results show that the accumu- lated difficulty modes and programs can support policy learning beyond the backbone used to collect them. Both condi- tions reach similar final in-distribution success rates, but shaping reaches this level approximately 20,000 policy-update steps earlier.
