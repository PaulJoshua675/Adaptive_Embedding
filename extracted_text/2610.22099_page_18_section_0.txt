B.2.6 Schedule shape and logit calibration We freeze the reference policy and vary the inference schedule κ(τ) = τ exp(γ). Table 8 and figure 6 distinguishes changes in γ from removing calibration altogether. The fitted value is γ = 0.14544; the other evaluated settings are −0.2, 0, and 0.2. Note that γ = 0 is a calibrated linear schedule, not an uncalibrated policy. The effect of schedule shape depends on the inference budget. Moving from the fitted value to γ = 0.2 raises accuracy at T = 16 from 43.16% to 45.70%, but reduces it at T = 32 from 56.15% to 53.22%; the T = 64 difference is only 0.29 points. More front-loaded visibility at γ = −0.2 is worse at all three budgets, particularly at T = 16. Therefore, nearby schedules can preserve much of the performance, but the schedule is not irrelevant and the best measured setting need not be the same at every budget. Removing calibration. We next bypass the scalar occupancy-matching shift while keeping the checkpoint and deterministic finalization unchanged. Accuracy falls to 26.66%, 25.20%, and 25.59% at T = 16, 32, 64, respectively. In this intervention, additional decoder calls no longer yield the improvement observed with calibrated TIPS. B.2.7 Policy architecture and runtime We replace the two-layer DiT with either a one-layer DiT or a parameter-matched per-token multilayer perceptron, while retaining the training and evaluation protocol. The per-token network cannot exchange information between response positions within the controller. It nevertheless reaches 54.88% accuracy at T = 64, compared with 58.20% for the two-layer DiT. Thus, a substantial fraction of the policy’s effectiveness can be retained with local processing of contextual decoder features. Cross-position processing still helps: the two-layer DiT exceeds the MLP at all three budgets and exceeds the one-layer DiT at T = 32 and 64. 18
