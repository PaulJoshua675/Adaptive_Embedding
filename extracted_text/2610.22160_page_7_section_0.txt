The external judge, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B (FP8), assigns scores from 0 to 10 without access to gold answers; feedback prompts forbid final-answer disclosure. Judge prompting and decoding settings are detailed in Appendix B.3. Reported accuracy uses task-specific answer extraction and gold-based correctness rules, independently of judge scores. Under judge-select, the highest-scoring response among the initial generation and K optimization rounds is selected, with ties resolved in favor of the earliest response. We separately report oracle coverage: the fraction of questions with at least one correct response in the candidate pool. Baselines and protocol. We compare Base (zero-shot decoding) and TextGrad (our E- step without the M-step), adapting TextGrad from task-level to per-question optimization to match our protocol. TextGrad and EMO share the initial context, baseline response, judge, and K = 4 optimization rounds, with judge-select over five candidates. EMO uses fixed defaults µ = 2.4 and α = 0.1 (Appendix B.5). Main results use greedy target decoding; budget comparisons use temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, and seeds {0, 1, 2} against self-consistency (SC) and best-of-N (BoN) (Section 4.4). Paired significance tests compare EMO at N = 9 with Base and BoN@10 (Appendix B.4.5). Full decoding settings and token limits appear in Appendix B.3. 4.2 Main Results Broad gains across the model grid. Table 1 separates overall gains over Base from in- cremental gains over context-only optimization. EMO improves over Base in 39 of 40 cells (47 of 48 before merging the two AIME editions), averaging +9.10 percentage points across the 40 model–task-group pairs. All eight models improve on average; EMO also exceeds TextGrad in 25 cells and ties in four, with a mean increment of +1.53 points. Using Qwen3-8B as a common reference, we organize the analysis along four axes: model scale, generation, family, and thinking mode. Scale: complementing model scaling. We first test whether adaptation benefits persist as target models scale. Qwen3-14B with EMO exceeds Qwen3-32B Base on all five task groups, averaging 69.80 versus 63.86. Likewise, optimized Llama-3.2-3B exceeds Llama-3.1-8B Base on every task group. Larger models also retain substantial gains: Qwen3-32B improves by 8.68 points on average and 30.0 on AIME. Accuracy headroom alone is insufficient to predict improvement: Qwen3 gains little on near-saturated GSM8K, yet Llama gains little on AIME despite near-zero baselines. Mean gains across Qwen3-8B/14B/32B are non-monotonic (9.42/7.44/8.68): model size alone does not determine adaptation benefit. Adaptation can close size-related gaps, but its benefit depends on whether feedback-guided generation can reach correct solutions. 7
