Semantically similar items share prefixes, so the identifier space itself carries item semantics, and the model can generalise to items with little or no interac- tion history [26]. Semantic IDs are typically produced by residual quantization—either RQ-VAE [17, 26] or the training-free residual 𝑘-means of Quantitative Alignment Multi-Modal Recommenda- tion [21]—and are therefore hierarchical by construction. Crucially, this makes the levels asymmetric: because every later code quan- tizes the residual left by the earlier ones, an error at a coarse level redefines the target of every level below it, whereas an error at the finest level perturbs only a local refinement. Despite this structure, essentially all existing generative recom- menders supervise semantic-ID generation with a flat causal cross- entropy (CE) loss over the extended vocabulary. The consequence is not merely that the model must rediscover the coarse-to-fine or- ganisation residual quantization already encoded—it is that flat CE loss actively erodes it. We show in Section 4 that fine-tuning under flat CE cuts the cosine coupling between adjacent SID levels by 35% relative to initialization (from 0.55 to 0.36), while a level-aware objective retains it at 0.50, a 9% reduction, and induces 2–4× less embedding drift from the shared initialization. A second, orthogonal limitation is that the supervision an item receives is proportional to how often it appears as a training target. Head items accumulate enough gradient to acquire useful represen- tations; data-sparse tail items do not. This matters because semantic IDs are quantized from holistic item embeddings, so they organ- ise the catalogue by overall similarity rather than by any single attribute: a property a user actually asks for (color, material, fit, pattern) is smeared across levels rather than localised. Measuring colour directly, the coarsest code alone yields only 37% mean colour purity while conditioning on the first two codes raises it to 83%, and a probing analysis shows the information is present but not lin- early organised—a linear probe underperforms the catalogue-mean baseline at every SID position, whereas a small MLP probe reaches Δ𝐸= 38.5 against a baseline of 56.3. The signal is latent in the representation and unexploited by the generation objective. Yet the LLM backbone already knows what a “ribbed burgundy wool cardi- gan” is: the interaction log is the bottleneck, not the backbone’s semantic knowledge. To address these limitations, we propose HASTE (Hierarchical and Attribute-aware Supervision for generaTive rEcommendation), a fine-tuning framework that introduce two complementary objec- tives. The Hierarchical Loss replaces flat causal CE with level- aware supervision: coarser levels carry larger base weights, and each level’s weight is gated by the model’s detached confidence in the upstream codes it is conditioned on, so fine-grained su- pervision is amplified only once the coarse decisions it depends on are actually resolved. Because confidence estimates are unre- liable early in training, the objective anneals from purely static hierarchical weighting to fully confidence-gated weighting. Sched- uled sampling [1] further perturbs the upstream prefix, exposing the model to the upstream errors it is likely to encounter dur- ing decoding rather than only to clean, teacher-forced trajectories. The Attribute-Specific Losses attach lightweight MLP regression heads to the hidden state already used to predict the fine-grained SID code, and supervise them against catalogue-derived attribute targets. A frequency-aware weighting further concentrates the sig- nal on infrequent items. We instantiate the framework for two complementary attributes—perceptual (CIELAB colour [4]) and compositional (material composition)—and find that the gains land where interaction supervision is weakest: mean top-1 colour error drops from 54.5 to 50.2 on tail colours and 33.5 to 29.0 on torso colours, while head colours are unchanged (12.5 to 12.6). Our contributions are as follows: • We propose a Hierarchical Loss that supervises semantic- ID generation level-by-level, combining decreasing level weights, detached confidence gating on upstream codes, an annealed transition from static to conditional weighting, and scheduled sampling of the upstream prefix to close the train– inference exposure gap. It exploits the hierarchy explicitly rather than leaving it to be rediscovered, preserves the in- herited structure, and yields ∼2× faster convergence than causal CE at equal or better recommendation quality. • We propose Attribute-Specific Losses that inject fine-grained item-attribute supervision into the generative recommender through auxiliary heads on the fine-grained SID prediction state. HASTE gives prominent gains across datasets and item-frequency regimes, with the largest improvements on the tail items. 2 Related Works Sequential and Generative Recommendation. Sequential rec- ommenders predict the next item from an ordered interaction his- tory. GRU4Rec [12] models sessions with recurrence, while SAS- Rec [15] and BERT4Rec [29] use unidirectional and bidirectional self-attention respectively. All three represent an item as an atomic embedding in a table that grows with the catalogue, so a rare item’s representation is learned almost entirely from its few occur- rences, and retrieval needs a nearest-neighbour index maintained separately from the model. Generative retrieval removes the in- dex: the model decodes the target’s identifier token by token [31]. GENRE [6] first cast retrieval this way, generating entity names autoregressively; DSI [32] replaced names with structured semantic identifiers obtained by hierarchical clustering; and NCI [35] added a prefix-aware decoder. P5 [10] first brought this seq2seq formula- tion to recommendation, and TIGER [26] specialised it to semantic IDs, quantizing content embeddings with RQ-VAE [17] and decod- ing the next item’s codes from the history; because those codes are derived from content and shared prefix-wise, a rare item is no longer represented in isolation. HSTU [40] showed the paradigm scales as a power law of training compute, and OneRec [7] and OneRec-Think [20] deploy it at industrial scale. In addition to the generation framework, item tokenization has also attracted consid- erable attention.
