We propose MindEval2, a multi-session benchmark for AI mental health support. It pairs MindSim, a patient simulator built on clinically grounded profiles, conversational moves and a memory shared across sessions, with automatic scoring against MHQR, a clinically developed framework for identifying clinical issues and aggregating reply-level severity. We show that frontier models mostly struggle with appropriate guidance, being focused on exploring the patient’s feelings rather than helping them move forward. Over multiple sessions, counsellors that build on previous sessions improve, whereas others keep asking about what the patient has already shared. Future work should examine patient-side signals, model alliance building over longer arcs, and extend the benchmark to crisis situations. 12
