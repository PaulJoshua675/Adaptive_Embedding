Two specialists trained from one base model each answer training problems of their own domain (1); answers that end in a repetition loop or fail grading are dropped (2); each specialist reads its kept answers once while hooks record the second moments Gd of every layer’s inputs (3); and each layer of the fused model is obtained by one linear solve (4). Steps 1 to 3 are forward passes and step 4 is linear algebra, so no gradient is computed at any point. Step 2 decides the hard problems (Section 3.4). Contributions. • We show that the capabilities of separately tuned specialists can be aggregated without training. On Olmo 3 7B, a closed-form fusion of a mathematics and a coding specialist keeps most of each specialist’s gain on seven benchmarks and is, on average, more accurate than either specialist alone, after a few GPU-hours of forward computation per specialist. • The method, TF-OPD, reads layer-wise distillation from several teachers as the closed-form problem of RegMean and feeds it statistics from the specialists’ own rollouts, using forward passes only. • We show that the composition of those rollouts decides the outcome on hard problems. Truncated answers ending in repetition loops are a small share of answers and a large share of tokens, and removing them separates a fused model that falls short of its mathematics specialist from one that matches it in the same evaluation format. • We analyze what matters and what does not: a close token-level fit does not protect hard problems; student rollouts, domain reweighting and mined hard problems do not improve on selection; numerical precision does.
