VI. DISCUSSION The results support treating scalable agent discovery as a joint retrieval–orchestration problem. Full-catalog orchestra- tion couples registry size directly to the amount of informa- tion presented to the LLM, whereas candidate pre-selection separates registry-scale retrieval from the compact candidate context used for final arbitration. RQ3 shows that this systems effect is not specific to SSR: both SSR and BM25 maintain small candidate pools under the same maximum candidate budget, while full-catalog context grows rapidly with registry size. This distinction is central to the interpretation of SSR. RQ2 shows that BM25 achieves higher raw retrieval effectiveness on the MS MARCO adaptation, so SSR should not be viewed as a general replacement for established lexical retrieval. Instead, SSR provides a deterministic, training-free, field- derived retrieval mechanism for Agent Cards. Within this mechanism, IDF weighting improves ranking effectiveness, the Inverted Signal Index (ISI) reduces explicit structural scoring, and the maximum candidate budget K controls the amount of candidate information presented to the LLM orchestrator. These objectives are related but distinct. A. Candidate-Context Scalability and the Role of K The principal systems property studied here is |Pq| ≤K, which places an explicit upper bound on the number of Agent Cards presented to the LLM independently of the total registry size. This does not imply registry-independent retrieval cost: SSR and BM25 still query indexes whose contents and post- ing distributions depend on registry composition. The bound applies to the final candidate pool, not to all upstream retrieval work. RQ3 also exposes the trade-off between candidate context and retrieval coverage. At N = 5000, SSR and BM25 achieve candidate recall of 0.950 and 0.970 and end-to-end accuracy of 0.885 and 0.895, respectively. Paired McNemar tests detect no statistically significant difference in end-to-end correctness after Holm correction, supporting a description of comparable observed effectiveness without establishing statistical equiva- lence. The fixed K = 20 operating point should be interpreted as an evaluated systems choice rather than a universal optimum. Independent retrieval-only validation on 1,000 disjoint queries also selected K = 20 under the predefined within-0.02 recall rule, supporting its robustness. Because this validation was retrospective, however, a fully prospective protocol should select and freeze K before evaluation on a disjoint end-to- end test set.
