We presented AURA, a utility-guided framework that supervises intermediate reasoning states and stops generation when predicted retrieval utility ceases to improve. AURA achieves the strongest average BRIGHT performance among the com- pared methods while substantially reducing reason- ing cost, without an external inference-time rea- soner. Controlled ablations and full-corpus anal- ysis show that the gains arise from utility-aware supervision rather than denser supervision or trajec- tory filtering, and that retrieval specificity closely tracks nDCG@10. Results on MS MARCO sug- gest limited in-domain degradation, while utility- guided greedy expansion further improves the performance–compute trade-off. Limitations Despite its efficiency gains, AURA remains slower than single-pass retrieval because reasoning is gen- erated autoregressively. Its utility labels are con- structed once from a warmed-up checkpoint and may become less accurate as the embedding space evolves during subsequent optimization. Finally, our evaluation focuses primarily on reasoning- intensive English retrieval; broader validation on web search, multilingual retrieval, and generation tasks is needed to establish generality and charac- terize the remaining retrieval–generation trade-off.
