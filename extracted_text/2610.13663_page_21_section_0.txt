A Multi-Agent LLM System for Automated Tuning • Bounded number of iterations. All reported runs are capped at 250 iterations for SNN tasks and 50 iterations for PINN tasks. These bounds were chosen to keep the wall-clock cost reproducible within the budgets reported in Table 4, but they also mean that the accuracy and relative-error values in Sections 5.2, 5.4, 5.5 and 5.6 are lower bounds on what the agent can eventually reach with a larger budget. • Single-seed evaluation in part of the regimes. For the most expensive configurations (Claude Opus 4.6 on the generalised nonlinear Schrödinger equation and on the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky benchmark) we report a single seed per configuration; the ten-run averages for best models are given only for the soliton with a Gaussian perturbation case. A full multi-seed sweep for every LLM/task pair is left for future work. • Dependence on proprietary LLM APIs. Two of the three evaluated models (Claude Opus 4.6 and Perplexity Sonar) are accessed through commercial APIs; their availability, pricing and behaviour may change between the time of the experiments and the time of reproduction. The open-weights Qwen3-30B-A3B run is provided as a partial mitigation, but its cost/quality trade-off is not directly comparable to the proprietary systems. • No head-to-head comparison with LLM-based NAS baselines. The reported baselines are a random-choice controller and the state-of-the-art results reported in the cited references. A direct comparison with recent LLM- driven neural-architecture-search systems on the same tasks and under the same iteration and cost budgets is desirable but was outside the scope of the present paper; we consider it the natural next step of this line of research.
