(1) Color features: To capture the route-specific paint schemes that distinguish otherwise structurally similar buses, color histograms were computed in the HSV color space. HSV was preferred over RGB because it separates chromatic information from intensity, making the descriptor less sensitive to the illumination changes that occur throughout the day in outdoor recordings. Accordingly, only the Hue and Saturation channels were used, and the Value channel was discarded. Because the expanded bounding boxes include surrounding background such as asphalt, sky, and other vehicles, a central crop covering 50% of the image area was applied before computing the histograms, so that the descriptor predominantly reflects the vehicle body. Because both components are unit-normalized, the squared Euclidean distance between two fused vectors decomposes as ∥vi −vj∥2 2 = α2 ∥ˆf morph i −ˆf morph j ∥2 2 + (1 −α)2 ∥ˆf color i −ˆf color j ∥2 2, (5) so that α directly regulates the trade-off between structural and chromatic similarity. Three weighting schemes were evaluated, with α ∈{0.35, 0.50, 0.65}, in order to analyze the effect of this balance on the resulting cluster structure. 3) Clustering and Evaluation: Distance-based clustering algorithms such as K-Means tend to degrade in high- dimensional spaces, where pairwise distances become less dis- criminative. Therefore, the fused vectors were first projected onto a lower-dimensional manifold using Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) [14], a nonlinear dimensionality reduction technique that preserves the local neighborhood structure of the data while retaining much of its global organization. K-Means clustering [15] was subsequently applied to the reduced representations for each number of clusters K ∈ {2, . . . , 30}. The optimal value of K was selected by jointly considering two internal validation criteria. The Elbow Method examines the within-cluster sum of squares as a function of K and identifies the point beyond which additional clusters yield diminishing reductions. The Silhouette Score [16] measures, for each sample, how similar it is to its own cluster relative to the nearest neighboring cluster, ranging from −1 to 1, where higher values indicate compact and well-separated clusters. This procedure was repeated independently for each value of α. IV. RESULTS The objective of the proposed method is to cluster the bus images and to determine whether the buses operated by RTP can be isolated into a coherent group without supervision. Accordingly, this section evaluates the proposed unsupervised early fusion approach against a baseline that clusters the images using morphological features alone. In addition, an ablation study was conducted to analyze the effect of the fusion weight α on clustering performance, considering three configurations: α = 0.65, α = 0.50, and α = 0.35. The resulting clusters were assessed both quantitatively, by comparing them against the manually annotated target class, and qualitatively, through visual inspection of their contents. A.
