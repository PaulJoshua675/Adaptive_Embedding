Guiding sequence generation with explicit linguistic structure stems from factored translation architectures [25] and prefix-based syntactic conditioning [28]. For polysynthetic languages, finite-state transducers (FSTs) supply robust morphological segmentation and grammatical disambiguation where purely statistical subword models fail [2]. My system builds on these insights, demonstrating that high-density FST syntactic prefixes provide reliable structural inductive bias without requiring vocabulary modifications. Parameter-Efficient Adaptation in Low-Resource Regimes. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) [11] provides an efficient mechanism for adapting massive multilingual models such as mT5 [27] while preventing catastrophic forgetting. Extending low- rank updates across all linear projections is essential for deep domain transfer [6, 10]. Data collection was guided by collective benefit and mutual accountability rather than extractive aggregation. 3.1 Cleaning and Provenance In machine translation, the main training unit is an aligned parallel pair consisting of a source text segment and its equivalent target translation. We compiled the primary corpus over nearly four years of studying Masewalkopa through classes and coursework with native-speaker instructors from Santa Ana Tlacotenco. All pedagogical exercises, classroom dialogues, and vocabulary notes were recorded and curated into structured spreadsheets with explicit instructor consent. We normalized whitespace and Unicode representations, removed empty fields, and deduplicated aligned Spanish–Nawatl segment pairs case-insensitively.
