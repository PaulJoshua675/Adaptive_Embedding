C.2 Tokenizers from an MAE encoder We also trained tokenizers from MAE-L/16 (He et al., 2022), whose features are learned by pixel reconstruction and therefore fall outside Def. 1. These runs use the same tokenizer and BAR-B generator recipes, with 250K generator steps and 50K samples without guidance at patch temperature 2.0 and allocation [4, 4, 4, 4]. They differ in three respects: they omit source-preservation regularization, use the last MAE layer as the feature target, and use MAE’s auxiliary class token as the global token, sampled at temperature 2.5 and allocation [2, 2, 5, 7]. MAE-based tokenizers generate poorly under every supervision. With patch-only generation, gFID is 5.74 for the pixel target, 5.94 for combined pixel and feature targets, and 6.40 for the feature target, and global-augmented generation reaches 5.55 and 5.75 with the combined and feature targets. Feature supervision thus does not improve MAE-based tokens, and every MAE configuration stays far above the gFID range of Tab. 11, so we do not pursue MAE further. C.3 Reconstruction results Tab. 12 reports reconstruction for four source encoders under pixel and feature supervision, using regularized tokenizers throughout. With pixel supervision, the pixel loss trains the encoder and quantizer. With feature supervision, the pixel gradient is cut at the discrete representation, and the feature decoder reconstructs the four-layer-average target. Across the four encoders, rFID ranges from 0.72 to 1.40. The six-space view complements rFID: feature targets improve rFDr6 over pixel targets for SigLIP (2.68 to 2.45), SigLIP2 (2.09 to 2.04), and DINOv3 (2.24 to 2.08), but not DINOv2 (1.82 to 2.06). These reconstruction scores alone do not explain the generation differences in Sec. 3.1. Pixel fidelity versus perceptual quality.
