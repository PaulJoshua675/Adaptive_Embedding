BEVDet [17] and BEVDepth [24] es- tablished the deployment-friendly LSS pipeline; Sim- pleOccupancy [13], OccNet [38], TPVFormer [18], Vox- Former [25], OccFormer [54], and COTR [30] explore com- pact, dense, plane-based, sparse, and transformer-style oc- cupancy representations. RenderOcc uses 2D rendering su- pervision [33]; BEVFormer [26], SurroundOcc [47], FB- OCC [27], OpenOccupancy [45], and GaussianFormer [19] buy accuracy at higher cost; SparseOcc [28] and OPUS [43] cut dense-voxel cost. We deploy FlashOcc [51], which keeps features in 2D BEV and recovers height with a channel-to-height plug-in. These works optimize mIoU and sometimes mean FPS, not the per-frame latency determin- ism that decides deadline feasibility (Sec. 4). Low-bit BEV/occupancy quantization is mostly GPU/TensorRT-targeted: QD-BEV [53] and PTQAT [46] use QAT, BEVFormer tensorrt [10] uses INT8 PTQ, and all rest on general methods such as AdaRound [32], BRECQ [23], SmoothQuant [50], HAQ [44], and clipping- based activation quantization [9]. The AMD/Xilinx DPU [2] with vai q pytorch [3] is more constrained: per-tensor power-of-two scales and min-max calibration only. We therefore use fixed activation clipping to stay inside the supported DPU path; prior quantized occupancy work rarely reports worst-case timing. On the deployment-operator side, BEVPoolv2 [16] pre- computes ranks and intervals for fast GPU LSS pooling, FastOcc [15] shares 2D BEV and perspective-view features, and TensorRT ports rely on custom plugins [10]. Their ker- nels remain GPU software with scheduler jitter, whereas our PL gather and resize turn fixed camera tables into on-chip memories with static trip counts and reported FPGA WCET. For embedded-FPGA perception, the argument is tim- ing: GPU per-frame latency is hard to bound [29], while ISO 26262 [20] values worst-case evidence over aver- age throughput. Restuccia and Biondi [36] analyze the Zynq UltraScale+ DPU, and FINN/FINN-R [6, 41] with PointPillars-on-FINN [39] is the closest embedded 3D precedent. Yet PointPillars-on-FINN is LiDAR-based and leaves scatter on ARM, while DPU WCET work treats the accelerator as a generic CNN. No prior work gives a camera-to-occupancy datapath both cycle-bounded view operators and an enforced delivery layer on programmable logic, including the two operations the DPU cannot run. 3.
