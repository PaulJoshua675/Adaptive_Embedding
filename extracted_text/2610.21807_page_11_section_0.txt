The controls are informative in their own right. Answer length dominates (OR = 2.59 [2.48, 2.71] per unit of log length), later answers are somewhat favoured (1.14 [1.12, 1.16] per rank), and question–answer noun overlap is strongly negative (0.13 [0.09, 0.18]): answers that merely restate the question’s vocabulary are passed over. Whether a question is answered at all turns on largely different clusters, which is the payoff of separating the two parts. Lower odds attach to Cluster 3 (places and regions, OR = 0.67 [0.63, 0.72]), Cluster 5 (0.81 [0.76, 0.86]), Cluster 4 (0.86 [0.80, 0.92]), and Clus­ ter 12 (calendar and dates, 0.89 [0.82, 0.97], not interpreted substantively); only Cluster 7 (cost, tax and government) raises them (1.17 [1.08, 1.25]). Seven clusters, including work and employment and delivery and ordering, are null. Question length is not significant (OR = 0.96, p =.075). Posting year is significant (OR = 1.38 per year), but we read this as an archival artifact, since older threads have had longer to lose answers to deletion, and retain the term as a control without interpreting it. Once a question is answered, the ordering changes again. The dispersion parameter is α = 0.13, confirming overdispersion rela­ tive to Poisson. Four robustness checks appear in Appendix Table 4. The earlier pooled logit agrees in sign with the conditional logit on 11 of 12 clusters, so stratification changes magnitudes rather than directions. Replacing binary indicators with log(1 + cluster token count) reproduces all 12 signs. Modelling answers and replies separately reproduces 12 and 11 signs, so the combined measure hides no opposing dynamics. Refitting the count model on 2004–2014 (n = 3,225) and 2015–2026 (n = 21,531) reproduces 9 and 11 signs; the ear­ lier, noisier subperiod’s three sign changes are not read as a temporal shift. Read together, no cluster behaves the same way across all three outcomes, and the clusters that win selection within a thread are not those that attract volume. Cluster 7 is the only one positive on all three. Cluster 6 is positive for selection and volume but null for receiving any answer. Cluster 3 is positive for selection yet most negative for receiving any answer. Clusters 1 and 2, the operational core of the corpus, are positive for selection and negative for volume.
