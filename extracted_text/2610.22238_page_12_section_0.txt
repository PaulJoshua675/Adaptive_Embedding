6 Experiments We test SIRS with the Rao-Blackwellized increment, denoted SIRS-RB, in two settings where the score function is exact, pθ,i = p∗ i . Then EProposal,t = ERS,t = 0 in Theorem 5.3, and the error of SIRS-RB is the clipping-induced error alone. The question is whether correcting a block of size n costs fewer score queries than parallel sampling with smaller blocks. Data model. We use two families of data distributions whose conditional marginals are available in closed form. • Ising chain. p∗(s) ∝exp(β P l<L slsl+1 + P l hlsl) on {−1, 1}L with fixed fields hl ∼N(0, 0.32), and the scores follow from forward–backward recursions. The dependence is local, and its strength grows with β ∈{0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 1.2}. We take L ∈{8, 16}. • P´olya urn. ϕ ∼Dir(α) and Xl|ϕ are i.i.d. categorical over V = 32 tokens, with α = α0a for a fixed base measure a ∼Dir(2 · 1V ). The tokens are exchangeable, so every pair is dependent, and the dependence weakens as α0 ∈{8, 32, 128, 256, 512, 1024} grows. The scores are p∗ l (v|y) = (αv + cv(y))/(α0 + |U(y)|), where cv(y) counts the revealed tokens equal to v. We take L ∈{256, 1024}. Setup. All samplers reveal blocks of a uniform size, taken as consecutive chunks of a uniformly random permutation of [L] drawn independently of the tokens. We run SIRS-RB with block size n and B ∈{1 8, 1 4, 1 2, 1, 2}, and compare it with parallel sampling with block size n′ and with sequential decoding (n′ = 1), which is exact. For each sampler we compute the KL-divergence KL(p∗∥bpB) from the data distribution to the output distribution, in nats per sequence and averaged over permutations, and the expected number of score queries per sequence. Parallel sampling uses ⌈L/n′⌉ queries; in SIRS-RB we flip the coins of the Poissonized Bernoulli factory one at a time and reject at the first failure, which leaves the output distribution unchanged and saves queries. Results on the Ising chain. Figure 1 shows that the comparison is decided by the coupling strength β. Under weak coupling, the SIRS-RB curves lie below the parallel-sampling curve: correcting a large block takes fewer queries than shrinking it to the same KL-divergence. Under strong coupling, SIRS-RB falls above the parallel-sampling curve, and the larger block (n = 16) loses its advantage first. Results on the P´olya urn. The P´olya urn, in which every pair of tokens is dependent, shows the same picture (Figure 2). When the dependence is weak, SIRS-RB dominates parallel sampling, and in the weakest settings its KL-divergence is indistinguishable from zero at a small fraction of the queries of sequential decoding. When the dependence is strong, SIRS-RB is worse than parallel sampling with the same budget. When does the correction pay off? With exact scores, EProposal,t and ERS,t vanish, and by Theorem 5.3 the only error of SIRS-RB is the clipping error, which arises when the increments ¯W ∗fall outside [−B, B]. When the dependence is weak, the increments are small, so a small B, costing only a few queries per step, removes most of the error. When the dependence is strong, the increments are large, and suppressing the clipping error requires a large B, at a cost that grows as eB (Proposition 4.1); reducing the block size is then the cheaper option. Finally, a larger block contains more dependent pairs, so its log-ratio, which ¯W ∗estimates, is larger and more often clipped; this is why n = 16 loses its advantage first. 12
