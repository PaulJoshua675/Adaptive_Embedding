So far, however, we have trained all policies on the calibrated population Dcal, which is constructed from the 32 fitted patients. We now train each type of policy on a broad population Dwide constructed without reference to the fitted patients, with α ∈[2, 8], β ∈[6, 10], and broad ranges of the initial tumor burden and the initial resistant fraction (see Appendix B). We then apply the resulting policies to the fitted patients without further training, which we refer to as zero-shot generalization, and evaluate them under shifts in the dynamics against best-response adversaries trained on the fitted patients. Figure 5 compares the two training populations, and Table 3 reports the policies trained on Dwide under errors in PSA and shifts in the dynamics.
