Under its target- conditioned protocol with the same GPT-4o-mini CRS, CSHI produces markedly less diverse simu- lated behavior than FULL: aspect entropy is 0.194 vs. 1.619 (dz = 1.85), item entropy 1.454 vs. 2.034 (dz = 1.13), and SBERT diversity 0.345 vs. 0.532 (dz = 1.30; all padj < 0.001), while JSD from ReDial does not differ. Because CSHI steers each conversation toward a predefined target item, these differences reflect both profile content and protocol, not profile enrichment alone. 4.4 Auditor Behavior The Audit Agent processed 300 interviews: AC- CEPT (209, 70%), PARTIAL (91, 30%), REGENER- ATE (0). Only one interview reached the REGENER- ATE threshold; the regenerated interview received PARTIAL. All other corrective action was therefore turn-level exclusion under PARTIAL, removing 196 interviewee turns from enrichment. The auditor raised 676 flags (372 caricature, 173 history con- tradiction, 131 fabrication); the full taxonomy and example dialogues are in Appendix B. Three observations follow. First, the dominant is- sue is not fabrication but caricature (372/676 flags, 55%), mostly invented rationalizations. For ex- ample, one interviewee explained a dislike of The Hangover and Superbad, both on the user’s dis- liked list, by calling them “loud and obnoxious,” although the user likes comparably broad come- dies; the dislike is attested, but the reason is not. Second, the human evaluation shows a matching pattern (§4.6): decision-making style, which has no quantitative anchor in the profile, is the least supported attribute (21.4% Unsupported; 28.6% Evidence-grounded), whereas pickiness, derived from the rejection rate, is among the best supported (97.0%); this correspondence offers limited, indi- rect evidence of audit calibration. Third, the ab- sence of final REGENERATE decisions indicates that gross structural failure is rare with capable LLMs at our interview scale; the dominant failure mode is local, which makes turn-level exclusion an appropriate response. 4.5 Human Evaluation of Audit Decisions We evaluated Stage 4 auditing on 105 synthetic interviewee turns, with one turn per historical Re- Dial user, sampled equally from the 35 excluded, 35 flagged-but-retained, and 35 unflagged audit outcomes.
