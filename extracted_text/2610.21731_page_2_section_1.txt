Temporal knowledge and web search. Prior benchmarks measure whether LLMs answer time- sensitive questions correctly (Mousavi et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2025; Wallat et al., 2025a; Cheng and Dou, 2025), but not whether the answers come from the retrieved evidence. Kale (2026) finds that four models from two families invoke native search on 87.5–93.9% of questions that need it, without checking whether the cited pages support the answers. Premature stopping and top-result re- liance also surface in evaluations of search agents and LLM-based search engines (Gupta et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2025). We extend these studies by examining snippet reliance in both proprietary and open-weight LLMs. Citation faithfulness. Citation quality is usually scored as whether a cited passage supports a claim (Gao et al., 2023). Wallat et al. (2025b) separate this correctness from faithfulness, that is, whether the source actually produced the answer. On a single open model in a controlled pipeline with adversarially modified documents, they report up to 57% post-rationalisation. Other studies examine how LLMs handle conflicts between parametric knowledge and external evidence, from external behaviour (Xie et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024) to internal representations (Ortu et al., 2024; Halawi et al., 2024). We use post-cutoff facts to study citation faithfulness under both live and controlled web search. 3 Setup Post-cutoff temporal facts. To study evidence use in a controllable setting, we construct post- cutoff temporal facts, whose values changed after the models’ training cutoffs. As a common thresh- old, we use 16 February 2026, the latest reported training cutoff among the evaluated models (Ope- nAI, 2026a). We exclude a change if it took effect on or before this date or was announced before 1 February 2026, and we exclude FDA claims that can be answered from standard medical knowledge. We draw 225 treatment facts across six domains (FDA 62, finance 52, sports 47, people 43, SEC 13, chemical 8), plus control facts that did not change across the cutoff. To estimate how much can be inferred without search, we report Luna’s closed-book accuracy on open questions (23.3%, 24/103). All extraction is deterministic. The one model-assisted step, rewriting FDA label clauses as atomic claims, is reviewed manually for every fact (Appendix B). Models and retrieval. We use two search set- tings to examine whether failures observed in de- ployment recur across models. First, we evalu- ate GPT-5.6 Luna (OpenAI, 2026b), a proprietary LLM, under its native web search (the deployed run). We then evaluate the same model and six open-weight model configurations under controlled retrieval: GLM-5.3-Flash (Z.ai, 2026), gpt-oss- 120b and gpt-oss-20b (OpenAI, 2025), gemma-4- 31b (with and without reasoning; Google Deep- Mind, 2026) and qwen3-8b (Qwen Team, 2025). Frozen retrieval covers 223 of the 225 facts, and for each one every model receives the same ten-result search menu, built once from Brave Search (Brave, 2026). Models navigate these results with the same search, open-page and find tools. The ground-truth page keeps its rank if it is in the organic top ten; otherwise, it is inserted at a seeded position, so it is always reachable. Temperature is 0 where exposed, and serving providers are pinned (Appendix A). Tasks. We test each fact with two main prompt types. An open question (“Which club does Bernardo Silva play for?”) is asked bare and with a source requested. A claim check (“Is the fol- lowing accurate? ...”) carries either the current or the outdated value, so endorsement of the stale claim is read against its true twin. The pooled rates below also draw on four further prompt types (Ap- pendix C): cued open questions, quote requests, ci- tation requests (attach a source to a supplied claim) and fact-sheet checks (find the one stale line among true ones).
