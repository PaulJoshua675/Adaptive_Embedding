Their attention is close to uniform on MNIST (H/Hmax = 0.97, about 44 of 49 patches) and moderately selective on CIFAR (0.90, about 42 of 64), but on both, freezing or swapping the query moves accuracy by at most a few tenths of a percent, and if anything upwards (frozen/swapped: +0.0%/+0.0% on MNIST, +0.3%/+0.2% on CIFAR), so the query plays no real role: where they attend does not depend on the thought. Only removing or flattening attention hurts. Entropy falls reliably on both, and there the state’s step does line up with the entropy gradient over part of the thought, then turns away from it while entropy keeps falling; the memory term takes over past the crossing. On CIFAR the alignment is immediate and brief (peak +0.69 at tick 1, crossing at tick 5), while on MNIST it builds up and lasts longer (peak +0.65 at tick 9, crossing at tick 17); on ImageNet the early, aligned phase is absent, and the memory carries the descent from the start. In all cases the learned forgetting rates confirm that the memory keeps integrating rather than saturating: the earliest contributions still carry substantial weight at the decision tick (median retention 0.90 on MNIST, 0.63 on CIFAR). Discussion CTM inference on ImageNet tracks a curve of fixed points, largely shared across images, while attention follows a schedule that is shared as well. The attracting, non-normal maps we uncover suggest how the CTM transiently amplifies activity while staying stable. Non- normal recurrence as a substrate for transient amplification and memory has been studied in neural circuits (Ganguli et al., 2008; Murphy and Miller, 2009; Goldman, 2009; Hennequin et al., 2014; Bondanelli and Ostojic, 2020) and built into trained recurrent networks (Kerg et al., 2019); locating and linearizing around the fixed points of a trained network follows Sussillo and Barak (2013); Golub and Sussillo (2018); Maheswaranathan et al. (2019). What differs here is that the map, and with it the fixed point, is redefined at every tick. On ImageNet, where the CTM attends next can be predicted one step ahead, and the model might gain freedom from image-dependent decay rates, a variant of fast-weight memories (Ba et al., 2016; Schlag et al., 2021). The results are for one checkpoint, a subsample of validation images, and image classification. 12
