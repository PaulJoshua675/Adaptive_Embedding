Full REPRO reaches the highest final ASR on both targets. Component-level interpretation. Semantic crossover is the most consistently impactful component, losing 4–5 behaviors on each target. Without multi-parent recombination, each attack lineage must discover the full jailbreak through mutation alone and fails on behaviors that require structural combination of independently discovered partial solutions. Bayesian strategy selection is equally impactful, losing 4–6 behaviors; replacing Thompson Sampling with uniform random selection removes the ability to concentrate the exploration budget on strategies with demonstrated effec- tiveness for the current target. Elitism contributes primarily on GPT-4o-mini, where the loss of elite preservation forces the population to rediscover partial solutions each generation; the weaker effect on Llama-3.3-70B (CI includes zero) suggests that elitism’s benefit depends on the frequency of partially successful candidates worth preserving. Failure analysis shows no measurable ASR contribution on either target under the 100-iteration budget. We attribute this to the iteration constraint: failure analysis is designed to guide systematic counter-strategy exploration on stubborn behaviors that resist many iterations of attack, and the 100-iteration budget may be insufficient for this mechanism to demonstrate its advantage over unguided mutation. On GPT-4o-mini, the convergence trajectory confirms that removing failure analysis actually accelerates convergence, indicating that the module adds per-iteration overhead not offset by improved guidance when refusal patterns are weak and unsystematic. On Llama-3.3-70B, where refusal patterns are more structured, no such overhead is detectable. Reflective model choice. As a complementary robustness check, we compare the default Mistral-7B reflective model against the larger Mistral-3.1-Small (24B) on the full AdvBench-50 evaluation. Both achieve 100% ASR across all evaluated targets, confirming that REPRO’s effectiveness is robust to model scale within the Mistral family. Cross-family variation (e.g., using Llama or Qwen as the reflective LLM) is an important direction for future work; our results establish that the framework does not require a large reflective model but do not rule out family-specific effects.
