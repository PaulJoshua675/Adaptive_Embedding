Similar pass@k can conceal substantial differ- ences in strategy diversity. Despite achieving nearly identical pass@128 on BIRD, ELITERE- PLAY produces almost 50% more distinct correct strategies than MaxRL, with U@128 of 9.1 ver- sus 6.2 (Figure 3, right). Thus, MaxRL recovers coverage while relying on fewer ways of solving each question, a distinction that pass@k does not capture. The diversity gap is even larger on Spider, where ELITEREPLAY produces nearly 80% more distinct correct strategies than the strongest base- line (9.4 versus 5.3). On BIRD, ELITEREPLAY also matches or exceeds the base model’s U@k up to k = 64, despite substantially higher pass@1, while all baselines fall increasingly behind it at larger budgets. Effective diversity provides complementary evi- dence (Figure 5). ELITEREPLAY achieves the high- est scores on both benchmarks, reaching 25.9% on BIRD versus 23.4% for the strongest baseline. On Spider, it is the only post-training method to exceed the base model (31.4% versus 27.9%). 5.2 Preserving base-model strategies ELITEREPLAY retains substantially more of the base model’s correct strategies. U@k counts distinct strategies, whereas strategy retention tracks which of the base model’s strategies survive post- training. As shown in Figure 6, the baselines retain only 34–39% of these strategies on BIRD and 30– 37% on Spider, whereas ELITEREPLAY retains 62% and 60%, respectively. ELITEREPLAY preserves broader coverage across individual strategies. To examine whether the lost strategies are concentrated in a few approaches or spread across many, we measure how often each of the base model’s most common correct strategies are still produced across questions, relative to its original coverage (Appendix Figure 9; details in Appendix B.6). The baselines lose coverage on nearly every strategy, with reductions of up to 28 percentage points on BIRD. In contrast, ELITEREPLAY shows smaller reductions on every strategy, never exceeding 9 percentage points. The same pattern holds on Spider (Appendix C.4). The baselines often collapse onto a single strat- egy, while ELITEREPLAY preserves alternatives. To examine how strategy loss manifests at the question level, we compare the distribution of cor- rect samples across strategies for three represen- tative BIRD questions (Figure 7; details in Ap- pendix B.8). While the baselines often concentrate nearly all correct samples on a single approach, ELITEREPLAY maintains multiple strategies closer to the base model’s original distribution. Notably, the baselines sometimes converge on an approach that the base model rarely used, showing that strat- egy collapse does not necessarily preserve the base model’s preferred behavior, as Zhao et al. (2025) also observe for RL on math.
