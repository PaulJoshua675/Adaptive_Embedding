The function σβ(u) = (1 + e−βu)−1 is a sigmoidal activation function whose steepness β = 5.0 reflects the cooperative threshold firing response of hypothalamic neuronal ensembles [39, 8]. The physiological inputs to the system are grounded in known neuroanatomy: • Homeostatic Somnogenic Drive (+wsvS(t), wsv = 1.6): Extracellular adenosine excites VLPO neurons directly via postsynaptic A2A receptors (excitatory G-protein coupled re- ceptors) and disinhibits the VLPO (relieving target neurons from upstream inhibition) by suppressing presynaptic GABAergic inputs via inhibitory A1 receptors [29, 8]. • Circadian Alerting Drive (−wcvC(t) and +wcaC(t), wcv = 0.9, wca = 1.1): Efferent projections from the SCN relay through the dorsomedial hypothalamic nucleus (DMH) to stimulate wake-promoting LHA orexinergic and monoaminergic nuclei during subjective day, while simultaneously providing indirect GABAergic inhibition to the VLPO [32, 16]. • Orexinergic Wake Stabilization (+ωorexin · Ωtone, ωorexin = 1.5): Peptidergic projections from hypocretin/orexin neurons in the LHA project excitatory inputs to monoaminergic nuclei (locus coeruleus, dorsal raphe, and tuberomammillary nucleus), stabilizing the wake-active attractor [26, 32, 34]. Ωtone ∈[0, 1] represents the intactness of endogenous hypocretin peptide availability (Ωtone = 1.0 in intact physiology; Ωtone ≈0 under complete neurodegeneration). • Hyperarousal Bias (Iarousal ∈R): Represents ascending depolarizing drive from the limbic system, prefrontal cortex, and sympathetic axis induced by psychological stress or conditioned cognitive arousal [30]. • Basal Rheobase Thresholds (θv = −0.5, θa = −0.3): Ensure that in the absence of net positive synaptic input, baseline neuronal membrane potentials remain subthreshold and quiescent [27]. Mathematical Derivation of Bistability and Hysteresis. The mutual inhibitory weights wav = 2.1 and wva = 2.0 are derived from bifurcation analysis of the two-variable Wilson-Cowan system [39, 27]. The nullclines of Equations (3) and (4) are obtained by setting dV/dt = 0 and dA/dt = 0: V = σβ (wsvS(t) −wcvC(t) −wavA + θv) , (5) A = σβ (wcaC(t) + ωorexinΩtone −wvaV + Iarousal + θa) . (6) In a mutual inhibitory dynamical system with sigmoidal activation functions σβ(u), bistability arises through a saddle-node or pitchfork bifurcation when mutual inhibitory coupling becomes sufficiently strong to split a single central uncommitted state into two stable steady states [39, 27]. Because max(σ′ β(u)) = β/4, the necessary criterion for bistability requires that the product of the mutual inhibitory gains exceeds the inverse product of the maximum slopes of the activation functions: wav · wva > 4 β . (7) For our population steepness β = 5.0, the critical bifurcation threshold is 4/β = 0.8. With wav = 2.1 and wva = 2.0, the interaction gain is wav · wva = 4.2 ≫0.8. This configuration places the hypothalamic switch firmly within the bistable regime, establishing two distinct stable attractors: a consolidated wake state (A ≈1, V ≈0) and a consolidated sleep state (V ≈1, A ≈0), separated by an unstable threshold manifold. This non-equilibrium bistability generates wide hysteresis (the dependence of the state of the system on its prior history, meaning greater excitatory drive is needed to switch states than to maintain an existing state), ensuring that behavioral transitions are decisive and resistant to baseline fluctuations [32, 33].
