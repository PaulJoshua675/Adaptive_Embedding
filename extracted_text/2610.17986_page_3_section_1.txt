We organize prior work by which axis of cost it addresses. Training-side scaling laws predict error from training data/compute. Evaluation-side work either subsamples the query axis or estimates metrics from sampled judgments. We occupy a different cell: predicting the metric along the dis- tractor/gallery axis. Distractor-scaling benchmarks (the cost we remove). Revisited Oxford/Paris add the R1M hard-distractor set (Radenovi´c et al., 2018), Google Landmarks indexes millions (Weyand et al., 2020), and ILIAS scales to 100M distractors (Kordopatis-Zilos et al., 2025). These works measure how accuracy falls as the gallery grows and report a few fixed operating points. None make the metric-versus-scale curve estimable from a sample or use it to cut evaluation cost. We do. The ILIAS authors do release mini-ILIAS, a VLM-mined 5M-distractor proxy that retains the hardest distractors as a cheaper stand-in for the full pool (Kordopatis-Zilos et al., 2025). It reports a number on a deliberately harder subset, whereas we predict the true 100M number from a uniform random sample. A hard-mined subset is non-uniform by design, so it does not yield the size-independent hardness our estimator needs. Collection-size analysis in classical IR (closest in spirit). The relationship between collection size and retrieval effectiveness was modeled analytically two decades ago. Madigan et al. (2003) derive the distribution of irrelevant documents outscoring the relevant ones (“contamination at K”). This is a Binomial/Poisson sampled-rank model whose K=1 case is our Theorem 1. Theirs as- sumes a single score distribution shared by all queries, with no sample-based estimation (which they pose as future work). The same authors later applied extreme-value theory to the growing-collection asymptotics of retrieval effectiveness (Madigan et al., 2006), still for a single shared score distribu- tion and without sample-based estimation or 100M-scale validation. Hawking & Robertson (2003) empirically observe that mAP rankings are largely preserved between a sample and the full collec- tion. We supply what this line lacked: the heterogeneous per-query law, a sample-based tail-aware estimator, and validation at 100M. Sampled / low-cost retrieval evaluation (judgment axis). A long IR tradition estimates met- rics from incomplete or sampled relevance judgments: bpref (Buckley & Voorhees, 2004), statAP/infAP (Yilmaz et al., 2008; Yilmaz & Aslam, 2006), and minimal test collections (Carterette et al., 2006). In recommendation, Krichene and Rendle (Krichene & Rendle, 2020) show sampled top-k metrics are biased and collapse toward AUC, and propose corrected estimators (an unbiased rank estimator and a bias–variance variant); a subsequent line learns the global rank distribution by maximum likelihood (Jin et al., 2021; Li et al., 2023). These estimate an aggregate rank distri- bution (Recall/NDCG@k over a catalogue) and cure the sampling “blind spot” by sampling more items, not by extrapolating the unobserved tail. Our count baseline (Sec. 5) is the Krichene–Rendle unbiased estimator specialized to the top rank; because mAP depends on the per-positive hard tail (invisible below 1/S) it fails to recover the large-N value, and enlarging the sample to resolve that tail is exactly the web-scale indexing we avoid—which is why we instead extrapolate it. 3
