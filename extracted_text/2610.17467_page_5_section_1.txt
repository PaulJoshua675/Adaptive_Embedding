We follow prior work (Tay et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2023) to report Hit@N, which is defined as the proportion of queries for which the relevant document appears in the top-N retrieved results. Because distinct documents can share one DocID, we solve such ties by expectation (McSh- erry and Najork, 2008): All documents sharing a retrieved identifier are returned, and each metric is its expected value over all orderings of the tied documents. 4.4 Baselines All baselines are generative retrievers, the paradigm we follow. Our core research question is whether an alternative numeric DocID design improves gen- eralization to newly added documents, so we focus on numeric DocID baselines. Following recent studies of generative retrieval over dynamic corpora (Zhang et al., 2025, 2026b), we cover four numeric DocID designs: hierarchi- cal k-means, used by DSI (Tay et al., 2022) and NCI (Wang et al., 2022); PQ, used by ULTRON- PQ (Zhou et al., 2022); RQ, used by MEVI (Zhang et al., 2023); RQ-VAE, used by TIGER (Rajput et al., 2023).
