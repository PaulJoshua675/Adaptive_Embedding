Intermediate rescue rates have not been mapped. 4.3 The pianist, exactly Polanyi observed that a pianist who shifts attention from the music to his fingers may falter (Polanyi 1958). Full rescue is the algebraic analogue. The attempt to make every step of the traversal exactly inspectable removes the small per-step imperfection, CR1 ≈0.997, whose accumulation was the traversal’s skill. The change is not metaphorical: the within-frontier ordering switches from a CR2 ranking with historical content to a lexicographic one without it. In both cases the skill was never stored as an item; it is a property of how the integration runs, and it returns when the integration is allowed to run again—for the pianist when attention goes back to the music, for VaCoAl/PyVaCoAl when RR goes back to 0. 4.4 Articulation at the level of law The valuation is regime-bound, but it is not ineffable in absolute terms. Its magnitude is predicted in closed form: with CR1 ≈0.997 at sufficient memory depth, CR2 at generation 56 should be about 0.99756 ≈0.846, against a measured value of about 0.905 across 25.5 million paths, with the residual consistent with survivor bias along deep paths (2026d). The system cannot say why it prefers direct routes. Its designers can, as a law about the whole regime rather than as an item retrieved from it. This is the structure of Collins’s somatic tacit knowledge: the cyclist cannot state the balancing rule, while physics can (Collins 2010). “We cannot tell” therefore splits into two statements that are both true at once—the system cannot tell at the object level, and an outside theory can tell at the level of law. Why does the regime produce a valuation at all? Because the search is bounded. On a DAG whose paths grow multiplicatively with depth, an absolute exact match over all paths cannot exist, so VaCoAl/PyVaCoAl builds in a cognitive bound, the frontier size, and ranks the candidates inside it by CR2 (2026d, Appendix C). Without the bound every path could be kept and none would need to be preferred. This places tacit valuation where Simon placed satisficing: it is what a bounded searcher uses to decide what to keep when it cannot keep everything (Simon 1955). Polanyi’s name for what guides a discoverer among too many possibilities was “intellectual passion” (Polanyi 1958, ch. 6); in the substrate, the corresponding guide is a closed-form decay that favours direct routes. 12
