In this work, E2E latency refers to the online query-time cost, consisting of two compo- nents: (1) query encoding into a sparse representation via LLM inference, and (2) sparse retrieval over the inverted index using conventional term matching. This two-part decomposition is crucial for understanding the practical deploya- bility of LLM-based sparse retrievers, as the encoding cost often dominates the overall latency in real-world settings. While Lion-sp achieves strong effectiveness (Table 3), it suffers from severe inefficiency due to highly dense representations (L0 = 1170), leading to sub- stantial storage overhead (83GB) and latency exceeding 1000ms per query.
