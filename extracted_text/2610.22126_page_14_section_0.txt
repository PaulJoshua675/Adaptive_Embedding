The Fourier component captures periodic structure: ϕfourier(xi) = [sin(ω1zi), cos(ω1zi), . . . , sin(ωKf zi), cos(ωKf zi)], (16) where ωf denotes the frequency associated with Fourier feature f; these frequencies are set as hyperparameters. Since both sine and cosine terms are included for each frequency, this component contributes 2Kf basis functions per feature. The total number of basis functions per feature is: Q = D + Kt + 2Kf. C Axiomatic properties Since our method belongs to the family of path methods [Sundararajan et al., 2017], it inherits many of the axiomatic properties of integrated gradients. We discuss each property in turn: a statement, a proof (or, for the one axiom we do not satisfy, an explanation of why), and, where our method requires more than the standard axiom does, the additional preconditions this needs. We would expect other dependency-aware attribution methods (e.g. causal Shapley values, Heskes et al., 2020; conditional SHAP, Aas et al., 2021) to require analogous preconditions on the supplied dependency structure, though to our knowledge this has not previously been examined for those methods.
