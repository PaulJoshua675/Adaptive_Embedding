We use R = 3 temporal nodes for the six-second AV2 horizon and R = 4 for the eight-second WOMD horizon. Map and social neighborhoods are radius-based, keeping the lifted graph sparse as the number of agents grows. We optimize FutureLift with AdamW [14] using a total batch size of 64. Training lasts 40 epochs: the learning rate is linearly warmed up to 5 × 10−4 during the first epoch and then de- cayed to zero with a cosine schedule over the remaining 39 epochs. We apply no data augmentation. For every AV2 and WOMD validation experiment trained in our study, we use seeds 42, 1337, and 2026 and report the arithmetic mean over the three runs. The same seeds are used for every com- parison refiner that we train; the daggered SRefiner values are published results and are therefore excluded from this seed-averaging statement. Metric-wise standard deviations do not exceed 3 × 10−3 across our validation runs, so we report the means in the main tables and summarize disper- sion here rather than appending a deviation to every cell. For each run, we select the checkpoint with the lowest vali- dation loss. This seed averaging applies only to validation- split results. For the official WOMD test submission, we randomly select one of the three trained seeds rather than choosing a model based on test performance. A.4. Efficiency Protocol We report only the computation added by refinement. Pa- rameter counts exclude the frozen proposal backbone, while GFLOPs and latency cover the refiner under a common batch and scene protocol. Measurements use an NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada Generation GPU with PyTorch 2.5, FP32 precision, and a batch size of one. After 50 warm-up runs, latency is reported as the arithmetic mean over 10 timed runs.
