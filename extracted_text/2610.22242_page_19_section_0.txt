Kaplan-Meier Estimator (Kaplan and Meier, 1958): A non-parametric statistic used to estimate the marginal survival probability from observed lifetime data. Because it does not take individual covariates into account, it serves as the most basic population- level baseline for survival functions and is consistent. Cox Model (Cox Proportional Hazards) (Cox, 1972): The standard semiparametric workhorse of survival analysis. It models the hazard rate as a prod- uct of a time-varying baseline hazard and an expo- nential linear combination of the covariates. It relies on the strong assumption of proportional hazards (the hazard ratio between any two individuals is constant over time).
