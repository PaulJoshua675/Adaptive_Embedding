The memory required to train large language models limits the batch sizes and sequence lengths that fit on an accelerator. Alongside weights, gradients, and optimizer states, backpropagation requires activations retained from the forward pass. Parameter-related storage is largely independent of input size, whereas saved activations and some temporary buffers grow with the number of tokens processed simultaneously. Reducing this input-dependent memory cost is therefore important even when the model weights already fit on the device. Feed-forward layers are a natural target. They expand each token’s hidden representation, typically by a factor of 3–5, and retain intermediate values for computing weight gradients. While the original Transformer Vaswani et al. (2017) used quadratic memory, more recent memory-efficient attention (Rabe and Staats, 2022; Dao et al., 2022) and state-space architectures such as Mamba (Gu and Dao, 2024) are both linear in the number of tokens, so the feedforward layers make up a significant portion of the memory cost. Certain activations produce sparse outputs that can be exploited to reduce memory usage. The ReLU activation (ReLU) maps nonpositive inputs to exact zeros (Nair and Hinton, 2010). Large language models (LLMs) have used alternative activations such as GELU Hendrycks and Gimpel (2016) and gated activations such as SwiGLU. However, recently squared ReLU (ReLU2) activations have demonstrated competitive efficiency in LLMs (So et al., 2021) and are used in the Nemotron family and Modded-NanoGPT (Blakeman et al., 2025; Basant et al., 2025; Adler et al., 2024; Jordan et al., 2024a). Sparsity naturally emerges in training without any sparsity-inducing objective (Li et al., 2023; Mirzadeh et al., 2024). In our measurements, the pretrained Nemotron-H model has approximately 92% feed-forward activation sparsity. Exploiting this redundancy requires an inexpensive enough representation to use during backpropagation with minimal latency. Alternatives such as activation checkpointing reduce storage by recomputing forward intermediates (Chen et al., 2016), while lossy activation compression trades accuracy for memory (Chen et al., 2021; Shamshoum et al., 2025). This raises a simple question: can the exact zeros already produced by ReLU-family LLMs be converted into activation-memory savings without recomputation or approximation? ∗Preprint.
