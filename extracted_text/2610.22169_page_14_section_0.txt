Context length. A longer context offers more positions to attend to and therefore more attention that a frame of finite order withholds, so long se- quences test the orders directly. We repeated the readings on 64 sequences of 1024 tokens, with the typical attention outputs estimated on the same number of calibration tokens. The divergence of LEXFRAME[1] from the model’s output rises only from 0.16 and 0.15 to 0.25 and 0.18 nats; the order of all methods is unchanged, with LEXFRAME[0] at 1.11 and 1.06, LEXFRAME[2] at 0.15 and 0.08 and the lenses at 6.04 or more, and LEXFRAME[1] still agrees with the model’s top token on 0.84 and 0.86 of the states. The attention among the prefix po- sitions that LEXFRAME[1] withholds grows with the context, but at intermediate layers it contributes lit- tle to what a state means to the model, so a longer prefix adds little to the divergence.
