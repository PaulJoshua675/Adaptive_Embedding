A Acquisition and compression calibration (Experiment I) The acquisition control is calibrated in a linear Gaussian setting where observation loss and compression loss can be distinguished. This follows the established structure of indirect source coding, rather than introducing a new non-recoverability principle (Shannon, 1959; Wolf & Ziv, 1970). Under squared error, for a target X, acquired data Y , and a predictor g(Y ), conditional-expectation orthogonality gives E∥X −g(Y )∥2 = E∥X −E[X | Y ]∥2 + E∥E[X | Y ] −g(Y )∥2. (4) The first term cannot be reduced merely by expanding a code constructed from the same Y . A representation constraint can further increase the second term. For two correlated three-dimensional targets mixed by a seed-specific random orthogonal transformation, we enumerate channel subsets at observation budgets Bobs ∈{2, 3, 4, 6} and fit optimal rank-dZ linear representations for dZ ∈{1, . . . , 6}. There are 8,192 training and 4,096 test samples per seed, across 20 seeds. The unconstrained mean distortion is 0.0004; acquisition-only and representation-only constraints increase it by 0.3956 and 0.3262, respectively. These are calibration contrasts from the acquisition–representation grid. The best two-channel subsets under the two target weightings have mean Jaccard overlap 0.0167, versus 0.2444 for random subsets. This is a manipulation check for task-dependent acquisition, not an identity forced by the target labels after mixing. A separate analytic check uses a rank-three encoder, for which balanced population error is DA = DB = (1 −ρ)/2. Across six correlations, gradient training with optimal readout matches this curve with RMSE 0.0030 and maximum error 0.0042. Expanding from three to six dimensions improves distortion by 0.3236 in this separate calibration, not the 0.3262 grid contrast. The sequential test supplies four observations, each of A or B, giving 16 fixed schedules. A noisy initial cue predicts the later target demand; a cue-dependent policy chooses a complete schedule once, rather than repeatedly tracking an internal learned state. Selection uses 30,000 training and 10,000 validation samples, followed by 20,000 independent test samples. The best fixed objective is 0.181864; the cue-dependent objective is 0.124223, a 31.7% reduction. Shuffling the cue yields 0.181039 with the same policy class. The gain therefore depends on cue information rather than the larger class alone. Temporal dependence also couples neighboring schedules. If Ls(W) and Lt(W) are endpoint-policy losses, the shared-decoder excess B(α) = min W {(1 −α)Ls(W) + αLt(W)} −(1 −α) min W Ls(W) −α min W Lt(W) (5) is nonnegative for 0 ≤α ≤1 by construction. For an interior mixture and attained minima, zero excess requires a common minimizing decoder. The measured result is the frequency with which this common- optimum condition fails: positive excess occurs on 0/32 neighboring paths at temporal correlation ϕ = 0, 4/32 at 0.4, and 18/32 at 0.8. The maximum is 0.039801, and dedicated decoders remove the excess. At ϕ = 0.8, the exact population policy objective has two local optima; 13/20 jointly trained seeds reach the oracle schedule. B Formation, current constraints, and feedback controls (II–III) Experiment II uses a hidden-width-24 tanh encoder and linear heads, with 236 parameters at dZ = 2 and 267 at dZ = 3. One target is updated per mini-batch. Its principal reinforcement rule is pA(st) = 0.5+α tanh(βst), with α = 0.40 and β = 1.5; exploration changes the plus to minus. Balanced updating sets pA = 0.5, and initial-fixed updating freezes pA(s0). The history-removal, no-history feedback, and mirrored-history tests are separate. In the first, a strong imposed history difference falls from 1.8822 to 0.0047 under common later learning (residual ratio 0.002); a weak common bias gives ratio 0.003. In the second, confirmatory seeds 300–339 have no imposed initial history: terminal |s| is 0.1817 (balanced), 0.4207 (fixed), 0.8683 (state-following), and 0.0580 (exploratory).
