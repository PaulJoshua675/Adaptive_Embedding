C.3 4D-DRESS: AMODAL 3D LOCALISATION Each of the 128 dynamic takes supplies sixteen frames from one camera, beginning at its first frame. All methods receive identical crops and predict points at the first frame’s reference time. For each method, one similarity transform is robustly fitted using only reference-frame pixel corre- spondences to the scan, then applied unchanged to every frame’s predictions. Each method discards its least confident 5 % of points across the sixteen-frame union; reference-frame and temporal statistics use this same retained point set. We report mean one-sided point-to-scan distance in centimetres and the fractions exceeding 2 and 5 cm, averaging these statistics over takes. The distance is one-sided because the observations do not cover the full scan; regions hidden in the reference image are included when observed in other frames. Error bars are standard errors over takes. C.4 EVALUATION USING SYNTHETIC DATA The conditioning ablation uses 128 tracks selected at a fixed stride through BEDLAM2’s sorted official test split, with four evenly spaced frames per track. We measure Euclidean distance from each predicted point to its ground-truth reference-time position. Known reference-camera geometry and each clip’s ground-truth scale (Eq. (2)) convert errors to centimetres before averaging, without fitted alignment or confidence filtering. The temporal analysis uses the same error and scale convention (Appendix D.2). D ABLATIONS AND ANALYSIS D.1 CROP ZOOM AND VIEWING DIRECTION The crop embedding encodes both the centre ray and crop zoom (Eq. (3)); we examine their roles separately. Removing the entire embedding increases BEDLAM2 pointwise reconstruction error from 2.99 to 4.06 cm and reduces Panoptic Studio Humans tracking from 38.4 to 30.4 AJ and from 50.3 to 41.4 APD (Tab. 4). The unconditioned variant has higher reconstruction error on 111 of 128 test tracks and lower tracking AJ on 150 of 156 clips. Retaining crop zoom while fixing the three centre-ray channels to (0, 0, 1) at inference gives equiva- lent reconstruction and tracking performance at the reported precision. This is expected because the rotation-induced homography already reorients each crop towards the person. The result suggests that crop zoom carries the useful conditioning information after this rectification. The reported checkpoint retains the centre-ray inputs used during training, but their variation provides no measurable benefit in this experiment.
