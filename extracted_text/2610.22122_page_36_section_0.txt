E.1 Local Hit Enrichment and Discovery Gains on Real Tasks Local hit enrichment. We compare the hit fraction around hit candidates with the overall hit fraction in the analysis sample to measure how hits cluster in candidate representation space. Neighbors are determined by cosine distance after standardizing the fixed candidate representations 𝜙(𝑥) and applying L2 normalization. For each task, we randomly sample hit candidates and other candidates in their proportions in the full candidate pool, forming an analysis sample P of at most 10,000 candidates. Let P+ = {𝑥∈P : 𝑧(𝑥) = 1} be its hit set. For each hit candidate 𝑥, let N10(𝑥) contain the ten nearest other candidates within P. We compute the hit fraction among these ten neighbors, average it over all hit candidates, and divide by the overall hit fraction in the sample to obtain Enrich10 = 1 |P+| ∑︁ 𝑥∈P+ 1 10 ∑︁ 𝑦∈N10(𝑥) 𝑧(𝑦) |P+|/|P| . (74) The numerator is the mean neighbor hit fraction around hit candidates, and the denominator is the overall hit fraction in the analysis sample. For example, an overall hit fraction of 5% and a mean neighbor hit fraction of 15% give 3× enrichment. Values above one indicate that other hits are more common near hit candidates. Values near one indicate a similar hit fraction to the sample overall, and values below one indicate a lower local hit fraction. We repeat sampling and calculation three times for each task. For each task, we divide each method’s final cumulative hit count by Random’s count under the same seed, then average over five seeds. The shared methods are PoH, OAE, UCB, EI, TS, TopUncertain, BADGE, CoreSet, and KMeans-Embedding. Their average gain is the mean of these nine methods’ results. Across the eight tasks, this average gain increases with local hit enrichment (Spearman 𝜌= 0.95, 𝑛= 8). Both enrichment and discovery gains are near random levels on NK and higher on Simpson K562, showing a close association between local hit structure and active discovery gains across tasks. We construct landscapes in which hits are dispersed or concentrated in six clusters. The 17,660 candidates have fixed two-dimensional coordinates that are defined without phenotype information. KMeans divides these coordinates into six regions, each with a response peak at its center. Within each region, candidates closer to the center receive higher response scores. We sort candidates by distance, negate their ranks, and divide by the region’s hit quota. The quotas are 148 for one region and 147 for each of the other five, giving 883 hits in total.
