Comparing compute-optimal losses at each budget separates two effects (Figure 2): the backbone cost, the gap between dense RWKV and the Transformer, and the spiking cost, the gap between spiking and dense RWKV. The backbone cost stays between 0.15 and 0.17 nats at every budget (Table 2, Appendix A). The spiking cost falls from 0.338 to 0.263 to 0.227 nats between 3 × 1017 and 3 × 1018 FLOPs, then holds at 0.225 at 1019. With four budgets we cannot tell whether it has stopped falling or is only falling more slowly. At 1019 we add a width-2048 dense RWKV run above the compute-optimal size, so its minimum is not at the edge of the sampled widths. Finally, spike rates depend on training progress rather than model size, rising toward a ∼0.36 firing fraction as tokens-per-parameter grows (Appendix H). 5 Conclusion and Limitations Under total-parameter counting, the compute-optimal frontier of a spiking language model is NT,opt ∝C0.556, close to dense Transformers’ 0.5; larger non-embedding exponents come from parameter counting, not the architecture. About 0.16 nats of the gap to a Transformer comes from the RWKV backbone and does not change with compute, while the cost of spiking falls to about 0.23 nats by 1019 FLOPs. These results come from four tiers between 3 × 1017 and 1019 FLOPs, far below Chinchilla’s 1021–1024, on FineWeb-EDU only, with the aspect ratio d/l drifting from 38 to 53 across widths. The baselines use one seed per configuration, so whether the spiking cost stays near 0.23 nats at larger scale is untested; more tiers beyond the 1020-FLOP point (Section 4.2) would test both this and the power law.
