Across methods, matching depth almost matches the parameter count: TIPS has 331,395 parameters at one layer and 628,739 at two, compared with 331,265 and 628,609 for the unmasking-only policy. TIPS retains its advantage at either matched depth (Table 11). At T = 32, the one-layer full-canvas TIPS policy reaches 53.03%, compared with 43.85% for the one-layer comparison policy; at two layers, the respective accuracies are 56.15% and 40.33%. Moreover, doubling the comparison policy’s depth does not reproduce the improvement from TIPS. Its accuracy falls from 32.32% to 20.70% at T = 16, and from 43.85% to 40.33% at T = 32, while the two depths become similar at larger decoder budgets. These single-seed comparisons show that the reported advantage is not removed by matching controller depth, without attributing it to any one of TIPS’s other design choices. For this depth comparison, we use the vanilla (non-expert-steered) variant of Jazbec et al. (2026) at both depths, motivated by its stronger low-budget performance in our reproduction. table 1 instead reports the expert-steered variant, which achieves higher accuracy at large budgets. The validation split additionally places greater weight on the harder MATH dataset (50%, compared with 27.48% in the test mixture): it involves high-school competition-level mathematics requiring complex, multi-step symbolic reasoning, whereas GSM8K comprises elementary- and middle-school-level math word problems involving only basic arithmetic and simple multi-step algebraic manipulation. Thus, differences from table 1 reflect changes in both the baseline variant and evaluation population. The compute interpretation also differs between methods. Both TIPS depths use their entire allocated horizon, whereas the comparison policy may terminate early. At cap 64, its deeper controller consumes 50.30 decoder calls on average, compared with 35.42 for the one-layer controller, despite their nearly identical accuracies.
