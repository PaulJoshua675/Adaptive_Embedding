On average, HL achieves a 1.99× speedup over CE, demonstrating that hierarchical supervision not only improves recommendation quality but also enables more efficient optimization. Ablation Studies. We conduct ablation on the Toys dataset to examine the contribution of hierarchical supervision and its indi- vidual components. Table 4 shows that progressively supervising additional semantic-ID levels consistently improves NDCG@10. Performance increases from 0.0355 with supervision at the first level to 0.0492 when all four levels are supervised, demonstrating that finer-grained semantic codes provide complementary supervi- sion. Table 5 further isolates the contributions of the hierarchical loss components. Static weighting (SW) assigns fixed weights of 4:3:2:1 to the four SID levels, emphasizing coarser semantic decisions. Con- ditional gating (CG) uses confidence-aware weights, strengthening downstream supervision when preceding predictions are reliable. Scheduled sampling (SS) gradually replaces the ground-truth 𝐿1 to- ken with model predictions to reduce the teacher-forcing mismatch. Each component improves over causal CE, with CG providing the largest individual gain. The full configuration combines SW, CG, and SS, yielding the best performance and improving Recall@10 and NDCG@10 by 44.3% and 50.0%, respectively, over causal CE. The substantial improvement from the combined setup suggests that the three mechanisms provide complementary benefits: level- aware weighting, confidence-adaptive supervision, and robustness to autoregressive errors. Further details on the hyperparameter settings used in this ablation are provided in the supplementary material.
