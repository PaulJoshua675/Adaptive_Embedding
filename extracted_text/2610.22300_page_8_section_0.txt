Re-randomization. Each new relation involves two random choices: the bijection π from answers to labels, and the fine-tuning seed. To measure how much Generalization depends on them, we train some model–relation pairs three times, each time with its own π and fine-tuning seed. The three runs share everything else: the subject split, the label subset L, the templates and the exposure per fact. We run every relation three times on MiniCPM5-2B-Base, and the Hernandez and Town relations three times on every model except Qwen3.8-27B. All other results use the first of the three runs. 5 RESULTS Our main finding is that pretrained relations whose answers are linearly readable in the pretrained model give rise to new relations that, after fine-tuning, generalize better. This holds in all 11 models we test. Across models, Pre-linearity and Generalization on relations correlate at r = 0.87 to 0.92 and ρ = 0.85 to 0.90, and no 95% interval falls below 0.72 (Table 1, Figure 2). Base and post- trained models from 2B to 27B parameters land in this band. We then check three things: (i) that the association survives controls for Recall, answer count, subject count and subject frequency; (ii) that Pre-linearity is a better predictor of Generalization than Recall (Figure 3a); and (iii) that the association depends on neither the label set nor the random bijection and fine-tuning seed behind each new relation (Figure 3b; §4, Table 2). Finally, we look at the errors. When a fine-tuned model fails to generalize, its wrong answers follow the Pre-linearity probe more than Recall (Figure 4). Controls. Several properties of a relation could raise both measures (Pre-linearity and General- ization) at once: how well the pretrained model recalls it, how many answers and subjects it has, and how frequent its subjects are in pretraining text. None of them explains the association away. Controlling for any one of the four leaves every model’s r between 0.77 and 0.91 and ρ between 0.79 and 0.90.
