These datasets cover struc- tured offline retrieval, open-domain document re- trieval, and online search for long-form translation. Baselines. All open-model systems use Qwen2.5- 7B-Instruct as the default backbone. Direct trans- lates without explicit reasoning or retrieval. Self- CoT reasons without external evidence, while KB- CoT (Li et al., 2024) conditions reasoning for trans- lation on retrieved knowledge for every case. Lad- der is a retrieval-free baseline fine-tuned for trans- lation (Feng et al., 2024). CSC further fine-tunes the translator with retrieved context under a fixed always-retrieve pipeline (Wang et al., 2025b). We compare the full adaptive policy with two matched policy ablations. SRAT-N denotes outcome-based RL that converges to a near-zero retrieval policy, while SRAT-A adds a consistent retrieval bonus and converges to always retriev- ing. The full SRAT model uses the retrieval- exploration curriculum and is ultimately optimized without a target retrieval rate. For retriever align- ment, Joint denotes Plackett–Luce joint translator– retriever training, while Contrastive denotes translation-utility contrastive retriever training. Metrics. We report BLEU (Papineni et al., 2002), COMET (Rei et al., 2020), MQM, and task-specific LLM-judge metrics. MQM exactly follows the LLM-judge protocol of Zhan et al. (2026). For Id- iomKB, I3 follows the three-level idiom-translation judge from Li et al. (2024), and we extend it to a finer-grained five-level criterion, noted as I5. For RAGtrans, GRF and GRB exactly fol- low its reference-free and reference-based LLM- judge templates (Wang et al., 2025b). All LLM- based evaluations use Claude Sonnet 4.6 as default. Higher is better for all metrics; MQM is reported as a negative error score, so values closer to zero are better. We also report the percentage of test examples where there exists retrieval action.
