The performance curves largely saturate once roughly half of the measurements have position infor- mation, indicating that additional labeled observations provide diminishing benefits after sufficient spatial coverage has been established. At low availability, Marginal Prob. + FM initially outper- forms Diffusion + FM, whereas the ordering reverses as more position-labeled observations become available. This behavior reflects the different inference mechanisms: the marginal formulation ap- plies an explicit geometric fusion rule that remains effective with sparse anchored evidence, while the diffusion model benefits more from a sufficiently rich set of observations that it can jointly inte- grate through the learned posterior. Finally, the near-constant performance of Diffusion + Raw Meas. across the entire sweep further indicates that its poor reconstruction quality is not primarily caused by insufficient position infor- mation, but by its inability to effectively exploit the raw channel measurements as conditioning information. It is worth noting that the diffusion decoder, treats each embed- ding as precise evidence about a specific location and has never seen mislocalized evidence during training. Observations whose evidence is shifted in different directions then conflict. Posterior sam- ples disagree on the affected cells, and their marginals move toward 1/2, and the consensus readout suppresses footprints that are otherwise recovered. This mechanism mainly removes true positives, which is consistent with the larger drop in F1 and IoU than in balanced accuracy. The sensitivity of Diffusion + FM is a train–test mismatch rather than a limitation of the posterior for- mulation, and it can be addressed directly. The position error/incompleteness can be treated as part of the observation model by training the decoder on embeddings computed from perturbed/missing positions, while keeping the clean occupancy s as the target. The decoder then learns pθ(s | ˜Z) under such conditions. The architecture and training objective will be unchanged. We leave this implementation for future work. 22
