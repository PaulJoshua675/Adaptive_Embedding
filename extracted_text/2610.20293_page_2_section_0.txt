We introduce CASI (Credit Assignment via Set Intervention for Agentic Retrieval), which constructs this information through snapshot-grounded set intervention. For each realized action set, CASI generates and retrieves once, caches per-action retrieval results, holds replayable snapshot compo- nents fixed, and evaluates alternative subsets under common conditions. Shapley weighting converts the resulting contextual marginals into within-set credit, and bounded zero-sum advantage shaping incorporates their relative credit into the rollout-level optimizer. Reusing cached retrieval outputs keeps exhaustive coalition scoring practical in our five-action setting without repeated generation, retrieval, or auction replay during RL. Empirically, exhaustive auction replay over 1,200 requests reveals substantial coalition dependence. Under the training-time evaluator, CASI attains the largest median cosine similarity to the replay- defined Shapley reference among the compared credit rules: 0.424 versus 0.388 for Singleton, a difference in median cosine of 0.036 (95% CI [0.004, 0.068]). Under the sensitivity backend, CASI does not outperform Singleton on this metric. Across five independent training runs, the CASI- trained policy achieves a held-out auction-replay set value of 66.70 ± 3.40, compared with 60.32 for the SFT initialization, 55.00 ± 4.00 for Singleton, and 43.30 ± 4.70 for Broadcast. Finally, in a randomized seven-day production A/B test on 10% of traffic, we observe a 3.51% lift in the primary advertiser-value metric over production control. Our contributions are threefold. First, we formalize the information gap between valid set-level optimization and coalition-based within-set attribution for jointly generated retrieval actions. We show that, for m ≥4, singleton, leave-one-out, and full-set endpoint observations do not generally determine the Shapley allocation, while under additive set values additional coalition evaluations are redundant. Second, we introduce snapshot-grounded set intervention to construct comparable coalition evaluations within a realized action set while holding replayable snapshot components fixed. Shapley weighting aggregates these evaluations into action-level credit, and bounded zero- sum advantage shaping incorporates the relative credit into policy optimization. Third, we evaluate CASI through coalition structure, credit-rule alignment with the replay-defined Shapley reference, held-out policy value across independent training runs, and randomized production deployment. 2
