For a fixed computational-basis input and a particular sequence of measurement outcomes m, we call the resulting evolution a computational-basis trajectory. A kickmix circuit may perform several such measurements. Their outcomes may be combined by classical XOR and may control diagonal corrections, but cannot control gates that change the stored basis string. Every measurement record therefore induces the same basis permutation: Vm |x⟩= (−1)ϕ(x,m) |π(x)⟩. (15) Exact coherent correctness additionally requires ϕ(x, m) = g(x) (16) for every allowed input and every record of nonzero probability. The measurement register then factors from the logical output. Allowing an outcome to control a basis-changing gate would instead make the permutation depend on the record and could leave the logical output correlated with the measurement environment. Validation and statistical guarantees The circuits are ultimately applied to coherent superpositions, whereas the evaluator follows one computational-basis trajectory at a time. Each test must therefore capture enough information to determine the trajectory’s coherent action. For a basis input x and measurement record m, the evaluator checks that the output matches the target, required inputs are unchanged, every ancilla has returned to |0⟩, and the phase agrees with the target exactly. These checks compare the complete final state rather than only its classical output. An ancilla that retains input-dependent information can become entangled with the logical registers, while an incorrect sign can alter subsequent interference without changing any output bit. Resetting or tracing out a dirty ancilla is therefore not a substitute for uncomputing it. If every pair (x, m) passes these tests, the measurement record factors from the logical output, and linearity extends agreement on every trajectory to any coherent input. For large input domains, however, the evaluator samples these trajectories rather than checking them all.
