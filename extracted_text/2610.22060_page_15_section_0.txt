B Generator implementation details We follow BAR (Yu et al., 2026) to implement the generative model, with our global-augmented generative modelling. BAR generative model. Recall that BAR learns tokens with FSQ in a bit representation and models their distribution with an adapted autoregressive model. Instead of a single categorical prediction over the entire codebook, BAR uses a lightweight masked bit modeling head. During training, some bits of the target token are masked, and the head learns to recover them with a bit-wise cross-entropy objective. During sampling, it starts with all bits masked and progressively fills them in, conditioning later predictions on the bits already revealed. The autoregressive context stays fixed during these within-token steps. Once the token is complete, it becomes part of the context for the next token. This avoids an output distribution with one logit per codebook entry while allowing dependencies among a token’s bits. Our implementation. We instantiate the conditional cross-entropy in Eq. 4 with BAR’s masked bit modeling objective (Yu et al., 2026). Let Bj = (Bj1, . . . , Bjd) ∈{0, 1}d be the clean bit representation of token zj, with d = 16, where ℓ∈{1, . . . , d} indexes the bits. For each token, we sample a masking ratio rj and mask nj = round(d rj) of its bits, chosen uniformly at random, giving a mask mj ∈{0, 1}d with mjℓ= 1 if bit ℓ is masked. Following BAR, patch tokens draw rj = max(1 −t, 1/d) with t = σ(ϵ), ϵ ∼N(0, 0.82), and σ the sigmoid. The global token, which BAR does not have, draws t ∼U[0, 1] instead.
