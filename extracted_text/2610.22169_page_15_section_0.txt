Settling depth. Counted back from the last layer, the unbroken run of layers whose reading names the model’s top token shows from which depth a prediction is settled, the quantity on which early exit relies. Over all 8128 positions and every layer (Table 7), LEXFRAME[1] is settled over 0.82 and 0.81 of the layers, the Jacobian lens over 0.09 and 0.09 and the logit lens over 0.03 and 0.11, and each order lengthens the run, from 0.58 and 0.58 for LEXFRAME[0] to 0.89 and 0.90 for LEXFRAME[2]. An exit cannot know the model’s top token; it leaves at the first layer whose reading is confident beyond a threshold. With the threshold chosen on half of the sequences for 0.95 agreement with the model and applied to the other half, LEXFRAME[1] leaves 0.45 and 0.42 of the layers unused, the lenses at most 0.04, since their confidence does not track whether they are right. LEXFRAME[1] saves no computation, as it transports a state through the later layers, but it shows how much depth a lens-based exit wastes. Ablation of fitted terms.
