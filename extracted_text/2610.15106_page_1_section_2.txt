Solving a combinatorial optimization problem in a single, end-to-end pass of a neural network is inherently difficult. A learned model must navigate a constrained and exponentially large solution space with limited computation. Increasing network depth provides more computation, but also raises training and inference costs [1]. Neural-based routing methods commonly construct routes autoregressively or iteratively refine complete solutions [2–4]. Both approaches simplify the task by breaking the solution process into a series of simpler decisions. However, this simplicity comes at the cost of an information bottleneck. If only the updated solution is carried between iterations, information that exists only in the latent state, such as intermediate reasoning, is lost. This creates a form of representational ‘amnesia’ that makes it difficult to accumulate a richer understanding of the problem. Latent recursion avoids this bottleneck by carrying the internal state, rather than the candidate solution, between iterations. A solution can be decoded at any stage but is not fed back into the model: each subsequent stage continues directly from the preceding latent state. Every depth therefore produces both an intermediate computation and a valid solution point. Recent latent-recursive models perform strongly on Sudoku, maze solving, and ARC-AGI [5]. However, whether latent recursion transfers from supervised reasoning to reinforcement learning for graph combinatorial optimization remains unclear. 40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026).
