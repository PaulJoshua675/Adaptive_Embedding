Small-scale learning-rate sweeps are used to study training instabilities that also arise at larger scales (Wortsman et al., 2024). Pre-LN, LLaMA-style, QK-Norm, nGPT, and GCT train for 600M tokens with learning rates in {3, 6, 12, 24, 48} × 10−4, spanning a factor of 16. The protocol includes the strong QK-Norm baseline in every stress test. Each architecture also has three seeds at the default learning rate. UVI-based early stopping. At the additional learning rate 9.6 × 10−3, we apply the same preset stopping rule to Pre-LN, LLaMA-style, nGPT, and GCT. At diagnostic steps within the first 10% of the training budget, excluding initialization, the monitor terminates a run if UVIres + UVIattn > 2.79. Pre-LN and LLaMA-style cross this threshold at step 100 of 1220, while nGPT and GCT complete training under the same rule. These early stops record threshold crossings, not observed numerical divergence. Depth probes and ablations. Separate fixed-budget depth probes use L = 24 and L = 48, which are respectively 3× and 6× the tuning depth. These probes use 400M tokens with unchanged hyperparameters for the same five architectures, and the depth table reports the primary seed. The GCT component ablations remove P ⊥, proposal canonicalization, or both components under the 600M-token L = 8 protocol. We additionally repeat the P ⊥ablation under the 400M-token depth protocol to measure how the projection’s effect changes during depth transfer. Larger language models. The same shared recipe uses learning rate 6 × 10−4 with an identical schedule and data order at larger scales. The 350M models use d = 1024, L = 24, 2B tokens, and three seeds. The 1.0B models use d = 1792, L = 24, 4B tokens, and two seeds. Pre-LN, LLaMA-style, QK-Norm, nGPT, and GCT are all represented. These models extend the residual-stream quantization and parameter-fingerprint studies beyond the 124M setting. Scale perturbation. Using the trained 124M models, we measure validation loss under h →ch at the embedding output and at mid-depth for c ∈[10−2, 102]. We also measure per-block equivariance or invariance error in fp32 (Figure 5; Table 8). H.2 Quantization and deployment evaluations PTQ. We apply PTQ to the same trained models to evaluate sensitivity to finite-precision storage and computation. The protocols include symmetric per-channel weight quantization at 8 and 4 bits and int8 activation quantization at every linear input, using both dynamic and calibration-based static scales in the standard integer-inference format (Jacob et al., 2018). The per-tensor, per-channel, and per-token granularities compared in Sec. 5.5 are the standard PTQ options (Nagel et al., 2021). Weight quantization uses round-to-nearest rather than error-compensated rounding (Frantar et al., 2023), because the comparison isolates activation- and stream-scale management rather than weight-rounding quality. We also quantize the residual stream at every block boundary to int8 and int4. This setting arises when hidden states are cached, offloaded, or transmitted across pipeline stages. The stream protocols use static or dynamic per- tensor scales and per-token scales. We additionally evaluate scale migration through SmoothQuant with α = 0.5 and per-site static calibration on the linear path (Xiao et al., 2023), together with calibrated per-channel static scales for the stored stream. The logit head remains in full precision as specified in App. H.6. A microbenchmark compares the deployment cost of per-token and per-tensor activation-scale management on the same device. Off-the-shelf audit. To examine the quantization pattern in independently trained models, we record the depth-scale span κdepth := maxℓ¯rℓ/ minℓ¯rℓ, where ¯rℓis the mean token RMS at block input ℓ. We then apply identical int8 and int4 quantization hooks to the four publicly available pretrained OpenAI GPT-2 models from 124M to 1.5B parameters (Radford et al., 2019). The depth-scale span κdepth is a descriptive depth-drift statistic and is distinct from the within-tensor κscale of Prop. 5. We evaluate these pretrained models on the same FineWeb-Edu validation bins with the same GPT-2 BPE through an independent PyTorch reimplementation. The implementation reproduces the published WikiText-103 (Merity et al., 2017) validation perplexities 27.7, 20.3, 17.5, and 16.0 for the 124M, 355M, 774M, and 1.5B pretrained models on non-overlapping blocks, confirming faithful weight loading.
