Identifying a robot task from a short instruction is sensitive to which descriptions are available for retrieval. We investigate this effect in the natural-language instructions included with the RLBench manipulation benchmark. We extract 296 fixed descriptions from 62 publicly available task files and compare five training-free lexical rankers over 80 random choices of one indexed description per task. Exact strings assigned to conflicting task labels are excluded from the primary score. Character 𝑛-gram TF–IDF achieves 61.3% mean top-1 accuracy on the resulting held-out instructions, compared with 55.6% for BM25 and 54.9% for word TF–IDF. Across gallery choices, character TF–IDF ranges from 55.2% to 66.8%; its 5th–95th percentile range is 56.9%–64.8%. Adding a second indexed description improves character TF–IDF by 7.0 percentage points on matched queries. These results quantify the influence of reference wording and representation choice on instruction-to-task retrieval under a consistent evaluation protocol. Keywords: robot task retrieval; language instructions; lexical search; gallery sensitivity; RLBench; BM25; TF–IDF. 1
