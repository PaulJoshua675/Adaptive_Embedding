The following experiments measure these separately. Codebook candidates are designed from training labels, and the 4/8 codebook below is used for the final model. All learned comparisons, however, retain the old 72K-QA gradient-training budget, including only 22K length examples. Coverage comes before precision. The historical codebook has 12 positive intervals ending at 200 m, two special roots, and 32 fine cells per positive interval. It covers all old 72K length labels but overflows on 12K expanded-corpus labels. The scale audit compares uniform, log, quantile, task-specific, and hierarchical allocations on all 1.6M accepted lengths (Table 25). These are label encode/decode oracles, assuming every classification decision is correct. Uniform 960-cell spacing up to 16,384 m gives MAE 6.4545 m because most labels are short. Quantile spacing improves the proportion within 1 cm to 80.78%, yet produces MAE 4.2371 m and maximum error 3935.55 m: a dense central distribution leaves the sparse tail poorly represented. Shared log allocations balance these scales more effectively.
