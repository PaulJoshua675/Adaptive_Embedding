As illustrated in Figure 2 (Bottom), roughly 43.7% of the search turns fall into the second and fourth quadrants, where the prior OPD reward and post-retrieval gt disagree in sign. Specifically, 19.8% of search queries retrieve useful evidence (gt > 0) yet are penalized, while 23.9% retrieve misleading evidence (gt < 0) yet are rewarded. These results show that inherent teacher preference prior to retrieval is not a faithful proxy for actual evidence utility, causing beneficial search actions to be penalized while misleading ones are falsely reinforced. These empirical findings naturally motivate us to use IG as a post-retrieval signal and propose IG-OPD to address each symptom in turn, using |gt| to recalibrate the budget (ITC) and the sign of gt to recalibrate the direction (IQC). In deep search, the final response turn, which merely consolidates collected evidence, is typically far longer than the search turns that acquired it. Consequently, the critical search behavior receives the least supervision, dictated by token volume rather than information value. The tanh prevents a single extreme-gain turn from monopolizing the budget, while the ad- ditive constant guarantees that every turn, including the response, retains a baseline share of teacher supervision, so answer synthesis remains distilled rather than discarded. Therefore, a student query that brings decisive evidence can still be suppressed. IQC mitigates this by using the signed gain gt as a post-retrieval calibration signal.
