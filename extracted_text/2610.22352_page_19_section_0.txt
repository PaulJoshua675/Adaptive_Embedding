SUPRA-Tex A Additional Method Details A.1 Geometry and Surface Observations Geometry foundation and camera projection. Our framework operates on an underlying mesh M and camera projection π, provided as ground-truth inputs or estimated from the reference image. For end-to-end inference, we follow the default MoGe-2-based camera-estimation pipeline of Pixal3D⋆, whose geometry module generates the mesh. For the 3D position xs of latent coordinate s, the model samples image features through camera projection: fs = Sample(Φ(I), π(xs)) , (11) where Φ(I) denotes a 2D feature map. In the geometry refinement stages, Pixal3D⋆projects both the original and spatially upsampled image features and concatenates the sampled features. Combined with global image conditioning, these features align the predicted mesh M and camera projection π with the reference view, serving as the geometric input for our material generation. Surface observations and latent coordinates. The full surface defines the active latent-coordinate set S. Valid visible surface voxels form the partial PBR input Po, which is encoded before any latent-level conditioning is constructed. The resulting coordinates So identify cells with observations, but a cell in So can also contain unseen surface voxels; U = S \ So therefore denotes cells without any observation, not all unseen surface. Coverage ρs is the observed fraction of the surface voxels actually present in a cell, and repeated image samples of one voxel do not increase it. Boundary distance ds uses the six-neighbor graph of active latent coordinates and is −1 if no unobserved cell is reachable. Inference-time boundary filtering. At inference, we additionally apply foreground-mask erosion and local depth- discontinuity filtering to reduce texture contamination from unreliable correspondences. Foreground erosion removes mixed pixels along the silhouette, and depth-discontinuity filtering rejects correspondences whose neighboring visible depths differ by more than a threshold, where a small projection error would move the observation onto a different surface. All material channels share the retained observation support, from which local material encoding, coverage, and boundary distance are computed. A.2 Interleaved Flow Transformer 3D generator architecture. Material and geometry latents have 32 channels each on a sparse latent grid of resolution 643. The generator has 60 Transformer blocks with hidden dimension 1536 and 12 attention heads, 30 of each update type; its self-attention modules are initialized from TRELLIS.2 and the remaining components randomly. Two states at an observed coordinate. At every s ∈So, the read-only observation token Os represents a partial material observation, while the generation token Xs predicts the complete material of the cell. Geometry and material latents use fixed per-channel normalization; observation latents are mapped to the material-latent scale before the shared input projection. Even-indexed blocks use queries from XU, odd-indexed blocks from all of X; generation states in U are therefore updated in 60 blocks and those in So in 30. Observation tokens pass through each block’s normalization and adaptive modulation with the clean-time indicator t = 0, produce no queries, and receive no residual or feed-forward updates. Queries from X, and keys from both O and X, carry rotary positional encoding (Su et al., 2024) extended to 3D latent coordinates; values are not rotated.
