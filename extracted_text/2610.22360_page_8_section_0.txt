Appendix H further provides details of category-wise quantitative analysis of these verification failures. 5.4 How Does Evidence Representation Affect Verification? Multi-view provides a practical protocol for 3D verification. Multi-view representation generally performs better than raw 3D input, indicating that rendering 3D evidence into views is currently a more effective way to elicit reliable judgments from existing MLLMs. We compare multi- view RGB and raw OBJ input on matched Object, Part, and Material subsets in Table 3(a). The multi-view results are also broadly consistent with the corresponding results in Table 1. This choice preserves the core difficulty of 3D verification, requiring models to aggregate partial observations, resolve occlusions, associate parts across views, and disentangle underlying geometry from viewpoint or appearance changes. As an early benchmark for 3D verifiers, 3DVerifierBench therefore adopts multi-view rendering as a practical and extensible protocol for grounding verification in 3D content. Native point-cloud LLMs show strong response bias and unstable output formatting under our verifier interface; we therefore report them only in Appendix E.4. For motion, no single representation is always the best. Motion verification requires both temporal dynamics and geometric evidence. We evaluate three representations on the Motion Understanding task in Table 3(b): 16 uniformly sampled frames, raw 3D motion in .obj sequences, and native video. We report binary and 4-class Accuracy. No single representation consistently outperforms others across all models and metrics. Native video is the highest in binary (59.7%), whereas 16-frames yield the highest in 4-class (43.8%). The preferred representation also varies by model: Gemini-3.8-Flash benefits most from native video, whereas Qwen3.6-35B-A3B excels with .obj sequences.
