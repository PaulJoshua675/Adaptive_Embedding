The training mixture consists of ChatTS instruction-following data, TSExam perception questions, the numerical corpus, and a separately constructed reasoning corpus. The raw instruction-tuning sources contain 6,373 ChatTS IFT examples, 44,802 ChatTS SFT exam- ples, 20,640 TSExam perception examples, 11,000 numerical corpus examples, and 100,000 reason- ing examples. Rather than sampling this mixture according to raw source frequency, we explicitly control its effective composition. The 100,000 reasoning examples and 11,000 numerical examples are retained in each epoch. The remaining 71,815 draws are divided approximately equally among ChatTS IFT, ChatTS SFT, and TSExam perception data, yielding approximately 23.9k draws from each source and 182,815 total draws per epoch. The reasoning corpus is designed to cover a broad range of temporal reasoning capabilities and inter- action formats, including numerical reasoning, event prediction, entity recognition, causal discovery, sequence continuation, dynamical reasoning, temporal reasoning, abductive reasoning, and induc- tive reasoning. While these capabilities overlap with those evaluated by TSRBench, the underlying time series, questions, and supervision in the reasoning corpus are constructed independently. Evaluation-set separation. No evaluation examples from TSRBench, TSExam-HF, or the ChatTS evaluation sets are included in the training mixture. This separation applies to both training stages. D.4 REASONING CORPUS SPECIFICATION The Stage-II reasoning corpus contains 100,000 examples spanning nine task families and 25 gen- eration templates. Table 18 summarizes the size of each family, the underlying data-generating process, the number of input channels and answer choices, and the supervision format. The task families are denoted NR (numerical reasoning), EP (event prediction), ER (entity recognition), CD (causal discovery), TSF (time-series forecasting), DR (dynamical reasoning), TR (temporal reason- ing), AR (abductive reasoning), and IR (inductive reasoning). The corpus deliberately combines tasks with different forms of supervision. NR, EP, and IR require explicit numerical computation or intermediate multi-step inference and together account for 36,328 examples. For these families, we retain rationale supervision followed by the final answer. The remaining six families—CD, ER, TSF, AR, DR, and TR—account for 63,672 examples and are trained using the final option letter directly. Although deterministically generated rationales are available for 97,844 of the 100,000 examples, we discard these rationales for the latter families in the final training configuration rather than applying chain-of-thought supervision uniformly across all tasks. 22
