Matched training with original-image context. The original-image variant removes crop placeholders during training as well as inference, while retaining the selected rectangle and region options in text. Training still has three supervised decisions per pointing QA, and choice/scalar token sequences and targets remain unchanged. RefSpatial recovers to 46.50 and Point-Bench macro to 52.55 (Table 5). This recovery shows that much of the inference-only collapse is an input-distribution mismatch. The remaining gap to crop re- encoding suggests that original-image context and region text do not fully replace newly resolved local visual information in this setting. ROI feature replay. The next variant encodes the original pixels once per forward and appends existing merged visual tokens whose spatial supports have positive overlap with the selected ROI. Tokens retain row- major source order and their original multimodal rotary positions; there is no interpolation to a new fixed grid. This exposes region-focused representations without new pixel encoding. Boundary tokens may still include content outside the ROI, and copying cannot recover detail absent from the first visual tokenization. ROI attention K/V replay. A second replay variant appends the same visual tokens but replaces their keys and values in complete full-attention layers L4–L28 with differentiably gathered K/V from original-image positions after rotary encoding. Queries, gates, residuals, and FFNs execute normally. Source K/V remain in the current computation graph, and the original-image prefix precedes all question histories. The matched comparison is reported in Table 5. Neither replay scheme closes the overall gap to crop re- encoding. ViT replay reaches 47.00 RefSpatial and 52.87 Point-Bench, only slightly above matched original- image training. KV replay improves Where2Place to 68.95 but decreases RoboSpatial pointing to 54.92 and increases length MAE to 0.4097 m. Choice and numerical scores also change despite identical non-point inputs, consistent with interactions through the jointly trained backbone. These outcomes support crop re-encoding as the final common representation. Direct visual-token candidates. We train two further models, KEV and native mean, whose point candidates are the original image’s merged visual tokens in the language sequence. The query is the question- end decision state; keys use the same language-side projections as their respective readout. Let H′ = H/s and W ′ = W/s denote the dimensions of the merged visual-token grid; each cell is a candidate. Cell (r, c) decodes to ((c + 1/2)/W ′, (r + 1/2)/H′) in original normalized coordinates. Point training uses one decision. Ground-truth points are mapped to occupied cells by floor-and-clamp, with equal target mass across distinct occupied cells. Repeated points in one cell do not multiply its weight, and target masks do not enlarge the positive set. This preserves one unit of loss per QA while changing both the spatial representation and number of stages.
