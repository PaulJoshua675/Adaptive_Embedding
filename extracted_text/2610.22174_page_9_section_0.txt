RQ2: Ablation Study of Mosaic RQ2 asks whether each component of Mosaic contributes to its overall performance. Figure 3 (c)(d) presents the ablation results. Relative to Broadcasting, both levels of components in Mosaic, namely trajectory-level session-aware advantage estimation and session-level advantage shaping, contribute to the enhanced final performance. Combining the two components yields a clear additional gain in the compact scenario, but only a modest gain in the subagent scenario. One possible explanation lies in their session structures: compact trajectories are linear, whereas subagent trajectories have a nonlinear structure that more closely resembles a directed acyclic graph. In the latter case, a session may influence multiple succeeding sessions. Its immediate reward, estimated from marginal gain, may thus differ from its actual contribution to the final outcome. 4.4. RQ3: Mechanistic Analysis of Mosaic RQ3 asks how the design of Mosaic contributes to the training process. First, we examine whether session-aware trajectory-level advantage estimation produces the intended ordering. As shown in Figure 5, within mixed-outcome groups, advantages approximately decrease with session count for successful trajectories and increase for unsuccessful ones, while successful trajectories retain higher advantages. In homogeneous-outcome groups, the fallback mechanism introduces session-dependent variation, providing differentiated learning signals despite identical outcome rewards. These patterns illustrate how Mosaic leverages session signals to increase the within-group variance of relative advantages, thus improving sample efficiency during training. Second, we examine the effect of reward normalization on session-level shaping signals. As shown in Figure 5, the distributions of raw marginal information gain differ across session types, primarily because heterogeneous sessions serve distinct functions. Under global normalization, compaction sessions, whose raw marginal gains have a lower mean, are further downweighted, introducing a systematic bias against them. In Mosaic, type-conditioned normalization instead centers and rescales each type separately, enabling both types to receive positive and negative signals relative to their respective baselines. This reduces type-dependent distributional bias in advantage shaping. 4.5. RQ4: Understanding Multi-Session Optimization Multi-session optimization introduces an additional degree of freedom in how computation is dis- tributed across sessions. However, the session count used by Mosaic serves only as a proxy signal and is not inherently tied to exploration intensity or execution cost. Moreover, at inference time, the model has no access to hindsight: it does not know how many sessions a trajectory will ultimately contain or whether the trajectory will succeed.
