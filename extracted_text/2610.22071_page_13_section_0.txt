At one-symbol lookahead, the trained UBE and UBE-BE predictors support the importance of byte order for grapheme boundary recovery and for retained subword boundary selection. In Korean and Chinese, where the every-cluster target is already recoverable from scalar ends, UBE reduces retained-parent errors relative to UTF-8 by 72.91% and 35.29% at the same lookahead; in eight more scripts, by 67.32% to 99.55%. Which byte comes first, not only how many bytes a scalar takes, is therefore a design choice for byte-level boundary predictors. Impact Statement This work examines how text encodings affect boundary prediction and representation length across writing systems. It provides ways to identify and measure design choices that fragment written units or disproportionately lengthen some languages’ representations. The selected languages and translated corpus do not cover all users, dialects, or writ- ing practices, and Unicode grapheme clusters provide an operational definition of written units with language-specific limits. Changes in representation can redistribute compu- tational cost and model quality across scripts. Assessing their social benefit requires native-language evaluation that reports both gains and regressions.
