These results demonstrate that GeoState supports effective long-horizon autoregressive geometry forecasting, including at horizons beyond those seen during training. Figure 2 visualizes depth forecasts across future horizons and query views, alongside comparisons with prior methods. The Copy-Last variants further disentangle the effects of camera conditioning and scene-state evo- lution. Re-querying the last observed state with future cameras substantially outperforms simply repeating the last observed depth map, demonstrating that viewpoint-induced changes can be ac- counted for by querying a fixed scene state from future viewpoints without evolving the state itself. Our predicted states consistently outperform their camera-matched Copy-Last counterparts at every horizon under both GT and predicted cameras, showing that the World Model captures future scene evolution beyond what can be explained by camera re-querying alone. Motion-stratified evaluation further shows that these improvements are more pronounced in Dynamic than in Static windows under both GT and predicted cameras, supporting the effectiveness of GeoState in modeling scene dynamics.
