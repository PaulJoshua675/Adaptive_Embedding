Our approach is distinguished by pushing the idea of decentralization further. In particular, Qi et al. (2026) maintain a population of agents that is selected by each agent’s economic balance (agents with zero balance drop out), but the agents are not economic actors: they do not structure their participation to optimize a local economic incentive. Concurrent work by Liu et al. (2026) orchestrates LLMs using hierarchical reverse procurement auctions where contractors subcontract tasks. In contrast, our sequential spot market (section 2) resolves multi-step credit assignment through peer-to-peer state resales without top-down subcontracting trees. Another difference is that Liu et al. (2026) centralize value estimation in a single model (the participants estimate only their own costs), while we require each participant to estimate its own expected return. This aligns with our vision of a market composed of an evolving set of niche service providers. Economic Alignment Benchmarks. Several benchmarks evaluate whether prompt-driven LLMs act rationally in simulated retail or auction games, including Magentic Market (Bansal et al., 2025), Vend- ingBench (Backlund and Petersson, 2025), CoffeeBench (Sugiura et al., 2026), MarketBench (Fradkin and Krishnan, 2026), and Agent Bazaar (Karten et al., 2026). Whereas those works study whether raw LLMs natively behave like homo economicus, we use mechanism design and RL-trained bidding policies as an algorithmic organizing principle to coordinate multi-agent pipelines. 7. Discussion and Conclusion We present a decentralized market framework for LLM orchestration. Whereas prior frameworks assume perfect clones—ignoring participation incentives—we establish imperfect competition as a viable regime. Under 1st-price auctions, agents pairing frozen LLMs with trainable bidding networks learn strategic bid-shading. This secures positive margins and conserves credit across pipelines, avoiding the unbounded subsidies of 2nd-price and the severe credit attenuation of CCV. Empirically, complex cognitive workflows emerge organically via price discovery, bypassing top-down orchestration. Furthermore, our density sweeps confirm that scaling competition efficiently compresses individual margins while systematically increasing social welfare and task accuracy. Our framework has several limitations: it requires sufficient competition (collapsing under monop- sonies), assumes strictly sequential task transformations (precluding parallel execution), relies on myopic agents rather than globally strategic planners, and utilizes a centralized assessor rather than fully decentralized feature discovery. Future work may jointly fine-tune LLM backbones directly on market profit signals and investigate robustness against malicious bidding and collusion. Ultimately, however, this work shows that decentralized market-based routing offers a scalable blueprint for transitioning from rigid AI controllers to open, self-sustaining agentic economies.
