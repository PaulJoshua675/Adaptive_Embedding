For evaluation, we drop a first-level branch only when p(a1) < 0.01 maxj p(j), so each retained root adds ten decision nodes and 81 leaf cells, and deeper levels are fully expanded. Unvisited cells remain unknown, and the omitted mass is recorded without renormalizing the retained probabilities. On the resulting map, cells above 10% of the retained global peak form an eight-connected superlevel set, and each connected component outputs the center of its peak cell. The threshold, connectivity, and root cutoff are fixed without test labels; target masks and true point counts are used only by the scorer. This turns distributed spatial mass into a variable number of points without a learned stop decision or a ground-truth top-k. The tree costs more than the three-node pointing path, and its pruning and decoder sensitivities are measured in Section F.2. B Data Conversion and Validation B.1 From Spatial Annotations to Classification Supervision We convert spatial annotations from the training collection curated for Molmo2-ER (Fang et al., 2026), which contains 5M source records and 21.1M QA. The annotations originate from RefSpatial (Zhou et al., 2025a), SAT (Ray et al., 2025), SenseNova-SI (Cai et al., 2026), VSI-590K (Yang et al., 2025a), VST-P (Yang et al., 2025b), and RoboPoint (Yuan et al., 2024); Table 7 reports their contributions to the final corpus. Each accepted QA contains the ordered images, question, answer type, candidates, and target. Supplied options retain their semantic content, numerical labels determine the target intervals, and point coordinates determine the target regions and training crops. Source answers and rationales are excluded from model inputs. Answer options. Supplied text and image options are retained when exactly one candidate matches the source answer. Questions with ambiguous matches or unresolved image bindings are excluded. Question text, including comparison clauses, is preserved separately from the candidate descriptions. Numerical supervision. When no explicit answer menu applies, the requested quantity determines the task and units. Length targets are converted to meters, counts are exact integers in 0–1023, and angle direction follows the question. We accept a scalar target only when its value and unit are unambiguous. Ranges, inequalities, conflicting quantities, and unsupported area or volume answers are excluded. Point and relation supervision. Single-point labels with a verified coordinate convention are normalized to the original image. Multiple-point lists, region-only annotations, unresolved image bindings, and examples with more than five source images are excluded. Boolean and relation labels use fixed candidate vocabularies when no supplied answer menu applies. RoboPoint-spatial (Yuan et al., 2024) contributes no final QA because its available multi-point or region supervision is outside this adaptation. B.2 Data Quality Checks We compare 245 converted numerical QA with their source annotations and semantically review 75 cases. A targeted review of 66,574 QA with known option-conversion issues recovers 65,902 QA and excludes 672 ambiguous cases. We remove adapter-added option labels and answer-format instructions while preserving the meaning of can- didate text. This cleanup affects 15.2M candidate texts across 6.5M QA; the final corpus contains 12,304,058 QA. We check 3,422 nontrivial cleanup cases against their source annotations and validate 3,427 rendered training examples across five sources and 200 display categories, including teacher-forced crops. A focused content review examines 393 cases. Another 256 pointing questions are flagged for coordinate-wording review. B.3 Duplicate Filtering and Evaluation Separation We exclude invalid images and training examples whose images exactly match public evaluation images or satisfy a conservative near-duplicate criterion. Near-duplicates require a 64-bit difference-hash (Krawetz, 2013) Hamming distance at most 2, aspect ratios within 1%, and mean absolute RGB difference at most 6 on 8 × 8 thumbnails.
