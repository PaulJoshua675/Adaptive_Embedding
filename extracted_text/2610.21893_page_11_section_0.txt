HELMET, Loong, MeetingBank, and L-Eval account for 9,455 items, corresponding to 93.8% of the complete source pool, whereas LongBench- v2, LongCodeBench, and SWE-Verified provide smaller but complementary collections. The inner ring reports the number of source items contributed by each collection. The outer ring further decomposes each collection according to its content categories, revealing substantial vari- ation in both source scale and domain composition. HELMET is dominated by web and media content and learning materials. Loong primarily contains academic and technical texts, legal and government documents, and financial texts. MeetingBank con- sists mainly of conversational and meeting data. L- Eval covers a broader range of categories, including learning materials, code, conversations and meet- ings, narratives and scripts, web and media, refer- ence texts, legal and government documents, and financial texts. LongBench-v2 exhibits similarly diverse textual coverage, while LongCodeBench and SWE-Verified focus on code repositories. Together, these distributions establish a hetero- geneous source pool spanning multiple document types and content domains, supporting the evalua- tion of goal-conditioned context compression under varied source characteristics. Figures 5–7 summarize the benchmark com- position at the goal, source-context, and question levels across the seven source benchmarks. The source contexts include both static documents and code-related interaction records. In particular, the SWE-Verified subset is derived from rollout-agent trajectories collected during software-engineering tasks, which contain sequences of agent actions, observations, tool interactions, and execution feed- back. This design extends the benchmark beyond conventional document compression and enables evaluation of whether compression preserves task- relevant evidence in interactive agent contexts. At the structural level, 160 of the 281 source contexts (56.9%) are associated with a single goal, whereas the remaining 121 contexts support multi- ple goals, with at most six goals per context. Each goal contains 4–12 questions, with a mean of 5.14 questions. In total, 420 goals (84.0%) contain be- tween four and six questions. Context lengths are long-tailed, with a median of 5.9k tokens, a 90th percentile of 76.9k tokens, and a maximum of 337.7k tokens under the cl100k_base tokenizer. These statistics characterize the multi-goal and multi-question structure used to evaluate goal-level context retention. 11
