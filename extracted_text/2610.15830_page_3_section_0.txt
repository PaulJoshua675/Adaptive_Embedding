We hold one control fixed while intervening on the other, and then cross them. 2.2 What is measured To measure what remains readable, we freeze the encoder, fit fresh linear readouts on unused probe data, and evaluate them on separate held-out data. Unless specified otherwise, both targets receive the same high-precision evaluation input. Let DA, DB be their variance-normalized mean squared reconstruction errors. The allocation index s = DB −DA DA + DB (2) is positive when A is more precisely readable and negative when B is. Its magnitude measures relative specialization depth. We retain the individual errors and their sum because the same relative advantage can occur at different overall accuracies. An earlier transition experiment used a lower-resolution probe; its common-input remeasurement is reported in Appendix E. These measurements describe functional accessibility. Encoder geometry is measured separately, for example by linear centered kernel alignment (CKA), and the persistent learning state also includes model parameters and Adam’s accumulated gradient statistics. Matching s and reconstruction errors therefore defines a specific, testable equivalence of measurements, not identity of the networks. This distinction becomes the experimental question in Section 5. 2.3 Evidence design and supporting constructions In the central comparisons, each seed supplies a checkpoint and paired input/noise sequences. Conditions branch from that checkpoint with the same initial model and, unless directly intervened on, the same optimizer state. Pilot seeds set intervention levels and decision margins; disjoint confirmatory seeds evaluate them. The principal experiments use 40 confirmatory seeds each. Mirrored target labels are averaged within seed for inference, rather than counted as independent replicates. Intervals are 95% seed-level bootstrap intervals; equivalence requires an interval to lie inside the specified margin. The evidence comes from several minimal constructions rather than one full factorial system. The central nonlinear network supports acquisition, update, and optimizer interventions (Experiments IV-A–E). A related network isolates state-following feedback (II); a linear bottleneck isolates formation and current constraints, and an externally coupled model tests a candidate dynamical coordinate (III). Three-target formation is tested in V-A. Acquisition–compression calibration and a finite-state behavioral platform provide supporting controls in Appendices A and D.
