We have presented LangDiT , a Transformer backbone developed through a systematic study of architectural design for continuous diffusion language models. Its design separates feature-scale control from noise modulation, decouples the likelihood shortcut’s forward contribution from its direct codebook gradient, and distinguishes clean-state estimation from the weighting of self-conditioning inputs. Experiments on OWT-1024 demonstrate improved generation performance within LangFlow and an adaptation of compatible components to a Plaid-style model, while preserving each model’s representation, objective formulation, and inference sampler. Evaluating GenPPL alongside unigram entropy allows these gains to be interpreted together with changes in lexical diversity. These findings highlight architecture as a reusable source of improvement in CDLMs and motivate further study at larger scales and across broader generation tasks.
