By (i), the closed form reduces to weight averaging, not to task arithmetic, when the teachers cannot be told apart by their inputs. By (iii), interference arises only where input distributions overlap; Equation (4) below measures how much. 3.2 On-policy statistics The solution is only as good as µd. We take µd to be the distribution of layer inputs when teacher d processes its own answers to problems from its domain. This is the distribution the layer operates on in use, since a reasoning model’s context is dominated by its own generation, and it cannot be obtained from reference solutions, which the model did not write. We refer to statistics gathered on model-generated rollouts as on-policy, in contrast to statistics gathered on a fixed corpus; the rollouts in our main configuration are the teachers’, as in sequence-level distillation [16], and Section 4.5 evaluates the variant that uses the student’s. Concretely, teacher d answers a set of training problems from its domain, sampled exactly as in evaluation. After selection (Section 3.4), the teacher reads each kept sequence, prompt and answer, in a single teacher- forced forward pass. A forward pre-hook on every linear layer of every decoder block accumulates X⊤X over all token positions, and Gd is that sum divided by the number of tokens, so that domains with longer answers do not dominate by length. Layers that read the same tensor share a Gram matrix: in the architectures we use, the query, key and value projections form one group, the gate and up projections another, and the attention output and down projections one each. The grouping is detected automatically from one short forward pass, and the same statistics are collected for the final hidden states to solve the output head. 3.3 Solving For each group, Equation (2) is solved once per member layer in double precision. Tensors that are not linear maps of a hidden state — embeddings, normalization gains and biases — change little and are combined by task arithmetic. Figure 2 illustrates the procedure, and Algorithm 1 in Appendix B states it in full. 3.4 Which rollouts to use Every token of every kept answer contributes one outer product to Gd, so the statistics are a token-weighted picture of the teacher’s behavior. That picture is distorted by a failure mode of long-form generation. Some answers do not terminate: the model enters a repetition loop and continues to the length limit. Such answers are few, but each is as long as the limit allows, and the layer inputs inside a loop are nearly periodic, so their contribution to the Gram matrix is large and concentrated on a few directions. The least-squares solution then spends its capacity reproducing the teacher on degenerate input. We detect loops without supervision from the compressibility of the text: an answer is flagged when the last 20,000 characters compress, under DEFLATE, to less than 5% of their length, far below ordinary reasoning text. Our default selection keeps answers that finished on their own and are not flagged.
