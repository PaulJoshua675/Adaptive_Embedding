In unCLIP, the embedding specifies semantic content, while dif- fusion stochasticity supplies visual detail the embedding leaves undetermined, making diversity desirable. In our setting, a single physical scene underlies the observations, and variation across posterior samples reflects genuine ambiguity that the measurements leave unresolved. We therefore seek the structure consistently supported by the observations rather than diverse scenes, which is what the consensus readout in equation 5 provides: it retains footprints supported across samples and suppresses structure arising from unresolved uncertainty. 4 EXPERIMENTS We pretrain the foundation model with the proposed physics-guided self-supervised objective on a digital-twin corpus of 11,000 outdoor environments and 30 million ray-traced channel observa- tions. The pretrained model then provides physics-grounded representations that condition the pos- terior diffusion model in our hierarchical pipeline, a configuration we refer to as Diffusion + FM. To provide an interpretable alternative to the diffusion formulation and isolate the contribution of joint posterior modeling, Marginal Prob. + FM replaces the diffusion posterior with a determin- istic per-observation decoder: each observation yields a cell-wise occupancy belief and a confi- dence score marking the propagation-relevant region over which that belief is meaningful. The predicted beliefs are then fused into the final reconstruction. To our knowledge, prior work does not directly address scene reconstruction from communication-infrastructure data and without ge- ometric priors, precluding a direct head-to-head comparison; we therefore use controlled baselines to isolate each component of our design. Diffusion + Raw Meas. conditions the identical dif- fusion model on raw observations and user locations, testing whether the scene posterior can be learned without the intermediate scene-aligned representation; this is closest in spirit to (Hehn et al., 2025), which reconstructs indoor geometry from a single link’s received power with the assump- tion of knowing floor and ceiling geometry. Marginal Prob. + Raw Meas. predicts occupancy over the full grid from each measurement under a binary cross-entropy objective and aggregates the per-measurement predictions, without modeling the observation-specific propagation-relevant region. Training objectives and implementation details for all models are given in Appendices G–I. All models are evaluated on ∼700 environments held out from every stage of training, spanning layouts from dense urban blocks to open areas with isolated structures. Each observation consists of the full wireless channel collected by a single base station together with the user’s position. We report balanced accuracy, F1, and intersection-over-union (IoU) of the reconstructed occupancy grid against ground truth. Appendix G details the array and waveform configuration, Appendix J defines the metrics, and Appendices L–M study the effect of incomplete/noisy position information. 4.1 MAIN RESULTS Figure 4 presents scene reconstruction produced by our framework on previously unseen represen- tative environments. Given 500 wireless channel measurements from a single base-station node, our framework recovers the surrounding building footprints, their location, extent, and arrangement, from the wireless channel observations and user position, without any prior over scene informa- tion. The reconstructions closely follow ground-truth layouts across environments with substan- tially different spatial density and structure. To our knowledge, this is the first demonstration that communication-infrastructure channel measurements can support reconstruction of arbitrary out- door scene geometry without assumptions on the number, shape, or placement of structures. By performing posterior inference over physics-grounded representations, our framework makes this ill-posed inverse problem tractable and allows existing radio links to act as environmental sensors. The scope of what is recovered is set by the measurements: with a ULA at the base station, the channel resolves paths only in azimuth, so building height is severely underspecified; hence we re- construct the scene as a two-dimensional occupancy grid of building footprints. A three-dimensional extension using a uniform rectangular array that can resolve paths in elevation is left for future work.
