Pooling counts does not give languages equal weight. Each sentence also supplies a same-context continuation pair. At a site where a base letter precedes a scalar on the same 256-codepoint page, the true continuation is paired with an NFC-stable replacement from that page requiring the opposite decision. A pair is correct only when both decisions are correct. Encoding alone determines the minimum lookahead needed to distinguish them: UTF-8 requires two bytes for 9,313 pairs and three for the remaining 7,891; UBE requires one symbol for every pair, and UBE-BE requires two. UTF-8 and UBE-BE therefore have zero fully correct pairs at k = 1 and exactly 50% decision error on these pairs by construction. The trained UBE k = 1 predictors answer every pair correctly, whereas UTF-8 at k = 2 answers 27.2–28.8% of all pairs correctly (4,676 and 4,949 pairs in its two seeds), although two bytes distinguish 9,313 pairs. Contrasts are differences of two-seed means, with 95% percentile intervals from 2,000 paired source-sentence bootstrap draws that preserve parallel translations. These pointwise intervals condition on the two trained seeds and include neither multiplicity correction nor training-seed uncertainty.
