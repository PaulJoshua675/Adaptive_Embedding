(i) Corrected disagreement, SCD, consistently outperforms uncorrected weak–strong disagreement, Sdisagree, across all budgets. (ii) In operational regimes where the escalation budget is constrained (B ≤0.4), SCD and Sdisagree outperform the weak-model correctness baseline. The reason is, while correctness indicates when the weak model is wrong, it cannot anticipate whether the strong model will succeed. (iii) Because SCD replaces ground truth with the strong model’s predictive distribution, the approximation gap of SCD is smaller for more accurate strong models. 5 Finite-Sample Routing Algorithms We now turn the corrected-disagreement score into a practical routing algorithm. We describe two components: first, how the learned score is used at test time to route between the weak and strong models. Second, how this score can be learned using only weak- and strong-model generations. Routing algorithm. Following Theorem 3.1, we fix a sampling policy σ and a selection policy ϕ. Recall from Section 3 that σ(p) specifies the number of weak responses generated for prompt p, ϕ(hσ) selects one of these responses, and hσ = (p, yw,1, . . . , yw,σ(p)) denotes the resulting history. Given a routing score bS(hσ) ∈R, test-time routing proceeds as follows: 1. Generate σ(p) weak-model responses, forming hσ. 2. Compute the routing score bS(hσ). 3. If bS(hσ) > τ, query the strong model; otherwise, return the weak response selected by ϕ(hσ). The threshold τ can be calibrated on held-out data to meet a desired strong-model budget Bs. More generally, the threshold can be updated online to maintain budget constraints over time, as in Kiyani et al. [21]. To recap, Theorem 3.1 shows that the optimal routing rule is obtained by setting the routing score to the accuracy gain ∆(hσ) in (5). In Section 4.1, we approximated this optimal score by SCD(hσ) in (7), which, unlike ∆(hσ), depends only on model disagreement and does not require ground-truth supervision. We now complete the framework by showing how SCD can be learned in practice using only model-generated data. Learning corrected disagreement. The corrected-disagreement score in (7) consists of two quantities: weak–strong disagreement and strong-model self-disagreement. We learn both offline using only model-generated responses, without ground-truth labels. For each training prompt p, we generate Kw independent responses from the weak model, yw,1, . . . , yw,Kw, and Ks independent responses from the strong model, ys,1, . . . , ys,Ks.
