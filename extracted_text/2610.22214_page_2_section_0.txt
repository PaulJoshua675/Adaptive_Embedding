Moreover, a minimum edge-to-edge spacing between templates is required to ensure stable patterning. This idea drives our approach. As illustrated in Figure 1, a time- limited ILP solver first provides an initial solution and intermediate features. A graph neural network (GNN) [10] trained on these features then predicts which vias are likely to benefit from reas- signment. The solver re-optimizes only the selected vias while keeping all others fixed, converging to high-quality solutions in a fraction of the original runtime. Analogous to a lever, the solver’s intermediate features serve as a fulcrum: a small, targeted effort produces a disproportionately large improvement. However, effi- cient solving alone is not enough if the underlying conflict model is inaccurate. Existing methods [5–8] define conflicts based on center-to-center distances between vias, a metric carried over from conventional MPL. In DSA-MP, 193i lithography fabricates guid- ing templates instead of direct vias, and these templates may take non-circular shapes [11]. Simply evaluating manufacturability by checking center-to-center distances between vias fails to reflect the true process constraints. Since the final via quality is highly dependent on template geometry, sufficient spacing between tem- plates is required to provide a correction margin against proximity effects and ensure stable template formation. Therefore, we con- struct a conflict graph that explicitly captures edge-to-edge template spacing and refine the EILP formulation accordingly. Our major contributions are as follows: • We propose a novel conflict graph model for DSA-MP and refine the formulation of EILP to account for edge-to-edge template conflicts. • We propose a GNN-guided local refinement strategy, where the trained GNN model efficiently selects vias for refine- ment while keeping others fixed, significantly accelerating convergence. • Our method outperforms state-of-the-art works by achieving about 45× speedup with only 0.1% cost increase on 7 nm synthetic benchmarks, while generalizing well to other 7 nm designs and 5 nm industrial layouts. The process involves template definition, BCP deposition, annealing, and pattern transfer, enabling the formation of dense and uniform vias [12]. As illustrated in Figure 2(b), we restrict feasible configurations to 1-D templates (n × 1 holes, 𝑛≤2) to ensure high yield and robust- ness, since templates with fewer holes generally provide more stable and reliable patterning [6]. The spacing of vias/contacts formed by the DSA process within a template is governed by the natural pitch 𝐿0 of the BCP, which limits the grouping distance to a specific range (52–63 nm in this work). In addition, sufficient edge-to-edge spacing between adjacent templates must also be reserved. With- out sufficient spacing, OPC and other correction techniques lack the room to counteract proximity effects, making it impossible to ensure stable and accurate template profiles. However, the edge- to-edge spacing constraints of templates were often neglected in previous studies. For example, in Figure 3(c) and Figure 3(d), previ- ous work considered both assignments feasible, but in reality only Figure 3(d) satisfies manufacturability, as the simulated contour in Figure 3(c) fails to match the required template profile. The contours are obtained using the rigorous lithography simulator S-Litho [13]. 2.2 Problem Formulation Definition 1.
