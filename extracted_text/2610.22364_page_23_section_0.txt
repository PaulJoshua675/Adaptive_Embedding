Circles show training-time environment latents, and triangles show latents adapted from test-time interaction trajectories. Colors indicate the underlying physical setting or causal class for visualization only; these labels are not provided to the model. For each task independently, we center its training-time 32-D latents by their mean ¯z, compute the leading two right singular vectors V1:2 of the centered matrix without feature rescaling, and plot (z −¯z)V1:2. Test-time latents are projected using the same mean and basis; they do not enter the PCA fit. Thus the PC axes are task-specific rather than shared across panels. E.4 COMPUTATIONAL EFFICIENCY We profile a representative Friction setting on one H200 GPU. For training, each method is warmed up for two optimizer steps and timed for ten further steps. Figure 10 reports the median compute- only optimizer time per update, excluding dataset reads, and peak allocated GPU memory rather than full-run training time. Each measured update has six loss-bearing video chunks; method-specific conditioning computation remains included. The world-model and latent updates of our method are separate phases and are therefore shown separately. LoRA TTA starts from the Standard World Model and has no distinct world-model training row. For inference, all methods receive the same test-time interaction trajectory and five disjoint query video chunks. We use 25 denoising steps and exclude model loading, dataset reads, and MP4 writing from timing; query generation includes pipeline sampling and VAE decoding. Interaction process- ing is performed once and reused across queries. DINOv2 Context encodes the interaction trajectory without gradient updates, our method runs 40 latent updates, and LoRA TTA runs 50 adapter up- dates.
