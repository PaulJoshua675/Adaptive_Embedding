Figure 3 shows the board output following the INT8 reference and preserving visible VRU structure. We evaluate deadlines in two frames. The relative frame sets each hard deadline as a fixed margin over the plat- form median, testing whether a platform reliably meets a deadline sized to its own throughput: at a 10% margin over 100k frames the dedicated KR260 probe (one ResNet- 50@224 inference per frame) misses 0.011%, against 4.5% for the loaded GPU and 0.5% for the thermally-throttled Orin at their own margins (Fig. 5c); shorter traces can show zero misses at this margin, so measured maxima are reported as envelopes, not bounds. The absolute frame scores the deployment against a fixed 100 ms perception budget: at urban 50 km/h the vehicle travels 1.4 m per bud- get interval. On this budget, the six-inference DPU frame- budget probe (six sequential ResNet-50@224 INT8 infer- ences per frame, 6× the relative-frame probe’s accelerator work, held fixed; exact configuration in the released code and traces) runs at 95.5 ms median and misses on 0.027 to 0.152% of unenforced frames across three 100k-frame seeds (0.070% pooled), with per-seed maxima of 124.3, 133.2, and 169.0 ms; the enforced run of Fig. 5b is a sep- arate trace, on which the watchdog converts every miss into a flagged fallback, every output landing within 7.8 ms of the deadline over 100k enforced frames (10.2 ms worst over all three enforced runs). The full-resolution FlashOcc pipeline is far heavier than this proxy (Tab. 2): accelerated stages compose to roughly 0.8 s per frame serial, and the CPU head, a pointwise two-layer MLP over the 200×200 grid (22 GFLOP), measures 6.7 s as an unoptimized FP32 NumPy reference on the A53 cores; recompiled as two 1×1-convolution DPU subgraphs with an int8 lookup-table softplus bridge it runs at 505 ms median (stage medians 89.6, 389.3, 31.7 ms; independent medians need not sum to the end-to-end median; 97.3% argmax agreement with the FP32 head), 13× faster than that unoptimized reference. The full pipeline thus composes to about 1.3 s per frame. Input downscaling does not buy this back: the BEV stage is independent of image resolution, and halving the trunk input without retraining collapses accuracy (4.32 vs 20.48 mIoU paired, under a superseded view-transform scale, so the ratio is the evidence), and halving the BEV grid needs an architecture-consistent neck. A 10 Hz tier therefore re- quires a retrained reduced-grid model, a measured engineer- ing boundary rather than a knob. 7
