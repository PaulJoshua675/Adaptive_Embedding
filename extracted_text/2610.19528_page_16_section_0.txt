Our human evaluation provides complementary ev- idence to automatic metrics, but remains relatively limited in scale. In addition, retriever alignment is learned for a particular translator, making the transfer of retrieval policies and evidence prefer- ences across models a valuable direction. Finally, exploring SRAT on tasks that require combining evidence from more sources could further reveal the potential of sequential retrieval for translation on more complex and general scenarios. D Ethical Considerations SRAT aims to improve translation quality, but re- trieved sources may contain inaccurate or biased information that influences the resulting transla- tions. Careful source selection and human review remain important, particularly for sensitive or high- stakes content. When using online retrieval, queries may expose information from the source text to external services, motivating privacy-aware deploy- ment for confidential material. Translation quality may also vary across languages and communities, warranting broader evaluation before deployment. Finally, policy training and external model evalu- ations consume computational resources; reusing trained components and exploring more econom- ical adaptation strategies could help reduce their environmental impact.
