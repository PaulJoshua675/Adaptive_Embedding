The process transitions from global coarse-grained event alignment and audiovisual synchronization to fine-grained character-level attribution. 4 Experiments 4.1 Experimental Setup Implementation Details: We adopt Qwen2.5-Omni-7B [45] as our backbone model. During training, the vision and audio encoders are kept frozen, while only the adapters and LLM parameters are updated. Videos are sampled at 2 FPS, with a maximum per-frame pixels of 512×28×28 and total pixels of 30×512×28×28. And the maximum generation length is set to 2048. For SFT, we train the model for two epochs with a batch size of 128 and a learning rate of 1 × 10−5. For GRPO, we conduct training for two epochs with a batch size of 16, a learning rate of 2×10−6, a KL penalty of 0.04, and a group size of 8, following a progressive reward weighting strategy. We employ Qwen3.5-27B [33] as the reward judge. All training is conducted on 32 NVIDIA H20 GPUs. Benchmarks: For general audiovisual captioning, we evaluate EVA-Cap under two settings: direct quality assessment (video-SALMONN-2 testset [29] and UGC-VideoCap [43]) and QA-cascaded evaluation (Daily- Omni [62] and WorldSense [15]). The former directly evaluates the overall quality of generated captions through dedicated scoring mechanisms, while for the latter, we first generate descriptive captions with our model and then adopt Gemini-2.5-Pro [8] to answer QA questions solely based on the produced captions. To assess the capabilities in event-centric alignment, we further conduct evaluations on subsets of ChronusAV [7] and AV-SpeakerBench [23]. For ChronusAV, we select 1,066 videos under 200 seconds to assess audiovisual synchronization via video-to-audio (V2A) and audio-to-video (A2V) grounding.
