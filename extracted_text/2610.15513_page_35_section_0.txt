Two interpretive cautions apply. First, these rankings are specific to REPRO’s strategy library and hybrid-weighting scheme, and a differently designed optimizer might surface different associations. Second, rank order alone does not quantify absolute success-rate differences between strategies, and the raw per-strategy success counts that would support such a quantification were not retained for this paper. From a software-engineering standpoint, the descriptive takeaway is that no single filtering mechanism is likely to cover the full attack surface: pattern-based filters rank low against semantic transformations, persona-based defenses rank low against progressive escalation, and temporal and authority heuristics rank low against sophisticated framing. A comprehensive defensive evaluation should exercise the full strategy space that evolutionary optimization explores, regardless of whether the family-level patterns generalize to other models. 4.5.2 Attack Prompt Evolution. REPRO’s generation cycle is visible in the lineage of individual attacks. A representative trace against a malware-generation behavior begins from a single-strategy seed (past-tense reframing), which fails against modern safety training. Semantic crossover then combines that lineage with a persona parent to produce a nested fictional scenario reaching partial success, and continued mutation under a semantic-drift strategy yields a knowledge-graph formulation that distributes the harmful request across several conceptual nodes and succeeds. The progression illustrates the core dynamic: single-strategy seeds fail, crossover between Pareto- frontier parents produces qualitatively different candidates, and continued mutation drives them toward attacks that no single strategy would have produced directly. Full traces for this and other behaviors are in the released artifact. 4.5.3 Emergent Patterns and Short Attacks. Three patterns appear consistently across REPRO’s successful attacks beyond the family-specific strategies already discussed. Emergent strategy combinations. Semantic crossover frequently discovers novel combinations absent from the initial library. Combining deep inception (nested fictional scenarios) with past-tense reframing produces attacks that frame harmful requests as historical fiction within a story within a simulation, a triple layer of semantic distancing that consistently bypasses filters across model families. The attack space extends beyond the union of individual techniques; their interaction creates novel vulnerability surfaces that are difficult to anticipate from the component strategies alone. Cross-version transfer within families. We observe qualitatively that attacks discovered for earlier versions of a model family (for example, Grok-2) often succeed against later versions (Grok-3, Grok- 4.1) with minimal adaptation, though we did not systematically measure cross-version transfer rates. Model families appear to share underlying vulnerability patterns that survive version updates, suggesting that safety improvements between versions target specific attack surfaces without addressing the shared architectural vulnerabilities that REPRO discovers.
