Table 11 gives the values, intervals and trace counts. Proposition 1 (Population optimum of outcome supervision). Let N be the number of valid steps in a trace and assume 0 < Eπ[N] < ∞. Under a strictly proper per-step Bernoulli objective with final label Y broadcast to every valid step, the population risk minimizer over measurable [0, 1]-valued functions of the hidden prefix is g⋆(h1:t) = EQ[Y | h1:t]. Here Q is the step-weighted training law, which counts each trace once per valid step. Moreover, g⋆(h1:t) = EQ[V π t | h1:t], and it equals V π t under representation sufficiency. Proposition 2 (Exact recovery from a prefix). Under the completed-incorrect-trace law, fix step t, condition on T ≥t, and let st denote the prefix. A measurable function f with f(st) = V ∗ t almost surely exists if and only if Var(V ∗ t | st) = 0 almost surely. No function of st returns the realized V ∗ t exactly where that variance is positive, regardless of training label or loss. tPIT is a function of st if and only if its conditional law given st is a point mass. For mt = V ∗ t −V π t , Var(mt | st) = Var(V ∗ t | st). A hidden-state probe is no stronger than a probe of st, since h1:t is a deterministic function of st. The claim concerns exact recovery of the realized quantity; a probe may still estimate its conditional mean. Proposition 2 applies only where Var(V ∗ t | st) > 0. We do not measure this variance, and 0 < V π t < 1 does not determine it, so Proposition 2 does not explain the localization results below. The values along a completed trace determine tPIT (Lemma 1); the probes below estimate those values or predict the PIT step directly. Both propositions are proved in Appendix C. Length rule. The length rule predicts the PIT step t = round(f (L −1)) of a trace with L steps, with one parameter f. PIT steps occur late in a trace: the median relative depth of the PIT step is 0.70 at 4B and 0.75 at 8B (Figure 2). We score the probes and the length rule by Hit@±2, the fraction of PITs for which the predicted step lies within two steps of the PIT step. In each comparison, f is fitted to maximize Hit@±2 on PITs of problems outside the evaluation set. The length rule is therefore refitted for each evaluation set, because its score depends on that set’s trace lengths and on the relative depths of its PIT steps (Appendix B). Pointer probe. We call the probe trained to predict the PIT step the pointer probe. It differs from the confidence probe in input and target: it reads a completed trace, which Proposition 2, a statement about prefixes, does not cover, and is trained on the PIT step itself. The pointer probe is a transformer encoder with bidirectional attention over the steps of a completed trace.
