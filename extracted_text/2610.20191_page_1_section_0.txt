HEM-Med: A Hierarchical Agentic Memory Framework for Safe Multi-Center Medication Recommendation Min Xiao1,†, Jin Li2,†, Shoujin Wang2, Yeguang Zhen1, Yishuo Li1, Wenpeng Lu1,* 1 Key Laboratory of Computing Power Network and Information Security, Ministry of Education; Shandong Computer Science Center (National Supercomputer Center in Jinan); Qilu University of Technology (Shandong Academy of Sciences); Shandong Provincial Key Laboratory of Computing Power Internet and Service Computing; Shandong Fundamental Research Center for Computer Science, Jinan, Shandong, China Email: min.xiao02@foxmail.com; zhenyeguang92@gmail.com; yishuoli@foxmail.com; wenpeng.lu@qlu.edu.cn 2 University of Technology Sydney Email: Jin.Li-4@student.uts.edu.au; shoujinwang@foxmail.com † Equal Contributors * Corresponding author Abstract—Medication recommendation (MR) is critical for clinical decision support. While existing MR methods are mostly designed for single-center learning, where only one independent hospital is involved, multi-center MR with hospitals of different scales has become increasingly demanding in practice. In multi- center settings, abundant data from large medical centers can improve recommendation performance for low-resource centers. Despite a few preliminary attempts on this task, two critical gaps remain. Gap 1: Existing multi-center methods fail to disentangle transferable cross-hospital clinical experience from hospital-specific prescribing patterns, limiting clinical feasibility and physician acceptance. Gap 2: They commonly overlook multi- label safety constraints of MR, including drug-drug interaction risks and local formulary feasibility, which are essential for safe real-world deployment. To address these gaps, we propose HEM-Med, a novel hierarchical agentic memory framework for multi-center MR. To address Gap 1, we design a disentangled hierarchical memory module that builds patient-level medication- context attribution records and disentangles them into cross- hospital experience memory and hospital-specific residual mem- ory. To address Gap 2, we introduce a safety-aware medication set generation module, which integrates dual-level experience retrieval with safety-aware set decoding to produce clinically relevant, safer, and locally adaptive medication recommendations.
