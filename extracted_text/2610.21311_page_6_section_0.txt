The scalar fitness of an individual is derived from the total order estab- lished by NSGA-II [DPAM02]. Specifically, for all individuals sharing the same skill factor (i.e., assigned to a same specific task Ai, where i ∈[1..ℓ]), the algorithm first performs a ranking based on non-dominated sorting and crowding distance. Non-dominated sorting partitions this group into suc- cessive fronts F1, F2, . . . . Here, Fj is defined as the set of non-dominated solutions within Si \ ∪j−1 r=1Fr, where Si denotes the entire set of individuals for task Ai. For each front Fj, the crowding distance (cDis) is computed for every solution. These individuals are then sorted into a total order: an individual X precedes Y if X belongs to a front with a smaller index, or if both are in the same front but cDis(X) > cDis(Y ) (ties are broken ran- domly). Each individual is subsequently assigned a factorial rank equal to its position in this sorted list. Finally, its scalar fitness is set to the reciprocal of this factorial rank. The detailed procedure is outlined in Algorithm 2. Algorithm 2 Scalar-fitness(S, τ(S)) 1: Let Si = {X ∈S | τ(X) = i} 2: for each Si do 3: Use fast non-dominated sorting [DPAM02] to divide Si into F1, F2, . . . 4: Use crowding-distance() [DPAM02] to separately calculate the crowd- ing distance cDis of each individual in F1, F2, ..., and separately sort each front Fj, j = 1, 2, . . . , in order of decreasing cDis values: Fj.1, Fj.2, . . . 5: List Si as F1.1, F1.2, . . . , F2.1, F2.2, . . . , and denote this list as Si.1, Si.2, . . . 6: Assign the factorial rank ri(Si.k) = k, where k is its integer index in flattened list Si, and assign the scalar fitness φ(Si.k) = 1/ri(Si.k) = 1/k 7: end for Now it remains to introduce how the MO-MFEA generates an offspring population Qt from the current population Pt. First, a parent population P ′ of size N is generated via binary tournament selection (based on the scalar fitness). Then P ′ is divided into N/2 parent pairs, each of which is used to generate two offspring individuals. The crossover is applied to this parent pair if both parents share the same skill factor, and is also applied with probability prm even for parents from different tasks to facilitate the knowledge transfer. Then the mutation is applied. The skill factor of each offspring is inherited directly from its own parent if no crossover is applied, but is randomly picked from the parent pair if the crossover is activated.
