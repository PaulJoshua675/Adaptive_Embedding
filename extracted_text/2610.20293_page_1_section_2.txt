LLM-based retrieval systems increasingly generate multiple retrieval actions for a single input, for example by decomposing a question or producing parallel rewrites that cover different facets of an information need (Tan et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2026). Post-training on downstream outcomes directly optimizes what the system ultimately delivers rather than surface similarity alone (Jin et al., 2025). This challenge is especially pronounced in sponsored search, where several rewrites retrieve candidate ads whose pools are merged before downstream ranking and auction. An action that is useful alone may become redundant, or compete with candidates from other rewrites, once the pools are combined. In our setting, individual actions do not receive separately observed downstream outcomes; only the realized action set receives an observed reward. This raises a central question: when multiple retrieval actions jointly determine one outcome, what information is needed to construct context-dependent attribution within the realized set? A rollout-level reward provides a valid score-function estimator for the gradient of the expected set- level objective (Shao et al., 2024). The difficulty is that the realized set reward does not provide the alternative-subset values needed for coalition-based attribution. For an action qi and S ⊆A \ {qi}, the marginal R(S ∪{qi}) −R(S) can vary with S. Singleton and leave-one-out capture the empty- context and full-complement extremes, leaving intermediate coalition contexts unobserved.
