B.3.2 Recovery from controlled token corruption We test whether a policy can recover from deliberately introduced token errors. For each policy, we first generate one T = 32 response per test problem and retain verifier-accepted references. At within-block phase 0.75, we construct matched clean and corrupted partial states, retaining only references with at least eight eligible visible tokens. We replace k ∈{1, 2, 4, 8} visible tokens with uniformly drawn vocabulary tokens, excluding the original token, the mask, and tokenizer-defined special tokens. Corruption sets are nested across k, and every k for a given policy and intervention block uses the same reference responses. The intervention leaves eight calls for the full-canvas policy. For the two-block policy, corrupting the first block leaves four calls in that block and sixteen in the second, while corrupting the second block leaves four calls. Table 14 gives the resulting populations. Token restoration and answer correctness. TIPS frequently restores the exact substituted token, even though neither the policy nor the decoder receives the reference during continuation. As seen in table 15, for first-block corruption, TIPSblock restores 76.53% of substituted tokens at k = 1 and 66.55% at k = 8; the corresponding second-block rates are 66.35% and 57.23%. The full-canvas rates range from 42.50% to 46.41%. Exact restoration and answer correctness nevertheless measure different outcomes: an alternative token can preserve a correct solution, and restoring one token need not repair every consequence of the corruption. All corrupted conditions have lower answer accuracy than their matched clean continuations. Restoration pathway and collateral drift. Most finally restored tokens last return through a learned reveal rather than deterministic finalization (Table 16). For example, with eight corrupted tokens in the first block, 5,011 of the 5,830 finally restored tokens return through learned reveals, and 819 return at finalization. However, corruption can also affect positions that were not substituted. Table 17 compares untouched-token disagreement with the reference against its paired clean-control rate. Excess disagreement is positive in all twelve corrupted conditions and generally grows with k. These measurements show both recovery and propagation of perturbations. 23
