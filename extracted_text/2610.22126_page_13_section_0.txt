A Implementation for longer dependency paths The topological ordering used to define the path is a bookkeeping device, not a modeling choice: any two valid topological orderings of the same DAG agree on every parent-child pair, so Ri is invariant to which one is used. (10) The last (two-hop) term uses the full derivative dγi dα rather than the local ∂γi ∂α used in the direct and one-hop terms: it is the deepest dependency explicitly tracked, so it must fold in any effect of xi’s own ancestors that is not otherwise given its own term, exactly as the one-hop formula’s indirect term already does for its (only) tracked layer. Paths of greater length can be handled analogously, tracking one additional layer with local partials and moving the full-derivative fold to the new deepest layer; our current implementation stops at two hops, which sufficed in all our experiments. This construction keeps P i Ri = F(x) −F(b) exact at any truncation depth H (the number of explicitly tracked hops): the untracked remainder beyond the deepest tracked layer is always folded into that layer’s full derivative rather than discarded. B Details of the basis expansion To increase the expressive capacity of the surrogate while preserving interpretability and edge-wise decomposition, each parent feature is transformed through a structured basis expansion applied independently per feature. Let xi denote the value of feature i. Inputs are first standardized: zi = xi −µi σi , (12) where µi and σi are the feature-wise mean and standard deviation estimated from the training data. Standardization ensures that the basis functions operate on comparable scales across features and improves numerical stability during optimization.
