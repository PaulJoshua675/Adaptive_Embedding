Preprint. Under Review. Prompt 2: Source-Document Summarization Prompt System: You are an expert research scientist. You read a paper (title plus full text or abstract) and distill it into a compact, structured card in a fixed SI schema (problem gap / core claim / method) plus its empirical outcomes (results / takeaways). The spine (problem gap / core claim / method) captures the IDEA -- the conceptual approach -- stated independent of how well it turned out; results / takeaways carry what the work actually found. Be faithful to the source: report only what the paper claims, and use the numbers it states. Return only a single JSON object. User: Summarize the paper below into a structured card with five fields -- problem gap, core claim, method, results, takeaways. The spine fields (problem gap / core claim / method) play DIFFERENT roles; a card whose fields collapse into restatements of each other is not useful. Read the definitions carefully. • problem gap: the gap this paper addresses -- the open question, tension, or unresolved difficulty in the area that motivates the work. State it as one clear gap: a contradiction, a missing explanation, a scope mismatch, an untested assumption, a disconnect between lines of work, or a bottleneck holding the area back. Name the gap itself, NOT the paper’s specific solution to it (1--2 sentences). • core claim: the paper’s load-bearing, non-obvious claim -- the observation, mechanism, or reframing that gives the problem a specific, newly tractable angle and distinguishes this work from others on the same problem. It is a claim about how the world is (an asymmetry, a hidden dependency, a failed assumption, a reinterpretation), stated as the idea itself, independent of how well it turned out. WHAT approach/mechanism/hypothesis does the work introduce, and WHY would it work? (1--2 sentences). • method: at a high level, HOW the paper turns the claim into research -- the kind of move it makes (combining ideas, extending a result to a new setting, making a fragile result robust, deriving something formally, mapping a space empirically, building an artifact) and how that move addresses the gap. Keep it at the level of an approach and setup, not a step-by-step protocol (2--3 sentences). • results: the main findings -- key quantitative/qualitative outcomes, comparisons against baselines, notable ablations. Include negative or null results and approaches the paper reports did NOT work, not only the wins. Report the concrete numbers the paper states where available (2--4 sentences). Do NOT report the paper’s own untested conjectures as if they were findings -- those belong in takeaways. • takeaways: implications -- what worked and what did not, limitations, and what the result enables or rules out going forward. If the paper makes speculative claims or conjectures it does not test, note them here and mark them as speculative (1--3 sentences). IMPORTANT split between the spine and the outcomes: • problem gap, core claim, and method must read the SAME whether or not the idea has been tested. State the gap, the mechanism/claim, and the approach; do NOT put results, metrics, benchmark numbers, or evaluation verbs (‘‘show’’, ‘‘achieve’’, ‘‘outperform’’, ‘‘improves accuracy by X%’’) in them. • core claim is NOT a prediction awaiting test (that’s a hypothesis), NOT a plan of action (that’s the method), and NOT a restatement of the gap. • Keep problem gap broad enough that core claim is doing real narrowing work; if the gap already contains the claim’s commitments, state the gap more broadly. • results and takeaways are where the empirical story and the numbers live. Be concise and specific.
