(7) and Sec. 3.4), shown as coloured trajectories. 3 METHOD 3.1 DENSE 4D CORRESPONDENCE WITH HUMAN POINT MAPS Given images {It}S t=1 of one person, person masks and focal lengths ft, we predict the geometry and correspondence of the person’s body, clothing and hair across time. Using each mask and focal length, we resample the person into a virtual camera aimed at the centre of the person’s bounding box, keeping the camera centre fixed. At inference, focal length is Dense4D’s only supplied camera parameter; the model neither receives nor predicts world-camera poses. Below, pixel coordinates and foreground sets Mt refer to these crops. Ground-truth masks define the training targets; at inference, the masks specify the person to be processed. The first input is the reference, r = 1; input order need not be chronological. For p ∈Mt, the HPM target Xt(p) ∈R3 is the position at the reference time of the surface point observed at p in frame t. This position is expressed in the reference image’s virtual camera coordinates. The scalar depth target Dt(p) is the same point’s optical-axis depth in frame t’s virtual camera at time t. Both targets use a shared normalising scale s. To define these targets formally, let xt→u(p) denote the world-space position at time u of the surface point observed at pixel p in image t. For supervision, the known virtual-camera extrinsics Ev,t ∈R3×4 map homogeneous world points into the crop’s camera coordinates.
