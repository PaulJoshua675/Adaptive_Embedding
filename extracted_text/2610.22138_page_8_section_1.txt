We asked whether a multi-agent LLM discussion can correct a false claim that is repeatedly asserted by a few participants. To answer this question, we tracked each agent’s private answer alongside its public speech, revealing information unavailable to transcript-only evaluation. In open-ended factual discussion, the group largely fails to correct the claim, and the failure is mostly invisible in the chat: agents that retain the correct answer often do not defend it, and agents that lose it rarely say so. The shift persists when agents are asked for their own view, spreads among benign agents once it begins, and rarely reverses; in our experiments, neither a larger group nor a more knowledgeable base model prevents it. Evaluating multi-agent systems solely through their transcripts or final answers therefore misses much of what the group holds, and robustness to peer pressure has to be measured directly, at the level of individual agents. 8
