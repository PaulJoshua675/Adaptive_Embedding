Ablating the graph-aware weighting mechanism. Table 4 and Fig. 6 validate both the effectiveness of GAIW and the necessity of its stabilization design. Compared with no weighting (𝑤(𝜏) = 1), using either graph centrality or collision-risk weighting alone al- ready improves the score (81.74/81.82 vs. 81.61) while reducing collision rate, lane deviation, and jerk, showing that both structural complexity and explicit risk cues help prioritize planning-critical trajectories. Among the single-factor variants, risk-only weighting yields slightly better score and safety metrics than centrality-only, whereas centrality-only achieves slightly lower jerk, suggesting that the two cues capture complementary aspects of planning diffi- culty. When both cues are combined, stabilization becomes crucial: removing either𝑤-clipping or mean-normalization degrades perfor- mance, while Full + stabilization achieves the best overall result, with the highest score (82.07) and the lowest collision rate (0.0519), lane deviation (0.0154), and jerk (0.1252). This trend is consistent with Fig. 6, where the stabilized variant maintains higher reactive closed-loop score and lower collision rate throughout training than variants without clipping or normalization, indicating improved optimization stability. Latency remains nearly unchanged across all variants, showing that the gain comes from better training signals rather than additional inference cost. Overall, these results support our design intuition: centrality emphasizes structurally complex scenes, collision-risk weighting highlights immediate safety-critical interactions, and clipping plus mean-normalization prevents overly heavy-tailed importance weights from destabilizing optimization.
