REPRO: Reflective Evolutionary Prompt Optimizer for Automated LLM Red-Teaming 17 detected, REPRO selects a restart intensity from a five-level taxonomy ranging from a soft mutation- rate bump to a full strategy-weight reset combined with clearing of family-exhaustion tracking. Escalation is also ASR-aware: at very high ASR (≥90%), the optimizer escalates faster because small improvements are valuable; at lower ASR, escalation is slower to allow more exploration before aggressive interventions. The five levels range from a soft mutation-rate bump with elites and strategy weights untouched, through progressive elite reduction and strategy-weight decay, to a full strategy reset; the highest level additionally clears family-exhaustion tracking. Per-level parameters are in the released configuration. Termination occurs only when the optimizer has exhausted 𝑅max restarts (default 3) and stagnation persists; on termination REPRO returns the global-best candidate tracked throughout the run. Knowledge transfer via pattern abstraction and amortization. Caching only the literal text of successful attacks loses the abstract “what made this work” knowledge. REPRO additionally extracts an attack pattern from each successful attack, capturing seven structural dimensions including element ordering, goal-embedding method, authority type, normalized goal position, nesting depth, benign ratio, and provenance. A dedicated pattern library indexes these abstractions by category, goal-embedding method, and authority type. When attacking a stubborn behavior, REPRO retrieves patterns matching the behavior category and converts them into actionable structural guidance for the reflective LLM (framing, target length, goal position, nesting depth) rather than literal text, enabling transfer of attack architectures to new goals. Pattern-guided seeds are injected during restart phases as warm-start candidates. A complementary amortization cache stores literal successful attacks for warm-starting related behaviors, with two design choices reflecting category difficulty. Caching thresholds are category- specific: hard categories like explosives and weapons use lower thresholds (so that partial successes still produce warm-start seeds), while general categories use higher ones. Retrieval uses cross- category bonuses, so that a successful weapons attack can warm-start an explosives behavior. Specific threshold values are listed in the hyperparameters table (Table 3). In-context learning example optimization. The reflective LLM’s effectiveness depends on the quality of its in-context examples. REPRO maintains a curated library of jailbreak examples from the literature [29, 32, 39, 40, 42], organized by strategy. Rather than uniform sampling, REPRO applies Thompson Sampling over per-example success statistics: each example maintains a Beta posterior updated whenever attacks generated with it are scored, and selection probabilities are computed via temperature-scaled sampling without replacement. Unseen examples receive an exploration bonus to ensure all examples are tried at least once. When generating attacks under a specific strategy, the example pool is filtered to examples annotated with that strategy. 3.6 Design Rationale: Bi-Level Characterization Having described the components, we now characterize REPRO at the algorithmic level. REPRO’s nested structure of population evolution guided by adaptive strategy selection admits a principled characterization as a bi-level optimization problem, which connects its design to established results in evolutionary computation and online learning. We state three design properties, each linking a REPRO component to a known result and clarifying the conditions under which that result strictly applies. These are design rationale rather than new theoretical contributions: the first follows immediately from the construction, and the second and third import results from the literature and then describe how REPRO’s setting departs from the assumptions those results require. They explain why the architecture is the way it is and serve as hypotheses that the ablation study (Section 4.4) tests empirically.
