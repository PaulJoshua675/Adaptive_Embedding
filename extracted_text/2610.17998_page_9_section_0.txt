For Tasks 1, 5, 10, and 20, we show the input image, the selected patch location, the SAM generated immediately after learning the task from which the patch is selected, and the SAM obtained after training on all 20 tasks. 5.3 Explanation Drift Analysis Figure 4 compares SAMs generated immediately after learning a task with those obtained after completion of the task sequence. Across all tasks, the salient regions remain largely consistent, indicating preservation of task-relevant visual representations. Interestingly, for earlier tasks, the final SAMs are often more concentrated on the target object and less affected by background regions. This suggests that continual learning not only preserves relevant evidence but can also refine shared feature representations as the network is exposed to additional visual concepts. 6 Conclusions We presented SNN-adapted EPR, a SAM-guided replay strategy for CL in deep SNNs. Experiments on Split-CIFAR10 and Split-CIFAR100 showed favorable ac- curacy–memory trade-offs compared to standard replay, particularly under tight memory budgets. Qualitative analysis further indicated that task-relevant repre- sentations are preserved and may become more localized over time. Limitations include dependence on SAM quality, the use of a fixed patch size, and evaluation on small-scale image classification benchmarks. Future work could explore pre- trained backbones to improve early explanation quality, enabling better patch selection and memory initialization. Another direction is to make SAM more class-sensitive through output-conditioned scores or temporal attention, enabling the introduction of regularization terms to explicitly prevent explanation drift.
