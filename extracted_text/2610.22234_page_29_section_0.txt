We further analyze infeasible tasks as a complementary limitation of the current data pipeline. Both OSWorld and WindowsAgentArena contain a non-negligible fraction of infeasible tasks, roughly 10% of their task sets. These tasks mix at least two phenomena: genuinely impossible instruc- tions whose target condition cannot be satisfied, and GUI-infeasible tasks where the visual route may be blocked while a programmatic route could still modify the underlying state. Our synthesis pipeline is designed around executable goal states and therefore does not naturally generate infea- sible training tasks. As a result, the model can under-learn when to stop and declare infeasibility, often continuing until the maximum interaction budget is exhausted. Conversely, for tasks that are infeasible only under GUI interaction, a hybrid agent may legitimately solve them through code, which can still be judged as incorrect if the benchmark expects infeasibility recognition. As a diag- nostic, after removing infeasible tasks from the evaluation set, the average success rate on OSWorld and WindowsAgentArena increases by up to 3–5 absolute points. This suggests that future task de- sign and data synthesis should explicitly model infeasibility, distinguishing true task impossibility from modality-specific infeasibility so that agents can learn both effective execution and calibrated refusal. 29
