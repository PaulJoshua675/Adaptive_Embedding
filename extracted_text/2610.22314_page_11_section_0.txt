Every counsellor reply is judged once, given the counsellor’s system prompt, including its memory section, and the session up to that reply: 61,819 replies across the 18 counsellors. 5.2 Results We present the severity of counsellor replies and the MHQR of each counsellor LM in Table 6, broken down by issue category in Figure 4. The three GPT-5.6 models obtain the lowest MHQR (0.84–1.13). Since the judge belongs to the same family, this gap may partly reflect self-preference. However, DeepSeek- V4.1-Flash also places the three GPT-5.6 counsellors first, with broad ranking agreement with GPT-6.1 Sol (Kendall’s τ = 0.82; Table 15). Among the remaining counsellors, Sonnet 5 obtains the lowest MHQR (2.22), followed by Kimi K3, GLM-5.3 and Opus 5. We note that Opus 5 sometimes fails to end its turn properly: in 5.8% of its replies it takes over the patient’s role, accounting for 0.22 of its MHQR (Figure 4). Nevertheless, Opus 5 is still ranked worse than Sonnet 5 after removing these instances. Differences in MHQR come mostly from how often a reply is a major concern. The share of minor replies is similar for every counsellor (4–21%), whereas replies with no concern fall from 61–70% for GPT-5.6 to 5–16% for the smaller open-weight models. Critical replies are rare (at most 1.85% for any counsellor) and concentrate in a few sessions. Session scores can also vary widely within a counsellor: the worst 5% of sessions are generally as severe as an average session of the next tier of models. 5.3 Findings and Discussion Guidance calibration dominates. Over-exploring, under-guiding and giving bad advice account for 60–88% of every counsellor’s MHQR. A common pattern is a counsellor repetitively asking questions even after it is clear the patient is seeking guidance, so under-guiding and over-exploring are usually flagged together. Among replies observed at each turn position, under-guiding is flagged in 12% of replies in the first two turns and about 70% from turn 9 onwards (Figure 9). Some counsellors reinforce distortions. Distortion Reinforcement is the second-largest allocated MHQR category for eight counsellors, including GLM-5.3 (0.57, 22% of its MHQR) and Kimi K3 (0.46, 18%), flagged in 30% and 25% of their replies. For example, when a patient wonders whether a small gesture “was probably her trying”, GLM-5.3 asks “Does knowing she’s been trying change what you’d want to say to her now?”. This shift from “probably” to “knowing” strengthens the patient’s tentative interpretation, consistent with the concern addressed by Treating perspective as fact. 11
