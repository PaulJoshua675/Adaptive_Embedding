Our AI tutor aims to incorporate all documents in a course, including lecture materials and readings, lab and project description documents, quizzes and past exams, as well as the course website, by developing appropriate data pipelines and prompt techniques for each type of course material. 2.2 Non-Native English Speakers in Computing Educa- tion The enrollment of international students in computing programs has increased by 14% to 298,523 during the 2022-23 academic year. Notably, 23% of them pursued mathematics and computer science, and 19% studied engineering [4]. These students often face language barriers and confidence issues, which negatively impact their learning experience in computing courses. In response, researchers are exploring tailored supportive strategies for this demographic. Polishchuk [15] surveyed 124 first-year non-native English speaker (NNES) engineering and agriculture students, identifying several key communication barriers, including vocabulary and grammar difficulties and challenges with accents and slang. Notably, 85.5% of students reported translating utterances to their native language, which hindered their fluency, while 61.25% were afraid of making mistakes, and 47.5% felt nervous speaking in front of the class. Polishchuk recommended enhancing collaborative work and com- munication practice outside the classroom. Guo [2] noted how confidence issues affect NNES’s communica- tion with instructors and TAs. Via a survey of 840 respondents from 86 countries on a programming education website, this study found students reluctant to participate in discussions or ask questions due to fear of errors and being misunderstood. They preferred instructional materials in simplified English, supplemented with visuals and inline dictionaries. Guo suggested peer mentoring and language support workshops to improve participation and confidence. Soosai Raj et al. [16, 17] conducted a four-week experiment with second-year engineering students in India, comparing those taught only using English to those receiving bilingual instruction in English and Tamil. The study found that bilingual teaching reduced cognitive load, improved comprehension of programming concepts, and boosted student engagement and confidence. Lau and Yuen [8] noted that K-12 students in Hong Kong understood programming better with bilingual instruction, highlighting the value of native language incorporation for NNES students. To address the challenges faced by NNES students, we aim to develop an AI tutor that accepts questions in the student’s native language and provides bilingual answers to facilitate understanding of complex computing concepts. This approach will also help build confidence among students who may hesitate to approach their instructors and TAs. Since our authors are mainly native Mandarin speakers, we implemented our prototypical system for Mandarin. 3 Method 3.1 Students’ Questions We collected 620 questions from a semester’s Piazza posts for an intermediate-level Data Science (DS) programming course of 400 students1. We excluded 238 posts that included images for two reasons: (1) they are difficult to transcribe, either because they are entire desktop screenshots with only a small portion containing relevant information or they involve complex plots and graphs; (2) the GPT-4 API vision service was prohibitively expensive prior to the release of GPT-4o. The analysis focused on the remaining 382 text- based posts, which we cleaned by removing duplicates, identifiable information, unnecessary HTML formatting, and enclosing code snippets and error messages in backticks. For Piazza posts, students must tag their posts with instructor- defined folders: exams (exam 1, exam 2, final), quizzes, programming projects (P1, P2, ..., P7), labs, logistics, and others. One author used these student-tagged folders and the post content to sort the posts into four categories: 90 logistics, 54 conceptual, 41 environment setup, and 197 programming posts. Logistic questions dealt with syl- labus specifics and demands for clarifications on acceptable coding practices and plotting requirements. Conceptual questions typically pertained to quiz and exam content. Environment setup questions covered the setup and maintenance of virtual machines and Jupyter- Lab, package installations, and git-related issues. Programming questions primarily revolved around project development challenges, addressing issues such as implementation of specific functionalities, debugging, and optimization for code efficiency. We then generated the Mandarin dataset by translating the Piazza posts into Mandarin using the Google Translate API2. To ensure precision, all translations were manually reviewed and edited by a native Mandarin-speaking author. Sections previously marked with backticks were excluded from the translation. 3.2 AI Tutor Data Pipeline A flowchart of our pipeline is shown in Figure 1.
