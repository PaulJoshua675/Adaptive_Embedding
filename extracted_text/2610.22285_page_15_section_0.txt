C User Study Details Procedure After providing informed consent, participants were familiarized with the task and the preference-query interface. They were told that the robot was learning to carry a mug of coffee across a cluttered table and that it had not yet learned to do so correctly. Four task objectives were explicitly stated: (1) keeping the mug upright, (2) staying close to the table, (3) staying away from the laptop, and (4) staying away from the person. These objectives were repeated before each condition. Participants did not provide any demonstrations: the underspecified teaching signal that began each task was generated by a simulated Boltzmann-rational demonstrator (rationality βu = 0 on the task’s underspecified feature) and held fixed across all participants and conditions using a single random seed, so that every participant began each task from an identical reward belief and contributed only preference responses. The study crossed the prior (uninformed vs. informed) with the query delivery (no explanation vs. explanation), yielding four conditions: Uninformed, Informed, Uninformed + Explanation, and Informed + Explanation. Each condition began with a short familiarization phase in which the participant watched three example trajectories drawn under the ground-truth reward.
