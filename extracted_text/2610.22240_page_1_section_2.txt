Multimodal deep search agents address knowledge- intensive visual questions through interleaved rea- soning and external retrieval (Wu et al., 2025; Geng et al., 2025). Recent advances move toward long- horizon investigation, connecting evidence across webpages and using acquired visual observations to guide subsequent searches (Huang et al., 2026; Du et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026b). Two challenges limit training for this setting. Ex- isting multimodal datasets contain many questions solvable through parametric knowledge or short re- trieval, while constructing harder questions through graph composition and visual enrichment entails re- lation collection, image curation, and evidence val- idation (Chen et al., 2026; Chu et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026b). Reusing existing image–question pairs offers a starting point for constructing ques- tions with greater search demands. Meanwhile, useful long-horizon investigation may end in re- dundant verification, premature stopping, or an incorrect answer. Terminal rewards provide lim- ited process feedback, and trajectory-level advan- tages supply a shared scalar signal throughout a rollout (Shao et al., 2024). Identical returns also remove group-relative learning contrast. Process rewards and teacher guidance address these lim- itations (Chen et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026); we combine outcome-based optimization with selec- tive token-level teacher guidance. To address these challenges, we propose MMSearch-OPD, combining controllable ques- tion refinement with joint reinforcement learning (RL) and on-policy distillation (OPD). Our Refine pipeline reuses simple multimodal questions and their candidate reasoning chains. Expand extends chain depth, Blur increases disambiguation require- ments, and Visualize introduces retrievable visual evidence. These configurable operators control the intended evidence requirements while reusing seed assets (Figure 1). After question validation and supervised initialization, joint training combines answer rewards and behavior penalties with token- level OPD on imperfect student trajectories. The shaped reward gap adjusts guidance strength, while an exponential schedule reduces the OPD coeffi- cient as training proceeds.
