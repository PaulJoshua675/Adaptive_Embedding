Source matching is checked separately by a quotation ver- ifier V (e, C) ∈{0, 1} for each quotation e. It matches normalized conversational text, permits bounded typographical variation while protecting negations, pronouns, particles, conjunctions, and digits, and requires ellipsis-separated fragments to retain source order. Only text from the supplied conversation can serve as evidence. Let E≤j denote quotations introduced up to block j. A block receives credit when its assess- ment is positive and at least one of these quotations is verified: gj = Jj max e∈E≤j V (e, C), H = N X j=1 gj, (3) with the maximum defined as zero for an empty set. The judge determines semantic support and novelty; the verifier checks quotation occurrence within the reasoning prefix. Reusing evidence can support a new inference, but repeating the same in- ference earns no additional credit.
