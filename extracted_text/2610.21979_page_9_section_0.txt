Theoretical Analysis What is lost in aggregation? P2BPO compares individual reference blocks; element-group feedback pools multiple blocks, such as those of the same type, and page feedback pools the groups. For common matched rollouts, let Q contain scores of 𝐾blocks, H center across rollouts, and B = HQ. With fixed positive normalized weights, M pools blocks into 𝐶nonempty groups and 𝜶combines their scores into an additive page utility: B |{z} reference blocks ↦−→ BM |{z} element groups ↦−→BM𝜶 |{z} page . (5) When 1 < 𝐶< 𝐾, both maps are non-invertible: grouping can cancel opposing block contrasts, while page aggregation can additionally cancel contrasts between groups. Retaining reference-block identity preserves distinctions that these aggregations can erase before normalization. Appendix D.1 proves this result (Proposi- tion D.1) and quantifies the coefficient-reconstruction error imposed by coarser routing (Lemma D.2). Appendix D.2 establishes how UAP activates and routes feedback for omitted or uniformly imperfect outcomes. Appendix D.3 proves SCR’s source-block budget conservation and the conditions on redistribution strength and mask precision under which more credit reaches the intended targets. 6. Discussion and Limitations P2TPO constructs element-level feedback from ground-truth references, so its alignment and quality scores depend on annotation accuracy and serialization conventions. Annotation noise, repeated text, and alternative valid serializations can introduce ambiguity into element matching and token labels. Reliable references and alignment procedures are therefore important conditions for applying the framework. Our experiments focus on text, formulas, and tables; code and figures are not evaluated as distinct element types. Extending the framework to these elements requires suitable representations, alignment procedures, and quality measures. Future work could investigate their joint optimization within an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework. Finally, SCR currently allocates credit between aligned correct and erroneous token regions, with a shared redistribution weight within each region.
