End-to-end document parsers transcribe images of digital documents, scans, and photographs into a single structured sequence in reading order (Blecher et al., 2024; Taghadouini et al., 2026; Ouyang et al., 2025; Du et al., 2025). We study full-page Markdown transcription, with faithful text, LATEX formulas, and HTML tables. Dense heterogeneous content, skew, warping, and uneven illumination challenge accuracy and com- pleteness (Ouyang et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2026). Errors are often local: an otherwise accurate output may corrupt a formula, omit words, or miss an entire element. Supervised next-token learning serves as the foundation for these parsers, while reinforcement learning (RL) is used for post-training optimization of reference-based measures for fully generated documents. These measures evaluate content accuracy, structural fidelity, and completeness. Recent parsers highlight the ef- fectiveness of reinforcement learning in post-training through layout-aware rewards and verifiable unit tests (Wang et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Poznanski et al., 2025). A key question for full-page transcription is how to convert these measurements into feedback that corrects missing or corrupted content while preserving correctly transcribed sections. With page-level outcome rewards, GRPO assigns one response advantage to all generated tokens (Shao et al., 2024). Yet two outputs of the same page can receive similar overall scores while making opposite local errors: one transcribes a formula correctly but omits a table, whereas the other recovers the table and corrupts the formula (Figure 1(a)). A shared response coefficient does not preserve these element-specific contrasts. GDPO normalizes reward dimensions separately before combining them into a shared response coefficient (Liu et al., 2026b); CSPO routes table-wide functional-attribute advantages to corresponding tokens (Yang et al., 2026).
