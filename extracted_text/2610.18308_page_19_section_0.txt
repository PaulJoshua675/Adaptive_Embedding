This comparison is descriptive for the selected, audit- stratified sample; its class balance differs from the full Stage 4 population. Illustrative disagreements and supported exclu- sions. In CASE-066, the auditor excluded a syn- thetic response expressing appreciation for Get Out based partly on an alleged aversion to scary films. However, the corresponding historical ReDial user requested horror and a combination of comedy and scares (ReDial conversation 3189); two annotators favored retention and one exclusion. This illus- trates the risk of treating genre preferences as ex- clusive. Conversely, in CASE-071 all three annotators endorsed the auditor’s exclusion of a detailed recol- lection from Arrival; the historical user had earlier stated that they did not think they had seen the film and would add it to a watch list (ReDial conversa- tion 12034). Such temporal evidence is suggestive rather than conclusive: a user might watch the film subsequently. Finally, in CASE-082, all three annotators fa- vored excluding a synthetic response that the au- ditor left unflagged. The historical user had pre- viously stated that they had not seen The Greatest Showman (ReDial conversation 14457). However, expressing a positive opinion about a film does not necessarily imply having watched it. We therefore treat this case as a disagreement requiring further examination, rather than a confirmed auditor error. These examples illustrate turn-level judgments, not independent validation of individual audit flags. Scope and limitations. The equal 35/35/35 al- location, together with at most one sampled turn per historical ReDial user, means that the reported sample accuracies are not unbiased estimates of ac- curacy over all Stage 4 turns. Consecutive presenta- tion blocks may confound audit stratum with order, learning, or fatigue effects. The ReDial excerpts were retrieved automatically; although matched to historical users, their relevance and completeness were not exhaustively adjudicated, and missing con- text may affect individual decisions. In addition, the annotators were co-authors and majority vote is an operational human reference rather than an independently established ground truth. The eval- uation therefore characterizes human agreement with Stage 4 turn-level outcomes, not the validity of each issue flag or population-level recall. The 14 three-class disagreements, particularly the five affecting the remove-versus-keep action, remain priorities for further evidence-based adjudication.
