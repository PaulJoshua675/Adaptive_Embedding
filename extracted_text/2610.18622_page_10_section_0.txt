5 Discussion of the Results and Practical Implications 5.1 Discussion of the Results Overall, the gains from segmentation are larger on NormasTCU than on Ulysses-RFCorpus. The characteristics of Ulysses-RFCorpus may help explain this difference. Its whole-document baselines are already strong, leaving less room for improvement through segmentation. This may be related to both the documents and the queries. On the document side, each legislative proposal includes a justification in which the deputy describes the problem to be solved and the proposed solution. This section semantically enriches the document and mitigates the vocabulary mismatch problem of lexical retrieval. On the query side, deputies use natural-language descriptions of the intended legislative proposal. In contrast, NormasTCU has no justification sections and usually has short keyword queries whose terms may not appear in the normative text. The gains on Ulysses-RFCorpus are also limited by the documents that do not follow the expected hierarchical structure. Approximately one-third of the collection cannot be segmented and is therefore represented as a single indexed unit. For these documents, the segmented indexes behave like whole-document indexing, reducing the potential gains from segmentation on this collection. The results allow us to answer the proposed research questions: RQ1. Does structural segmentation improve Brazilian legal document retrieval compared with whole-document indexing? Answer: For dense retrievers, the three representations that include contextual information (Hierarchical Chunks, Core Chunks + Full Context, and Core Chunks + Metadata) outperform whole-document indexing, whereas Core Chunks + No Context does not.
