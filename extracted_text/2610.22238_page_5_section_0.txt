In other words, correlations within the block Bt can induce error in the sampling process of MDM, even when the score function is accurate, i.e., pθ,i(·|y) = p∗ i (·|y) for all y and i ∈M(y). 4 Algorithm Design In this section, we introduce the algorithm design based on the rejection sampling technique. We first consider a simplified setting where the ground-truth conditional probability p∗ i (·|y) is available. The rejection sampling framework can be viewed as a proposal-selection loop: • Proposal: XBt is sampled from the distribution q∗(xBt|y), which is the distribution of XBt in parallel sampling, i.e., q∗(xBt|y) = Y i∈Bt p∗ i (xi|y). • Selection: XBt is accepted with probability r∗ Bt(x, y) = e−B · p∗ Bt(x|y)/q∗ Bt(x|y). which is the ratio of the ground-truth probability and the sampling probability multiplied by a constant e−B which ensures that the acceptance probability is no larger than 1. The proposal-selection loop is repeated until XBt is accepted. In this way, rejection sampling ensures that XBt follows the joint distribution p∗ Bt(·|y), and the selection step can be viewed as the correction from the biased parallel-sampling distribution q∗ Bt to the real joint distribution. The proposal step can be implemented in a standard way, but two major challenges arise in the selection step: (i) calculation of the probability p∗ Bt, and (ii) ensuring r∗ Bt ∈[0, 1] with proper choice of B and calculation of probability ratio. Anari et al. (2026) resolved the first challenge by noticing the decomposition of the unknown block-level probability into the product of known token-level probabilities p∗ i (xi|z). In detail, let πt = (it,1, . . . , it,nt) be an arbitrary ordering of the block Bt. We define the sequence {zk}k∈[nt] recursively, which represents a trajectory between y and x ⊕y: z0 = y, zk = xit,k ⊕zk−1. Then p∗ Bt can be factored into the product of terms p∗ it,k(·|zk−1) directly given by the score function: p∗ Bt(x|y) = nt Y k=1 p∗ it,k(xit,k|zk−1). 5
