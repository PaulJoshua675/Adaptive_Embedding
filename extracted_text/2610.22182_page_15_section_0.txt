Layer depth, pooling, and calibration We examine the source of LAMP’s gains through three groups of controls: the information used by the kernel branches, the representation retained at each depth, and the calibration and weighting of their corrections. All comparisons use paired support banks at 4-shot. Information from different depths. MULTIBAND constructs three kernels from the same final CLIP global descriptor, using bandwidths 0.5βg, βg, and 2βg. Each calibrated branch fits the shared support residual independently, and their corrections are mixed with the original global predictor. This control tests the benefit of changing the similarity scale while keeping the visual representation fixed. Across the ten non-ImageNet datasets, native-region kernels outperform MULTIBAND by 1.286 points with ViT and 1.635 with RN50 under matched 113-candidate mixture searches (Table 6, top). The family-adjusted 98.75% intervals are positive for both backbones, supporting the value of information from multiple encoder depths. Depth and spatial pooling. We compare four representations on Pets, Food101, DTD, and EuroSAT. REGION retains four regional descriptors at each of three depths. CEN- TROID averages the four normalized descriptors at each depth and normalizes the result; WHOLE pools each feature map directly into one descriptor. FINAL uses only the deepest native feature map, with three bandwidths. Thus WHOLE versus FINAL tests the use of multiple depths, while REGION versus CENTROID or WHOLE tests the effect of retaining separate regional descriptors. All four families evaluate 113 mixtures; REGION, CENTROID, and WHOLE use fixed regional bandwidth ratio r = 1. WHOLE exceeds FINAL by 1.193 [0.648, 1.806] points with ViT and 0.905 [0.499, 1.384] with RN50, with pointwise 95% intervals in brackets. Combining depths therefore improves accuracy even when each map is pooled into a single descriptor. REGION and WHOLE differ by −0.028 [−0.162, 0.129] and 0.072 [−0.083, 0.220] points, respectively. The similar mean accuracies of REGION, CENTROID, and WHOLE indicate that compact descriptors retain much of the observed gain from native-layer information. 15
