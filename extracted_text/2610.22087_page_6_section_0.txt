6 Results To test whether current pathology FMs allow pathologists to query an institutional archive without task-specific training, we developed the following search engine (Fig. 1). Users can select a region of interest (ROI) in two ways: automatically, using our ABMIL model that identifies the most diagnostically relevant, spatially contiguous region; or manually, by selecting a rectangular ROI of arbitrary size from a WSI, allowing the pathologist to query tissue they consider diagnostically relevant (Fig. 1a). This ROI is divided into non-overlapping patches (Fig. 1b), and each patch is converted by a FM into a numerical representation that captures its visual features (Fig. 1c). These representations let us measure how similar any two patches are to each other. If the patches within the region are highly similar to one another, the region is treated as homogeneous, and the representations are combined into a single representative query. If the patches are dissimilar, the region is treated as heterogeneous: the patches are grouped into subsets of visually similar tissue, and each subset contributes its own representative query (Fig. 1d). This step reduces redundancy from near- duplicate patches while still allowing heterogeneous tissue to be searched. A homogeneous region therefore generates one query, while a heterogeneous region generates several. Each query is then compared against the representations of previously diagnosed archive cases, extracted in advance using the same FM (Fig. 1e). Matches are scored using our modified Chamfer similarity (Fig. 1f), and the pathologist is shown a ranked list of the most similar archive cases, each labeled with its ICD-O diagnosis. For any retrieved case, the individual patches driving the match can be inspected at their original location within the slide, letting the user verify that the retrieved tissue is genuinely comparable. Figure 1 – Overview of the search engine. (a) Starting from a WSI, a region of interest can either be selected by a pathologist or automatically through attention-based multiple instance learning. (b) The region of interest is tiled into non-overlapping 512×512-pixel patches. (c) Each patch is passed through a foundation model, producing a matrix of patch-level embeddings. (d) Based on pairwise similarity among the query embeddings, feature vectors are either averaged or greedily clustered. (e) Query vector/s are searched against a FAISS index of pre-extracted patch embeddings. (f) Retrieved patches are scored by a modified Chamfer similarity, returning each match's ICD-O diagnosis alongside its WSI. Abbreviations: d = embedding dimension of the foundation model. n = number of patches after tiling. ICD-O = International Classification of Diseases for Oncology.
