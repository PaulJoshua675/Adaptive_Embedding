Partisan identity shifts baseline exposure more than rewrit- ing a single webpage. Across all engines, baseline Democratic SOV follows the same Democrat > Independent > Republican ordering, showing that explicit voter partisanship systematically shifts Demo- cratic exposure. The Democrat–Republican baseline gap reaches 11.6, 3.4, and 3.0 percentage points on gpt-4o-mini, gpt-5-mini, and gemini-2.5-flash, respectively. For comparison, Compara- tive Distinction increases Democratic SOV by only 2.20, 1.76, and 2.16 points under the Democrat persona. Thus, explicit partisan identity can shift baseline exposure more than rewriting a single webpage. The Independent persona produces higher Democratic SOV than persona-free generation across all three engines, consis- tent with prior evidence of left-leaning tendencies in default LLM outputs in U.S. political contexts [15]. Despite these baseline shifts, rewriting remains effective across all three partisan identities. Baseline shifts from richer voter profiles vary across en- gines. We use a shallow decision tree to identify three interpretable, preference-enriched subgroups from ai-persuasion and construct questionnaire-derived profiles from their defining attributes. These selected profiles are not intended to represent all voters in their respective preference groups. We exclude explicit ideology and candidate-evaluation variables and use voting-lean labels only for profile construction, not in the generation prompts. For reporting, we refer to the profiles as Lean left, Swing, and Lean right. Their prompts describe, respectively, a voter with high political interest, low religiosity, and strong emphasis on candidate personality, a voter with low political interest, lower emphasis on policy posi- tions, and moderate emphasis on candidate personality, and a voter with high political interest, high religiosity, and low emphasis on candidate personality.
