2.1 Graph-based Collaborative Filtering Conventional Matrix Factorization (MF) models [29] primarily capture first-order interac- tions between users and items, often overlooking the intricate topological dependencies inherent in user-item data. To address this limitation, NGCF [12] introduced a message-passing mecha- nism via Graph Neural Networks to explicitly model high-order collaborative signals. Building on this, [13] proposed a streamlined architecture by removing redundant feature transformations and non-linear activations. This simplification not only reduced computational complexity but also established LightGCN as a state-of-the-art benchmark in graph-based recommendation. However, these models remain heavily reliant on the connectivity of the interaction bipartite graph. In scenarios characterized by long-tail items or extreme data sparsity, the high-order infor- mation propagation mechanism often falters [16], which fundamentally constrains the model’s representation capability. 2.2 Cold-start Recommendation To compensate for the scarcity of interaction signals, researchers have extensively explored leveraging item metadata, such as textual descriptions, categories, or multimodal attributes, to enhance recommendation performance. Current cold-start solutions generally bifurcate into approach-driven and data-driven strategies. Approach-driven strategies, such as Dropout- Net [18], explicitly optimize cold-start performance by randomly dropping collaborative features during training.
