B.5 On the Load-Balancing Loss We use an MoE-100M-A38M to compare the standard load-balancing loss with our modified loss in (6). The two objectives differ in whether they balance the mirror routing probabilities {𝑝𝑙,𝑒}𝑒or the original routing probabilities {𝜉𝑙,𝑒}𝑒. As shown in Tab.6, balancing {𝜉𝑙,𝑒}𝑒reduces perplexity, at the cost of a slight increase in per-iteration training time. Since our primary objective is model performance, we adopt the modified load-balancing loss throughout our experiments. B.6 MirrorMoE performance degrades for MoEs without shared experts We further evaluate MirrorMoE on MoEs without shared experts. For ease of parallelization, we use 𝐸∈{64, 128} and 𝐾= 8, with slightly adjusted model depths. These results are therefore not directly comparable to those in Tab.1. As shown in Tab.7, without shared experts, MirrorMoE with 𝜌0 = 0.4 and a cosine schedule performs comparably to standard training, with no significant improvement. This result is consistent with our motivation: when shared experts are absent, the exploration dilemma is less pronounced, leaving less need to shrink the exploration space. B.7 Comparison with top-𝑝routing We additionally compare MirrorMoE with another adaptive routing approach, namely top-𝑝routing (Huang et al., 2024). Top-𝑝routing activates the smallest set of experts whose cumulative probability exceeds 𝑝, where 𝑝∈(0, 1) is a threshold parameter. Intuitively, it also adapts the number of activated experts over training. Early in training, when routing probabilities are relatively uniform, more experts are required to exceed the threshold 𝑝. As routing becomes more concentrated, fewer experts are sufficient. However, this adaptivity makes a fair comparison challenging. In our experiments, we observe that the average number of activated experts per token decreases over the course of training. As a result, top-𝑝routing uses different amounts of activated computation at different training stages, making direct comparison with MirrorMoE and the baseline difficult, as both use a fixed number of activated experts. Tab.8 reports the number of activated experts (including shared ones) averaged over layers and tokens for MoE-100M-A38M with 𝐾= 8 and 𝑆= 2. In terms of training performance, we use entropy regularization with a coefficient of 10−4 for top-𝑝 routing.
