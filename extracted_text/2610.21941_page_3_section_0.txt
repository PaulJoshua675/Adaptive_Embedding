Multi-teacher distillation objective For each attribute a, the student branch is trained to maximize the cosine similarity with the corresponding teacher representation. The attribute-specific loss is defined as: La i = 1 −cos(zi,a, ya i ). A central difficulty in holistic multi-teacher distillation is that the different teachers have heterogeneous embedding dimensions, training dynamics, and levels of difficulty. Uniform loss weighting may therefore over-emphasize attributes that are easier to optimize or whose losses have larger magnitudes. Mono-task learning provides an estimate of how much useful information can be transferred from each teacher into the student architecture. Attributes exhibiting larger learning gains tend to cor- respond to teachers that are easier for the student to approximate. Assigning them larger weights encourages the Multi-Attribute optimization to focus on supervision signals that are better aligned with the student capacity, while avoiding over-emphasizing attributes that are intrinsically difficult or noisy. We introduce a gain-based weighting strategy derived from mono- task learning dynamics. For each attribute a, we first train a mono- task student using the same architecture but retaining only branch a. Let Lmono a (e) denote the normalized validation loss of this mono- task model at epoch e. The learning gain for attribute a is defined as: ga = Lmono a (1) −min e Lmono a (e). The final Multi-Attribute weight is obtained by normalizing the gains across attributes: λa = ga P b∈A gb . This strategy assigns larger weights to attributes for which the student shows stronger learning progress in the corresponding mono- task setting, while reducing the contribution of attributes that rapidly saturate or provide weaker training gains. Unlike dynamic weighting strategies such as uncertainty weighting or GradNorm [18], the pro- posed weights are estimated once from mono-task learning dynamics and remain fixed during multi-teacher training, avoiding additional optimization variables and training instability. IV. EXPERIMENTS In order to evaluate the relevance of our proposition, we benchmark it on different tasks related to the targeted sentence-level attributes. A. Teacher supervision We target four utterance-level attributes in our multi-teacher stu- dent learning framework: semantics, speaker, language, and emotion continuous representations. For each attribute, we used a specialized teacher: the BGE-M3 text encoder (when transcription is avail- able) or the SENSE speech encoder for semantics (BGE-M3 and SENSE share the same semantic continuous space), an ECAPA- TDNN speaker embeddings trained on VoxCeleb for speaker attribute, an ECAPA-TDNN language embedding model trained on VoxLin- gua107, and emotion2vec-base [16] as a teacher for emotion attribute. All teacher models remain frozen throughout training.
