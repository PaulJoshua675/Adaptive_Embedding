Deployment cost (top), episode reward (bottom left), and explained variance (bottom right) comparing X-RIC variants with RL and TLM-RL baselines. a) Episode-level filtering: Per-step reward differences from the outset would require comparing all steps across both TLM and RL runs—potentially thousands of compar- isons—many of which may derive from episodes offering no benefit. To measure the impact of the TLM-guided controller during early training, we calculate ∆¯Rk = ¯RTLM,k −¯RRL,k for episode k, where ¯RTLM,k = 1 Tk PTk−1 t=0 rTLM k,t , with rTLM k,t representing the reward at step t in episode k and Tk de- noting the number of steps in episode k; ¯RRL,k is computed analogously. The metric ∆¯Rk serves as an initial coarse filter to identify promising episodes. This episode-level comparison provides a fast and reliable method to assess whether the TLM enhances overall performance across full control sessions, effectively smoothing out noisy or unstable per-step rewards that may arise from random network events (e.g., traffic spikes or signal interference). By computing a single score per episode, we can discard unhelpful episodes, thereby avoiding the computational cost of analyzing every individual step. Episodes with ∆¯Rk > δ (margin δ ≥0) are admitted into a raw pool Draw, assuming they encapsulate meaningful policy improvements. This step removes trajectories where agentic reasoning underperforms while maintaining full automation. b) Action-level filtering: Episode-level selection may in- clude trajectories with mixed-quality decisions. We therefore retain a state-action pair (sk, ak) only if the critic prefers the demonstrator’s action to the current policy’s proposal, Q(sk, ak|θQ) > Q(sk, π(sk|θπ)|θQ) + ϵ. (1) with optional margin ϵ ≥0. Here, ak is the TLM-guided action, π(sk|θπ) is the current policy, and Q(·|θQ) is the critic with parameters θQ. This Q-filter removes suboptimal actions and yields a distilled demonstration buffer Dfil suitable for imitation. Q-filtered BC Objective. Given Dfil, we define a selective BC loss, LBC = 1 |Dfil| X (sk,ak)∈Dfil ∥π(sk|θπ) −ak∥2 2 · I [Q(sk, ak|θQ) > Q(sk, π(sk|θπ)|θQ) + ϵ] , (2) where |Dfil| is the number of retained samples and I[·] is the indicator function. The actor update direction balances RL and selective imitation, gθπ = λ1∇θπJ(θπ) −λ2∇θπLBC, λ1, λ2 > 0. (3) where J(θπ) is the standard return-maximization objective (e.g., DDPG policy gradient). We anneal λ2 as the policy approaches demonstrator performance, preventing the actor from being constrained below expert level.
