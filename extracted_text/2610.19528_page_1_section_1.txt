Retrieving external knowledge can improve large language model (LLM) translation by pro- viding supplementary information such as id- iom interpretations and terminology. However, existing retrieval-augmented machine trans- lation systems typically apply predefined re- trieval strategies uniformly across all inputs. We argue that translation operates in a partial- evidence regime: LLM’s parametric knowledge suffices for many inputs, while external evi- dence benefits a subset and usually supports rather than directly provides the desired trans- lation. This creates two challenges: deciding when to retrieve and what evidence to acquire. We introduce Selective Retrieval-Augmented Translation (SRAT), an agentic reinforcement learning framework that learns when to trans- late directly or retrieve, what to retrieve, and how to integrate these decisions by optimiz- ing final translation quality. To align evidence retrieval with downstream translation quality rather than simple semantic similarity, we further develop joint Plackett–Luce optimiza- tion and translation-quality contrastive train- ing for retriever-training. Experiments on id- iom, knowledge-intensive, and document-level translation with offline or online retrieval show that SRAT consistently outperforms never- and always-retrieve baselines, and quality-aligned retriever training provides further gains when similarity-based retrieval is insufficient. The results demonstrate the benefit of treating re- trieval as a learnable translation action rather than fixed preprocessing. 1
