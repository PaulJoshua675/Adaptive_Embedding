B. RQ2: Retrieval Effectiveness and Efficiency Table III shows that BM25 is the strongest raw retrieval baseline, reaching R@10=0.3890 and MRR@10=0.1829 ver- sus 0.3378 and 0.1544 for SSR. SSR therefore does not establish general retrieval superiority over established lexical methods. Within the SSR representation, IDF weighting consistently improves all reported ranking metrics relative to Structural Overlap + ISI, including R@10 by 5.3% and MRR@10 by 5.6%. The ISI affects computation rather than the positive- scoring ranking: direct exhaustive execution on the common 200-query subset verifies exact unpadded top-K agreement with full-scan Structural+IDF. Exhaustive scoring evaluates all 8,841,823 passages and requires 69,848.3 ms per query on average, whereas SSR explicitly scores approximately 286,638 passages in 941.5 ms. This corresponds to 96.8% fewer explicitly scored passages and a 74.2× mean retrieval-latency speedup. Answer to RQ2. RQ3: End-to-End Agent Discovery RQ3 compares full-catalog orchestration with SSR and BM25 candidate pre-selection under the same maximum can- didate budget K = 20 on 200 independently sampled MS MARCO queries and registries of N ∈{100, 500, 1000, 5000}. 1) Candidate-Budget Robustness: On the 200 RQ3 queries, SSR candidate recall with K = 20 is 0.980 for N ≤1000 and 0.950 at N = 5000. Increasing the budget to K = 50 raises recall at N = 5000 only to 0.960 while substantially increasing the candidate pool. Independent retrieval-only validation on 1,000 disjoint queries corroborates the chosen operating point. At K = 20, candidate recall is 0.973, 0.969, 0.965, and 0.941 for N = 100, 500, 1000, and 5000, respectively, satisfying the predefined within-0.02 decision rule at every registry size. Only K = 20 and K = 50 satisfy the rule, so the smallest qualifying budget is K = 20. At N = 5000, increasing to K = 50 improves recall by 0.018 while increasing mean pool size from 17.822 to 41.311 (2.318×). The validation supports the robustness of K = 20 but remains retrospective rather than a prospective held-out selection protocol. 2) Retrieval and Selection Decomposition: Table IV shows comparable observed end-to-end effectiveness for SSR and BM25 candidate pre-selection. For N ≤1000, candidate recall is 0.980 for both methods. At N = 5000, BM25 has higher candidate recall (0.970 vs. 0.950), while SSR has slightly higher conditional selection accuracy (0.9316 vs. 0.9227), yielding E2E accuracies of 0.895 and 0.885, respectively. Retrieval coverage and LLM arbitration therefore contribute separately to end-to-end error. Paired exact McNemar tests on end_to_end_correct detect no statistically significant difference between SSR and BM25 at any registry size after Holm correction (adjusted p = 1.000 throughout). Candidate recall likewise shows no sta- tistically detectable paired difference after correction; at N = 5000, the adjusted p-value is 0.500. These results support de- scribing observed effectiveness as comparable under the eval- uated sample, but do not establish statistical equivalence.
