C Model-Assisted Generation and Validation We use Claude Code with an Opus-family model and Gemini as backends for three model-assisted stages. In layout editing, the model receives a BDDL scene specification, preference language, object geometry, and spatial constraints, and proposes a modified BDDL layout. In personalized planning, it receives the original task and preference instructions, baseline plan, available objects and skills, and a scene image, and returns a sequence of (skill, object) pairs plus required, prohibited, and ordering constraints. In image inspection, independent VLM judgments assess task completion and visually observable preference compliance from rollout frames. Inspired by single- and multi-agent evaluations [76, 77, 78], the layout and plan candidates follow a generate-and-trial loop: each proposal is instantiated and tested in simulation, and any candidate violating the output format, physical constraints, task-success condition, or preference constraints is rejected. The observed failure is included when generating a revised candidate. The reported VLM failure rate in each stage was below 23%. For image inspection, inconsistent independent judgments are flagged for re-evaluation rather than accepted directly. Image judgments are restricted to visible properties such as contact location, approach direction, gripper orientation, and final placement. Motion speed, acceleration, trajectory smoothness, and clearance height are instead measured with trajectory-based geometric or kinematic metrics. The following condensed prompts specify the inputs, output formats, and constraints used in the three stages. Complete prompts, model configurations, and validation logic will accompany the code.
