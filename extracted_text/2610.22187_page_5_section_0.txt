3.5.1 Normalization Because raw scores are marked on different scales, we first normalize all scores to a common [0, 1] range. Let ri,s denote the raw score assigned to item i on scale s, and let as and bs denote the scale’s specified minimum and maximum values. The normalized score is zi,s = ri,s −as bs −as ∈[0, 1]. (1) 3.5.2 Minimal Scale Pairs The 16 rating scale conditions cross response for- mat (continuous, ordinal), scale range (3, 5, 11, or 21 points) and scale sign (signed, unsigned).2 To isolate sensitivity to each design element, we compare only minimal pairs, defined as two rating scale conditions that differ in one design element while the other two remain fixed. For example, comparing a continuous signed 5-point scale with an ordinal signed 5-point scale isolates sensitivity to response format because both scales have the same sign and range. This procedure leads to 40 minimal pairs. Eight pairs differ only in format, 24 differ only in range, and eight differ only in sign. We assess sensitivity to each design element using the pairs that differ in that element. 3.5.3 Numerical Scale Sensitivity Numerical scale sensitivity measures how far a text’s score moves when only the scale changes. For a minimal pair (s, t) and the N items with a valid score on both scales, it is the mean absolute difference between the normalized scores: SSMAE(s, t) = 1 N N X i=1 |zi,s −zi,t| . (2) SSMAE ranges from 0 to 1. A value of 0.10, for example, means that scores move by one tenth of the full scale on average. 3.5.4 Semantic Scale Sensitivity Semantic scale sensitivity measures whether a change in scale alters how the output is interpreted. We use three categories because this is the finest distinction that all 16 rating scale conditions can express. The shortest scales offer only three values, low, neutral, and high, and every prompt defines the low end, the middle, and the high end of the scale, even when ordinal scales also label intermediate levels such as slightly or strongly. Splitting the normalized range into equal thirds places the three values of a scale with 3 points in three different categories and applies the same boundaries to every scale. 3.5.5 Analytical Framework We compute SSMAE and SSswitch for each mini- mal pair within every combination of task, prompt- ing strategy, and model. This yields 1,200 esti- mates per metric across 3 tasks, 2 prompting strate- gies, 5 models, and 40 minimal pairs. To answer RQ1, we average the estimates from the minimal pairs that change each design element, separately for format, range, and sign. We report these averages for numerical sensitivity, SSMAE (RQ1a), and for semantic sensitivity, SSswitch (RQ1b). Each task, prompting strategy, model, and pair receives equal weight. We additionally analyze whether sensitivity to one design element depends on the values of the other two elements. For example, we calculate range sensitivity sep- arately on ordinal and on continuous scales (i.e., format), and format sensitivity separately on scales with 3, 5, 11, and 21 points (i.e., range). To test RQ2 to RQ4, we fit a weighted least squares (WLS) regression of the 1,200 estimates on the design element changed (format, range, or sign), task, prompting strategy, and model.
