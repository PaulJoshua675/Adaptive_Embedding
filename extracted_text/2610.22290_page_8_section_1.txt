Diversity in RL post-training. RL post-training is widely observed to reduce output diversity, with studies showing that it lowers diversity relative to supervised fine-tuning (Kirk et al., 2024), collapses policy entropy (Cui et al., 2025), and concentrates probability on behaviors already present in the base model (Zhao et al., 2025), so that base models of- ten solve more problems at large sampling budgets (Yue et al., 2025; Oh et al., 2026); supervised fine- tuning shows a similar collapse (Dang et al., 2025). Mitigations optimize objectives tied to pass@k (Walder and Karkhanis, 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Tajwar et al., 2026), reward novel or diverse out- puts (Song et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025, 2026a; Jiang et al., 2026), regularize toward the base model (Li et al., 2026b), or control entropy and sampling tem- perature (Cui et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2025; Oh et al., 2026), and are typically evaluated by pass@k (Yu, 2025). ELITEREPLAY instead replays an archive built for coverage, and we measure diversity at the more meaningful level of solution strategies. Mixing demonstrations and replay into RL. RL has long been combined with supervised learn- ing from demonstrations or past experience (Lin, 1992; Hester et al., 2018; Oh et al., 2018). In LLM post-training, the off-policy data range from pre- training text and expert solutions (Ouyang et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2026; Yan et al., 2025) to re- played verified trajectories (Zhang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025) and base-model samples (Li et al., 2026b). These works use replay to learn efficiently from good solutions or to stay close to a reference model. ELITEREPLAY uses a replay set built by quality-diversity search to keep distinct correct solutions available throughout RL. Quality diversity and evolutionary RL. QD al- gorithms such as novelty search and MAP-Elites seek collections of solutions that are both high- performing and diverse (Lehman and Stanley, 2011; Mouret and Clune, 2015; Pugh et al., 2016; Cully and Demiris, 2018). In deep RL, they have been combined with policy gradients through shared replay buffers (Khadka and Tumer, 2018; Pourchot and Sigaud, 2019), gradient-based vari- ation (Nilsson and Cully, 2021; Pierrot et al., 2022), and archive-driven exploration (Ecoffet et al., 2021). In these works, QD discovers new behaviors, and archived solutions come from a sep- arate search process. ELITEREPLAY instead builds its archive once from the base model’s own so- lutions and replays it to preserve its diversity of behavior. 7
