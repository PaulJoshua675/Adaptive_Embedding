Specifically, if W Q ℓ and W K ℓ are initialized with the same variance, as is standard, the squared Frobenius norms have the same expectation when their shapes match; independent samples are not exactly balanced. Training can then change the imbalance. Residual-output initialization and GQA also give systematic norm differences, so λ = 0 means no deliberate tilt, not exact balance of every pair. SAGGS enforces balance at executed hard events. Soft SAGGS contracts the current log-ratio. Their effects on validation PPL are evaluated against the unperturbed training controls. H.5 Training Stability Gradient geometry distortion can also affect training stability. When the reparameterization amplifies gradients of Bℓby eλℓwhile attenuating the gradients of Aℓby e−λℓ, the spread of gradient norms can change in either direction, depending on the baseline gradients. The amplified Bℓgradients may dominate the global norm and cause clipping to scale the current gradients of every block. This clipping leaves every nonzero pair-gradient norm ratio unchanged. For gradient descent, the clipping scales the data-update direction by the same factor, but the effect on AdamW depends on both moment histories. Theorem 2 excludes clipping, whose multiplier can change under an orbit edit. Decoupled decay is separate from this gradient clipping. We track gradient norm spikes, defined as steps where the total gradient norm exceeds µ + 3σ, across our experiments. While the absolute number of spikes does not change substantially with moderate λ under AdamW in the reported runs, the distribution of gradient mass across parameter groups shifts systematically, which can be detrimental for training efficiency. SAGGS controls pair-norm imbalance; the resulting gradient-norm distribution still depends on the training trajectory.
