In the second layer (online), adaptive step-sizes are governed by curvature surrogates, such as the Generalized Gauss-Newton (GGN) or the empirical Fisher In- formation Matrix (Martens, 2020). These sur- rogates function as a dynamic braking mecha- nism, estimating the steepness of the learning landscape without the massive processing over- head of exact second-order calculations. Because the GGN approximates the Hessian using only first-order derivatives and remains positive semi- definite, fast power iterations can dynamically scale the learning rate matrix Γ. This dual-layer strategy ensures that the neural adaptation re- mains slow enough to satisfy time-scale separa- tion, respecting the physical mechanics of the robot. In practical nonlinear deployments, such as the saturated cubic plant evaluated in Section 6, analytically bounding the global Lipschitz con- stant Lθ and verifying δ-ISS across the entire oper- ational manifold is intractable. Consequently, the gradient clipping applied in our experiments acts as an empirical heuristic to enforce this slow-loop condition. As demonstrated in the subsequent ablation studies, removing this clipping bound vi- olates the time-scale separation, causing the net- work to overreact to transient dynamics and in- ducing mechanical chatter. Bounding λmax(Γ) beneath this inverse curva- ture threshold ensures adaptation remains suﬀi- ciently slow. Violating this threshold breaches time-scale separation, coupling gradient updates with transient dynamics and destabilizing ge- ometric safety bounds. This principle aligns with dual-loop architectures that pair fast inner safety constraints, e.g., Control Barrier Functions, with slower neural adaptation (Annaswamy et al., 2023; Fisher & Annaswamy, 2025). As the online learner stabilizes the system, adapting on instantaneous gradients (utilizing a learning batch size of Bk = 1) causes the network to act as a localized adaptive filter. While this yields high tracking performance on smooth, con- tinuous limit cycles by overfitting to the local tan- gent dynamics, it induces catastrophic forgetting of the global state space (Dohare et al., 2024). For simple periodic tasks, this loss of global plas- ticity is benign. However, in discontinuous envi- ronments (e.g., hybrid switching in exoskeletons (Lippi et al., 2021)), maintaining an experience replay buffer may become necessary to relax the requirement of persistent excitation and preserve geometric safety. 6 ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE To validate the Action Error and the necessity of bounded continuous learning, we simulate a 1- DOF nonlinear Duﬀing oscillator.
