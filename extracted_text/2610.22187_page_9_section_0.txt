In our experi- ments, models appeared to treat 0 as “not toxic” on signed scales, assigning substantially more texts to the high-toxicity category than on unsigned scales. Therefore, We recommend unsigned scales for unipolar constructs, with the lowest endpoint representing the absence of the attribute. Also, researchers should consider evidence of validity when choosing a rating scale. When suit- able human labels are available, they can compare scores across alternative scales with these labels to inform their choice. Ideally, the labels should come from trained annotators following coding guide- lines aligned with the study’s goals. Agreement with these labels shows evidence of validity. When suitable reference labels are unavailable, as may often be the case for many social constructs, ro- bustness checks across alternative scales become especially important for assessing the stability of the findings. In short, careful scale design, robust- ness checks, and validation are needed to support more reliable use of LLM-generated scores. Our findings highlight the importance of these practices when using LLMs to complement human annota- tion in future studies. Limitations We acknowledge the following limitations. First, the generalizability of our findings is limited by the tasks and datasets examined. Future research is welcome to test whether the findings extend to broader cultural contexts, languages other than En- glish, and additional tasks. Also, we compared only two prompting ap- proaches: zero-shot and rubric-based. We did not include few-shot, chain-of-thought (CoT), or chain- of-verification (CoVe) prompting because these strategies introduce additional factors, such as the selection and ordering of demonstrations or inter- mediate reasoning steps, that could confound the comparison. Moreover, CoT has shown limited gains on subjective tasks (Chochlakis et al., 2024), while CoVe is designed to verify factual claims (Dhuliawala et al., 2024), making its applicability less clear for constructs that lack verifiable ground truth.
