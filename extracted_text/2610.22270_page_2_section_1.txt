Vision-based vehicle classification has evolved from hand- crafted descriptors, such as Haar-like features and histograms of oriented gradients (HOG), combined with shallow classi- fiers such as support vector machines and AdaBoost, to con- volutional neural networks trained end-to-end [1]. Supervised deep models now reach high accuracy. Butt et al. [2] fine- tuned several CNNs on 10,000 images of six vehicle types captured under adverse illumination and reported 99.68% accuracy, and Neupane et al. [3] fine-tuned YOLO detectors on nearly 30,000 manually annotated samples of seven classes, including buses. The latter study also shows the cost of domain shift: accuracy roughly doubled (from at most 30% to 71%) only after fine-tuning on data from the target cameras. These systems therefore depend on large, site-specific annotated datasets and on fixed taxonomies in which “bus” is a single class, so they cannot distinguish buses of different routes or operators. Self-supervised learning (SSL) reduces this dependence by learning representations from unlabeled images [4]. In the vehicle domain, Ma and Yang [5] showed that Vision Trans- former features learned with DINO and data2vec, combined with wheel-position cues, reach 97.2% top-1 accuracy on the 13 axle-based classes of the U.S. Federal Highway Adminis- tration. However, SSL is used there only as pre-training for a supervised classifier, and buses again form a single class. Deep clustering removes labels altogether by coupling a representation-learning module with a clustering module [8]. Zhou et al. [8] group these methods into multistage, gen- erative, iterative, and simultaneous approaches; our pipeline belongs to the multistage family, in which features are ex- tracted first and clustered afterwards. Hou et al. [9] note that, before deep learning, image clustering relied on hand-crafted descriptors such as color histograms, and identify robustness to noise and real-world applicability as open challenges for deep image clustering. In the vehicle domain, clustering has mainly been used for unsupervised re-identification (Re-ID). Qiu et al. [6], for instance, generate pseudo-identity labels with a camera-aware DBSCAN scheme and refine them with a focal contrastive loss, outperforming previous unsupervised methods on VeRi-776 and VERI-Wild. Re-ID, however, groups images of the same physical vehicle, whereas our goal is to group different vehicles that share a semantic category. In summary, existing work either classifies vehicles into coarse, predefined types [2], [3], [5] or matches individual vehicles across cameras [6]. To our knowledge, the unsuper- vised, category-level partitioning of the bus class, in which vehicles of different routes share a body structure and differ mainly in livery, has not been addressed. Moreover, none of the reviewed works combines self-supervised deep features with explicit color descriptors, even though color is a decisive cue for route identity. This work addresses both gaps.
