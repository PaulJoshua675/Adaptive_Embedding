Impact sounds encode material properties that appearance cannot reveal. Owens et al. [2016a] es- tablished this connection with the Greatest Hits dataset, demonstrating that visual models can learn to predict impact sounds. RealImpact [Clarke et al., 2023] extends this with force profiles and cali- brated measurements. ObjectFolder [Gao et al., 2023] quantifies the relative contributions of vision, audio, and touch for material understanding, finding that audio often provides complementary infor- mation. In robotics, audio frequently outperforms vision for physical property estimation: Thankaraj and Pinto [2023] show that auditory feedback improves manipulation, and SonicSense [Liu and Chen, 2024] demonstrates object perception through acoustic vibration. We connect this evidence to representation learning: if audio encodes physics, can this structure be distilled into vision? 2
