The complete multidisciplinary synthesis had higher pooled Judge means for every criterion shared with the isolated primary-role components (Table 8). Relative to structured role reasoning, the structured final report was higher by 0.650 points in factual grounding, 0.302 in reasoning coherence, 0.225 in safety, and 0.210 in problem prioritization; data use, differential diagnosis, and management were also higher. Relative to the role-specific clinical opinion, the final report was higher by 0.515 points in problem prioritization, 0.377 in factual grounding, 0.357 in reasoning coherence, 0.111 in clinical-conclusion quality, and 0.104 in safety. The largest gains therefore concerned integration of case facts, prioritization of the clinical problem, and construction of a coherent account. These findings identify a clear value of the complete synthesis stage: it transformed isolated role-specific material into a more highly rated, integrated clinical second opinion across every comparable dimension. Structured prompting had its clearest descriptive advantage in actionability. Management was higher by 0.304 points (2.740 vs. 2.436), with the same direction in seven of eight model pipelines; problem prioritization was higher by 0.056 and factual grounding by 0.040. The overall final-report mean was likewise higher in seven of eight models. The criterion-level advantage was concentrated rather than uniform: reasoning coherence was essentially unchanged, while data use, differential diagnosis, clinical-conclusion quality, and safety were modestly lower. The broad model-level advantage of structured reports was therefore driven primarily by the comparatively large management gain, which outweighed smaller decreases in several other criteria.
