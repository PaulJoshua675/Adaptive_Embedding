The prompts emit only YES or NO, so nothing in the output records the reasoning behind a call. The 91.8% quoted in the main text is a bound and not a corrected accuracy. It is what accuracy becomes if every threshold- disagreement cell is re-scored as correct wherever the model and the note agree the symptom is present. Reaching it defensibly would require re-adjudicating the ground truth prospectively, under a severity threshold written into both the annotation guideline and the prompt, rather than relabeling the current errors after the fact. Intended use. The operating point we report, precision 0.714 and recall 0.638 on diarrhea, is well suited to cohort-level inference and triage but not to autonomous per-patient decision-making. At this precision and recall the pipeline is best used to estimate phenotype prevalence and trajectories across a population, where over- and under-calls partially offset in aggregate, and to surface candidate notes for a human reviewer. Errors are also non-random, concentrating in threshold-ambiguous cases, so any per-patient use should keep a clinician in the loop and treat a positive call as a prompt for review rather than a conclusion. We therefore frame the extracted rates as a real-world evidence and hypothesis-generation tool, and the individual note classifications as a triage aid. Supplementary Note S5.
