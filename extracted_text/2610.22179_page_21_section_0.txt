Proof of Proposition 2: boundary entropy correction. For the proposition’s nonzero input, set A = R(X) and B = R(aX). Let D be the event that A̸ = E + 127 or B̸ = Ea + 127, and write ε = Pr(D). This event captures departures from the ideal exponent labels due to rounding and finite range. On Dc, the same two-bin argument gives |H(B | D = 0) −H(A | D = 0)| ≤1. On D, each byte has only 256 possible values, so the corresponding difference is at most eight bits. Expanding entropy by the binary event D yields |H(B) −H(A)| ≤min{8, 1 + 7ε + h2(ε)}, (19) where h2 is binary entropy. Indeed, the weighted conditional differences contribute at most (1 −ε) + 8ε, and the remaining term I(B; D) −I(A; D) has absolute value at most H(D) = h2(ε). Dropping the trivial eight-bit cap gives the bound in Proposition 2; its correction vanishes as ε →0. If scaling is by a power of two, the conditional difference on Dc is zero and the bound improves to min{8, 8ε + h2(ε)}. For actual normal BF16 inputs, power-of-two scaling changes only the exponent when the output stays normal and finite, and is exactly representable. Reusing the same centered symbols also requires the estimated center to shift by that integer. Sampling, the half-integer rounding convention, center clamping, and reserved-zero remapping can affect this correspondence. Thus neither the proposition nor its entropy bound implies an unchanged code length for every implementation configuration. D.3 Exponent distributions from continuous densities This section studies the distribution of exponents for data drawn from a particular data distribution. Continuous change of parameterisation. We first derive the formula for the distribution of exponents. Let X > 0 have density fX and first use natural logarithms, e = ln X.
