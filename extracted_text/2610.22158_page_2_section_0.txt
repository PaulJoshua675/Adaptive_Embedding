However, this assumption is frequently violated in realistic scenarios. User questions may contain logically inconsistent conditions, false schema as- sumptions, missing premises, or requests beyond the capability of SQL and the underlying database. Nevertheless, state-of-the-art NL2SQL models often generate executable SQL even for flawed queries, producing SQL that is syntactically valid but semantically invalid (Ji et al., 2023; Whitehead et al., 2022; Ren et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2025). In many cases, models implicitly repair problematic questions by injecting unsupported assumptions (Lin et al., 2022). Figure 1 illustrates this failure: although "person with id 5" is underdetermined by the schema, a model may silently map person to actor and emit executable SQL. The resulting SQL is syntactically valid but rests on an unsupported entity choice. We introduce premise critique ability in NL2SQL, namely the ability to recognize flawed queries and abstain from generating misleading SQL. Premise critique ability is orthogonal to con- ventional SQL generation accuracy: a model may produce executable SQL, or even generate SQL that coincidentally matches the user’s intent, while still failing to recognize that the provided question itself is ill-posed. Concretely, it consists of two components: (1) premise validation, namely assess- ing query validity in the corresponding database context and identifying actual flaws; and (2) absten- tion, namely refraining from generating misleading SQL for flawed queries while maintaining SQL generation for valid queries. To systematically evaluate this capability, we introduce PremiseSQL, to our knowledge the first benchmark specifically designed to evaluate premise critique ability in NL2SQL systems. Built upon two widely used Text-to-SQL datasets, Spider 1.0 (Yu et al., 2018) and BIRD / BIRD-filtered (Li et al., 2023), PremiseSQL pairs 1,800 constructed flawed queries with their 1,800 unmodified source queries. It evaluates whether models can recognize flawed premises and abstain from generating mis- leading SQL, while maintaining SQL generation for original queries. We further evaluate frontier LLMs under three prompting settings, including Base, Implicit, and Explicit, to measure both intrin- sic and prompt-induced premise-aware behavior. In summary, our contributions are as follows: • We formalize premise critique ability as a dis- tinct requirement for reliable NL2SQL. • We introduce PremiseSQL, a taxonomy- guided benchmark comprising flawed queries as positive samples and their originals as neg- ative samples. • We develop an evaluation framework that sep- arates intrinsic critique from prompt-induced behavior. Experiments on 16 frontier LLMs reveal a shift from trusting flawed premises un- der standard prompting to frequently rejecting valid queries under explicit critique instruc- tions. 2
