Limitations Clinical Taxonomy. Annotator agreement on MHQR is moderate for severity and lower for individual clinical issues. We argue that some disagreement is inherent to open-ended evaluations and annota- tors often diverge for legitimate reasons such as subjectivity in annotation or multiple plausible answers (Plank, 2022). Nevertheless, larger multiply-annotated samples and further iterations on our taxonomy would help to sharpen definitions of individual issues. User Simulation. While clinicians rate Mindsim patients as more realistic than those of existing sim- ulators, they remain distinguishable from real users (3.55 against 4.20 on a five-point realism scale). In particular, annotators found some of its pushback as performed rather than felt. Additionally, human realism ratings for MindSim cover Sonnet 4.6 patients in first sessions of up to 15 patient turns (due to other simulators not supporting early termination), so it is unclear how realism evolves as conversations run longer or across sessions. Population and Setting. Evaluation is restricted to English text conversations in a mobile-chat support setting, with a single counsellor system prompt. Demographic seeds come from a synthetic persona dataset grounded in US population distributions, while presenting-problem rates and the real-user ref- erence derive from one online mental health app. We explicitly exclude harmful behaviours, and the benchmark does not systematically probe crisis handling or other high-risk situations. Results may not transfer to other populations, cultures, languages, products or system prompts. Ethical Considerations Real user data. The patient validation and profile construction is based on 1,584 sessions from 886 patients of an online mental health app. An initial anonymisation step removed named entities and other personally identifiable information before any analysis. Apart from the realism annotation, every analysis ran on locally hosted models, and only aggregate statistics are reported in the paper. The only numbers derived from real patients that we release are aggregate presenting-problem rates, fitted from these annotated patients, which set how often each problem occurs in the synthetic population. Annotators. Annotations were conducted by three clinical psychologists, full-time employees as part of their regular duties. All have extensive experience annotating AI-generated content, and their anno- tations were informed by their work conducting safety audits on anonymised real conversations from the product. The annotated conversations contain no crisis content, and reviewing conversations of this kind is part of the annotators’ day-to-day role. Annotation was blind to the source of each conversation, and annotators are identified only by anonymous IDs. Synthetic patients. All profiles used in this work were synthetically curated. Demographic seeds are inherited from Nemotron-Personas-USA (Meyer and Corneil, 2025) (CC BY 4.0) and the clinical profile is written by a language model from the proposed taxonomies. Mitigation strategies were implemented to reduce caricature and bias such as the inclusion of clinical anchor informed by real usage and automated filtration steps. The pool of profiles used in the benchmark is selected to maximize clinical presentation diversity. Scope and intended use. MindEval2 is intended as a diagnostic benchmark for everyday, non-crisis mental health support. Performance on this benchmark alone does not establish clinical safety or efficacy and should not be taken as sufficient evidence for deployment without clinical oversight. We recommend using MindEval2 alongside dedicated safety evaluations (Bentley et al., 2026; Weilnhammer et al., 2026).
