The result therefore extends the formation claim; testing persistence and latent susceptibility at three targets requires their corresponding interventions. 7 Relation to prior work and implications 7.1 From unequal representation to intervention points Unequal functional accessibility connects this work to biased representation learning, gradient starvation, and modality competition (Lampinen et al., 2024; Pezeshki et al., 2021; Huang et al., 2022). We follow this established phenomenon across interventions before and after formation. Representation learning and information-bottleneck theory address what is preserved after input is available (Bengio et al., 2013; Tishby et al., 1999); indirect source coding separates observation loss from subsequent coding loss (Wolf & Ziv, 1970). Active perception and feature acquisition intervene earlier, on available information (Mnih et al., 2014; Shim et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2022). These distinctions support our acquisition calibration and the comparisons of developing and already formed specialization. Loss weighting, gradient balancing, and multi-objective methods address competition among tasks (Kendall et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2018; Sener & Koltun, 2018; Yu et al., 2020; Kurin et al., 2022). Curricula and replay alter which experiences drive updates (Oudeyer et al., 2007; Graves et al., 2017; Schaul et al., 2016). Sampling frequency and loss weight can even agree under full-gradient optimization yet yield different stochastic convergence through gradient variance (Li et al., 2025). Our acquisition intervention changes input precision, not example frequency, while independently controlling explicit loss weights. It exposes two distinct routes into the gradients of one bottleneck. Their effects compete and interact; the experiments do not turn them into independent additive resources. A related biological distinction separates signals that gate plasticity from signals that steer it (Roelfsema & Holtmaat, 2018a).
