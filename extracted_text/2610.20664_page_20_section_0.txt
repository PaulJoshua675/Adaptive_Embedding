All subsequent queries and tool-selection decisions are generated by the current student policy. B.3 TRAINING CONFIGURATIONS SFT cold start. Both student scales start from Qwen3-VL-Instruct and undergo full-parameter SFT before on-policy training. The vision tower and multimodal projector remain trainable, al- lowing the model to learn visual inspection, multi-turn reasoning, and the structured tool protocol jointly with language generation. We use the qwen3 vl conversation template, a maximum se- quence length of 24,000 tokens, maximum image and video budgets of 262,144 and 16,384 pixels, and BF16 precision. SFT uses a global batch size of 128, a learning rate of 10−5, cosine decay with a 0.1 warmup fraction, two epochs, gradient checkpointing, and ZeRO-3 over eight GPUs. The 2B and 4B distillation runs are initialized from their corresponding update-154 SFT checkpoints. Shared on-policy training backbone. OPD and IG-OPD share the same student initialization, frozen teacher, prompt ordering, rollout engine, optimizer, and distributed configuration. Each up- date samples 64 prompts and one student trajectory per prompt. The asynchronous SGLang engine executes up to 64 agent trajectories and 256 tool calls in parallel. Rollouts use temperature 0.7, top-p = 1, and disabled top-k truncation. We use tensor parallelism 2, a maximum prompt length of 20,000 tokens, a maximum generated length of 70,000 tokens, and at most 20 agent turns. The long response budget accommodates interleaved reasoning, tool calls, retrieved observations, and the final response without truncating multi-hop search trajectories. The student actor is trained in BF16 with FSDP on eight GPUs. We use a learning rate of 5 × 10−6, cosine decay with a 0.1 warmup fraction, a PPO minibatch size of 64, one actor epoch per rollout batch, clipping radius 0.2, and dynamic token-based microbatching.
