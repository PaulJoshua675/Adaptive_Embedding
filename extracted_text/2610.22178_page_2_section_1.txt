Open pretraining. Many VLAs release their weights, and often their finetuning code, but not the code or the exact data mixture used for pretraining (Physical Intelligence, 2025; Bjorck et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026). The strongest VLAs are pretrained partly on proprietary data, collected (Physical Intelligence et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026a; Liu et al., 2026) or synthesised (Yuan et al., 2026), and several have no public weights (Physical Intelligence et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026a; Yuan et al., 2026). OpenVLA, Octo and RDT-1B released their full pipeline on public data such as Open X-Embodiment (Kim et al., 2024; Octo Model Team et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025), followed by SmolVLA, MolmoAct and MolmoAct2, which also releases bimanual data collected by its authors (Shukor et al., 2025; Lee et al., 2025; Fang et al., 2026). These releases have helped the research community pretrain models again, change their data or design, and compare them under the same conditions. Models pretrained on larger in-house data, with more recent design choices, have nonetheless kept a lead on most benchmarks. Building on this line of work, REKI is pretrained on a broad mixture of public robot, egocentric human and vision–language data, and released in full, from data curation to deployment on a real robot. It reaches the best results among VLAs pretrained on public data on three benchmarks (§6.1), and is optimised for fast inference (§6.3). Memory in VLAs. Most of these models act on the current observation alone, yet past context helps a policy identify the task state and recover from failed attempts (Shi et al., 2026; Torne et al., 2026; Gao et al., 2026), and methods differ in where they pay for it (Fig. 2). The most direct approach stacks or compresses past frames in the VLM (Yuan et al., 2026; Torne et al., 2026; Physical Intelligence et al., 2026; Jang et al., 2025). Its cost then grows with the window, at least in the vision encoder (Koo et al., 2026). A recurrent state (Cherepanov et al., 2026; Xiao et al., 2026) or fast weights (Jiang et al., 2026) keep the input short, but must be unrolled step by step in training. Others store past features or keyframes in an extra module (Shi et al., 2026; Koo et al., 2026; Mark et al., 2026; Sridhar et al., 2026). Like µVLA and HAMLET, REKI carries the past in a few learnable tokens, but as registers (Darcet et al., 2024) in the action expert. The VLM encodes each frame once, a training window is one batched pass, and at inference memory adds under 1% to the prediction time of a model without memory (K=1, Appendix A.1). Without memory, a policy can also switch between modes at each new action chunk (Agarwal et al., 2026; Lian et al., 2026), which is attributed to perceptual aliasing in reinforcement learning (Whitehead & Ballard, 1991; Singh et al., 1994; McCallum, 1996). In REKI, memory makes such switches about four times rarer (§6.2), without the extra policy queries of temporal ensembling or bidirectional decoding (Zhao et al., 2023b; Liu et al., 2024b). 2
