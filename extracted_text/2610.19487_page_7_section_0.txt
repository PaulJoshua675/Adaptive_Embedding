Control: asymmetric mixing via electrotonic attenuation The symmetric average is intentionally a strong many- to-one projection, so the result should not be mistaken for a claim that every somatic mixing rule produces the same conversion. To test the dependence on the projection itself, we replace the symmetric mixture by Z = 0.6B1 + 0.4B2. Unequal weighting is biologically natural as an effective description because dendritic sig- nals undergo location- and state-dependent electrotonic transfer before reaching the soma12. A branch physically closer to the soma will experience less electrotonic at- tenuation than a distal branch. The particular 0.6/0.4 values are not intended as a fit to a specific morphology; they provide a controlled perturbation of the projection geometry. The asymmetry removes the degeneracy generated by symmetric averaging. The branch states (2, 0) and (0, 2), which both produced Z = 1 under equal mixing, now produce 0.6(2) + 0.4(0) = 1.2 and 0.6(0) + 0.4(2) = 0.8, respectively. The nine branch states now map to nine distinct somatic values, so branch identity is recoverable from the scalar somatic coordinate. For this control, the relevant comparison is the linear- moment family. The fully categorical family is uninfor- mative for distinguishing two one-dimensional scalar pro- jections, because an arbitrary function of a single scalar variable is direct by construction. The linear family in- stead asks whether the remaining input–output depen- dence is simple in the particular numerical coordinate produced by the projection. At the fine quantization used in Fig. S1, changing the somatic mixture from 0.5/0.5 to 0.6/0.4 lowers the linear-family direct fraction F from 98.9% to 78.4%. The control therefore exposes the role of the map it- self. The microscopic law in Eq. (18) has not changed, nor have the six input variables. Only the projection from branch states to the measured somatic coordinate has changed. Yet the apparent directness of the result- ing description changes substantially. Directness is con- sequently not an intrinsic label that can be attached to the neuron independently of resolution. It belongs jointly to the underlying distribution, the coordinates retained by the observer, the model family used to describe those coordinates, and the biophysical projection that connects one ruler to the next. E. From One Weight To A Field Of Contacts The branch-to-soma cascade showed how spatial ag- gregation can make a neuron appear increasingly direct even when its fine-scale input–output law contains ex- plicit higher-order interactions. We now apply the same argument to a more elementary object in network mod- els: the synaptic weight. The question is what a single edge wij represents when the biological connection that it summarizes is resolved at progressively finer spatial scales. At point-neuron resolution, one connection is naturally represented by one effective coefficient. At contact res- olution, however, the same anatomical relation can con- tain multiple spatially embedded synaptic events whose influence depends on where they arrive and on the local state into which they arrive. The purpose of this section is therefore not to deny the usefulness of a scalar weight. It is to identify what has already been integrated out when a spatially distributed, state-dependent biological connection is represented by one number. 1. The scalar-weight abstraction In a standard point-neuron description, the influence from presynaptic cell j to postsynaptic cell i is repre- sented by a scalar coefficient wij. Depending on the model, that coefficient may multiply an instantaneous activity variable, a filtered spike train, or another effec- tive presynaptic signal. In each case, however, the spatial structure of the connection inside neuron i has been re- moved from the representation. The model retains one edge between two neuronal nodes rather than the indi- vidual sites at which that edge is physically realized. At a finer microscopic ruler ε, the appropriate object need not be a single scalar. Equation (22) is not intended as a detailed synaptic model. It expresses the change in ontology produced by changing the ruler. Through this lens, what was one edge at neuronal resolution becomes a set of spatially distin- guished degrees of freedom at contact resolution. The distinction matters because dendritic location is computationally consequential. Synaptic efficacy at the soma depends on electrotonic distance, local membrane conductances, neighboring excitation and inhibition, and the nonlinear state of the dendritic compartment through which the signal propagates12,13,15. Thus two contacts associated with the same presynaptic neuron need not participate in the same local computation merely because a coarse graph assigns them to the same edge.
