2.1 Few-Shot Prompting on a Shared Canvas We consider a partially observed canvas containing D demonstrations, J target sites, and relevant surrounding context. Let z ∈(V ∪{m})N denote this canvas, where V is the vocabulary, m is a mask token, and N is the canvas length. The original target positions are T = M(z) = {i ∈[N] : zi = m}, where [N] = {1, . . . , N}; these positions form J masked output regions. The task is to fill these regions while preserving the visible content of the canvas. In Figure 2, D = 2 demonstrations illustrate alphabetical sorting, while J = 2 missing outputs occupy six masked positions in total (|T| = 6 in the schematic). At the target sites, the original word lists remain visible, while their sorted versions must be generated. More generally, demonstrations and targets may be interleaved with surrounding context; conventional few-shot prompting is recovered when a single target follows the demonstrations. For now, we assume the target sites are specified and their output lengths are given by an oracle, fixing the mask allocation before generation. 2.2 Masked Diffusion Language Models Like BERT-style masked language modeling (Devlin et al., 2019), masked DLMs predict missing tokens using visible context on both sides of each masked position (Austin et al., 2021; Sahoo et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024a). At each masked position i ∈M(z), the model predicts a distribution PDLM(vi | z) over candidate tokens vi ∈V . Turning these predictions into complete infills requires a decoding algorithm that determines which masked positions to reveal and when. Common strategies select positions by prediction confidence or a prescribed order, potentially revealing multiple tokens in parallel (Fruytier et al., 2026; Kim et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2026). As illustrated in Figure 2, newly revealed tokens become visible context for subsequent predictions, including those at other target sites.
