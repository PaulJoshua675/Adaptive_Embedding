Table 8 compares the usual order, where N-3.5 Lightning-8K goes straight to the long-context stage, with a 100B-token N-3.5 Lightning midtraining run at 256K before a long-context stage. Midtraining alone brings N- 3.5 Lightning-8K within about five points of the long-context base model on RULER (Hsieh et al., 2024) up to 128K, but trails by 14 points at 256K. After the long-context stage, the midtrained model beats the long-context base model at every length from 64K to 256K and stays within two points at shorter lengths. Midtraining thus does part of the work of the long-context stage, and this carries over to the final model. Appendix E.2 gives more results for N-3.5 Super. 5.2 MASKING TOOL RESPONSES IN TRACES Agent traces interleave the model’s own actions with tool responses, the outputs the environment returns after each tool call. Some recent work also trains on tool responses, so that the model learns to predict how the environment reacts, a form of world modeling (FAIR CodeGen Team, 2025; Zeng et al., 2026). We tested this with the setup of §4.2, midtraining N-3.5 Lightning-8K for 100B tokens with and without tool responses in the loss, followed by identical SFT with an earlier version of our SFT recipe. In our experiments, training on tool responses did not work out of the box and lowered SWE-bench Verified pass@1 by about 10 points (Table 9), so we train only on assistant turns, as in SFT. Tool responses are often long and noisy, such as file dumps, test logs, and error traces, and predicting them may take capacity away from learning the agent’s own actions. 5.3 PRETRAINING REPLAY AND LEARNING RATE Replay keeps the model close to its pretraining distribution, so that midtraining shifts the data dis- tribution gradually rather than abruptly. We started from 20% replay as a reasonable default, with the goal of no regression on general-capability benchmarks. Table 10 shows that N-3.5 Super does not regress through 65B tokens, with every benchmark group within 1.2 points of the starting check- point. We also tried 40% replay, which gave no clear benefit over 20%, so we kept 20%. 8
