Forty years after Hopfield’s model [1] and the statistical- mechanical theory of Amit, Gutfreund and Sompolinsky (AGS) [2, 3], Hebbian neural networks have returned to the centre of the machine-learning stage through two independent developments. On the one hand, dense as- sociative memories [4, 5, 6] and their exponential limit [7, 8, 9] have shown that a Hebbian network with suf- ficiently steep interactions stores an exponential num- ber of patterns, that the steepness of the interaction controls a transition from feature-like to prototype-like memories [4], and that one step of the zero-temperature dynamics of the exponential model is a softmax atten- tion update with the stored patterns as keys and values [7, 11], a connection also read as a nearest-neighbour or kernel-smoothing view of attention [21, 22, 23]. On the other hand, the Hebbian prescription itself has been turned from a storage rule into a learning rule: in [12, 13] the network is supplied, instead of the archetypes ξµ, with M corrupted examples ηµa of each of them, and the Hebbian coupling is built on the ex- amples, without labels (unsupervised, [12]) or with the examples averaged class by class (supervised, [13]); the same protocol has been solved for dense p-body net- works in [14, 15], where the load becomes αp = K/N p−1 (see also [16, 17, 18]). The resulting phase diagram is controlled by the AGS load α = K/N and by a new parameter, the dataset entropy ρ = (1 −r2)/(Mr2), with r the quality of the examples; archetypes are re- trieved although they were never presented, provided ρ is small enough and α lies below a ρ-dependent critical load. Two sources of noise limit the retrieval: the fi- nite number and quality of the examples, measured by ρ, and the interference of the other stored patterns on the field acting on each neuron, usually called crosstalk, which grows with the load α. When either is too large the network enters a spin-glass phase, in which its states are correlated with none of the archetypes: in the learn- ing setting this phase is the thermodynamic signature of overfitting. The present work merges the two lines. We ask what happens to the (α, ρ) phase diagram when the examples themselves become the stored keys of the exponential Hopfield model, that is, of an attention layer, with no use of the labels.
