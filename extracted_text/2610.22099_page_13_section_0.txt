B.2.1 Supervised warmup and reinforcement learning We compare the complete supervised warmup–RLVR recipe with its supervised parent and a policy trained with RLVR alone. The RLVR-trained policies use the same 935-update continuation length, while the warmup-only policy receives no RLVR updates. As shown in Table 5 and Figure 3, both stages contribute to the final performance. Warmup followed by RLVR improves over warmup alone by 14.55, 17.87, and 14.45 percentage points at T = 16, 32, 64, respectively. Warmup also provides a useful initialization for RLVR: although the gain over RLVR alone is only 0.49 points at T = 16, it increases to 8.50 and 7.81 points at T = 32 and 64. Thus, the supervised policy is not simply sufficient on its own; supervised initialization and terminal-reward optimization complement one another. B.2.2 The contribution of remasking To separate dependence on an already learned behavior from the value of the action space itself, we evaluate two controls. The first disables remasking at inference for the frozen reference policy. The second retrains a monotone policy with supervised warmup and RLVR. In both cases, visible tokens retain their identities, and only masked positions can become newly visible.
