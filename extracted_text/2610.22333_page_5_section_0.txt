Each language was reviewed by one annotator who could see the judge’s label. Ethical Considerations This study measures refusal, so it necessarily in- volves harmful requests. Every harmful prompt comes from the Toxic Matrix split of AI4Bharat In- dicAlign (Khan et al., 2024), a peer-reviewed and publicly released corpus, and the benign prompts come from the Dolly-T split of the same resource. We wrote no new harmful requests; we only trans- lated existing English prompts into five Indic lan- guages. We release all 47,880 judged responses on GitHub for research on model safety. Some re- sponses comply with harmful requests, so the re- lease carries a content warning. Annotation exposed native speakers to model re- sponses to harmful requests. Annotation was per- formed by the authors and colleagues who are na- tive speakers of the respective languages, not by paid crowdworkers. All annotators gave consent and could stop at any time. We used Claude Opus to translate the harmful prompts and LLM judges to label refusals (Sec- tion 3).
