These findings also carry practical implications for operational Earth observation pipelines that must produce map products under real-world time and annotation constraints. The label efficiency analysis (Section 4.6) shows that SparK-SAR’s advantage over a non-pretrained base- line on Flood-S1 is largest precisely at the most extreme low-label setting we test (1% labeled samples, a +6.26 pp gain in mIoU over random initialization), the operating regime closest to a real rapid-response flood mapping scenario such as Copernicus Emergency Management Ser- vice (EMS) products, where annotated post-event imagery for a specific flood event is scarce or unavailable at the time a map is needed. Similarly, the pretraining data scaling results (Section 4.5) show that classification and change detection performance saturate with only 10–25% of the full 243,968 tile pretraining corpus, in- dicating that agencies producing regional land-cover products in the style of CORINE or the ESA Climate Change Initiative (CCI) Land Cover map from Sentinel-1 archives need not assemble a global-scale SAR corpus to benefit from MIM pretraining. SparK-SAR pretraining is a one-time cost across four V100 32 GB GPUs, completing in under nine hours (Table 3), while finetuning the resulting checkpoint across all four Copernicus- Bench tasks evaluated in this paper then completes in a further ≈18.6 GPU-hours per seed on a single V100 GPU, well within reach of operational and research groups without dedicated large- scale HPC infrastructure, a practical constraint that we view as at least as relevant to real-world adoption as the accuracy gains reported above. The broader case our results build is a practical one: for SAR remote sensing, a compute- efficient, domain-specific CNN can be a real alternative to chasing ever-larger multimodal ViT foundation models. A 27.8M-parameter encoder pretrained on one modality, on four V100 32 GB GPUs, in under a day, holds its own against, and on most tasks beats, frozen encoders with five times the parameters and orders of magnitude more pretraining data and compute be- hind them. 6. Conclusions This work investigates how masked self-supervised pretraining can be adapted to the par- ticular characteristics of Sentinel-1 GRD imagery. The proposed SparK-SAR framework com- bines a high-resolution ConvNeXt-RS encoder producing 14×14 feature maps with lightweight reconstruction decoders designed to avoid the numerical difficulties observed during mixed- precision pretraining on SAR data. Together with layer-wise learning rate decay during finetun- ing, these modifications enable a 27.8M-parameter encoder to transfer effectively to four distinct Copernicus-Bench S1GRD downstream tasks within a limited computational budget. The experiments also provide evidence that increasing pretraining data does not yield uni- form benefits across downstream tasks. The 27.8M-parameter encoder exceeds the performance of frozen ViT-based encoders with as much as 5× more parameters on three of the four evalu- ated tasks. Moreover, the data-scaling experiments show that classification performance largely reaches saturation after using 10–25% of the SSL4EO-S12 v1.1 corpus, whereas dense predic- tion tasks continue to improve significantly up to 25% and keep gaining, more gradually, through 50% of the training set.
