The hyperparameter sweep tests correction strength and retained token support separately. On Qwen3-8B/GPQA-D, accuracy stays at 54.5–56.6 for µ = 1.6–3.2 at α = 0.1. At µ = 2.4, thresholds α = 0.1–0.4 yield 56.6–58.6, whereas the more permissive α = 0.05 reduces accuracy to 50.0 (Appendix B.5). Gains persist across the tested strengths but weaken at the most permissive threshold, supporting confidence- constrained execution. A mask-only ablation would be needed to separate the contrast signal’s contribution from the support constraint. 4.4 Comparison at Matched Generation Budgets Budget accounting. We compare EMO with SC and judge-selected BoN under top-p sampling, using three seeds (Table 4, Figure 2; full results in Appendix B.4.4). SC/BoN share a sample pool and match EMO’s decoding settings, per-response token limits, and seeds; BoN also uses the same judge and tie-breaking rule. The separately generated N = 1 responses can differ in accuracy despite matching seed identifiers (e.g., 40.3 for EMO vs. 37.8 for SC/BoN on Llama-3.2-3B/MATH-500), so endpoint gaps compare the complete procedures rather than isolating the effect of later feedback rounds. One initial generation and two context-conditioned streams per optimization round give EMO@K a budget of N = 2K + 1 target-model generation equivalents, matched to N independent samples for SC/BoN. The EMO pool contains only K + 1 candidates because the two streams jointly generate one response per round. This accounting excludes judge/rewrite calls and response-length differences; total FLOPs and latency are not matched. Clear advantages on Llama, task-dependent gains on Qwen. At N = 9, EMO exceeds BoN on all six Llama tasks: Llama-3.1-8B GPQA-D (37.4 vs. 32.2), MATH-500 (65.6 vs. 60.8), MMLU-Pro (68.2 vs. 62.5), and Llama-3.2-3B GPQA-D (36.5 vs. 30.0), MATH-500 (60.3 vs. 52.7), MMLU-Pro (66.2 vs. 53.5). It also exceeds SC on all six. The margin widens on the smaller Llama-3.2-3B, reaching 12.7 points on MMLU-Pro.
