This lack of generalization is especially pronounced for numeric DocIDs (Zhang et al., 2025), but also textual DocIDs lead to sub- stantially lower effectiveness on unseen than on seen documents (Zhang et al., 2025). While incremental training methods adapt nu- meric DocIDs to new documents (Mehta et al., 2023; Kishore et al., 2023), updating model pa- rameters remains computationally expensive. Re- cent model-editing approaches update only a small subset of parameters while achieving performance comparable to full retraining (Zhang et al., 2026b). However, editing the model for newly added docu- ments still requires computation with each update, making frequent updates costly. To address the poor adaptation of numeric Do- cIDs and the cost of updating model weights, we propose Computed Bit-Factorized Identifiers for Generative Retrieval (CoBIGR). CoBIGR repre- sents each document with a sign-projection binary code of its document vector and factorizes the re- trieval score over its identifier bits, which inherit the model’s knowledge of the document rather than a memorized embedding learned from indexing training. Additionally, the proposed bit-factorized IDs are flat and their score is permutation-invariant. This property enables multi-tree beam search dur- ing inference, which substantially improves effec- tiveness on unseen documents, while scoring with one forward pass keeps decoding efficient (see Fig- ure 1). Our contributions are: (1) We propose a bit- factorized numeric DocID: its bits derive mean- ing from the language model, so unseen DocIDs can still be retrievable. (2) We study direct DocID adaptation to dynamic corpora without weight up- dates, addressing an underexplored gap in costly generative retrieval training. (3) Our experiments show that our approach achieves comparable perfor- mance on seen documents to prior DocID designs while substantially improving performance on un- seen documents, with 40–60 percentage points higher Hit@1 and Hit@100 than other numeric DocIDs. It also remains within 3 percentage points of methods that update model weights. Our source code is available at https://anonymous.4open. science/r/bit_factorized_token-2C13/ 2
