Clinical Syndromes and Involved Mechanisms The main syndrome is unilateral prepubertal gynecomastia with a contralat- eral breast bud. It is confirmed by an 18-month history of a right subareolar mass that enlarged over 6 months, a firm mobile 2x2 cm right retroareolar mass on exam, and a sonographically well-circumscribed hypoechoic retroareolar mass with benign features (DOI: 10.1056/NEJMcpc1503831). The mechanism is proliferation of ductal and periductal fibroglandular tissue driven by an imbalance between estrogen and androgen action at the breast bud. In this prepubertal boy with normal bone age and prepubertal testes, any estrogenic drive is likely peripheral (adrenal, exogenous, or increased aromatization) rather than centrally mediated. A concurrent syndrome is premature adrenarche, confirmed by fine pubic and scant axillary hair with prepubertal 3 ml testes and a prepubertal phallus (DOI: 10.1056/NEJMcpc1503831). This is realized through adrenal zona reticularis secretion of weak androgens (DHEA/DHEA-S) independent of gonadotropin stimulation. Central precocious puberty is rejected because testicular volume is 3 ml (prepubertal), the phallus is prepubertal, and bone age is not advanced, which are incompatible with central HPG axis activation. Diagnostic and Treatment Tactics Diagnostics: The immediate priority is to review the pending laboratory panel (Table 1) and the results of the unspecified diagnostic procedure. Required lab results include LH, FSH, estradiol, total testosterone, DHEA-S, androstenedione, 17-hydroxyprogesterone, serum hCG, AFP, and prolactin. If estradiol is elevated with suppressed LH/FSH, obtain testicular ultrasound and adrenal CT/MRI to localize an estrogen-secreting source. If hCG is elevated, perform testicular ultrasound and chest/abdominal/CNS imaging to exclude ectopic hCG-secreting tumors. If all hormones are prepubertal/normal, consider a GnRH stimulation test to detect subclinical gonadarche or proceed to tissue diagnosis if the mass persists or enlarges. A scrotal/testicular ultrasound is mandatory to exclude an occult testicular neoplasm not palpable on examination. Treatment: If benign physiologic gynecomastia is confirmed by normal labs and imaging, reassurance and observation with 3-6 month follow-up is the primary approach. Avoid empirical anti-estrogen or aromatase inhibitor therapy until a hormonally active tumor is excluded. If a specific cause is identified (e.g., tumor, CAH), treat accordingly. If the mass persists, enlarges beyond 3-6 months, or causes significant psychological distress despite a negative workup, consider excisional biopsy or surgical referral for definitive histology.
