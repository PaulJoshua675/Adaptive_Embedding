We take the argmax at both stages and output the midpoint of the selected fine cell by Equation (4). A conditional expectation over the fine cells of the selected root reads the same logits but gives higher error at the 72K scale (Section 4.3), so we do not use it. Counts and angles. Counts are discrete: n ∈[0, 1023] is encoded as (⌊n/32⌋, n mod 32) and reconstructed exactly when both choices are correct. Angles use twelve 30-degree positive intervals over (0, 360], 32 linear cells per interval, and zero and overflow roots, with the angle frame and direction declared by the question. The adapter rejects unsupported quantities and ambiguous units, so meters, degrees, and counts never share a numerical interpretation. Scalar counting is also kept separate from locating a set of points. Point prediction. Point prediction also requires fine resolution, but selecting a smaller region creates an additional need for local visual detail. We therefore combine three nine-way region decisions with crop re- encoding: after each of the first two selections, a new crop provides the visual evidence for the next decision.
