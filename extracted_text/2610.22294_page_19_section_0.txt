A Proofs We prove the one-token-at-a-time decoding and reward-guidance results of Section 3, then extend the construction to unknown target lengths. A.1 One-Token-at-a-Time Decoding Lemma A.1 (One-token-at-a-time decoding). For any deterministic one-token-at-a-time position- selection policy, Equation (2) defines a normalized distribution P(u | z) that can be sampled and scored exactly. If the DLM predicts exact data conditionals, then P(u | z) recovers the conditional data distribution wherever defined, independently of the reveal policy (Ou et al., 2025). Proof of Theorem 3.1. Fix a canvas z and a designated set T ⊆M(z) of masked target positions. Let O = {i ∈[N] : zi ∈V } denote the initially visible positions, so that any masked positions outside T remain unresolved. We consider a deterministic decoding policy that selects one unrevealed target position based on the current canvas, then samples its token from the DLM. For any complete assignment u ∈V T , replaying this policy determines a unique sequence of selected positions i1, . . . , i|T|, which may depend on previously revealed tokens. Normalization and exact sampling. Consider a decision tree representing all possible decoding trajectories. Each node corresponds to a partially revealed canvas, and the decoding policy selects the next target position to reveal. The outgoing edges correspond to the possible tokens at that position, with probabilities given by the DLM that sum to one. Since the policy is deterministic and eventually reveals every target position, each complete assignment u ∈V T corresponds to exactly one leaf. The probability of reaching that leaf is the product of its edge probabilities, precisely P(u | z) in Equation (2). Summing the probabilities of all leaves therefore gives X u∈V T P(u | z) = 1. Thus, P(· | z) is a normalized distribution, and sequential decoding samples exactly from it. Moreover, replaying the policy on any candidate u recovers its reveal trajectory and permits exact evaluation of P(u | z). Recovery of the data conditional distribution. Now suppose the DLM predicts exact data conditionals, and let X ∼Pdata be a clean sequence. We assume that the initially visible context has positive probability, Pdata(XO = zO) > 0. For a fixed candidate u, define the agreement events As as follows: A0 := {XO = zO}, As := As−1 ∩{Xis = uis}. Thus, As is the event that a clean data sample agrees with the visible context and the first s revealed tokens of u. Because the reveal policy selects is using only previously revealed information, the exact-conditional assumption implies PDLM(uis | z(s−1)) = Pdata(As | As−1) = Pdata(As) Pdata(As−1), whenever Pdata(As−1) > 0. 19
