This finding is consistent with the 4B-model ablation in the KSA report (Chu et al., 2026, Table 7), where increasing 𝑘𝑠from 8 to 64 reduces the mean RULER score over 4K–64K from 82.97 to 77.79. This advantage may stem from the additional computation devoted to fine-grained Summary processing in C-KSA, which may help preserve retrieval-relevant information at the same remote-memory compression ratio. Actually, the additional computation as in two aspects: (1) the C-KSA takes 8 as the summary chunk, which will add more tokens at input sequence level; (2) the compressed KV memory is further computed by an additional module, which can be seen as a two-step summarization. Compression granularity with Full attention. Table 9(a) compares 𝑘𝑚∈{4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128} in C-KSA+Full. The six-length RULER mean ranges from 71.92 to 72.74, with no systematic decline as compression increases. The Full layers may help preserve retrieval quality by providing access to original-text details lost during compression. At 128K, 𝑘𝑚= 128 leaves about 128 Meta entries per C-KSA layer, so larger 𝑘𝑚would save little further cache; we therefore select 𝑘𝑚= 128 and leave higher factors to million-token contexts. Block size and routing methods. Table 9(b) compares sparse-attention schemes on the same 𝑘𝑚= 128 C-KSA+Full backbone, including S-KSA with 𝑏∈{2, 4, 8}, token-level DSA (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025), and Qwen Sparse Attention (QSA) (Qiu et al., 2026). QSA provides a simple alternative to Summary-guided aggregation: it averages the projected token-level index keys within each block. We follow Qwen’s QSA indexer design, using 𝑏= 4 and eight indexer heads to match our comparison settings. All methods are compared under the same experimental setup. We report only RULER in this panel, since most general-capability sequences fit within the combined remote and local attention budget and offer limited discrimination of routing quality. S-KSA with 𝑏= 4 achieves a mean RULER score comparable to those of 𝑏= 2 and token-level DSA, with only half and one quarter as many routing candidates, respectively. It also outperforms QSA at the same block size. Increasing to 𝑏= 8 reduces the candidate count further but degrades retrieval quality, particularly at 128K. We therefore select 𝑏= 4 as a balance between retrieval quality and routing cost. 19
