B.3 Robustness to LLM Evaluators Our main evaluation uses Claude Sonnet 4.6. To test whether the observed gains depend on a partic- ular judge, we re-evaluate all systems using Claude Opus 4.8 and DeepSeek 3.2. Results are shown in Table 10 and Table 9. Across both datasets and both alternative judges, SRAT achieves the best score on every reported metric. More importantly, the relative system order- ing is highly consistent with the Sonnet-based eval- uation in the main experiments. The gains there- fore persist across substantially different evaluator models, indicating that they are not artifacts of op- timizing toward one particular LLM judge. B.4 Robustness to a Stronger Backbone We replace Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with Qwen3-8B to test whether selective retrieval remains effective with a stronger backbone. We evaluate RAGtrans En→De and IdiomKB En→Zh and report results in Table 12. The results reproduce the main pattern with a stronger backbone: SRAT achieves the best COMET, task-specific judge, and MQM scores in both settings while retrieving selectively. On RAG- trans, MQM improves by 7.6% over CSC and 4.7% over SRAT-A; on IdiomKB, it improves MQM from −1.87 for CSC to −1.33. Thus, the benefit of selective retrieval is not specific to the Qwen2.5-7B backbone. B.5 Effect of the Number of Retrieved Documents By default, SRAT retrieves only the top-1 docu- ment for each query. We further vary the number of returned documents to k ∈{1, 3, 5} to exam- ine whether exposing the translator to more can- didate evidence improves downstream translation. The policy and training procedure are otherwise unchanged. Table 13 shows that retrieving more documents does not consistently improve translation qual- ity. On IdiomKB, k = 1 achieves the strongest COMET, I5, I3, and MQM, while increasing to k = 5 substantially degrades all metrics. On RAGtrans, k = 3 slightly improves BLEU and COMET, but reduces GRF, GRB, and MQM rel- ative to k = 1; increasing further to k = 5 also decreases the performance.
