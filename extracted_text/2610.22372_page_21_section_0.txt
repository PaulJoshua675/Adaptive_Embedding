D Human Review and Initial User Validation Construction and cross-review. Human contributors participated in three stages: 1) providing one reference demonstration for each newly introduced skill; 2) inspecting sampled generated demonstrations together with their satisfaction labels and metrics; and 3) rating preference plausibility and demonstration human-likeness. Before beginning, contributors received common instructions and task-specific training. Contributors were assigned to different portions of the generated data, after which a second group cross-reviewed the demonstrations and labels. Disputed cases were jointly re-examined before inclusion; the initial reviewer disagreement rate was 11.8%. All participants provided informed consent, and contributors were credited according to their level of involvement. Survey protocol and results. For initial user validation, 15 participants rated the selected preferences and their demonstrations on a 10-point scale along two dimensions: real-world preference plausibility, whether each preference was plausible and applicable in a household setting; and human-likeness of demonstrations, how closely the corresponding demonstration resembled how a human would naturally perform the task while satisfying the preference. Table 7 summarizes 37 preferences in three categories. These results provide initial evidence of plausibility and human-likeness, but they do not substitute for real-robot, longitudinal, or larger-scale user studies. Before participating, survey respondents were informed of the study’s purpose and procedure and provided informed consent. They received compensation via WeChat in accordance with fair labor practices. We did not identify any risks beyond those ordinarily associated with completing a questionnaire.
