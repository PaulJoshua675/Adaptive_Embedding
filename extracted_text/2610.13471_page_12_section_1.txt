Optimizer ordering depends materially on the evaluation budget, so optimizer selection for simulation-expensive con- troller tuning should use a budget representative of the intended application. REMBGO is not universally best: at 1040 evaluations it holds the lowest observed mean rank at D=10 and GWO at D=20, while FP-SHADE leads at large budgets. Selecting a nominal winner on one stochastic trajectory does not by itself establish robust generalization. In the corrected transfer study all ten frozen champions remained feasible across twenty pre-specified unseen stochastic arcs. The average shift from the STO-SR training objective to the unseen-arc mean was +0.0198, with smaller mean degradation for the DQ-PI vectors (+0.0059) than for the Lyapunov vectors (+0.0338), and the arc realization and its interaction with the selected vector accounted for far more of the validation variation than the choice of vector itself. The matched REMBGO−EMBGO paired differences were controller-dependent in sign and not statistically significant for either architecture, so the EAF experiment supports fixed-gain transfer robustness rather than an application-side REMBGO superiority claim. Future work should examine multi-trajectory or risk-aware controller tuning under the same restricted evaluation-budget setting. Data availability.
