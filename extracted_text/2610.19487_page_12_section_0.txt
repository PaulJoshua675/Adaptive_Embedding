In the aligned construction, the grouping respects the three pairwise terms in Eq. (39): contacts associated with x1 and x2 occupy one subunit, those associated with x3 and x4 occupy another, and those associated with x5 and x6 occupy the third. Each subunit therefore supplies the readout with a local nonlinear feature capable of ex- pressing coincidence structure that is absent from the six-dimensional linear point representation. In the language of Eqs. (6)–(8), the refinement does not create the interaction; rather, it changes whether that in- teraction must be represented as a higher-order residual or is available as a direct coordinate of the effective de- scription. The qualification “pairwise” matters. The aligned three-subunit architecture is designed around the three pair products in Eq. (39). This is not handed the third- order term x1x3x5 explicitly. The reduction in error therefore does not result from simply inserting every term of the target into the feature matrix. Rather, the exper- iment asks whether a small collection of appropriately organized local nonlinear features gives a better approx- imation basis than the unresolved point representation. The results are positive. The aligned three-subunit model reduces the mean held-out MSE from 0.0437 to 0.0309 (Fig. 3a). The same six axons are present in both descriptions. What has changed is the resolution at which their local combinations are exposed to the read- out. Contact count alone, however, cannot establish that this improvement comes from organization. We therefore construct a shuffled control with the same number of ax- onal inputs, the same number of contacts, and the same number of nonlinear subunits, while randomizing which contacts occupy the individual subunits. The shuffled ar- chitecture retains the additional nonlinear machinery but destroys its systematic alignment with the pair structure of Eq. (39). Its mean held-out MSE is 0.0393, interme- diate between the point and aligned models. The ordering 0.0437 > 0.0393 > 0.0309 separates two effects. Merely exposing additional nonlinear features im- proves on the point representation, but organizing those features relative to the structure of the target improves it further. The approximation gain is therefore not ex- plained solely by adding contacts or parameters. The arrangement of those contacts determines which interac- tion directions become readily available to the readout. This distinction is closely related to the biological mo- tivation of the paper. A neuron with many synapses does not obtain its computational repertoire from synapse count alone. Which inputs converge onto the same local nonlinear domain determines which coincidences can be represented economically. Contact number specifies how many microscopic elements are available; spatial orga- nization specifies the basis functions those elements can collectively generate.
