Finally, in Section 3.6, we attain on the 32 fitted patients a performance comparable to that obtained with a population calibrated to them, by training on a broad population constructed without reference to these patients. 3.1 Experimental Setup We train all policies on a calibrated population Dcal of virtual patients, obtained by multiplying the initial cell numbers of the 32 fitted patients of [52] by independent lognormal factors, with the competition and growth parameters kept at their nominal values (α, β) = (6, 8). For evaluation, we consider two test populations, namely the virtual test population of 1,000 patients drawn from Dcal independently of the training patients, and the 32 fitted patients, whose empirical distribution we denote by Dfit. All methods are evaluated on the same test patients with common random numbers. The construction of Dcal and the evaluation protocol are given in Appendix B. All learned policies are parameterized by a gated recurrent unit (GRU) network [8] acting on features of the history ht, and trained by proximal policy optimization (PPO) [42] for 2,000 updates, each with 512 patients newly sampled from the training population. For each type of policy, we train three policies with different random seeds, and the remaining details are given in Appendix B. We compare the learned policies with two clinical rules, namely continuous treatment, i.e., at = 1 for all t, and AT50. In our model, we implement AT50 as the policy that pauses treatment once yt < log 0.5, resumes it once yt ≥0, and otherwise repeats its previous action. Both satisfy the forced-treatment rule. Since we impose no explicit penalty in (2.1) on patients who fare worse than under the standard of care, we monitor such patients separately. To this end, we compare the progression-free time Gπ(c) of each patient with the progression-free time Gon(c) of the same patient under continuous treatment. We say that π harms the patient if Gπ(c) < Gon(c) −3, and that it benefits the patient if Gπ(c) > Gon(c) + 3, where the margin of three months corresponds to the interval of 12 weeks between two consecutive imaging assessments in the pilot clinical trial [52]. We refer to the fractions of patients harmed and benefited as the harm rate and the benefit rate, and report them together with the mean progression-free time ¯G.
