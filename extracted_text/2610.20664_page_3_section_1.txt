Multimodal search agents. Multimodal search agents extend question answering beyond paramet- ric memory by retrieving related web evidence. Recent systems mainly use reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize multi-step retrieval and reasoning (Zhang et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2025; Hong et al., 2025). For example, MMSearch-R1 and WebWatcher train search policies with GRPO (Wu et al., 2025; Geng et al., 2026), while OpenSearch-VL combines multi-hop training data with safeguards against cascading tool errors (Chen et al., 2026). However, sparse outcome rewards complicate credit assignment and make training costly, motivating more sample-efficient supervision. On-policy distillation. On-policy distillation (OPD) provides dense teacher feedback on student- generated trajectories, offering a sample-efficient approach to capability transfer. Existing studies mainly address three vulnerabilities in OPD. Training instability arises from high-variance distilla- tion rewards, motivating reward normalization (Hao et al., 2026), reward clipping (Ko et al., 2026), and proximal teacher distributions (Xie et al., 2026). Indiscriminate imitation overlooks differ- ences in uncertainty and supervision quality across tokens and trajectories. To solve this, related methods introduce entropy-aware objectives (Jin et al., 2026), token selection (Huang et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2026b), or selective filtering and reweighting (Li et al., 2026c; Zheng et al., 2026). In multi-turn agents, compounding errors and distribution shift further undermine teacher guidance as early mistakes alter subsequent interactions (Zhou et al., 2026b; Liao et al., 2026). TCOD pro- gressively extends the distillation horizon (Wang et al., 2026b), while teacher intervention steers trajectories toward more reliable guidance (Li et al., 2026a). SOD and SAGE instead adapt super- vision across steps or turns to mitigate unreliable signals on diverging histories (Zhong et al., 2026; Zhou et al., 2026a). Besides, other approaches combine distillation with RL objectives (Xu et al., 2025; 2026a; Zhang et al., 2026), with ATOD gradually shifting from OPD to RL (Tan et al., 2026). While these strategies refine supervision delivery from various angles, they fundamentally suffer from evidence blindness in multi-hop search: supervising student actions without considering the post-retrieval utility of generated queries, ultimately giving rise to EBS. To overcome this, IG-OPD leverages information gain as a post-retrieval signal to calibrate both turn budgets and update direc- tions, effectively rectifying the misalignment between OPD priors and realized evidence utility. 3 PRELIMINARIES 3.1 PROBLEM FORMULATION: MULTI-TURN SEARCH AGENTS Following Wang et al. (2026a), we consider a dataset D = {(x, y⋆)}, where each query x = (q, v) pairs a textual question q with an optional image v, and y⋆is the gold answer. Starting from x, the agent iteratively alternates between internal deliberation and tool invocation (e.g., text search, crop) until concluding with a final response, yielding a multi-turn rollout τ = (τ1, . . . , τT ). Each intermediate turn τt = (ht, at, ot) (t < T) encapsulates a chain of thought ht in <think>, a tool call at in <tool call>, and the resulting evidence ot. For the final turn τT = (hT , ˆy), the search agent will organize and reason over collected evidence to produce a final synthesis response ˆy wrapped in <response>. Because observations are externally produced by the environment, training losses are computed exclusively on tokens generated by the student policy. To better for- malize IG-OPD in the next section, we denote Ht as the cumulative interaction history after turn t, initialized with H0 = x. Moreover, we use It to index the generated tokens in turn t, with nt = |It| and total trajectory length N = PT t=1 nt.
