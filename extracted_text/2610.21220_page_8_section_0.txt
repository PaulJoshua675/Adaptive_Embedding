Overall, across three backbones and datasets, RecSoup achieves a favorable balance between Pareto efficiency and controllabil- ity. Compared with strong model-merging baselines such as Soup and TATR, RecSoup consistently attains higher Hypervolume. On MovieLens-1M, where Soup and TATR already outperform earlier controllable approaches, RecSoup further improves Hypervolume by up to 8.5% on SASRec and 7.3% on TiSASRec, while maintain- ing controllability close to the upper bound of 1.0. These results suggest that linear task-vector merging alone is insufficient to fully resolve complex interactions among conflicting objectives, whereas RecSoup more effectively explores the Pareto frontier without sacri- ficing controllability. MMR exhibits a different trade-off behavior: al- though it achieves competitive Hypervolume under certain settings, this often comes with much shorter Pareto fronts, indicating that its gains are concentrated on a limited number of operating points. In contrast, RecSoup consistently produces longer Pareto fronts, offering a richer set of controllable solutions, enabling flexible se- lection for practical use. Parameter generation approaches exhibit unstable behavior: despite high controllability, their Hypervolume degrades sharply under sparse or noisy feedback, particularly on Amazon. In these settings, RecSoup outperforms them with around 20–50% higher Hypervolume in most cases while preserving con- trollability above 0.89. Notably, RecSoup occasionally surpasses the oracle, likely due to its ability to explore feasible Pareto solutions beyond the optimization paths induced by retraining. The ablation results show that adaptive merging is necessary for stable controllable recommendation. Removing coefficient learner consistently reduces Hypervolume, which indicates that directly us- ing the preference vector as merging coefficients cannot sufficiently capture their relationship. The role of pruning is more conditional. In several settings, w/o Prune performs comparably to or even better than RecSoup, suggesting that pruning may be unnecessary when task vectors are already compatible or when sparsification re- moves useful objective-specific updates. However, pruning becomes important when task vectors contain stronger interference or noisy updates, especially on more challenging Amazon settings. By se- lecting parameters according to both objective-specific importance and cross-objective relevance, pruning provides a cleaner basis for adaptive merging. Figure 6: Alignment between user preferences and learned merging coefficients. 6.3 Analyses 6.3.1 RQ1: How do Two-stage Design Facilitate Control- lable Multi-Objective Recommendation? Figure 5 explains why pruning synergizes with adaptive merging. Figure 5(a) reports the support overlap ratio between pairs of task vectors, computed as the intersection-over-union of parameter supports whose absolute update magnitude exceeds a small threshold. Pruning clearly re- duces this overlap, indicating effective parameter decoupling across objectives. By reducing shared and potentially conflicting param- eters, pruning lowers cross-objective interference and provides a cleaner basis for multi-objective merging. This decoupling does not largely change the overall structure of task vectors. As shown in Figure 5(b), the singular spectrum and main directions remain nearly unchanged after pruning, suggesting that pruning preserves the main structure of parameter updates. Figure 5(c) further shows that support concentration increases after pruning, meaning that objective-specific updates become more focused on a smaller set of important parameters. Together, these changes make the task- vector space cleaner and more controllable, which leads to an ex- panded Pareto front in Figure 5(d).
