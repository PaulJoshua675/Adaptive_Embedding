Let the active bank of M2,4,L, whose window has 64 cells and whose records have 26 bits, hold the three records (1, 1, −1; 13, 14, 15), (−1, −1, 1; 13, 14, 16), (1, 1, −2; 15, 16, 13), written (w0, w1, θ; a0, a1, ao), ready, so that its fourth slot is empty. The first record writes the disjunction of cells 13 and 14 into cell 15, the second their negated conjunction into cell 16, and the third the conjunction of cells 15 and 16 into cell 13. From a window with x13 = x14 = 1, the four steps of a pass write 1 into cell 15, 0 into cell 16 and 0 into cell 13, and move the slot head from the empty slot back to slot 0. From every window the pass replaces x13 by x13 ⊕x14 and keeps x14: this is Lemma 3.3 for the clocked netlist on the state bits x13 and x14 whose next values are x13 ⊕x14 and x14, its units compiled into records as Example 2.2 and Lemma 3.4 prescribe. A record that set HO in the fourth slot would pass control to the other bank at the end of the pass, and the other bank would then run whatever it holds. 4 A self-reproducing constructor This section gives the main example, the organism: a stored network of 143 records that carries out von Neumann’s cycle in MK,S,L, the ring machine with 64 window cells. It reads the description on the read tape twice. In construct mode it decodes the description into the next bank, in copy mode it copies the description onto the next tape, and then it hands control to what it built. Descriptions are written in a code of its own, in which a two-input gate costs four bits besides its addresses. Definition 4.1 (The description code). A gate is a record that computes the conjunction or the disjunction of two literals ℓ0, ℓ1, each a window cell or its negation, or a copy of one literal. Its weights are ±1 by the polarities u0, u1 of the literals, with bias −u0 −u1 for a conjunction and 1 −u0 −u1 for a disjunction; a copy has weights (±1, 0) and bias −u0. Its codon is the four bits (k1, k0, u0, u1), with k1k0 equal to 00, 01 and 10 for the three kinds and u1 = 0 for a copy, and it determines the first eight bits of the record, its weight codes and its bias. The codons 1100, 1101 and 1110 are escapes and 1111 is end. A description of an image X is a word that the following reading carries to X: from a gate codon it emits the gate’s eight bits and the next 18 bits, from an escape the next 26 bits, and at the end codon it stops, having read the whole word. The description code(X) of an image X ∈{0, 1}26n lists, record by record, the codon and the 18 address bits of each record whose first eight bits are those of a gate, and the escape 1100 followed by the 26 bits of every other record, and ends with 1111. For an image X of n records of which e are not gates, code(X) has 22n + 8e + 4 bits. Every unit that Lemma 3.4 produces is a gate. Lemma 4.2 (Completeness of the code).
