2.1 DIFFUSION SAMPLING ACCELERATION Methods that cut sampling cost differ in what they modify. Some change the network or a single evaluation of it: distillation trains a student that needs fewer steps (Song et al., 2023; Salimans & Ho, 2022; Luo et al., 2023), while sparse attention (Yuan et al., 2024; Xi et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025b;a) and token reduction (Bolya et al., 2023) make each step cheaper. Others change the grid: higher-order solvers reach a given quality in fewer steps (Song et al., 2021a; Lu et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2023), and schedule design places the steps where they matter most (Karras et al., 2022; Esser et al., 2024; Sabour et al., 2024). We work in a third family, caching, which changes neither the network nor an evaluation of it: it exploits the redundancy between neighboring states along the trajectory and reuses computation from earlier steps, without training. 2.2 FEATURE CACHING Caching methods differ in what they store and when they refresh it. The stored quantity ranges from intermediate activations of U-Net or transformer blocks (Ma et al., 2024; Selvaraju et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025b) to the residual of a span of blocks or of the entire stack (Chen et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025a; Chung et al., 2026), and it may be refreshed for only a subset of tokens (Zou et al., 2025; 2024; Zheng et al., 2025b). Fixed schedules refresh periodically (Ma et al., 2024; Selvaraju et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025b) or at steps chosen offline, by thresholding a reuse error calibrated on a few samples (Ma et al., 2025; Peng et al., 2026) or by optimizing for a fixed budget of full evaluations (Cui et al., 2026; Aliev et al., 2026).
