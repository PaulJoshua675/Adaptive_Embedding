Methods 3.1 Robot model and environment We reconstructed the e-puck in its version 1 based on the official Webots model [15]. All measurements were copied from the model file. The body is a cylinder with a 37 mm radius and 45 mm height, with a mass of 0.15 kg, and the center of mass and inertia matrix are those of the original model. The wheels have a 20 mm radius and are 5 mm wide, and are spaced 52 mm apart by an axle. The maximum wheel angular velocity is 6.28 rad/s, resulting in a linear speed of 0.126 m/s. Two changes were necessary to adapt the model to PyBullet [16]. Webots makes the robot slide on a small box under its body, but in PyBullet, that box drags. We replaced it with two low- friction spheres at the front and rear, half a millimeter above the floor. Additionally, the collision engine adds a margin of about 1 mm to the cylinders, causing the robot to move 5% faster than it should. Therefore, the collision radius of the wheels is 19 mm, with an effective rolling radius of 20 mm. We validated the model against Webots' kinematics before using it. In a straight line, the robot reaches 0.1255 m/s compared to the expected 0.1256 m/s, and when turning on its axis, it reaches 4.62 rad/s compared to the expected 4.83 rad/s, a difference attributable to wheel slippage. At rest, it tilts a maximum of 1.1 degrees. The physics runs at 120 Hz, and the controller acts every 8 simulation steps, that is, every 66.7 ms. We verified that this simulation step produces virtually the same kinematics as a 240 Hz step (0.1253 m/s and 4.65 rad/s at 240 Hz) while reducing the computation time per 60 s episode from 5.75 s to 3.08 s on the machine used for all experiments. These kinematic checks can be reproduced with the validation script included in the repository (test_step1_kinematics.py; see Data and code availability). The robot has eight infrared proximity sensors and three floor sensors. Its positions, orientations, response tables, and noise levels are those of Webots.
