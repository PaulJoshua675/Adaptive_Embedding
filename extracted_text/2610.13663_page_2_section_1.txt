Large language models (LLMs) have become the foundation of a new class of tools —agent systems that can not only respond to queries but also plan actions, use tools, execute code, and accumulate experience from previous steps Xi, Chen, Guo et al. (2023); Yao, Zhao, Yu et al. (2023b). Such agents are already used in programming, ML engineering, and individual scientific disciplines Jimenez, Yang, Wettig et al. (2024); Chan, Chowdhury, Jaffe et al. (2024); Lu, Lu, Lange et al. (2024); Schmidgall, Su, Wang et al. (2025); however, their use as a meta-optimizer for specialized neural network models remains understudied. A detailed review of related work is given in Section 2. The quality of specialized neural network models depends strongly on the task: the representation of inputs and outputs, the method for decoding internal activity, the balance of loss-function terms, and other components must be selected anew for each problem, and no universal selection rule exists. In practice, this selection is performed manually, which makes the process labor-intensive and poorly reproducible. At the same time, models of different types can be decomposed into similar modules: input encoding and preprocessing, a trainable feature transformation, a decoding or output transformation, and a loss function.
