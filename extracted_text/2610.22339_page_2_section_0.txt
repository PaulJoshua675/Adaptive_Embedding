To capture and exploit this structure, we introduce LEANGRAPH- SEARCH (Fig. 1), a graph-based retrieval framework for Lean 4 theorem proving. It constructs a directed graph from Mathlib’s forward dependencies, representing declarations as nodes and link- ing each declaration to the premises it directly depends on. Searching this graph from semantically relevant declarations extends retrieval beyond direct query matching, helping users and automated provers discover supporting premises that may otherwise be difficult to locate. To search the constructed dependency graph, LEANGRAPHSEARCH uses the target query to guide traversal along formal dependencies. Given an informal description or a formal goal, it first re- trieves declarations using embeddings and reranks them against the query to select semantically relevant seeds. The seed relevance scores are normalized to form the personalization distribution for personalized PageRank (Brin & Page, 1998), which propagates relevance along dependency edges while repeatedly returning to the seeds, thereby expanding the candidate set. Finally, LEANGRAPH- SEARCH reranks the expanded candidates against the original query and combines these second-pass relevance scores with query-specific graph scores to produce the final ranking. We evaluate LEANGRAPHSEARCH on both standalone premise retrieval and downstream formal theorem proving. For premise retrieval, LEANGRAPHSEARCH improves Recall@100 from 82.7% with LeanSearch v2 to 94.7% on the 810-query fair subset of MathlibQR (Gao et al., 2026), confirming that dependency graph search effectively recovers additional relevant lemmas. On MathlibMPR (Gao et al., 2026), it improves proof route-level premise coverage, increasing Cov- ered@20 from 39.1% to 42.8% and Covered@50 from 41.3% to 44.2%. These results suggest that dependency-aware retrieval better recovers complete premise sets along annotated proof routes. For formal theorem proving, we integrate LEANGRAPHSEARCH into an iterative proving loop equipped with Lean’s diagnostic feedback and problem-local failure memory. LEANGRAPHSEARCH con- sistently outperforms the state-of-the-art retrieval baselines across FATE-H (Jiang et al., 2026), MathlibMPR-Prop (Gao et al., 2026), and Lean-IMO-Bench (Kung et al., 2026), demonstrating that premise retrieval improvements translate into higher formal proof success rates. 2
