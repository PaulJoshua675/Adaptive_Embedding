Preprint 5.4 ABLATIONS We ablate on FLUX with 50 prompts from DPG-Bench (Hu et al., 2024), kept separate from the DrawBench-200 test prompts. Each variant disables one term of the sampler and keeps the rest of SoftCache. Removing the jump sets J=1 in Eq. (9), so every cached node takes the scheduled step and the sampler stays on the grid. Removing headroom scaling replaces Hi by 1 in the same equation, so a cached node jumps the full J∆i whenever it jumps rather than in proportion to the reuse budget left. Removing tail re-spacing holds mi fixed in Eq. (11), so the rebuilt tail retires one node per jump however far the jump lands, and the extra distance saves no nodes. Removing residual motion sets β=0 in Eq. (10), so a cached node holds ra as in Eq. (5). Every removal trails Soft- Cache at 3×, and removing the jump costs the most at 7×. Tab. C.4.1 adds 5× and residual motion. At 3× every removal trails SoftCache on all three metrics. At higher speeds the removals fall into two groups. Removing the jump or the tail re- spacing trails on every metric at ev- ery band. Without the jump the sam- pler stays on the scheduled grid, so it gains speed only by refreshing less often, and at 7× it trails SoftCache by 4.18 dB PSNR and 0.219 LPIPS. Holding mi fixed costs less, 1.21 dB, because the jump still lands where the headroom allows, but the tail keeps its node count, so the distance the jump covers buys no speed. Remov- ing headroom scaling or residual mo- tion behaves differently. SoftCache stays ahead of both on LPIPS and DreamSim at every band, while both pass it on PSNR at high speed (Fig. 6 and Tab. C.4.1). PSNR averages pixel error, so it rewards an output that blurs fine detail over one that keeps the detail but places it slightly off, which the perceptual metrics prefer. Sec. D.1 shows such a case. The scale β in Eq. (10) sets how far a cached node follows the linear extrapolation. The extrapolation grows with distance from the last fresh evaluation, through λi, so at high speed it reaches beyond the two evaluations it was fitted to and becomes less reliable. Damping it to β=0.5 loses to β=1 at 3× but beats it by 1.65 dB PSNR and 0.080 LPIPS at 7× (Tab. C.4.2). We therefore use β=1 below 4× and β=0.5 above it, the same rule on all three backbones. Raising the cap instead gains less: at 7× even J=20 gains 1.12 dB and 0.050 LPIPS, and it loses fine texture that β=0.5 keeps (Sec. C.4). Removing residual motion scores a higher PSNR than β=0.5 at 7× but a worse LPIPS (Tab. C.4.1). Comparing the images ourselves at that band, blind to the variant, we prefer β=0.5 to J=20 and to removing residual motion (Sec. C.3). 6
