Each evaluation budget is run as a separate, independently configured experiment rather than read as a checkpoint from a single long run. Because the safe radius, mutation scale and step schedules are driven by the consumed fraction τ of the iteration budget, a run configured for a large budget and halted early is a different configuration from a method configured for the smaller budget. For a requested budget B the iteration count is TB = lB−N N m , (17) and strict function-evaluation accounting is enforced. The common ladder spans fourteen budgets per dimension: D=10: 200, 316, 502, 796, 840, 1040, 1261, 2000, 3169, 5023, 7962, 12619, 20000, 200000 FEs; D=20: 125, 227, 414, 754, 840, 1040, 1373, 2500, 4551, 8286, 15085, 27464, 50000, 1000000 FEs, for a total of 5 ×12×30×28 = 50,400 optimizer runs. The largest budget in each dimension matches the CEC2022 technical-report budget. The 1040-evaluation rung is one ordinary rung of this common ladder and is the exact budget used in the EAF–STATCOM tuning application (N = 40, 25 updates, 40 × (25 + 1) = 1040); it is referred to as the exact application-aligned budget. The complete twenty-eight-rung ladder is tabulated in Supplementary Material S1. 4.3 Statistical methods The terminal error of a run is e = fbest −F∗, the gap between the best objective found and the known optimum; runs with e < 10−8 are recorded as e = 0, and where a base-10 logarithm is taken the per-function median error is floored at 10−8 (that is, log10 max(e,10−8)) so that the ∆log10 values of the component-removal study are well defined. Each benchmark function is one statistical block. For every (dimension, budget) condition the per-function summary is the median terminal error over the thirty runs; the algorithms are ranked once per function, giving a rank matrix that drives the Friedman test and the mean ranks. Because the algorithms share seeds and identical initial populations, the primary pairwise test is the two-sided exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the thirty matched pairs per function, with the direction taken from the signed-rank statistic and Holm correction applied across the twelve functions separately for each planned comparison [19]. Win/ tie/loss (W/T/L) counts report REMBGO better / no significant difference / EMBGO better after this correction. The Holm family is the twelve functions within a single budget; cross-rung multiplicity is not controlled, so budgets are not compared inferentially against one another. The component-removal analysis of Section 5 uses the same matched CEC2022 records at 1040 evaluations; its ranking, paired-comparison, familywise-correction and exact-zero/tie conventions are specified in Supplementary Material S5. 4.4 Reproducibility Each benchmark run is archived separately with its optimizer, dimension, function, seed and evaluation-budget meta- data. The reported tables and figures regenerate directly from these stored records without rerunning the optimizers. For the application transfer study, replay of the ten tuning-arc cases reproduces the stored Jtot values to a maximum absolute difference of 2.03×10−8. 5 Results 5.1 Optimizer ranking at different evaluation budgets Figure 1 shows the Friedman mean rank of each optimizer as a function of the evaluation budget, and Table 2 lists representative rungs at the two dimensions. The ranking changes materially with the budget, and three regimes are visible. At the smallest budgets the swarm methods lead: at D=10 with 200 evaluations PSO and GWO share the lowest mean rank (1.917), ahead of REMBGO (2.667), and at D=20 with 125 evaluations GWO leads at 1.250, so REMBGO is competitive but not strongest at these very restricted budgets. At the exact application-aligned budget of 1040 evaluations the picture is dimension-dependent. At D=10 REM- BGO obtains the lowest observed mean rank (2.250, ahead of PSO 2.750 and GWO 2.833), but the omnibus test does not reject (p = 0.089), so REMBGO holds the lowest observed mean rank rather than being the statistically strongest optimizer. At D=20 GWO leads (2.250), followed by PSO (2.500) and REMBGO (2.583), yet REMBGO still ranks below its parent EMBGO (3.833) while the omnibus test rejects equal ranks (p = 0.022); the planned EMBGO contrast is reported next. At large budgets FP-SHADE obtains the lowest observed mean ranks—1.625 at D=10 with 200000 evaluations and 1.542 at D=20 with 106—while GWO falls to last in both.
