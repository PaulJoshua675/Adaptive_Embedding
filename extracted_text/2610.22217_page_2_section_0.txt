Nevertheless, directly aligning the predictions of expert policies does not explicitly distinguish reusable capability-level strategies from capability-specific patterns, which may include overfitting and reward-hacking behaviors inadvertently encoded in these experts. To address these challenges, we introduce Skill-DeCoupled On-Policy Expert Distillation (SCOPED), a framework that decouples capability-specific skills from expert policies to better guide multimodal post-training. First, SCOPED independently optimizes a set of capability-specific experts with RL, where each expert is trained on a dedicated dataset with a capability-level reward objective. This independent optimization allows different experts to explore diverse solution strate- gies for their respective visual understanding capabilities without interference from heterogeneous training objectives. Next, to acquire complementary visual understanding capabilities, SCOPED extracts capability-specific skills from the reasoning trajectories of these experts. These skills ex- plicitly summarize reusable solution patterns underlying successful and failed expert behaviors. We then augment each expert with its corresponding skill to provide explicit procedural guidance during reasoning, thereby reducing unnecessary or capability-irrelevant reasoning patterns that may inter- fere with capability learning. Finally, SCOPED applies On-Policy Distillation (OPD) to transfer the complementary capabilities from these skill-augmented experts into a unified student model. Extensive experiments across eight multimodal benchmarks show that SCOPED enables a compact 8B model to achieve the highest average score among the evaluated models, surpassing larger open- source and leading closed-source baselines. Ablation studies demonstrate the benefits of expert distillation and the additional contribution of skill augmentation. Further analyses reveal limitations of mixed-capability optimization, including lower training rewards than independent capability RL, weak alignment with expert-guided directions, and recurring conflicts between capability-specific gradients. We also find that skill conditioning increases trajectory consistency within each capability and directs greater attention toward task-relevant visual evidence, while the distilled student better preserves expert-induced behavior. 2
