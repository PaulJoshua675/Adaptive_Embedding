We briefly discuss the works most closely related to our evaluation setting and baselines, and defer a discussion to Appendix A.1. Learning to defer and supervised routing. The fundamental problem of knowing when a model should abstain or seek help has been extensively studied under the umbrella of learning-to-defer and selective prediction [19, 33]. With the advent of large language models, this challenge translates into determining when a weak model should autonomously escalate to a stronger one. In parallel to frameworks that study deferral for verification [22], our focus is on routing model generations. Many current cascade methods rely on ground-truth correctness labels to train quality scorers, process verifiers, or token-level confidence predictors [1, 5, 6, 15, 38]. In our experiments, we represent this paradigm using a ground-truth supervised baseline highlighting that our proposed framework fundamentally eliminates the need for such expensive, task-specific supervision. Label-free routing signals. To avoid human annotations, recent work extracts reliability signals directly from a single unlabeled generation using internal uncertainty metrics or hidden-state probes [10, 15, 24]. A prevalent alternative assesses reliability by generating multiple responses to measure internal agreement or semantic overlap [12, 25, 46, 51].
