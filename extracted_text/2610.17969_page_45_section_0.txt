Sec. 2.3 identifies the stability-relevant quantity as the positive measured RMS ratio αℓ. In the zero-floor formulation of GCT, proposal canonicalization makes that pre-retraction ratio exactly the magnitude |αℓ| of the learned scalar coefficient, initialized at 1/ √ 2L. With the fixed 10−12 implementation floor, that identity is exact only up to the finite-floor defect documented in Eq. F.1. Residual-scaling methods control the measured ratio αℓ, often indirectly through skip or branch coefficients. These coefficients should not be identified with the RMS ratio unless branch and stream scales are matched. The original Post-LN addition uses raw coefficients of one. ReZero and SkipInit initialize a branch gate at zero (Bachlechner et al., 2021; De and Smith, 2020). DeepNet uses the skip coefficient (2N)1/4 together with a matching initialization down-scale calibrated for the very-deep regime (Wang et al., 2024). We next evaluate the coefficient-one Post-LN reference and report the corresponding depth sensitivity. In Post-LN, the conventional raw coefficient of one need not produce a measured ratio of αℓ= 1. Under the shared recipe, the five L = 8 anchor runs span losses from 4.14 to 7.60, with mean 5.47 ± 1.64. The L = 24 and L = 48 runs reach 7.62 and 7.61. GCT reaches 3.973 and 3.903 under the same fixed-budget depth protocol (Table 1). These values illustrate why the coefficient-one anchor requires a different depth schedule or training recipe. Our experiments use the coefficient-one Post-LN anchor and do not evaluate the full DeepNet recipe.
