APPENDIX IV WBD-EX REGIONAL ROUTE COMPLETION WBD-EX policies are evaluated on fourteen standardized routes traversing six body regions (Table VII). A. Body Regions Evaluation is defined over six named regions of the torso frame, applied to the settled object center (Fig. 8). The region bands are fixed across all object sets so that scores are comparable; the voxels a given region admits differ across objects only because the reachable grasp set does. Front and back occupy the three innermost 2 cm columns on either side of the body within ±3 cm laterally and 0.090–0.19 m vertically, and both require a both-arm grasp. The under- arms lie beyond ±0.11 m laterally, within −0.04–0.06 m front-to-back and 0.090–0.22 m vertically, and require the corresponding single-arm grasp. The shoulders lie beyond ±0.05 m laterally, within ±0.06 m front-to-back and 0.29– 0.49 m vertically, also requiring the corresponding single- arm grasp. All expert evaluations use the completion and inactive-arm release requirements in Appendix III-C. B. Routes A route is a fixed sequence of regions. The environment is initialized inside the first region, and each subsequent region becomes a commanded goal that must be reached in order. We name a route by its region sequence, abbreviating front as F, back as B, the left and right underarms as LA and RA, and the left and right shoulders as LS and RS. A route’s name therefore states its itinerary: route F LA B starts at the front, passes through the left underarm, and finishes at the back. Table VII lists all fourteen routes.
