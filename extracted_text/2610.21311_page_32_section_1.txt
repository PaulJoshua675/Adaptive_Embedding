This work presents the first mathematical runtime analysis of evolutionary multitask optimization in the multi-objective domain, thereby bridging a critical gap between its empirical success and theoretical foundations. By analyzing the representative MO-MFEA on pairs of bi-objective benchmarks, we demonstrate that when a complex, multimodal problem (e.g., OJZJ or DLTB) is paired with a structurally similar yet simpler one (e.g., OMM or LOTZ), the algorithm can solve both tasks simultaneously in asymptotically the same time required to solve the simpler task alone. Specifically, we proved an expected runtime of O(n2 log n) for the multi- task problem (OMM, OJZJ), which translates to a speedup of Θ(nk−1/ log n) over optimizing OJZJ in isolation. Similarly, for the pair (LOTZ, DLTB), the algorithm achieves a runtime of O(n3)–matching that of optimizing LOTZ alone–while attaining a Θ(n) speedup compared to optimizing DLTB alone. Beyond these specific findings, the theoretical framework and properties established for MO-MFEA in this work will facilitate future runtime anal- yses for a broader class of multitask multi-objective optimization problems. Extending this analysis to other popular evolutionary multitask algorithms, as well as to problems with more than two objectives or tasks, constitutes a compelling direction for future research.
