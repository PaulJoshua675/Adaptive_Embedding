B.3 RANK HISTOGRAMS A rank histogram counts, at each grid point, how many ensemble members fall below the reference value. For a calibrated ensemble, the reference is equally likely to take any of the N + 1 ranks and the histogram is flat. A U shape, with the reference too often below or above all members, indicates insufficient spread; a T shape, with the reference too often near the middle of the ensemble, indicates excessive spread; an asymmetry indicates a bias. A high last rank means the reference is often stronger than every member, i.e. the forecast winds are too weak; a high first rank means the opposite. We show 10m wind speed, for storm footprints (Figure 10), for forecast fields grouped into three lead-time ranges (Figure 11), and for each lead time (Figures 12 and 13). In 2021–2023, against ERA5, IFS-ENS places the reference below all its members too often, at every lead time and on footprints: its winds are stronger than ERA5’s, consistent with ERA5’s underestimation of peak winds (Dulac et al., 2024) rather than an IFS-ENS error. Both AI models have U-shaped footprint histograms, indicating too little spread, and GenCast’s larger excess at the last rank shows footprint winds weaker than ERA5’s. By lead time, the AI models underestimate the wind at short leads, and FCN3 also at intermediate leads; at long leads their histograms are close to uniform. 18
