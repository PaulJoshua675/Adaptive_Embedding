In clinical practice, definitive diagnosis requires CSF orexin-A concentrations ≤110 pg/mL (over 90% reduction relative to nor- mal) [11, 34]. This deficit is formalized by setting Ωtone = 0.05. In addition, the lack of orexinergic stabilization on REM-off monoaminergic nuclei disinhibits brainstem REM-generating structures such as the sublaterodorsal nucleus [9, 24]. This is parameterized by lowering the REM entry latency threshold from θrem = 70.0 min down to θrem = 5.0 min, reproducing diagnostic sleep-onset REM periods (SOREMPs) observed on the Multiple Sleep Latency Test (MSLT) [11, 3]. To provide comprehensive clinical evaluation, we compute nine standard PSG and restorative metrics: 1. Environmental Sleep Percentage (Sleep%): Cumulative sleep duration relative to observa- tion time: Sleep% = (TST/72.0 h) × 100%. 2. Total Sleep Time (TST): Cumulative hours scored in sleep stages N1, N2, N3, or REM across 72.0 hours [3]. 3. Sleep Onset Latency (SOL): Minutes from 23:00 lights-out to first scored sleep epoch (normal < 30 min; values > 30 min indicate initiation insomnia) [7, 3, 14]. 4. Wake After Sleep Onset (WASO): Cumulative nocturnal wakefulness across three nights (normal < 30 min/night, < 90 min total; higher values indicate maintenance insomnia) [7, 3, 30]. 5. Ultradian Cycles: Completed N1 →N2 →N3 →REM cycles lasting ≥5 min (normal 4–5 cycles/night, reflecting biological ultradian rhythms with a period shorter than 24 hours, here operating at a 90–110 min cycle) [15, 7]. 6. Sleep-Onset REM Periods (SOREMPs): REM episodes within 15 minutes of sleep onset (≥2 SOREMPs on MSLT indicates narcolepsy) [11, 3]. 7.
