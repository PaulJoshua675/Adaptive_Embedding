We refer to this setting as the partial-evidence regime, which captures two properties of retrieval in MT. First, external evidence is beneficial for only a subset of inputs where the model’s para- metric knowledge doesn’t suffices. Second, when evidence is needed, retrieval based solely on seman- tic similarity may fail to surface the most useful evidence. This raises two learning problems. The first is retrieval adaptivity: the model must deter- mine when additional evidence is useful instead of retrieving by default. The second is retrieval qual- ity alignment: once retrieval is invoked, the system must determine what evidence actually improves the final translation quality. To address them, we propose Selective Retrieval- Augmented Translation (SRAT), an agentic reinforcement-learning framework that treats rea- soning, retrieval, and translation as actions in a unified policy. Given a source input, the model’s action space at each step includes reasoning about the translation, generating a retrieval query and in- corporating the returned evidence, or terminating with the final translation. Rather than supervis- ing which examples should retrieve, we optimize complete trajectories using final translation qual- ity. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) allows trajectories with differ- ent retrieval decisions and queries to compete for the same input, thereby learning when retrieval is worthwhile and what to retrieve to improve final translation quality. Moreover, we develop two approaches for fur- ther aligning the retriever with downstream trans- lation quality rather than simple semantic similar- ity. First, a natural and principled approach is to jointly optimize the LLM translator and retriever end-to-end. To achieve it, we replace determinis- tic top-k selection during training with a Plackett– Luce ranking policy (Plackett, 1975), whose selec- tion log-probability allows trajectory-level transla- tion reward to update the retriever through a score- function gradient. However, this joint signal can be noisy: a trajectory contains many LLM policy decisions but only a few retrieval actions, mak- ing credit assignment to the retriever inherently high-variance. To address this, we further intro- duce translation-quality contrastive training, which fixes the translator, evaluates candidate retrieval evidence by their measured effect on translation quality, and converts these into direct contrastive supervision. This isolates the effect of retrieval from other policy decisions, providing the retriever with substantially more targeted training signals. We evaluate SRAT across three translation settings: idiom translation, knowledge-intensive translation, and document-level translation. The first two use offline retrieval corpora, while the document-level setting additionally evaluates SRAT with online retrieval. Across these settings, SRAT consistently outperforms never-retrieve and always-retrieve baselines, demonstrating the bene- fit of learning when to retrieve and what evidence to seek. Ablations show that quality-aligned re- triever training provides further significant gains when similarity-based retrieval is insufficient. We also find that the retrieval policy can be optimized with a frontier black-box translator, outperforming its direct translation and indicating that the adap- tive retrieval decision training can be decoupled from the translator and optimized as a modular component for external generation models. Our main contributions are threefold: • Selective retrieval as a translation decision. We characterize LLM-based RAG translation as a partial-evidence regime in which external knowl- edge is useful only conditionally. We formulate machine translation by LLM as a sequential pol- icy over reasoning, retrieval, and final translation, enabling the model to learn when and what to retrieve from translation outcomes rather than fixed routing rules. • Quality-aligned evidence retrieval. We pro- pose two retriever-training strategies. Joint Plackett–Luce policy-gradient training propa- gates trajectory-level translation reward to ev- idence retrieval, while translation-quality con- trastive training derives direct retriever supervi- sion from controlled retrieval evidence interven- tions. Both align the retriever toward the down- stream effect of retrieved evidence on translation quality rather than semantic similarity alone. • Evaluation across retrieval regimes. Ex- periments on idiom, knowledge-intensive, and document-level translation with offline and on- line retrieval consistently outperform fixed never- and always-retrieve strategies.
