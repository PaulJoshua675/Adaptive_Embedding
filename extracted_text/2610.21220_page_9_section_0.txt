A simple linear rule assumes that the input preference vector can be directly used as the merging coefficients. This assumption can be violated when objectives have different parameter scales, non- linear interactions, or residual conflicts. RecSoup addresses this linear regime violation by decoupling parameter selection from coefficient learning: pruning first builds a cleaner task-vector basis, and adaptive merging then learns non-linear coefficients condi- tioned on preferences. Thus, RecSoup can amplify or suppress each objective beyond the input preference value, enabling more flexible navigation along the Pareto front. 6.3.2 RQ2: Impact of Cross-Objective Relevance Score. We investigate the contribution of the Cross-Objective Relevance Score Talign defined in Eq. (5) to the pruning effectiveness of RecSoup. The pruning strategy in RecSoup is guided by two complementary signals: Tself and Talign. In contrast, TIES performs pruning solely based on Tself. To isolate the impact of Talign, we ensure that both RecSoup and TIES share identical merging coefficients and merging procedures. Figure 7 presents the Hypervolume (HV) results and the relative improvement of RecSoup over the TIES baseline across three backbones and multiple datasets. On MovieLens-1M, Rec- Soup consistently outperforms TIES, achieving relative HV gains of +4.95% (SASRec), +2.71% (GRU4Rec), and +1.78% (TiSASRec). These results confirm that Talign effectively guides the pruning process: by considering cross-objective relevance in addition to objective-specific importance, RecSoup selectively retains parame- ters critical to multiple objectives. This prevents the over-pruning of parameters that are essential for one objective but not heavily weighted by Tself, which TIES may discard. In summary, this abla- tion demonstrates that the similarity score is a key component in RecSoup’s pruning stage, directly contributing to improved multi- objective performance by preserving cross-task compatibility while maintaining a controlled merging process. 6.3.3 RQ3: Inference-Time Efficiency of Parameter Genera- tion and RecSoup. We first extend controllable recommendation to the Accuracy-Fairness setting by constructing Accuracy-oriented and Fairness-oriented specialist models. Fairness is measured using the absolute NDCG gap between male and female user groups. Table 3 reports the controllable results on MovieLens-1M with SASRec. RecSoup achieves the highest HV among all compared controllable baselines, showing stronger Pareto efficiency in the Accuracy-Fairness setting. The performance drop of “w/o MLP” and “w/o Prune” further verifies the contribution of the coefficient learner and the pruning strategy. We exclude Paragon from the main comparison because it collapses to a single Pareto solution in this setting, which cannot provide meaningful controllable transi- tions across different preferences. Overall, these results show that RecSoup can be effectively generalized to fairness-aware control- lable recommendation.
