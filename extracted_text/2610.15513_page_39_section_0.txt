Re-scoring under the JailbreakBench and HarmBench evaluation rubrics (Section 4.1.1) reproduced 100%. Against this, the cross-validation covers 365 replies drawn from five of the 36 targets, agreement is weakest on grok-4-1-fast (𝜅= 0.37 to 0.46), which is one of the frontier targets carrying the headline claim, and the rubric re-scoring examines only attacks the in-loop judge had already accepted. What would settle the question is a gold arbiter held out of the search entirely, applied post hoc to a stratified sample of solved behaviors, with the resulting false-discovery rate reported alongside the in-loop ASR, together with human annotation of the same sample. We regard this as the single highest-value extension of the present evaluation, and the released judge configurations and evaluation rubrics specify the scoring setup precisely enough for such a check to be reproduced. Until it is done, every ASR figure in this paper should be read as judge-referenced under an until-success protocol. Baselines are transcribed, and budgets are not matched. No baseline in this paper was re-run by us. Reported figures for PAIR, TAP, PAP, Adaptive RS, AutoDAN-Turbo, KDA, and DAGR are taken from their original publications, which used different judges, different target-model versions, and in several cases query budgets that differ from ours by one to two orders of magnitude (Section 4.1.2). Differences between REPRO and these methods in the comparison tables therefore reflect method and protocol jointly and cannot be attributed to method alone. Budget-matched re-runs under a common judge, and ASR-versus-budget curves that place all methods at a common operating point, are the direct remedy and are left to future work. Unevaluated objectives. REPRO is formulated and implemented as a four-objective optimizer, but only attack success is exercised in the reported experiments. All experiments run under a single deployment environment, which collapses XFER to ASR (Section 4.1.3), and detectability and prompt length are optimized internally but never reported as results. The paper therefore contains no Pareto front, no hypervolume measurement, and no evidence that the multi-objective machinery yields trade-offs a practitioner could use. The three-environment protocol defined in Section 3.1 would supply this evidence at modest cost and is the natural next experiment. Accumulated state across runs. The strategy library and the amortization cache persist and grow across runs, so the 36-target evaluation is a sequence rather than a set of independent trials, and targets evaluated later benefit from discoveries made earlier. Cold-library, cold-cache runs on a subset of frontier targets would separate the contribution of accumulated state from the contribution of the search, and are not reported here. Coverage of the query-cost analysis. Per-behavior query logs were retained for 211 behaviors across five targets (Table 12), a subset of the full experiment matrix. The efficiency figures we report, including the median of 24 target queries, are computed over this subset rather than over every run behind the ASR tables. Comprehensive per-behavior cost accounting across all targets would require re-running the remaining experiments with logging enabled. 5.3 Implications for Software Engineering Practice REPRO’s query efficiency, full automation, and minimal hyperparameter surface (Table 3) make it suitable for integration into CI/CD pipelines: a development team can run a focused sweep on each model update or prompt-template change, surfacing new vulnerabilities before deployment. The median per-behavior cost of 24 target queries makes routine safety sweeps substantially cheaper than manual red-teaming engagements, even against commercial API endpoints.
