In this way, a spoken query can be embedded directly for retrieval without ASR. We evaluate SSEmb with text embedding models rang- ing from 0.6B to 8B parameters on English and six additional languages. SSEmb approaches the retrieval performance of cascaded ASR-then-retrieve systems with substantially lower latency, outperforms existing speech embedding model, and surpasses the cascaded system in some low-resource settings. It also generalizes to text-to-speech and speech-to-speech re- trieval without additional training. Controlled analyses val- idate the speech-transcript alignment paradigm across data scales, showing only modest performance degradation when training data is reduced to 10 hours and consistent improve- ments as more data becomes available. Together with further ablations on loss functions and trainable modules, these find- ings provide a broader understanding of the framework and establish practical guidelines for effectively transferring pre- trained text retrieval capabilities to speech. 2. SSEMB 2.1. Architecture Following prior adapter-based approaches [4], we construct a speech retriever by connecting a pretrained speech encoder to a frozen text embedding model through a lightweight adapter. We use Qwen3-Embedding [8] as the text backbone, de- noted by ftext. Given a text input x, representing either a query q or a passage c, we obtain its d-dimensional embed- ding by pooling the hidden state of an appended EOS token: zx = [ftext (E(x) ⊕E[EOS])][EOS] , x ∈{q, c}, (1) where E(x) denotes the sequence of token embeddings and ⊕denotes sequence concatenation. Retrieval scores are com- puted using cosine similarity of the embeddings. Given a speech input a, the speech encoder fspeech pro- duces a sequence of ds-dimensional acoustic representations, which the adapter gθ projects into the input space of the text model. The resulting representations are treated as pseudo- token embeddings and processed by the frozen text model: Ha = fspeech(a) ∈RT ×ds, (2) eEa = gθ(Ha) ∈RT ×d, (3) za = h ftext  eEa ⊕E[EOS] i [EOS] ∈Rd. (4) The resulting za serves as the final speech embedding. The architecture of gθ varies by language, as described in Section 2.3. Following Qwen3-Embedding, an instruction is prepended to text queries but not to passages. We omit this instruction from the notation for brevity. 2.2.
