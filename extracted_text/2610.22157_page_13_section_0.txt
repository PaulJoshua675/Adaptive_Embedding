The difference in training computational time between both architectures is more pronounced in 2D relative to 1D. Therefore, we used the 2D problem to analyse the training time scaling for key hyperparameters. Fig. 6 shows the mean training time for both architectures for varying numbers of internal collocation points and input functions, respectively, along with the relative speed-up of the ATenH architecture compared to the Vanilla architecture. We use the internal, rather than total, collocation points due to the former being much greater in number than initial/boundary points in practice (specifically, p2/3 I = pbx1 = pbx2 = p0). For both PI-DeepONet architectures, the training time increases linearly with the number of internal col- location points or input functions. These linear scalings are consistent with the theoretical cost analysis presented in Section 2.4. In particular, Equations 19 and 25 show that the computational cost of a training step is proportional to the number of collocation points and the number of input functions, leading to an overall O(p) and O(n) dependence. Moreover, the prefactors of these terms are smaller for the ATenH archi- tecture compared to the Vanilla architecture, which explains the consistently lower training times observed for ATenH across all tested collocation point and input function counts. Using the derived cost expressions in Equation 27 with the chosen hyperparameters yields the asymptotic theoretical speed-up factor ϕ →7.5 as p →∞, while remaining independent of n. The value of 7.5 is specific to the chosen values of m, M, e, L, d, and s. 3.3 3D Linear Advection In this example, the training of both PI-DeepONet architectures is compared for the 3D linear advection problem.
