We focus on generative engine citations in jp- election-26, with Google-SERP results in us-election-24 serving as a reference. 4.1 What Sources Do Generative Engines Use? We group each observed domain into four source channels ordered by how costly it is for a candidate or party to publish or mod- ify content: owned/self-published, e.g., candidate or party websites; open-platform UGC, e.g., Reddit; earned/paid press, e.g., news outlets; and independent institution, e.g., government agencies. Specifically, we first use claude-opus-5 with web access to verify the organi- zation behind each domain and assign an initial label, followed by manual review of low-confidence and ambiguous cases. The label- ing rules and representative examples are provided in Appendix A. For each engine on each day, we pool source occurrences across its query cells and compute the source share of each channel as its frac- tion of all source occurrences. For the 106-day U.S. Google-SERP reference, we use a 7-day centered moving average to summarize the longer-term pattern. The Japan generative engine series covers only 7 days and is therefore reported as raw daily values. Generative engines often cite sources from lower-cost chan- nels. Figure 1 shows that earned/paid press remains the largest source channel, accounting for 46.4–60.9% of citations across the four generative engines. At the same time, owned/self-published sources account for 24.8–44.3%, while open-platform UGC reaches as high as 22.2%. For reference, their shares in U.S. Google-SERP results are 5.1% and 4.5%, respectively, with these cross-setting differences treated descriptively. Independent institution sources account for only 1.4–3.0% of generative-engine citations, compared with 39.3% in the U.S. Google-SERP reference. These source pat- terns are consistent with prior studies showing that generative engines answering election-related queries cite candidate or party websites, UGC platforms, and news media [9, 10, 19, 28]. Source- channel shares also change little across the seven Japan collection days within each generative engine. The longer U.S. Google-SERP reference similarly shows a relatively stable source composition over the 106-day window, with earned/paid press and independent institution remaining dominant. These patterns indicate that gener- ative engines frequently use sources that candidates or parties can publish or modify at relatively low cost, raising concerns about the influence of externally controlled content on electoral information. 4.2 How Long Do the Same Sources Stay Visible?
