Parallelism. We first test whether ∆app encodes appearance independently of the subject it was extracted from. Residuals extracted from the same attribute but different subjects remain highly aligned, whereas residuals from different attributes are clearly separated. This indicates that ∆app captures a subject- agnostic appearance direction rather than subject-specific content. Transferability. We then add a ∆app extracted from one subject to the ⟨EOS⟩ of a different prompt which only contains subject and scene descriptions, and compare it against the embedding of an explicit prompt (subject + appear- ance + scene) that states the attribute in words (transfer target). The two are highly similar, while both the appearance-free base and a mismatched random attribute are clearly distinguished. Notably, this holds even for long, compo- sitional phrases, indicating that EOS-level residuals transfer rich appearance semantics—not merely single words—in a linear manner. Generative effect. Finally, scaling the injected residual as eossc+λ·∆app progres- sively strengthens the target appearance in the generated image (Fig. 11, bot- tom): as λ increases, the attribute (e.g., round glasses) emerges and intensifies, confirming that the residual exerts a controllable, monotonic influence on gener- ation. Together, these results show that ∆app functions as a subject-agnostic, transferable, and controllable appearance direction—justifying our EOS-level residual manipulation.
