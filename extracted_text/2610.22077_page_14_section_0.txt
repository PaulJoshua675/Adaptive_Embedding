A EVALUATION PROTOCOL AND REPRODUCIBILITY Events, initialization, and ensemble size. Selection uses each storm’s maximum IBTrACS USA WIND, with a threshold of at least 64 knots, i.e. category 1 to 5 on the Saffir–Simpson scale, yielding 125 storms in 2021–2023 and 53 in 2025. A storm’s first time is its first IBTrACS record with a reported USA WIND, and each forecast starts 12 hours before it, after moving any 06:00/18:00 UTC start to the preceding 00:00/12:00 cycle, since IFS-ENS forecasts initialized at 06:00/18:00 UTC do not cover the full 15-day window in the sources we used. Selection by lifetime maximum does not mean the storm is already at hurricane strength at initialization; this protocol evaluates selected known events, not unconditional cyclone occurrence. All forecasts span 15 days. We retain 50 members per model in 2021–2023. Missing individual files in the 2025 GenCast and IFS-ENS archives motivate a uniform 49-member comparison for all three models. IFS-ENS uses perturbed members, excluding the control. Equal membership is not an equal-compute comparison. Forecast and reference sources. We ran GenCast and FCN3 ourselves and retrieved the other forecasts from public archives: • IFS-ENS: the 50 perturbed members, excluding the control; for 2021–2023 from TIGGE (Bougeault et al., 2010) (ECMWF, CC BY 4.0), already on the 0.25◦grid, and for 2025 from ECMWF Open Data (CC BY 4.0) through Earth2Studio’s IFS ENS FX interface. The IFS cycles in operation are listed in Table 3. • GenCast: DeepMind’s implementation (Apache 2.0) with the released 0.25◦, 13-level checkpoint trained on ERA5 1979–2018 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), initialized from ERA5 (see Reference fields below); about 30 minutes per 15-day member on H100/H200 GPUs, which limited the number of storms. • FCN3: NVIDIA’s released weights (Hugging Face nvidia/fourcastnet3, revision 76ef0c6; Apache 2.0) run through Earth2Studio (Apache 2.0), initialized from ARCO- ERA5 (Carver & Merose, 2023); about one minute per member on H200 GPUs. • FGN: precomputed forecasts from the WeatherNext 2 archive, weathernext/ weathernext_2_0_0/zarr/2025_to_present (CC BY 4.0). • GenCast operational: precomputed forecasts from the WeatherNext Gen archive, weathernext/126478713_1_0/zarr/126478713_2024_to_present (CC BY 4.0). Model size and compute. The models also differ in size and in the compute required for inference. GenCast has a single network of about 57M parameters, each FGN network has 180M parameters, and FCN3 has 711M parameters (Alet et al., 2025; Bonev et al., 2025). GenCast requires significantly more compute at inference (20 diffusion solver steps, 39 network evaluations) for a 12-hour forecast step (Price et al., 2024), whereas FGN and FCN3 generate a 6-hour step in a single forward pass. However, GenCast reportedly has a lower training budget than FGN (Alet et al., 2025; Price et al., 2024). IBTrACS (NOAA NCEI, public domain) supplies observed positions in both settings; ERA5 and HRES-fc0 supply the gridded references. These are different verification targets, so absolute field scores should not be compared across cohorts. Reference fields. ERA5 (Copernicus Climate Change Service, CC BY 4.0) for 2021 was retrieved from WeatherBench 2 (Rasp et al., 2024), and for 2022–2023 from the Copernicus Climate Data Store, at 0.25◦. HRES-fc0 was retrieved from ECMWF Open Data (CC BY 4.0) through Earth2Studio’s IFS interface: for each valid time, the 0-hour state of the operational high-resolution forecast initialized at that time, at 0.25◦, i.e. after data assimilation of observations from ±3 hours. Common fields and evaluation window. All field comparisons use a 721×1440 latitude–longitude grid at 0.25◦spacing and 30 forecast times, 12–360 hours at 12-hour intervals. The 12-hour step is that of the coarsest model, GenCast; the models with 6-hour output are subsampled to it so that all models are compared at the same times. The seven CRPS variables are mean sea-level pressure, the two 10m wind components, geopotential at 300 and 500 hPa, and the two 850 hPa wind components. 14
