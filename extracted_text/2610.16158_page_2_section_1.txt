2.1 Independent-Set Search and Structural Guidance MWIS heuristics exploit solution structure to combine intensifi- cation with exploration. METAMIS uses efficient local moves and path relinking within a GRASP framework [9], while CHILS com- bines iterated local search with cooperation based on differences among solutions [13]. Learning-based graph optimization [5, 19] offers another form of structural guidance: Li et al. [20] use graph- convolutional predictions to guide tree search for independent sets. These approaches share the goal of using graph and solution infor- mation to expose promising combinations [2]. CIPHEUR studies this relationship at the level of search inter- ventions. Resource-specific blocking evidence connects candidate activities to the trajectories that exclude them, providing a basis for choosing where to redirect search. A coordinated continuation then explores the opportunities created by the intervention. The emphasis is on converting structural evidence into decisions about the course of a population search, complementing work on local moves, solution exchange, and vertex-level guidance. 2.2 Adaptive and Temporally Extended Search Control Selection hyper-heuristics adapt the use of low-level heuristics to search state and history [10]; dynamic algorithm configuration learns policies for changing algorithm settings during execution [1]. Learned heuristic scheduling similarly allocates effort among ex- isting solver components [8]. Adaptive large neighborhood search coordinates competing removal and insertion heuristics through performance feedback [27], and learned neighborhood selection chooses variables for solver-based reoptimization [15, 34, 39]. To- gether, these studies establish policy adaptation, effort allocation, and intervention targeting as complementary dimensions of search control [32, 37]. Temporal abstraction provides a further dimension by treating an extended course of action as a decision unit, as formalized by the options framework [30]. SIUs combine structural targeting with an explicit intervention–recovery sequence. A conflict anchor guides reconstruction priorities across the candidate activities, and the accompanying continuation specifies how the altered trajectory contributes to further search. Feedback summarizes quality changes over the executed sequence.
