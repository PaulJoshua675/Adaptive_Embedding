A Tasks and Data Composition A.1 Task Definitions Object checks identity, global shape, and object-level properties. Part checks component presence, count, type, shape, and arrangement. Material checks color, texture, patterns, and visible surface appearance. Part Grounding presents a highlighted region and a named-part claim. Part Relation presents marked subject and object parts and a relation claim; the markings define its direction. Affordance Region checks whether a marked region supports a stated action. Grasp Validity displays a parallel-jaw pose relative to an object and uses source simulation labels. Identity Preservation asks whether two multi-view assets retain the same identity despite changes in expression or texture. Fine-grained Geometry asks whether a generated 3D candidate matches the reference object’s shape and structure, while explicitly ignoring differences in color, texture, and material. Motion Understanding evaluates the complete caption set and checks action correctness, naturalness, and geometric stability. Its three negative issue classes are internal diagnostics, not additional tasks. A counterfactual triplet contains a positive anchor, a specification-side negative, and a candidate-side negative. An anchor with only one available negative forms a pair rather than a complete triplet. A counterexample is not automatically a measured counterfactual pair. The latter requires the original and changed records, their shared specification or candidate, and the sibling link. An A/B identity comparison is likewise one verification input, not two independently scored pair/triplet branches. A.2 Model-Visible Inputs Object, Part, and Material receive the claim and eight frozen original views, without an additional task-specific instruction. Part Grounding, Part Relation, Affordance Region, and Grasp Validity receive their task-specific instructions and evidence sheets. Identity uses one 2-by-8 asset-pair sheet. Fine-grained Geometry uses one 2-by-7 reference/render sheet. Motion Understanding uses native MP4 or a frozen 4-by-4 sheet of sixteen uniformly sampled frames. A video and all its captions form one example. Reference answers, reference explanations, construction labels, and analysis labels are excluded from subject-model inputs. A.3 Benchmark and Training Resource The benchmark contains 2,581 examples: 970 True and 1,611 False. All ten models evaluate this set; the human comparison uses forty examples per task drawn from it. Starting from the 17,223-example automated construction pool, source-level grouping and exclusion of all benchmark source groups and their descendants yield a source-disjoint Training Resource with 14,126 examples. It contains 5,660 True anchors and 8,466 False examples in 4,011 source groups. Table A1 reports the task-wise composition. A.4 Source Grouping All examples derived from the same source asset are assigned to the same source group, including counterfactual branches and edited descendants; known duplicates across source collections are merged before partitioning.
