Self-evolving language models generate tasks and train on their own outputs (Huang et al., 2026a; Xia et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2026b). Each inherited update changes both the learner and the curriculum of later rounds, allowing progress or regressions to accumulate (Shao et al., 2026). Self-training can even raise a model’s target metric and then collapse it within the same training campaign (Lin, 2026). The central decision is therefore which learning outcomes to carry forward. Task-side selection leaves a selection-to-update gap: pass rate, disagreement, and frontier proximity describe pre-training behavior, not the transfer produced by an optimizer update. In our audits, 42–52% of native Agent0/R-Zero admissions leave the frontier under independent responses. Even with exact-valid tasks, 60 of 96 Skill Self-Play (Skill-SP) updates have nonpositive correct-target NLL gain. Learning opportunities must ultimately be judged by the states they produce. HEIRCOMMIT judges realized transitions: it freezes a successor, compares it with the current model (the root) on fresh probes, and inherits that state or retains root. Fresh evidence separates confirmation from the noise that favored the proposal during selection. The deeper question is the granularity of inheritance. SOAR uses actual student improvement to reward a teacher (Sundaram et al., 2026), and APE evaluates candidate fine-tunings before accepting updates (Mar´ın, 2025). Pre-commit skill verification (Shang et al., 2026) and anytime-valid certificates (Sengupta, 2026) gate agent updates while the base model stays frozen. Whole-state acceptance nevertheless couples a branch’s gains and losses. Must a useful experience donor also supply the next learner’s parameters? Separating these choices opens a learning path beyond whole-state acceptance (Figure 1). EVOHEIR separates parameter inheritance from experience inheritance. Screening picks the re- cipient that supplies the next learner’s parameters, either a branch endpoint or the current model. EXPERIENCE RECOVERY then repairs this recipient’s failures with current and archived reference solutions while preserving its successes. HEIRCOMMIT confirms the completed post-absorption state against root. Qualified experience enters the archive regardless of this decision: parameter inheritance can stop without discarding material that helps a later recipient. We evaluate HEIRCOMMIT on the native updates of three self-evolution hosts, and EVOHEIR on four backbones over ten rounds. HEIRCOMMIT cuts the harmful share of accepted updates by 68–83% relative to random inheritance at the same acceptance rate, on every host. EVOHEIR achieves the highest mathematics and general-reasoning averages on all four backbones and beats Agent0 on 45 of 48 benchmark–backbone pairs. References from eight of ten donors weaker than the current model still improve the recipient. Our contributions are: 1. We identify the selection-to-update gap and prove that pre-update behavior alone cannot identify an update’s value. Reusing screening evidence to accept a branch pays a union bound over branches, which fresh confirmation removes. 2. We introduce HEIRCOMMIT, a source-independent rule that commits a frozen successor only when it beats the current model on fresh probes. On the native updates of R-Zero, Agent0, and Skill-SP, it turns negative pool gain positive on two of the three hosts. 3. We develop EVOHEIR, which decides separately which parameters the next learner inherits and which experience it absorbs. Across four backbones it raises the base model’s math average by 7.9–30.1%, the most of any compared method. 4. We show that experience outlives the model that produced it.
