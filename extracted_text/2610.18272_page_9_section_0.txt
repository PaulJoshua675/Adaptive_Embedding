Limitations This work studies the coordination of fast and slow thinking at the level of agent architecture. JevGPT assigns local evidence judgments and research plan- ning to separate components. A natural next step is to integrate these capabilities within a unified model that learns when to make rapid local judg- ments and when to engage in extended reasoning. Joint training could allow the model to adapt this balance to the evolving needs of an investigation and allocate computation accordingly. Our evaluation focuses on question answering and document ranking over fixed corpora. Extend- ing this approach to open-web research and long- form synthesis would help explore how fast and slow thinking can support a broader range of re- search tasks. Ethical Considerations Data and Intended Use. We use the publicly released BrowseComp-Plus and NeuCLIRBench benchmarks and their fixed corpora for research evaluation. We collect no additional source data, involve no human participants or annotators, and do not redistribute either corpus. Research agents may reproduce errors and biases in retrieved sources. JevGPT returns original passages with source loca- tions to support evidence inspection. Source relia- bility still requires independent assessment. Use of AI Assistants. We used Claude Code and OpenAI Codex for code development and manuscript editing. GPT Image 2.5 generated il- lustrative artwork for Figures 1 and 3. The authors directed the research, reviewed the outputs, and take responsibility for the methods, results, and final manuscript.
