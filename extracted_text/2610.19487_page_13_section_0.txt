Nested resolution and the participation ratio The preceding comparison changes the representation across three model classes. To isolate resolution more directly, we next construct a nested hierarchy in which the underlying nonlinear architecture is held fixed while progressively more of it is exposed. For each trial, a 24-subunit sigma–pi architecture is drawn once. Each nonlinear subunit combines a sub- set of the available contacts through pairwise and third- order multiplicative terms followed by a local nonlin- earity. Resolution is then varied without redrawing that architecture: the n = 1 representation exposes the first subunit, the next resolution exposes the first two, and so forth through the tested sequence n = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24. Importantly, the original six di- rect input coordinates remain available at every resolu- tion. Refinement therefore adds nonlinear features to an existing representation rather than replacing one set of inputs by another. This nesting gives the word “resolution” a precise mathematical meaning. If the feature vector at resolu- tion n contains the six direct coordinates and the first n nonlinear subunits, then every coarser feature set is contained in every finer one. Writing this nested repre- sentation as Φn(x) ⊂Φn+1(x) makes the hierarchy ex- plicit: Φn is obtained from Φn+1 by discarding resolved nonlinear coordinates. Importantly, the original six inputs x remain present at every resolution, while every added subunit is itself a deterministic function of x. Consequently, I(Y ; Φn) = I(Y ; x) for every n: refinement does not create addi- tional information about the target. What increases is the approximation space available to the restricted read- out. If Hn denotes the family of linear functions of Φn, then Hn ⊆Hn+1. Increasing n therefore enlarges the hypothesis class without changing the underlying input– output information. Any improvement in held-out pre- diction can then be attributed to representational access rather than to an increase in Iavail, and it can be inter- preted against an explicitly nested hierarchy rather than against independently resampled architectures. Across 60 independently drawn architectures and train–test sets, the mean held-out MSE decreases mono- tonically over the tested resolutions, from 0.0421 at one exposed nonlinear subunit to 0.0152 at 24 (Fig. 4a). For comparison, the corresponding direct-input baseline, which contains the same six raw inputs but no nonlinear subunits, has mean MSE approximately 0.0436. To summarize the improvement, we define the normal- ized inverse-error score in the analysis as the error at the first resolved level divided by the error at resolution n, so that Capprox(1) = 1 and increasing values correspond to improved approximation. Over the tested range, its mean scaling is well described by Capprox ∝n0.31, 95% CI [0.30, 0.32], (40) with a log–log fit exponent of approximately 0.31. The relation in Eq. (40) is an empirical scaling summary of this constructive hierarchy, not an exact law. In partic- ular, its log-space R2 is 0.8613, so the purpose of the exponent is to quantify the systematic trend rather than to claim a universal power law for dendritic capacity. Lower prediction error alone does not establish that the underlying representation has acquired independent dimensions; additional subunits could in principle sup- ply largely redundant copies of features already present. We therefore measure the geometry of the representation itself, without reference to the target labels. Let λk de- note the nonzero eigenvalues of the covariance matrix of the centered feature representation. Its effective rank is defined by the participation ratio reff = (P k λk)2 P k λ2 k . (41) Equation (41) distinguishes nominal feature count from effective dimensionality. If variance is concentrated al- most entirely along a small number of covariance di- rections, reff remains low even when many features are present. If variance is distributed across additional inde- pendent directions, the participation ratio rises. The latter is what occurs in the nested hierarchy. Mean reff increases from approximately 6.86 with one exposed subunit to 14.03 with 24, and its normalized scaling is ap- proximately n0.24, with 95% interval [0.23, 0.24] (Fig. 4b). The corresponding log-space fit is particularly tight, with R2 = 0.9907. The increase in nominal resolution is there- fore accompanied by an increase in the effective dimen- sionality of the representation, not merely by accumula- tion of duplicate coordinates. The two measurements answer complementary ques- tions. Capprox in Eq. (40) asks whether the enlarged representation provides a better basis for the particular target in Eq. (39). The participation ratio in Eq. (41) asks whether that representation itself occupies a higher- dimensional space, independently of the target labels. Their joint increase is the relevant result. Explicitly, finer resolution both enlarges the accessible feature space and improves approximation within this controlled construc- tion.
