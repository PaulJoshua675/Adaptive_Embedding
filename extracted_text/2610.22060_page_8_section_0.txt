Following RAEv2 (Singh et al., 2026), we use BLIP-3o datasets (Chen et al., 2025) (Long-Caption, Short-Caption, and JourneyDB) for pre-training and BLIP-3o-60K for fine-tuning. We replace the class conditioning in FeaTok with a sequence of text embeddings provided by Qwen3-0.6B (Qwen Team, 2025) to enable T2I generation, re-using our FeaTok-SigLIP2 tokenizer trained on ImageNet-1k. We pre-train 75k updates with batch size 2048, while baselines use 150k updates with batch size 1024, giving the same total number of training examples processed. Then we further fine-tune the pre-trained model for 500 updates, while baselines are fine-tuned for approximately 3k updates. After pre-training, FeaTok is broadly comparable to RAEv2. It is slightly lower on GenEval (Ghosh et al., 2023) (0.611 versus 0.624) and DPG-Bench (Hu et al., 2024) (79.8 versus 81.7), but higher on GenAI-Bench (Li et al., 2024a) (0.668 versus 0.638). After only 500 additional updates, FeaTok exceeds RAEv2 on all three benchmarks: 0.837 versus 0.827 on GenEval, 0.698 versus 0.680 on GenAI-Bench, and 83.4 versus 82.3 on DPG-Bench. The comparison with BAR (Yu et al., 2026) isolates the effect of the tokenizer directly, whose tokenizer is trained with pixel losses and a feature-alignment loss using SigLIP2. After pre-training, FeaTok is ahead of BAR on GenEval, slightly ahead on GenAI-Bench, and tied on DPG-Bench. After fine-tuning, all three benchmarks rank FeaTok above BAR, with 0.837 versus 0.817 on GenEval, 0.698 versus 0.688 on GenAI-Bench, and 83.4 versus 81.7 on DPG-Bench. The advantage of feature-loss tokens over mixed tokens thus carries over from class-conditional to text-to-image generation. We provide several T2I qualitative examples in Fig. 6.
