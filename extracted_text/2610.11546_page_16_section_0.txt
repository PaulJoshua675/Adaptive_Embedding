In this mode, the trained agent autonomously governs all key algorithmic configurations, including the DE and CMA-ES operator parameters, effective population size, resource allocation between the two operators, and restart behavior, relying entirely on its internalized policy to navigate the search landscape. As this agent-inference capability tests generalization of the orchestration policy specifically, and the non-DRL baselines possess no such learned policy, while DRL-DE and DRL-CMA-ES fix β(t) and thus retain no such orchestration mechanism, only DRL-DCO is evaluated here. The agent-inference phase represents a qualitatively distinct mode of operation from both the adaptive EA baselines and the agent-training phase itself. The inference phase relies solely on the actor network's forward pass to determine each control action, eliminating the critic evaluation and temporal-difference weight updates required during training. This yields a notably lower per-generation computational cost while retaining the full adaptive intelligence encoded in the trained policy, allowing the inference-phase DRL-DCO to independently locate the global optimum across problem instances without requiring online supervision or feedback-driven adjustment.
