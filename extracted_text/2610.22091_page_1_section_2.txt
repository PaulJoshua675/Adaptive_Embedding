Whole-slide images (WSIs) provide a comprehensive view of tissue morphology, but their gigapixel resolution makes dense region-level annotation impractical. Weakly supervised and multiscale systems address this challenge without exhaustive region annotations [1, 2]; multiple instance learning (MIL) [3] represents a WSI as patch em- beddings learned from one slide-level diagnosis. Complementary work on zero-shot subtyping uses fine-grained patch–text alignment to connect local morphology with pathology-aware descriptions, as in FG-PAN [4]. Attention pooling [5] and pathology-specific methods such as CLAM [6] identify discriminative instances, while Transformer aggregators model their dependencies [7]. SemaMIL combines semantic patch reordering with retrieval-guided state space mod- eling [8]. Despite their different aggregation mechanisms, they usually assume the observed bag label is a mutually exclusive class that fully describes the slide, an assumption that may not match multiclass pathology labels. Preserving discriminative information is also studied in graph representation learning. This mismatch arises when a heterogeneous specimen is sum- marized by its dominant finding. A pathology WSI can contain re- gions with different diagnostic significance [11, 12], yet weak su- pervision may expose only the most severe lesion. The annotation therefore records the maximum severity of a latent lesion set, rather than the set itself: a slide containing malignant and atypical tissue is labeled malignant, so standard softmax treats atypical as an in- correct bag label. Across such cases, unreported secondary lesions become structural false negatives; lower-severity labels are censored whenever they co-occur with a more severe finding, and changing the aggregator cannot remove supervision introduced by this anno- tation rule. Recent work incorporates diagnostic severity into multiclass MIL. PAMS organizes classes hierarchically, penalizes clinically costly underestimation, and mixes features across priorities [13], but still targets only the final diagnosis, so lower-severity evidence need not remain positive. Multi-instance multi-label methods predict co- existing breast lesions but assume multiple diagnoses are observed during training [11]. Learning from a single positive label relaxes that assumption by leaving other labels unknown [14], yet generic missing-label objectives neither explain why one label was selected nor link bag-level presence to localized evidence. This gap calls for explicit modeling of the dominant-label annotation mechanism. We propose DomSet-MIL, a label-aware framework that pre- dicts the dominant diagnosis while preserving evidence for hidden lesions. At the bag level, Dominant-Label Censored Likelihood (DCL) converts a malignant presence gate and a residual atypi- cal gate into three observed class probabilities via the annotation’s maximum-severity rule; a malignant label supervises malignant presence without declaring atypical absence.
