(A–C) Performance comparisons with the corresponding production baselines across sparse-, medium-, and rich-history users for TransX-Base, TransX-Retrieval, and TransX-Ranking. gAUC is the unweighted average of per-user AUC. (D–F) Held-out learning curves and training ablations. The optimized base-training recipe converges faster, while retrieval and ranking specialization benefit substantially from pretrained TransX representations compared with task-specific training from random initialization. lower panels evaluate optimization efficiency and representation transfer. For retrieval and preference ranking, we compare post-training initialized from TransX-Base with otherwise matched training from random initialization, so that the benefit of the shared behavioral representation can be separated from the task-specific objective itself. Core response prediction. TransX-Base improves offline response-prediction quality over the pro- duction HSTU baseline across all evaluated history cohorts (Figure 9A). The gains are positive across the evaluated history cohorts and are largest for rich-history users, consistent with the online cohort results reported in the next section. The optimized multi-prefix training pipeline also reaches its held-out operating point substantially faster than the straightforward training baseline (Figure 9D), complementing the systems measurements in Section 5.2. Retrieval specialization. TransX-Retrieval improves Recall@𝐾and NDCG@𝐾over the production neural retrieval baseline across the evaluated behavioral cohorts, with the smallest advantage in the sparse-history regime where little behavioral state is available. Retrieval diversity, measured by ILD@𝐾 (Intra-List Average Distance), does not decrease. More importantly, initialization from TransX-Base yields better validation quality throughout the matched GPU-hour trajectory than training the same retrieval architecture from random initialization (Figure 9E). The result provides direct evidence that the behavioral representation learned by the core response task transfers to a different matching geometry rather than being useful only to the original prediction head.
