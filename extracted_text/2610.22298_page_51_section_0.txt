AI Consilium: a Benchmark of LLM Clinical Multidisciplinary Second Opinions A PREPRINT Score Assign this score when 3 All key clinical problems and risks are identified and arranged in an appropriate clinical order. Completeness and relevance of the data used Assess whether the report uses the data required to make a reliable diagnosis or select an appropriate management strategy. Score Assign this score when 0 Key data are omitted, making the conclusion unreliable or incorrect. 1 Only part of the available data is used; several important domains are omitted, such as history, physical examination, clinical course, laboratory results, imaging, treatment, or risks. 2 Most important data are considered, but one or two meaningful omissions remain, or excessive irrelevant detail reduces focus. 3 All data affecting diagnosis, risk assessment, and management are considered; irrelevant information does not drive the conclusion. Coherence of clinical reasoning Assess whether the report contains a valid causal and inferential chain rather than a set of disconnected statements. Score Assign this score when 0 Conclusions do not follow from the data, contradict the data, or are based on fabricated facts. 1 Logic is present only in fragments; important conclusions are not explained, and causal links are superficial. 2 Reasoning is generally coherent, but there are limited logical gaps, weak mechanisms, or incomplete chronology. 3 There is a clear chain from data to syndromes or mechanisms, differential diagnosis, conclu- sion, and management. Chronology and clinical dynamics are considered. Quality of differential diagnosis Assess whether plausible alternatives are considered rather than a single preferred diagnosis. Score Assign this score when 0 Differential diagnosis is absent, irrelevant, omits an obvious dangerous alternative, or is based on fabricated data. 1 Too few alternatives are considered, probabilities are not ordered, and arguments for and against the alternatives are largely absent. 2 The main alternatives are identified, but arguments, prioritization, or verification methods are incomplete. 3 Diagnoses are relevant and prioritized; for each important alternative, there are arguments for and against it and a way to confirm or exclude it. Quality of the final clinical conclusion Assess whether the final conclusion answers the main clinical question: what is most likely occurring, how well this is supported, and what remains uncertain. Score Assign this score when 0 The conclusion is absent, clinically incorrect, contradictory, or based on fabricated clinically significant facts. 1 The conclusion is overly general, incomplete, does not answer the main question, or merely repeats a list of possible diagnoses without a clear position. 2 The main conclusion is generally correct and understandable, but requires clarification regarding probability, conditions, limitations, or links to key data. 51
