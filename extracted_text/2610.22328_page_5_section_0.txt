This creates an asymmetry between the student and teacher. While the student applies each instruction to its own previous output, the teacher evaluates the result rel- ative to the original image and the accumulated instruc- tions. Consequently, the supervision remains anchored to the clean source image, even when errors accumulate across the student’s editing turns. At the first turn, the teacher’s conditioning (I0, h1) coincides with the student’s input (I0, c1), so every rollout also supervises an ordinary single edit; we evaluate single edits separately in Section 5.3. Loss. Following VSD [48] and its adaptation to genera- tive models in DMD [56, 57], we update the student Gθ using the difference between predictions of the pretrained teacher vϕ and an auxiliary flow-matching editor vψ trained alongside the student on its generated outputs. For the stu- dent’s clean-output estimate ˆxs e, both models evaluate the same noisy version zτ(ˆxs e) under the conditioning (I0, he). We denote their velocity predictions by: ˆvγ ϕ = vγ ϕ(zτ(ˆxs e); I0, he, τ), ˆvψ = vψ(zτ(ˆxs e); I0, he, τ), where γ is the teacher’s classifier-free guidance scale. The resulting distillation gradient is: ∇θLdistill = we(τ)(ˆvγ ϕ −ˆvψ)∂ˆxs e ∂θ , (2) where the weight we(τ) accounts for the conversion from velocities to clean-output estimates and normalizes the up- date. For classifier-free guidance, the teacher’s uncondi- tional branch drops the instructions but retains I0. Follow- ing common practice [57], the auxiliary editor does not use classifier-free guidance. Training Procedure. We alternate between updating the auxiliary editor vψ and the student Gθ. For efficiency, at each training iteration, we uniformly sample a denoising exit step s and average each training loss across all editing turns e = 1, . . . , E. Specifically, we generate a new rollout of E turns for each update of either the student or the auxil- iary editor. When updating the student, we retain gradients through the clean-output estimate ˆxs e at step s of each turn. The completed images I1, . . . , IE are passed between turns in latent space, without propagating gradients. The clean output estimates ˆxs 1, . . . , ˆxs E are used to train the auxiliary editor. Specifically, we add noise to these esti- mates and optimize vψ using the flow-matching loss: LFM(ψ) = 1 E E X e=1 ∥vψ(zτ(ˆxs e); I0, he, τ) −(ϵ −ˆxs e)∥2 2 . We first warm up the auxiliary editor for five iterations without updating the student. Subsequently, we update the student once for every five auxiliary-editor updates, com- puting the distillation gradients from the retained estimates ˆxs e and averaging them over all editing turns. The teacher remains fixed throughout training, while the student and auxiliary editor are updated alternately, with one held fixed while the other is optimized. 5. Experiments 5.1. Experimental Setup Implementation details. Our student is FLUX.2-klein- 9B [25], a 4-step distilled model, which we fine-tune with a LoRA [15] of rank 16 (27.8M trainable parameters, 0.3% of the model). The teacher and the auxiliary editor are both FLUX.2-klein-base-9B, the undistilled model of the same family. The teacher is frozen and uses classifier-free guid- ance with scale 2. The auxiliary editor trains its own LoRA of the same size as the student and is not guided. The stu- dent, being distilled, runs without classifier-free guidance, with 4 sampling steps in training rollouts as at inference. Each training rollout is a chain of 10 edits, and the student’s output latent is passed directly to the next edit, without go- ing through the VAE. The noise level τ is drawn uniformly over [0, 1), independently for every turn, both for the stu- dent’s loss and for training the auxiliary editor. The student and the auxiliary editor are optimized with AdamW [30] (β = (0, 0.999), weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping at 10, bf16), with learning rates of 4 × 10−5 for the student and 8 × 10−6 for the auxiliary editor. We train for 505 iterations: 5 warm-up iterations that update only the auxiliary editor, followed by 500 iterations in which the auxiliary editor is updated at every iteration and the student at every fifth, i.e., 100 student updates. Training takes about 4.4 GPU-hours on a single NVIDIA B200. At inference we use the same 4-step unguided sampler. Training data. The inputs for the training rollouts are 96 synthetic 1024 × 1024 images generated with FLUX.2- klein-9B from short scene descriptions. Each image comes with one cumulative chain of 10 edit instructions (960 in total). All edits are local: each adds, removes, replaces or resizes an object, or changes one of its attributes, such as its color. Our evaluation benchmark, EdiVal-10 (Sec. 5.3), consists of real photographs, so no image is shared between training and evaluation. Baselines. We compare our stable editor with its pre- trained backbone model, training-free strategies for reduc- ing multi-turn drift, and a trained multi-turn editor. Our backbone model baseline is FLUX.2-klein-9B without our fine-tuning, using the same 4-step sampler as our student.
