Validity scores 211–297 items across the six backbones, compared with 301–302 for Direct. The overall rate alone cannot distinguish changes in class composition from changes in the scoring process. Matching inputs changes the ranking comparison. On Qwen Orig- inal, Validity’s AUC of 0.895 uses 255 items, whereas Direct’s 0.875 uses all 302. Restricting Direct to the same 255 inputs raises its AUC to 0.886. The apparent gap narrows from 0.020 to 0.009 with neither score vector changed. Table 4 extends this comparison to six backbones. The other four intervals include zero. Matched comparisons expose backbone- specific ranking differences instead of conflating discrimination with which inputs reached scoring. Cross-backbone comparisons show the same population sen- sitivity. Original Validity’s six-way scored intersection contains 162 items. Across 135 cross-model pairwise contrasts over meth- ods and cohorts, 15 ordering signs differ between own-population and common-population AUC. These are descriptive changes in ordering, not evidence of a general ranking reversal.
