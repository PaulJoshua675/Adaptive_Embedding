Effectiveness of Preference Optimization. All RL methods are initialized from CoCo-VAE. As shown in Table 1, existing methods often struggle to improve preference ranking while maintaining retrieval capability. DPO-based methods underperform the SFT baseline, mainly because shared SID prefixes between chosen and rejected items lead to conflicting gradients and weaken learning signals. Moreover, DPO may suffer from reward hacking, increasing preference margins by lowering rejected-item probabilities rather than increasing those of chosen items. For on-policy RL, Vanilla GRPO underperforms SFT across all metrics, as sparse outcome rewards leave many training samples without effective learning signals. TPMA-GRPO improves credit assignment through prefix- matching rewards that do not directly reflect user preferences, while HCGRec uses prefix hints to recover learning signals but may introduce a training-inference mismatch. In contrast, DHPO progressively expands both reward evaluation and policy optimiza- tion horizons, reducing learning difficulty while providing reward signals at different levels of the SID hierarchy to guide preference optimization. Compared with TPMA-GRPO, DHPO achieves rel- ative gains of 2.18% and 2.03% in HR@6, and 2.66% and 1.45% in MRR@6 on Click and Order, respectively, demonstrating more ef- fective preference alignment while preserving retrieval capability. 4.3 Ablation Study In this section, we conduct ablation studies to evaluate the key components of CoCo-VAE and DHPO. Ablation of CoCo-VAE. To verify the effectiveness of each com- ponent in CoCo-VAE, we conduct ablation studies on the Click and Order datasets, as shown in Table 2. We consider four ablation vari- ants: i) w/o q2i removes query-to-item collaborative supervision; ii) w/o i2i removes item-to-item collaborative supervision; iii) w/o fid. removes the residual fidelity loss; and iv) w/o coll. removes the anti-collapse regularization. The results demonstrate the comple- mentary roles of these components. Removing q2i reduces HR@6 from 0.2759 to 0.2650 on Click and from 0.4103 to 0.3933 on Order, highlighting the importance of query-item co-occurrence in SID construction. Similarly, removing i2i reduces HR@6 to 0.2704 and 0.4042 on Click and Order, respectively, indicating the contribu- tion of item-level collaborative relationships. Moreover, the per- formance degradation caused by removing residual fidelity—with HR@6 decreasing to 0.2437 and 0.3944 on Click and Order, respec- tively—or anti-collapse regularization—with HR@6 decreasing to 0.2492 and 0.3987—highlights their respective roles in preserving semantic information and promoting effective codebook utilization. Together, these components enable CoCo-VAE to learn informative SID representations for generative retrieval. Ablation of DHPO. All results in Table 4 are reported as relative changes over the control group and are statistically significant (𝑝< 0.05). We evaluate three de- ployment configurations that differ in the extent to which WeGR replaces candidates from conventional retrieval channels. V1.0 (Additive Retrieval) introduces WeGR as an additional retrieval channel while retaining all candidates from the other retrievers. V1.1 (60% Candidate Removal) and V1.2 (80% Candidate Re- moval) remove 60% and 80% of the candidates contributed by the other retrieval channels, respectively. These configurations allow us to examine whether the business gains persist or improve as WeGR progressively replaces conventional retrieval candidates.
