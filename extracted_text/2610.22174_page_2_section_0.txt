To address this gap, we introduce a new setting termed Multi-Session Agentic Reinforcement Learning, which supports an arbitrary count of sessions and enables agents with diverse inference mechanisms to be optimized within a unified algorithm. A straightforward way to handle multi-session rollouts is through broadcasting, whereby a trajectory-level outcome reward or advantage is replicated to every token across all sessions (Accio-Lab, 2026; Xu et al., 2026a). However, we argue that this method provides only coarse supervision and offers no explicit control over optimization across sessions, resulting in poorly targeted learning signals as trajectories span more sessions. In this work, we propose Mosaic, a multi-session agentic RL method with critic-free hierarchical optimization. First, session-aware trajectory-level advantage estimation calibrates outcome-based advantages using within-group rankings of session counts conditioned on task outcomes, encouraging successful trajectories to solve tasks efficiently while allowing unsuccessful trajectories sufficient exploration. Its bounded modulation preserves outcome-induced ordering while incorporating session- usage guidance, thereby widening relative advantage margins for homogeneous-reward groups. Second, session-level advantage shaping calculates each session’s marginal outcome gain as an immediate reward, applies group-relative normalization separately by session type, and combines the resulting shaping signal with the trajectory-level advantage. Overall, the former provides a long-term, outcome-oriented global signal, while the latter captures short-term local marginal effects. They together form a hierarchical optimization target for multi-session agentic RL. To evaluate our method, we conduct experiments on BrowseComp (Wei et al., 2025) and select Qwen3.5-4B (Qwen Team, 2026) as the base model. Our Mosaic outperforms multiple single-session and multi-session agentic RL baselines under both context compaction and subagent forking scenarios. Moreover, Mosaic achieves up to a 10.1% relative improvement in accuracy over the broadcasting baseline while using fewer sessions per trajectory on average (3.17 →2.35). Ablation studies further demonstrate that session-aware trajectory-level advantage estimation and session-level advantage shaping each contribute to the final performance gains. Our analyses also reveal several key insights: (1) shaping trajectory-level advantages with session counts increases within-group advantage variance and improves sample efficiency; (2) different session types exhibit distinct distributions of session-level marginal gains, which our type-conditioned normalization helps stabilize; (3) effective multi-session optimization improves session utility and allocation, a pattern that Mosaic strengthens and that might offer a potential direction for future work. 2.
