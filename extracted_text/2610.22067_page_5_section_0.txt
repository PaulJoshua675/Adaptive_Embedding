To identify quality issues not captured by automated filters, two authors manually inspect five randomly sampled instances from each remaining source. The review checked whether (i) reference answers were non-empty and complete, (ii) answer for- mats satisfied the question’s requirements, and (iii) questions were unambiguous and answerable from the supplied context. We exclude a source if any inspected instance fails these checks. This small-sample review serves as a source-level screening step rather than an exhaustive validation of the retained instances. 4.2 EVIDENCE ANNOTATION For each question, we identify supporting evidence within its source context to measure scope and dispersion and construct reasoning graphs. We use benchmark-provided reference evidence where available. Otherwise, we retrieve candidate passages, expand them to their containing paragraphs or full tables, and merge overlapping or adjacent intervals. A verifier then answers the question using only the expanded evidence, without access to the reference answer; We sample three answers from the verifier for each question and retain the question only if all three meet the task-specific cor- rectness criterion when compared with the reference answer. This repeated check reduces reliance on a single successful response. Scope and dispersion therefore describe the expanded supporting context, not necessarily a minimal evidence set (more details in Appendix D). 4.3 REASONING GRAPH GENERATION After evidence retrieval and sufficiency validation, we use two separate annotators, Gemini 3.7 Flash and GPT-5.6 Terra, to construct answer-conditioned reasoning graphs. Each receives the question, reference answer, and numbered evidence statements and reconstructs a minimal evidence-to-answer derivation. We validate each graph by removing malformed or duplicate steps, invalid references, and cycle-forming edges, then prune steps that do not contribute to the answer; unparseable or empty graphs are invalid. Across all 6,550 instances, the two annotators assign identical reasoning depths in 65.6% of cases, with a Spearman rank correlation of 0.48 between their depth estimates. Two human auditors compare the two graphs on 40 instances. Neither auditor prefers GPT graphs more often than Gemini graphs, and one prefers Gemini on 14 of the 20 instances where the depths differ. We therefore compute the graph-derived properties using only Gemini generations. Details of annotator agreement, the audit procedure, and audit results are provided in the Appendix E.
