The prediction head estimates the likelihood of the clean bits conditioned on eBj, the masking ratio rj, and the autoregressive context Cj. The mask positions are already encoded by the mask symbols in eBj. During teacher forcing, Cglobal = c and Ci = (zglobal, z<i, c) for patch i. Let S be a set of token positions, either the global token Sglobal = {global} or the patch tokens Spatch = {1, . . . , N}. For a training batch of M sequences, indexed by b, the masked-bit loss over S is the weighted cross-entropy over all bits of the tokens in S: ℓMBM(S) = − M X b=1 X j∈S d X ℓ=1 w(b) jℓlog pϕ,ℓ  B(b) jℓ| eB(b) j , r(b) j , C(b) j  M X b=1 X j∈S d X ℓ=1 w(b) jℓ , (5) where pϕ,ℓis the head’s two-class prediction at bit position ℓand wjℓ= mjℓ+ η(1 −mjℓ) weights masked bits by 1 and visible bits by η = 0.1. The complete objective combines the global and patch losses: LMBM gen = λglobal ℓMBM(Sglobal) + λpatch ℓMBM(Spatch), (6) with λpatch = 1−λglobal, averaged over training batches and over the sampled masks. Each term −log pϕ,ℓ(Bjℓ| ·) is the cross-entropy between the observed bit and the head’s two-class prediction, so Eq. 6 specifies the token prediction loss ℓCE in Eq. 4 for our masked bit modeling head. Because the normalization pools the bits of all tokens in S, a token with more masked bits contributes more, so the patch loss is a bit-weighted average over patch positions rather than exactly the uniform average 1 N PN i=1 of Eq. 4. The bit prediction task additionally conditions on the visible bits within the current token, making it a denoising cross-entropy objective rather than an exact evaluation of the complete token’s autoregressive negative log-likelihood. FeaTok retains the BAR generator backbone and masked-bit prediction mechanism while changing the tokenizer that supplies its training codes. For global-augmented generation, we additionally prepend a sampled global token to the patch token sequence. This global token is image-dependent and thus distinct from the external class label or text condition c. BAR-B and BAR-L denote the base and large generator configurations. In our experiments for ImageNet-1k at 256 × 256, each patch token contains 16 bits, corresponding to 216 possible tokens.
