For each question x ∈DA, we maintain an archive Ax of verified solutions, organized into cells by solution approach. Each cell stores at most one solution, or elite, allowing the archive to represent multiple correct approaches to the same question. Defining the behavior space. Classical MAP- Elites relies on behavior descriptors designed by hand for each domain. Instead, we first collect a set of verified seed solutions from the base model and provide them to a separate LLM, the labeler, as representative examples of how the task can be solved. Using these examples, the labeler induces a broader vocabulary of possible approaches, in- cluding ones not observed in the seeds, to guide subsequent exploration. The labeler assigns each solution an approach label, capturing its general method of solving the question. To distinguish solu- tions that follow the same broad approach but differ in their structure, we combine this label with struc- tural features extracted deterministically from the solution (e.g., joins or subqueries in SQL, loops or recursion in code). The resulting behavior descrip- tor b(y) determines the solution’s archive cell. This descriptor is used only to guide search; we evaluate diversity with a separate, deterministic notion of strategy (§3). Appendix A.2 details the behavior space, and Appendices B.1 and B.2 give the fea- tures and labeler prompts used in our text-to-SQL experiments. Constructing the archive. Given this behavior space, we populate and expand the per-question archives through four steps. (1) Initialization. We insert the verified seed so- lutions into their corresponding archive cells, establishing the initial set of elites for each question. (2) Selection. We choose a target approach among those the labeler judges applicable to the ques- tion, prioritizing approaches not yet repre- sented in the archive while balancing attempts across targets (Appendix A.3). We then se- lect a parent elite, preferably one following a different approach. (3) Variation. We prompt π0 with the original question, target approach, and parent elite, ask- ing it to generate a new solution following the target approach. (4) Evaluation and insertion. We verify the can- didate and, if correct, assign it to the cell cor- responding to its actual approach, which may differ from the requested one. The candidate fills an empty cell or replaces an existing elite if it is shorter. We repeat selection, variation, and evaluation for a fixed search budget. Because selection favors untried approaches, search is directed toward ap- proaches that may be correct but rarely sampled by the base model, while candidates repeating an existing approach only compete for an occupied cell. Search more than doubles the cells occupied by seed sampling and yields more distinct elites than direct sampling (Appendices C.1 and C.2). The surviving elites, paired with their original task prompts, form the replay set.
