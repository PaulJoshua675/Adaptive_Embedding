We be- gin with supervised initialization, training the pol- icy on reasoning traces that connect quoted conver- sational evidence to task predictions, as shown in Figure 2. We then optimize the initialized policy with GRPO using task correctness and Grounded Information Utilization (IUG), following the train- ing process illustrated in Figure 3. This reward values new, evidence-supported inferences while accounting for the reasoning effort used to estab- lish them. Training parameters are provided in Ap- pendix A. 3.1 Setting and Supervised Initialization Let x = (C, u, d) contain a conversation C, pre- diction target u, and task definitions and inputs d, including a supplied culture identifier or dialog act where applicable. Turn-level contexts end at u; power prediction targets the exchange. The pol- icy πθ generates a completion y = (r, ˆY ), where r = (r1, . . . , rN) is a sequence of reasoning blocks and ˆY is the predicted label set. Blocks are sepa- rated by blank lines. Quoted source spans appear within <evidence> and </evidence> tags. The final answer follows </think>; the reference label set is Y ⋆. For supervised initialization, we refine model- sampled reasoning traces using a strong model guided by human-written refinement examples, preserving as much of the original reasoning as possible. We add <evidence> and </evidence> as two special tokens, initializing each token’s in- put and output embedding rows from the mean of its tag’s constituent subword rows. Grounded Information Utilization (IUG) IUG combines the assessment of new, evidence- supported inferences with their reasoning cost. During training, Qwen3.6-27B serves as the IUG judge. For each block, it identifies whether the block establishes a new, task-relevant inference supported by quoted evidence, relative to all sup- ported inferences established earlier. Relevant con- tributions include interpreting a relationship, iden- tifying an active expectation, resolving a plausi- ble alternative, or correcting an earlier inference. Quotation alone, repetition, and unsupported label elimination receive no credit for a new inference. Figure 3 provides a working example: identifying the speakers’ relationship and interpreting the re- quest each add information, while restating the re- lationship earns no further credit.
