Baselines. An explanation can match a wrong answer by coincidence, and the two baselines mea- sure how often. The shuffled baseline takes the wrong answers one fine-tuned model gave and hands them out again among its errors at random. In expectation, each error receives each of those answers, its own included, with equal probability. An answer equal to the error’s correct answer is skipped, since it would not be an error. This keeps the model’s preference for some wrong labels and breaks only their link to the subject. The chance baseline instead gives each error a uniformly random wrong label: any label of its new relation except the correct one. Both baselines are computed exactly as expectations, not by sampling. The most frequent answer is the one explanation the shuffle cannot test. It points at the same answer for every held-out subject of a fine-tuned model, so the number of wrong answers that equal it does not depend on which subject gave which answer. Shuffling leaves that number almost unchanged. Its shuffled share even rises, to 6.1% from 5.0% observed, because the other explanations match less often after the shuffle and leave it a larger part of the credit. Against chance (1.9%), the most frequent answer does account for more errors than coincidence. Intervals are 95% percentile intervals over bootstrap resamples of the 757 fine-tuned models. H CONFIDENCE INTERVALS Every interval is a 95% percentile bootstrap interval: we draw 10,000 resamples with replacement, recompute the statistic on each, and take the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. The resampled unit is what the statistic is computed over. Correlations across relations. For the correlations between Pre-linearity, Recall and Generaliza- tion (Tables 1, 2, 4 and 5, Figures 3a and 3b), the unit is the relation: each model’s coefficients are recomputed on each resample of its relations. A partial correlation is recomputed from scratch on each resample, with its regressions on the controls refit and, for ρ, the ranks retaken. All coefficients of one model in a table or figure share the same resamples, and the label-set comparison applies each resample to every label set. In Tables 4 and 5, one resample serves every model, and the median and mean over models are recomputed from the models’ resampled coefficients. Error shares. For the Shapley shares and their baselines (Figure 8), the unit is the fine-tuned model: each resample draws the 757 fine-tuned models with replacement and pools their errors. A model’s own interval resamples only its fine-tuned models. ±. Tables print an interval as ±h, where h is the larger distance from the estimate to an end of the interval, rounded to the nearest value.
