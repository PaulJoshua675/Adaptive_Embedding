In this exploratory study, we investigated what types of content are associated with two structural outcomes in logistics-related discussions on Yahoo! Chiebukuro: best-answer selection and sustained engagement. By examining both individual nouns and semantic clusters across a keyword-defined corpus, we show that these outcomes have distinct content profiles rather than reflecting a single dimension of content value. Method­ ologically, we modeled best-answer selection within question threads and decomposed engagement into two stages: whether a question receives any response and, conditional on receiving one, how many responses it attracts. This design separates answerability from subsequent discussion volume. Controlling for answer length and question-answer noun overlap was also important, as both are strongly associated with best-answer selec­ tion and could otherwise obscure content-level associations. Substantively, the results indicate that content associated with successfully selected answers generally differs from content associated with questions that attract responses. Thus, “answerability” and “dis­ cussability” appear not to lie at opposite ends of a single axis but to represent distinct dimensions of content-outcome association. These findings are conditional associations observed within a single keyword-defined corpus on a single platform, rather than causal relationships. Future research could extend this framework by incorporating social-lin­ guistic features such as sentiment, writing style, and register, and by linking engagement patterns to external logistics-related events through time-series analysis. Such exten­ sions could clarify whether the observed content-outcome profiles generalize beyond topical nouns and how external events shape engagement.
