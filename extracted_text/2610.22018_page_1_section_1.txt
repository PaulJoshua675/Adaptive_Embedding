Arabic calligraphy is a high-stakes cultural visual domain for Muslim communi- ties, yet VLM evaluations rarely test its fine-grained script traditions. We audit 12 VLMs on a cleaned 1,195-image six-way benchmark using numeric, visual-cue, and Diwani-first prompts. In this closed-set setting, the best numeric-prompt ac- curacy is 60.9% for closed/API models and 47.2% for open-weight models. The main failure is default-label collapse: every numeric run is dominated by a sin- gle label taking 33–63% of predictions, against an 18.8% majority-class baseline. For 8 of 12 models the default is Thuluth; GPT-4o labels 52.9% of all images as Thuluth although it is only 18.1% of the set. Prompting helps unevenly: visual cues move accuracy by more than 10 points for some models but hurt others, and foregrounding Diwani often relocates the default rather than removing it. Ara- bic calligraphy recognition is therefore a compact, culturally grounded stress test for VLM robustness and for measuring which visual traditions become legible to automated systems. 1
