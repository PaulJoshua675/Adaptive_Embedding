Our benchmark shows that recent probabilistic models can outperform numerical forecasts on cyclone location and surrounding fields, while still exhibiting intensity biases and calibration errors. The presence of systematically weak storms alone is therefore insufficient to conclude a general failure in cyclone representation. FGN’s wind–pressure dependence structure, comparable to that of GenCast, despite being trained solely on conditional marginals, further shows that meaningful joint structure can emerge implicitly, without being explicitly enforced by the training objective. These findings suggest more targeted routes to improvement. Comparing model outputs against observations and higher-resolution analyses could help disentangle errors inherited from training targets from limitations of the models themselves. Potential remedies such as intensity correction, loss functions that emphasize extreme winds, and improved uncertainty quantification should be evaluated not only for their effect on intensity, but also for whether they preserve spatial skill and wind–pressure dependence. Finally, comparing marginal and joint training objectives under matched architectures and compute budgets could clarify the extent to which the recovered dependence structure arises from the training objective rather than from the model’s inherent structural constraints. 9
