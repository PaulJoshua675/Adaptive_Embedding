XViewRank 15 5 Discussion What is new in XViewRank? The individual ingredients of XViewRank, namely BM25 scores, Jaccard overlap, and a small MLP, are established. The contri- bution lies in their use as a matched-view conditional fusion formulation for LLM-generated sparse retrieval. Unlike concatenation, XViewRank preserves each score together with the query view that produced it. Unlike fixed RRF or CombSUM, its nonlinear scorer lets query-level agreement change how view- specific score evidence is interpreted. The matched-input protocol is essential because it separates the effect of fusion from the quality or number of generated views. When does overlap help? The repeated perturbation and gradient analyses in- dicate that overlap acts as coarse context rather than direct relevance evidence. Agreement-stratified and continuous analyses further show that the improve- ment is concentrated in low-agreement queries on DL19, where the views ex- pose heterogeneous candidate rankings; DL20 follows the same broad direction but does not provide significant continuous or permutation evidence. This ob- servation does not imply that arbitrary diversity is beneficial. Low agreement can arise from useful complementary formulations or from harmful query drift; XViewRank only receives retrieval-side evidence and cannot fully distinguish these causes. Deployment scope. The LLM generation cost is shared with the LLM-expansion baselines and can be amortized when views are cached. After the three sparse runs are available, XViewRank adds only a six-feature MLP evaluation per pooled candidate and does not require joint query and passage text encoding. Neural rerankers remain complementary when additional latency is acceptable, as illustrated in Appendix B. Accordingly, the main comparison in this work is against fixed and learned retrieval-side fusion under identical views, while the cross-encoder experiment serves only to locate XViewRank within a multi-stage pipeline. 6 Limitations The study has several boundaries. First, DL19 and DL20 contain relatively few deeply judged topics, which limits the power of query-level significance tests. Only the DL19 MAP improvement over matched-view RRF is significant in the full-split comparison, and the DL20 gains should be interpreted as numerical rather than statistically established. The repeated permutation and continuous correlation analyses likewise support the overlap mechanism on DL19 but not on DL20. Second, the main controlled experiments use query views from one LLM configuration. Caching makes fusion comparisons fair, but does not establish robustness to a different generator or prompt distribution.
