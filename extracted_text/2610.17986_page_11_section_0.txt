A DERIVATION OF MAP UNDER DISTRACTORS Fix a query q with nq positives sorted by similarity, sp1 ≥· · · ≥spnq , so pj is the j-th best positive. Adding N i.i.d. distractors, the number scoring above pj is Kj ∼Binomial(N, xq,j) with per- positive hardness xq,j = 1 −Fq(spj) (Thm. 1 applied at threshold spj). The rank of pj in the full list is the count of items at least as similar—the j positives p1, . . . , pj plus the Kj distractors above it: rankj = j + Kj, E[rankj] = j + N xq,j. (3) at the realized fraction ¯xq,j = Kj/N gives rankj = j + Kj and reproduces this gallery’s mAP@k exactly (no expectation, no Taylor remainder; App. C). Feed- ing instead the population hardness xq,j—or its sample estimate ˆxq,j—predicts the metric, with the population→realized concentration error analyzed in App. C. We confirm the identity numer- ically by evaluating rankj = j + Kj with the observed Kj—counting each positive’s distractor exceedances in one streamed pass—and find the analytic form matches the measured mAP (Tab. 1). The protocol substitutes the sampled hardness estimate ˆxq,j (count or tail-aware) for xq,j and reports the tail-aware estimate with its bracket. Unlike the top-rank survival’s exponential (1 −xq)N, the per-positive precision j/(j + N ˆxq,j) is a gently varying rational response that damps errors in ˆxq,j: the tail estimate’s over-shoot becomes negligible, whereas the count estimate stays structurally over- optimistic (it cannot report hardness below the sample floor 1/S).
