A bound needs both a statically scheduled accelerator whose cycle count is fixed [36, 41] and a model with input-invariant work. This is indepen- dent of accuracy: recent occupancy models differ by about 25× in determinism, with an input-dependent sparse model at 27.5% coefficient of variation (CV) and a fixed-query sparse model at 1.1%. This paper builds such a front end on the AMD Kria KR260, an embedded FPGA at 4.3 W idle and up to 6.6 W under inference. We choose FlashOcc [51] over sparse SOTA because it is dense, fixed-compute, and DPU- compilable (Sec. 4.1). Two stages the DPU cannot run, the lift-splat view transform [16, 34] and BEV-neck bilin- ear resize, become custom HLS IPs: with a fixed cam- era rig their indices and weights are static constants, so both are fixed-trip-count operators whose compute WCET is a cycle count. Accuracy survives a DPU-compatible INT8 recipe with calibration-time activation clipping and no QAT [2, 3, 53]: on full Occ3D-nuScenes [7, 40] valida- tion the board reaches mIoU 29.88, 1.55 below the INT8 reference matched to its deployed configuration and 92.8% of an FP32 baseline the DPU cannot run (Sec. 4.3). Only PL compute (11.6 ms per frame) is bounded analyt- ically; DPU stages, DDR streaming, and the host path are measured envelopes, and the watchdog guarantees that an output, possibly stale, is published within the deadline plus a measured envelope. The 100 ms deadline runs use a fixed- work proxy of six ResNet-50 DPU inferences per frame, while the full pipeline composes to about 1.3 s (Tab. 2). Our contribution is threefold: 1. An evaluation axis for safety-critical occupancy. Latency-determinability: input-dependent computation, not sparsity, drives tails, and we decompose non- determinism into architecture, platform sharing, and host path (Sec. 4.1). 2. Two fixed-trip-count PL IPs that close the DPU gaps. Lift-splat gather and BEV-neck bilinear resize as cus- tom HLS IPs with cycle-bounded, input-invariant com- pute, numerically matched to their software references (Sec. 5). 3. A quantized board deployment and a measured, en- forced deadline. Every stage deployed and measured on the board (Tabs. 2 and 3); deadline behavior character- ized at 100k-frame scale on the fixed-work proxy against GPU and Orin (Fig. 5); delivery enforced, with its enve- lope and its fallback cost measured.
