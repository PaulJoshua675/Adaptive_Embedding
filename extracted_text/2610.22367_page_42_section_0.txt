After making this replacement, the circuit uses different factor orders for the links and for W. Each link is recoded as g = ωpCqErV mXu o , (84) whereas the frequently updated product is stored in the opposite order, W = ωP V MXυ o CQER, (85) with p, q, r, P, Q, R ∈Z3, m, M ∈Z4, and u, υ ∈Z2. This places the incoming and accumulated C, E factors next to one another in Wg, so they can be combined directly before a few fixed permutations account for the remaining factors. One complication remains: reordering the factors would ordinarily introduce recurring corrections to the coordinate P. The circuit stores eP = P + QR (mod 3), folding those corrections into the coordinate itself rather than recomputing them during every multiplication. The 192 links are recoded once before the first loop and restored after the last. Each recoded link remains in a compact nine-qubit encoding; storing every link one-hot would cost three additional qubits per link. In contrast, there is only one running product, so we can afford to store its three-valued coordinates eP, Q, and R one-hot (three qubits, exactly one set), increasing the size of W from nine to twelve qubits. Adding one modulo three then becomes a cyclic permutation of three qubits, while negation becomes a single swap. Controlled additions and the fixed transformations above can therefore use controlled qubit permutations rather than arithmetic on the two-qubit encoding. Together, these representation choices reduce each update of W from 146 Toffolis in the baseline’s general multiplication circuit to 35 Toffolis. Multiplying by an inverse link g−1 costs the same, so the circuit needs no separate link inversion. Algorithm S1 gives the complete coordinate rules for both representations. Reusing loop products. The baseline constructs each loop product from the identity and then reverses the construction. Neighboring loops, however, often share a sequence of factors. The discovered circuit therefore retains W after one trace evaluation and edits it into the product needed for the next, reusing the shared factors instead of rebuilding them. More loops share factors than first appears, because the real trace is invariant under cyclic rotation and inversion: f(h1h2 · · · hn) = f(h2 · · · hnh1) = f((h1h2 · · · hn)−1). (86) The first equality is cyclicity of the matrix trace; the second follows because these matrices are unitary and f takes the real part. For example, to change abcde into abcxy, the circuit right-multiplies by e−1 and d−1 to cancel the old suffix, then appends x and y, retaining the common prefix abc. Inverting W, which costs 21 Toffolis, makes the other end of the product available for the same operation. Cyclic rotations and inversion also give several equivalent ways to write each loop, which we call its representatives, all with the same value of f. As Figure S5(a) illustrates, a good choice can expose a longer segment shared with the next loop. A classical dynamic program chooses the representatives and the order in which the loops are evaluated, minimizing the cost of transforming W between consecutive representatives. The chosen schedule uses 3,136 link multiplications and 480 inversions of W for the 576 loops, about 5.4 multiplications per loop. By comparison, building and reversing each loop separately takes 6 multiplications for a plaquette and 10 for a rectangle. Reading the trace directly. The baseline in Figure S5(b) uses W as the address of a 216-entry QROM and loads a full 51-bit addend, although f(W) has only seven possible values. The discovered circuit instead computes which of the seven values occurs. In the specialized representation, a small Boolean circuit acting directly on ( eP, M, υ, Q, R) computes six flag qubits, one per nonzero trace value, of which at most one is set (Figure S5(c)). Each flag controls the addition of the corresponding constant a(L)v to k, where v is the flagged trace value, so the circuit never stores a 51-bit lookup result. Computing the six flags costs 20 Toffolis. Readout, addition, and cleanup cost 76 Toffolis for a plaquette and 75 for a rectangle, compared with 478 for the baseline QROM lookup, addition, and unlookup. We test the complete circuit on 100,000 random inputs and each group operation on all of its inputs. The discovered circuit uses 170,000 Toffolis and 1,859 qubits, for a TQ-cost of 3.2 × 108. Relative to the baseline, this reduces the Toffoli count by 7.4×, the qubit count by 3.5%, and the TQ-cost by 7.7×. Updates of W account for 110,000 of the Toffolis, trace readout and addition for 43,000, inversions of W for 10,000, and link recoding for 8,832.
