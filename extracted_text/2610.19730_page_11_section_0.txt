We found that DeNN had again a better ratio than the ANN. 5 Discussion The idea that synaptic delays constitute a genuine computational resource was formalized in (Maass, 1997b). In this paper describing spiking neurons as computationally more powerful than sigmoidal units, the authors argued that precise spike timing, and by extension the delays that shape when a presynaptic spike arrives at its target, expands the space of functions a network can realize beyond what is reachable with weights alone. They showed that a single spiking neuron equipped with programmable axonal delays can act as a coincidence detector across multiple input pathways, allowing it to compute Boolean functions that would otherwise require several threshold gates in a rate- based formalism. More recently, (Habashy et al., 2024) showed that synaptic delays were necessary to solve certain tasks, as opposed to synaptic weights. Delays thus introduce an orthogonal, purely temporal axis of computation, complementary to synaptic efficacy, that a network can exploit for tasks requiring fine-grained timing discrimination. Despite this early theoretical motivation, translating delay-based computation into trainable systems proved diffi- cult, and the field pursued several distinct historical strategies before delays returned to focus.
