Mean Jaccard similarity between shortest paths obtained under different con- figurations: (a) unweighted, (b) weighted, and (c) weighted versus unweighted paths. Fig. 3(a) compares unweighted paths, with mean similarities from 0.408 to 0.957 and the highest between the no-teleport, ego-only configurations with and without LIFO-cutoff (0.957). Adding teleport edges changes unweighted paths more as the inter-transaction network grows more restrictive: similarity with vs. without teleportation is 0.896 (all accounts, all edges), 0.755 (ego-only, all edges), 0.694 (all accounts, LIFO-cutoff), and 0.408 (ego-only, LIFO-cutoff). Weighted paths are markedly more stable (Fig. 3(b)), with mean similarities from 0.671 to nearly 1.000. Paths with and without teleportation are nearly identical under matching inter-transaction conditions (0.992–1.000), and the no- teleport, ego-only, LIFO-cutoff configuration is highly similar to all four teleport configurations (0.984–1.000), indicating the high teleportation penalty keeps the weighted search from using teleport edges when cheaper alternatives exist. Comparing weighted against unweighted paths (Fig. 3(c)), the unweighted no-teleport, ego-only, LIFO-cutoff paths closely match the weighted paths under every teleport configuration (0.954–0.970), suggesting weighting steers the search toward the ego-only, LIFO-cutoff path structure while retaining teleport edges’ added reachability. Along the diagonal, where the graph configuration is held constant, weighted vs. unweighted similarity is highest without teleportation under ego-only edges (0.888 all edges, 0.970 LIFO-cutoff) but much lower under teleport configurations (0.658–0.762), confirming that weighting changes paths that would otherwise rely on teleport shortcuts. 4.5 Frequent Shortest Paths Finally, using the teleport-enabled, all-accounts, all-edges configuration, we an- alyze frequent token sequence patterns in weighted shortest paths to understand how borrowed assets are transformed before repayment.
