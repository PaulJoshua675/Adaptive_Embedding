Deep learning has been widely applied across various fields [1–6]. Neural architecture design is central to task performance and deployment efficiency, and Neural Architecture Search (NAS) automates this process by searching a candidate architecture space for models that meet performance and deployment constraints [7–12]. However, evaluating candidates during search is expensive: accuracy typically requires full network training, while latency requires hardware measurement or profiling. A practical way to reduce this cost is to train predictors under a limited evaluation budget, where only a small subset of candidate architectures is evaluated and the remaining unseen candidates are estimated by the predictor [13–16]. ∗This is a preprint of a paper accepted at NeurIPS 2026.
