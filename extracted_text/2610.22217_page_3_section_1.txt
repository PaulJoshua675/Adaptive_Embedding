In this section, we present Skill-Decoupled On-Policy Expert Distillation (SCOPED), a frame- work for acquiring and transferring a diverse set of complementary visual understanding capabili- ties. As illustrated in Fig. 1, SCOPED consists of two stages. First, we independently train MLLMs on different capabilities with capability-specific reward signals, allowing each model to develop ex- pertise specialized to its target visual understanding capability (Sec. 3.1). Second, to bypass the instability of joint optimization, we abstract reward-evaluated expert trajectories into explicit skills, introducing a structural information asymmetry during on-policy distillation (Sec. 3.2). Serving as privileged directional anchors, these skills regularize expert supervision, compelling the student to internalize this guidance for conflict-free consolidation. 3.1 LEARNING MLLM EXPERTISE WITH CAPABILITY-SPECIFIC REINFORCEMENT While jointly learning diverse capabilities is essential for general visual understanding, optimizing a shared policy over heterogeneous data can introduce conflicting optimization signals and distract the learning process (Yu et al., 2020; Gao et al., 2026). As a result, improvements in one capability may interfere with the acquisition of another. To mitigate such interference, SCOPED decouples capability learning through independent reinforcement learning, producing specialized capability experts together with reward-evaluated trajectories that capture their acquired behaviors. Eliciting Capability-Specific Expertise. To elicit the visual understanding capabilities of MLLMs, we partition the training corpus into C capability-specific subsets {Bc}C c=1, where each instance consists of a multimodal input x and its ground-truth answer a. For each capability c, an expert policy is initialized from the shared base policy πθ and optimized independently on Bc. This decoupled optimization allows each expert to specialize in its designated capability without being directly affected by competing optimization objectives from other capabilities. For an instance (x, a) ∈Bc, the corresponding capability expert samples a rollout trajectory: τ ∼πθ(· | x), (1) where τ denotes the generated trajectory. Each trajectory is evaluated against the target answer a using a capability-specific reward function rc. Guided by these reward signals, the capability expert πθc is obtained by maximizing the expected reward over the subset Bc: θc = arg max θ E(x,a)∼Bc, τ∼πθ(·|x) [rc(a, τ)] . (2) The specific design of rc is described in Appendix A.3. Collecting Capability-Specific Experiences. After capability-specific reinforcement learning, we collect a diverse set of trajectories from each trained capability expert πθc. Specifically, for each instance (x, a) ∈Bc, we sample K distinct rollouts: τ1, . . . , τK ∼πθc(· | x). (3) These trajectories provide concrete behavioral traces of the strategies acquired through capability- specific reinforcement, including recurring solution patterns and capability-relevant actions (Guo et al., 2025; Xiong et al., 2026). To properly contextualize the expert’s behavioral process, we associate each sampled trajectory τk, where k ∈{1, . . . , K}, with its input context and target answer to form a trajectory tuple (x, a, τk). 3
