Each synthesized question is walked through from the input image, with min- imal wording fixes to ensure solvability and an- swer uniqueness. We check chain consistency, evi- dence provenance, image–text alignment, and ac- tual use of the designated Blur and Visualize evi- dence. These checks approximate Equation 1 and help ensure that the added requirements in Equa- tion 8 are non-trivial. Difficulty filtering and trajectory selection. We discard questions answered correctly by a direct- answer probe without tools. An expert model then performs k tool-augmented rollouts per ques- tion using the training search-and-vision interface. The probe removes residual parametric shortcuts that refinement alone cannot rule out, while tool- augmented rollouts test whether the questions are learnable through external evidence acquisition. The expert’s empirical Pass@k measures agent dif- ficulty, complementing the chain-based evidence burden DRefined. We retain successful trajectories as SFT demonstrations, sampling across Pass@k levels to balance difficulty and prevent easy ques- tions from dominating supervision. These esti- mates also guide the SFT/RL split described in Section 3.4. 3.4 Agentic Training Supervised initialization. Using the difficulty estimates in Section 3.3, we partition the curated training questions into an SFT subset and a harder RL subset. A larger model is fine-tuned on suc- cessful trajectories from both subsets and frozen as teacher πT. The student πθ is initialized by SFT on the first subset and jointly optimized with RL and OPD on the second. Joint RL and OPD for long-horizon search. Useful evidence collection can end in redundant verification, premature stopping, or an unsupported answer. Final-answer scores provide limited feed- back on these intermediate decisions, motivating guidance along the search process. We combine outcome-based policy optimization with token- level teacher guidance on the student’s own tra- jectories, supplementing the shared scalar feedback from trajectory-wide advantages. For each ques- tion, we sample G student rollouts and assign cor- rectness scores ci ∈[0, 1]. Guidance can vary in sign across tokens, remain nonzero when group advantages vanish, and apply to failed rollouts with positive RL advantage. Correctness controls eligibility, while the shaped reward gap controls strength. Being better than its peers does not im- ply that a rollout reaches the target reward. Fully correct trajectories receive no OPD, even when be- havior penalties lower their shaped reward; RL still encourages more efficient search. We minimize the combined objective L(θ) = LRL + λsLOPD + βLKL, (11) where LKL regularizes against a fixed reference policy with β = 0.01. We exponentially decay λs from 0.05 by a factor of 100 every ten RL steps, reducing imitation pressure as outcome-based opti- mization progresses.
