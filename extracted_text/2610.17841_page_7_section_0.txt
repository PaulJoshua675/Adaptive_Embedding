While R(q, p) measures overall query satisfaction, we retain individual attribute matches to diagnose fine-grained retrieval behavior. For each attribute a, we report the mean match score over queries that ex- plicitly specify a: AttrScore(a) = 1 |Qa| P q∈Qa ma(q, pq), where Qa denotes the queries specifying a. Experiments Cross-encoder derived attribute importance. The attribute weights in R(q, p) are distilled from a cross- encoder trained on real product-search data, including the ESCI [34] filtered to fashion relevant queries and inter- nal production-scale proprietary data. Since its proprietary training data precludes direct use as an evaluation model, we use the cross-encoder as a knowledge source and esti- mate attribute importance through matched twin-pair abla- tion and regression-based analysis. We normalize the re- sulting estimates to obtain non-negative weights wa that sum to one, which are used to aggregate per-attribute match scores into the model-agnostic query-relevance score. The resulting weights are shown in Table 3; full estimation pro- cedures, per-attribute confidence diagnostics, and sample sizes are provided in supplementary. Baselines. We evaluate FASHION-RQ using ten off-the- shelf retrieval baselines spanning sparse lexical and dense semantic retrieval. The sparse family includes BM25 [36] on settings. The dense family comprises nine pretrained encoders covering 355M–8B parameter scales, different pooling strategies, and both non-instruction-tuned and instruction-tuned query encoders. These include general- purpose sentence encoders as well as recent large embed- ding models. All baselines are evaluated without train- ing or fine-tuning on FASHION-RQ; queries and catalog products are encoded using their native published config- urations, and candidates are ranked by lexical matching or embedding similarity, respectively. This setup evaluates the extent to which existing retrieval representations generalize to the fine-grained, hierarchically structured fashion queries in FASHION-RQ. Results. Table 2 reports the primary results across the four FASHION-RQ tasks, using two complementary met- rics for each task. Overall, dense and learned sparse re- trievers consistently outperform the lexical BM25 baseline, demonstrating the benefit of semantic representations for fine-grained fashion retrieval. For T1, Qwen3-Embedding-4B achieves the best per- formance on both nDCG@10 (0.8719) and Recall@10 (0.8279), followed closely by E5-Mistral-7B-Instruct and SimCSE-RoBERTa-large. The dense and learned sparse models form a relatively tight performance range, while RoBERTa-large, which lacks retrieval-specific training, per- forms worst. For T2, Qwen3-Embedding-4B again achieves the best performance, with 0.5145 accuracy and 0.5146 macro-F1, narrowly followed by E5-Mistral-7B-Instruct.
