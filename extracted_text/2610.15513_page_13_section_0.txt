The failure analysis components together generate structured feedback blocks for the reflective LLM containing detected trigger words mapped to eu- phemisms, technique recommendations based on refusal type and run history, domain-appropriate framing scenarios, strategies to avoid, and contrastive hints about what worked elsewhere versus what failed here. Behavior-targeted mutation for stubborn behaviors. When optimization plateaus at high ASR (for example, stuck at 93%), random mutations become statistically inefficient: they have only a 7% chance of targeting a failing behavior. REPRO detects stubborn behaviors with failure rate above 70% after at least three attempts and treats them differently from the rest of the population. Each stubborn behavior is classified into a category (explosives, hacking, violence, fraud, and so on), and category-specific framing is injected into the mutation prompt (for explosives: use demolition-engineering framing, not cybersecurity; preferred strategies: framing, technical, obfuscation). A dedicated component additionally tracks which strategy families have been exhausted for each stubborn behavior, forcing exploration of unexplored families and clearing exhaustion state at SURGE-level restarts (§3.5) so that previously exhausted families can be revisited after structural changes to the population. 3.3.2 Bayesian Strategy Selection. Mutation strategy selection is a contextual multi-armed bandit problem: the algorithm must balance exploitation of strategies known to work against the current target with exploration of alternatives, while incorporating structured feedback from failure analysis. REPRO adopts hybrid Thompson Sampling for this sub-problem because of its known sublinear regret in stationary stochastic bandits (see §3.6 for the formal connection and its caveats). The mutation strategy library is seeded with 37 hand-crafted strategies drawn from the adver- sarial ML literature, organized into semantic families ranging from authority-based framing to encoding-based obfuscation. The library is not static: when failure analysis recommends a counter- strategy that is not yet in the library, the reflective LLM synthesizes a concrete strategy to match the recommendation, and the new strategy is registered and persisted across runs. Through this mechanism the library has grown to 58 strategies across 14 semantic families, with the 21 synthe- sized strategies collected in an LLM-generated family. New runs inherit the accumulated library, so discoveries on one target model benefit subsequent runs against other targets. Family-level orga- nization enables systematic exploration: when one strategy fails repeatedly on a stubborn behavior, REPRO can switch to an alternative family rather than exhausting near-duplicate strategies. The full family taxonomy and example strategies are listed in Appendix C.
