On G1, coupling is high: the raw query often contains the API’s name or brand directly, giving RtP a direct embedding match while a planning step only abstracts away this signal. On G2/G3, coupling is low: a single embedding cannot capture multi- faceted intent. G2 queries require a mean of 6.5 Ground-Truth APIs vs. 5.2 on G1; PtR generates 2.4–2.5 stubs per query, each anchoring to a different functional sub-need and retrieving independently. PtR’s G2 advantage grows with k (from +0.014 at k=1 to +0.066 at k=20) because each stub contributes its own top-k candidates. A natural concern is whether PtR’s G2 advantage is simply an artifact of broader search: with m≈2.4 stubs each retrieving top-k, PtR explores ∼2.4k candidates before returning k to the planner, while RtP searches exactly k. The k-ablation (Table 1) directly addresses this: at budget-equivalent k=10 (matching PtR’s effective search width at k=5), RtP G2 recall reaches 0.186, nearly identical to PtR at k=5 (0.184 in the ablation; 0.188±0.007 in the main experiment). However, this budget-equivalent RtP passes k=10 APIs to the planner, halving precision relative to PtR’s k=5 output. The G1 finding is unaffected: even at k=10, RtP G1 recall (0.453) far exceeds PtR at k=5 (0.279). Together, these results confirm that the crossover reflects structural properties of query-registry coupling rather than search breadth alone. 4.2 Stub Hallucination: The Mechanism PtR generates 79–99% unique stub names across subsets (Table 4), confirming the LLM is hallucinating tool names from scratch rather than recalling real API names. The cosine similarity between correct and incorrect stub-API matches is virtually identical (e.g., G1: 0.874 vs. 0.872; similarly small gaps on G2 and G3), meaning the embedding space provides no signal to distinguish good routing from bad. Only 40.6% of G1 stubs and 24.8% of G3 stubs match a relevant API at all. Despite this, G3 recall favors PtR because multi-stub coverage compensates for individual stub mismatches: 2.5 stubs per query span different functional domains, and finding the right tool for each sub-need does not require every stub to succeed.
