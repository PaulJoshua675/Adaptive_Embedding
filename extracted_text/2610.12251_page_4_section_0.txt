The depth of an input signal is 0, and the depth of a unit is one more than the largest depth of its predecessors, or 1 if it has none. A clocked netlist on n state bits is a netlist with I = {x1, . . . , xn} in which each xi is assigned the unit or input signal that computes its next value. Lemma 2.5 (Levelization). Let N be a clocked netlist on n state bits whose units have maximal depth d ≥1. There is an LTTM Lev(N) of depth d on {0, 1}n such that Lev(N)(x) is the next state computed by N for every x ∈{0, 1}n. Every unit of N that contributes to a next-state value occurs in Lev(N) with its weights and bias unchanged, exactly once if its depth is less than d and once for each state bit whose next value it is if its depth is d; all other units of Lev(N) are identity units H(x −1). Proof. Remove the units that no next-state value depends on, and place each unit v in layer depth(v) and the state bits in layer 0. For a signal u, let λ(u) be the largest layer of a unit that reads it, a signal assigned as the next value of a state bit counting as read at layer d + 1. In every layer ℓwith depth(u) < ℓ< λ(u), place an identity unit that reads the copy of u in layer ℓ−1. A unit v then finds each predecessor u in layer depth(v) −1, since depth(u) ≤depth(v) −1 < λ(u). Layer d holds one row for each state coordinate, in order: the unit assigned to it if that unit has depth d, and otherwise the copy of the assigned signal. Since H(x −1) = x on {0, 1}, induction on ℓshows that each row of layer ℓoutputs the value of its signal of N. 3 Weights as state: the ring machine This section gives a fixed network whose state holds threshold networks as data. The state is a window of cells, in which a stored network keeps its state, and a ring of banks and tapes; a bank lists the units of a stored network as records. Each step evaluates the record under the slot head of the active bank on the window and moves that head on by one slot. Three more heads read one tape and write the next bank and the next tape, and this is how one stored network builds another (Figure 1). Every head moves by absolute positions, so a bank or a tape need not have the size of what it holds. Definition 3.1 (The ring machine). Let α ≥4, K ≥2 and S, L ≥1, and put β = 8+3α. A state of the machine Mα K,S,L consists of a window w ∈{0, 1}2α, a bank index κ < K, banks Q0, . . . , QK−1 of S slots of β bits, each with a slot head and a bit head, and tapes D0, . . . , DK−1 of L cells, each with a head. Indices of banks and tapes are taken modulo K: bank κ is active and bank κ+1 idle, tape κ is the read tape and tape κ + 1 the copy tape; the arm is the bit of the idle bank under its bit head, in the slot under its slot head. Window cell 0 is a sink, which reads 0 and keeps nothing written to it. Cells 1, 2 and 3 are not stored: they name the cell under the read head, under the arm and under the copy head. Cells 4 to 12 are requests: AR, AA and AC move the read head, the arm and the copy head on by one cell, RR, RA and RC return them to cell 0, CB and CT clear the idle bank and the copy tape, and HO hands over. A record is a word (c0, c1, θ, a0, a1, ao) ∈{0, 1}β: two weight codes of two bits, read as w = 1 for 01, w = −1 for 10 and w = 0 otherwise, a bias θ ∈[−8, 7] in two’s complement on four bits, and three window addresses of α bits, most significant first.
