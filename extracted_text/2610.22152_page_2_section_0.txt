We then introduce a constrained reinforcement learning fix, built around a cost signal targeting the discovered exploits directly, and show that it eliminates the gaming behavior from the agent’s final policy while substantially recovering true-objective performance. Critically, we derive an exact accounting identity relating the proxy reward, the true reward, and our cost signal, and verify that it holds exactly against the trained agents, giving the fix a provable guarantee under a hard-constrained variant, not merely an empirical trend. Concretely, this paper contributes: • CleaningGridWorld, a gridworld with a constructed, interpretable proxy/true reward gap, and an empirical demonstration that a proxy-only DQN discovers two distinct gaming mechanisms unaided. • A Lagrangian-penalized constrained RL fix, with a cost signal targeting the discovered exploits, that eliminates gaming from the final greedy policy while recovering substantial true-objective performance. • An exact accounting identity relating proxy reward, true reward, and cost, proved for this environment and empirically verified against both trained agents – yielding a deterministic lower bound on true-objective performance under a hard-constrained version of the method. The proxy/true gap studied in this paper is not unique to toy domains. AI-driven biological design and screening tools, such as generative sequence design scored against an automated toxicity or pathogenicity classifier, face a structurally identical gap, where "passes the screen" and "is actually safe" can diverge under optimization pressure. We return to this connection as a motivation for future work in Section 6, without claiming biosecurity-specific experimental results in this study. 2
