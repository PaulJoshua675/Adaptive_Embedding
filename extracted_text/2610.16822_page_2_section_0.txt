Global direction prior refers to an explicit graph-level direction-aware structural or spectral prior, not merely local edge-direction cues. Learning-based predictors therefore rely on continuous representations of candidate architectures. This representation problem is nontrivial be- cause a neural architecture is an operation-labeled directed acyclic graph (DAG), rather than a mere undirected connectivity pattern. A directed edge u →v indicates that the output of operation u feeds into operation v, so the representation should preserve directed information flow. For accu- racy and latency prediction, this requires capturing operator composition along directed paths, branch splitting and merging, and reachability- related dependencies. If direction is discarded, the graph skeleton may still be retained, but upstream/downstream roles, split/merge structures, and forward dependencies can be blurred. Despite the need to preserve directed computational semantics, existing predictors cover these aspects only partially. Sequence-based methods [8, 17, 18] linearize architectures and can obscure explicit DAG topology and edge-direction semantics. GNN- based predictors [13, 19–22] explicitly model architecture graphs and local edge directions, but higher- order topology and long-range directed dependencies rely on repeated local message passing, where global structural and directional patterns can be weakened. Transformer and hybrid methods [14– 16, 23–25] use self-attention to capture long-range interactions and inject topology-aware cues through operation/topology tokenization, graph-aided attention, relation masks, or bidirectional aggregation. However, these designs still treat direction as scattered structural cues rather than a coordinated modeling principle. They provide partial directional signals, but usually leave them local or stage-specific rather than combining topology encoding, local direction cues, a global direction prior, and direction-aware updates in one encoder. Thus, the main gap is not whether graph structure is used, but whether directional semantics are modeled consistently across the encoder. The challenge is therefore to preserve the asymmetry induced by directed computation while still capturing DAG topology, motivating the following question: How can a neural architecture encoder explicitly model directed computational semantics while capturing DAG topology? Our key insight is that directionality in architecture DAGs should be preserved throughout represen- tation learning, rather than injected once as a structural cue. We define encoder-wide directional consistency as the requirement that directed computational semantics be preserved across node-role encoding, global direction-aware spectral encoding, pairwise interaction, and feature update. Without such consistency, direction-aware cues introduced at one stage may be diluted by later direction- agnostic transformations. This motivates preserving direction across the main encoder stages rather than relying on an isolated structural signal. We propose DTA-GT, a Direction- and Topology-Aware Graph Transformer designed to preserve directed computational semantics throughout the encoder.
