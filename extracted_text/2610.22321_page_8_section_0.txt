For evaluation, we avoid JavisBench because part of its prompt set was used to collect training sam- ples, and use AV-Phys-Bench (Cui et al., 2026), VABench-T2AV (Hua et al., 2026), and AVGen- Bench (Zhou et al., 2026b) as prompt sets. We report the metric set used by OmniNFT on JavisBench and add some audio-video consistency metrics to show changes in this core aspect more clearly. Results. AVC-Reward consistently improves external audio-video consistency metrics across all prompt sets: IB-AV by 20.65–28.35%, AV-Align by 18.78–59.47%, JavisScore by 20.47–28.74%, and AVHScore by 22.00–26.80%. Moreover, although OmniNFT directly optimizes DeSync while ours does not, our DeSync gains remain comparable. These gains demonstrate strong generalization to unseen audio-video consistency metrics. CAVP changes by less than 1.7% for both methods, in- dicating limited separation. As for other evaluate dimensions, because T-A, T-V, and A-V objectives are not perfectly aligned, stronger A-V optimization can slightly reduce text alignment. Within Im- ageBind, however, the AV gains exceed the observed TA/TV declines, while single-modality quality remains comparable at the OmniNFT level. These results show that AVC-Reward is not only an accurate evaluator but can also serve as an effective reward signal for optimizing audio-video consistency in generation. 5.3 HUMAN EVALUATION We randomly sample 100 prompts from AVGenBench, AV-Phys-Bench, and VABench, and com- pare LTX-2, OmniNFT, and Ours with blinded randomized human judgments, as summarized in Fig. 4. We ask 10 annotators with undergraduate degrees or higher to evaluate the samples. Ours receives the highest preference for both audio-video consistency and overall quality.
