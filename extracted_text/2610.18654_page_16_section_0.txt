Circles mark the checkpoints reported in Table 4. 3.4. Scaling to a 4B GQA Model To test transfer beyond the 1.9B MHA backbone, we adapt the 4B GQA model with 𝑘𝑚= 32 and 𝑏= 4. With 𝑘𝑠= 8, C-KSA compresses distant-history KV by 256× along the sequence dimension while preserving GQA’s shared KV heads. The C-KSA+Full checkpoint uses 6.7B tokens of module-only adaptation followed by 15.9B tokens of full-parameter adaptation. Starting from this checkpoint, S-KSA uses a further 4.2B tokens of module-only adaptation, leaving all inherited parameters frozen. The remote budget, local window, and sink settings remain unchanged from the 1.9B experiments; the 4B Indexer additionally normalizes its projected queries and keys as described in Section 2.2. Table 5 compares the adapted models with the six 4B baselines reported in KSA. C-KSA+S-KSA achieves a six-length RULER mean of 84.15%, compared with 84.45% for Hybrid KSA and 83.72% for its C-KSA+Full starting point. At 128K, it scores 74.41%, compared with 71.67% for Hybrid KSA and 65.86% for Full. On general-capability benchmarks, C-KSA+S-KSA stays within 0.40 percentage points of C-KSA+Full and 0.50–2.58 points below Hybrid KSA. Overall, C-KSA+S-KSA retains retrieval performance close to Hybrid KSA, with modest declines in general capabilities. These results support its effectiveness at a larger scale and compatibility with GQA. Indexer Q/K normalization. Without Q/K normalization, the 4B Indexer loss remains high, and RULER performance stays below its C-KSA+Full parent even after 21.8B adaptation tokens. Under the same adaptation setup, normalizing the projected queries and keys restores near-parent performance with only 4.2B tokens. A possible explanation is that differences in feature scale between the pretrained backbones affect index-logit magnitudes and optimization; RMSNorm reduces this scale sensitivity. We retain the original 1.9B setup for consistency and have not repeated its full experimental suite with this change. Whether normalization also reduces the adaptation data required at 1.9B remains an open question for future work. Compression granularity.
