Grounded understanding NormAd (Rao et al., 2025) evaluates reasoning with explicit norms, while NormSage (Fung et al., 2023) and cultural context schemas (Pujari and Goldwasser, 2025) ex- tract or organize conversational social knowledge. Ziems et al. (2024) report limited zero-shot perfor- mance on conversational power prediction. Social context also disambiguates political discourse (Pu- jari et al., 2024), while MagiC (Wu et al., 2026) evaluates reasoning against visual evidence. Social norm datasets Social Chemistry 101, Moral Stories, and NormBank represent norma- tive knowledge through rules, narratives, and sit- uational constraints (Forbes et al., 2020; Emelin et al., 2021; Ziems et al., 2023). SocialDial and NormDial extend the study of norm observance to dialogue (Zhan et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023). We use CCU conversations for their human annota- tions across emotion, dialog act, and norm viola- tion, supporting context discovery with verifiable feedback. Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards Verifiable rewards support mathematical reason- ing, coding, and instruction following (Trung et al., 2024; Lambert et al., 2025; Guo et al., 2025). Search-R1 learns retrieval during reasoning (Jin et al., 2025), while Math-Shepherd supplies feed- back on intermediate reasoning steps (Wang et al., 2024b). CARE combines answer and format re- wards with a retrieval reward checking that repro- duced spans occur in the supplied context (Wang et al., 2025). CaRR rewards facts that satisfy question-derived rubrics, are supported by cited webpages, and connect to the final answer (Zhang et al., 2026). Aggarwal and Welleck (2025) op- timize accuracy and adherence to constraints on reasoning length.
