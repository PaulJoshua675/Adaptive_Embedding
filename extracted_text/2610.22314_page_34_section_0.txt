Distances and uncertainty. A metric is computed per conversation and compared by calculating the Wasserstein-1 between the simulated and the real distribution, divided by the real standard deviation. Intervals are 95% percentile bootstraps that resample patients on both sides (1,000 draws). The noise floor is the same distance between two random halves of the real patients over 100 splits; we report its 95th percentile. Surface metrics. Seven metrics are computed per conversation over the patient’s turns: words per turn; words per sentence; the coefficient of variation of turn length; MTLD lexical diversity; and the rates of PSI-Bench’s absolutist, depressive and non-fluency markers per 1,000 four-character pseudo- tokens (Hoang et al., 2026). Narrative arc. Every patient turn, real and simulated, is labelled as a problem statement, a transition, change talk or filler with PSI-Bench’s prompt (Hoang et al., 2026), by GLM-5.2, seeing the four preceding messages.7 Each conversation is split into five equal stretches, and we report the share of each label per stretch. Simulated conversations are those held with the proprietary counsellor (100 per simulator). G Benchmark Pool. The benchmark runs a subset of 50 patients of the 100-patient validation pool (Table 14) that maximizes the largest total-variation distance to the 100. The main drifts are an unstable living situation (12% against 6%) and a secondary relationship or family problem (4% against 9%); poor quality of life is deliberately over-sampled (20% vs 1%).
