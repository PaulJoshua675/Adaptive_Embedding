These results show a substantial gap between batched GUI-only execution and the full OpenGCUA design in both task performance and interaction efficiency. The gains hold despite large differences in baseline performance and efficiency. The benefits are not limited to models with low initial task scores. GPT-6 Astra, which has the highest baseline score, improves from 67.1 to 75.7 and reduces steps per task from 102.1 to 33.5, a 67.2% reduction. At the other end of the efficiency range, Qwen3.8-Flash starts with 232.0 steps per task, yet gains 21.6 points while using 62.8% fewer steps. The consistent improvements across all 8 frontier models support the broad applicability of the OpenGCUA design. Fewer interaction steps do not necessarily require fewer output tokens. Output tokens per task decrease for six of the eight models, with an average relative reduction of 27.2%. Claude Opus 5 reduces its output from 378.3K to 89.3K tokens per task, and Kimi K3 from 302.5K to 139.6K. GPT-6 Astra, however, uses approximately the same output-token budget, despite its large reduction in steps. Fewer steps per task can reduce model round trips and their overhead, even without reducing output tokens. End-to-end latency must be measured separately, accounting for model response time and tool execution time.
