To ensure reproducibility, we made all code and data used throughout this paper, as well as additional figures, available in our Zenodo repository Anonymized (2026). 2 Overview 2.1 The CMA-ES Algorithm The CMA-ES is a stochastic, population-based algorithm for continuous black-box optimization, originally introduced in (Hansen and Ostermeier, 2001). At its core, CMA-ES maintains a multivari- ate normal search distribution N(m, σ2C) parameterized by a mean vector m, a global step size σ, and a covariance matrix C. In each generation, a population of λ candidate solutions is sampled from this distribution, evaluated, and recombined to update the distribution parameters. CMA-ES derives much of its effectiveness from its adaptation mechanisms. Step-size adaptation regulates the global scale of mutations by controlling σ, while covariance matrix adaptation gradually reshapes the sampling distribution to align with directions of successful search steps (Hansen, 2016). Together, these mechanisms confer strong invariance properties under affine transformations of the search space and make CMA-ES well-suited to solve ill-conditioned, non-separable optimization problems (Hansen and Ostermeier, 1996).
