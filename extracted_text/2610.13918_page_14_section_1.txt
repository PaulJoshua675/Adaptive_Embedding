A strong potential avenue for future development is the integration of formal pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics (PK/PD) modeling into the simulation pipeline. Current simulated drug effects are parameterized as static shifts in model variables (such as altering Ωtone or γinh). Incorporating compartmental pharmacokinetic models will allow explicit simulation of drug absorption, distribution volumes, metabolic clearance rates, and biological half-lives. This capability would enable virtual clinical trials that compare compound release kinetics (such as immediate-release versus extended- release hypnotic formulations) and predict nocturnal efficacy alongside morning residual sedation. Furthermore, DyssomniaNN provides a foundation for personalized computational medicine in sleep disorders. By fitting individual patients’ physiological parameters (∆ϕ, τr, τd, γinh, Iarousal) to ambulatory data from consumer wearables, actigraphy, and longitudinal home EEG headbands, the framework can be customized to generate patient-specific digital twins. Clinicians could test virtual therapeutic interventions in silico, optimizing chronotherapeutic schedules (such as light therapy timing and melatonin dosing) and identifying targeted pharmacotherapies tailored to an individual’s specific neurochemical phenotype before initiating clinical regimens. 14
