LEANGRAPHSEARCH Query-personalized Propagation. To exploit the dependency structure described in Section 3.2, LEANGRAPHSEARCH propagates a query-specific seed distribution over the graph. Specifically, LEANGRAPHSEARCH normalizes the semantic seed scores to obtain a seed distribution v over graph nodes, assigning zero mass to nodes outside the seed set S: vi = qi · 1[i ∈S] P j∈S qj . (1) Let P denote the row-stochastic transition matrix of G and D the set of dangling nodes. LEAN- GRAPHSEARCH computes personalized PageRank (PPR) (Brin & Page, 1998) by iterating: p(k+1) = rv + (1 −r)p(k)P + (1 −r) X i∈D p(k) i ! v, (2) where p(0) = v. The restart term rv keeps propagation anchored to the seed distribution, while the transition term (1 −r)p(k)P expands each seed toward its dependencies. Returning dangling mass to v preserves the query-specific distribution. Graph-aware Candidate Refinement. After PPR propagation, LEANGRAPHSEARCH merges the semantic seed set S with the top-N graph-expanded candidates ranked by PPR score. The retained candidates are then scored against the original query through the Qwen reranker in a sec- ond reranking stage. The final score mixes the second-stage relevance score ˜qi with the normalized PPR score weighted by a coefficient λ: fi = ˜qi + λ zscorequery(pi). (3) This procedure evaluates graph-expanded and seed candidates using the same relevance model, with the graph signal helping distinguish candidates with similar reranker scores. Finally, LEANGRAPH- SEARCH returns the top-K candidates with highest fi as R(q). 3.4 SEARCH LOOP POLICY To align with the search loop settings in LeanSearch v2, we evaluate two retrieval-policy variants of LEANGRAPHSEARCH that share the same retrieval mechanism but differ in when and how it is invoked. Standard Mode invokes graph-based retrieval after a failed proof attempt using queries conditioned on the current proof state. Reasoning Mode additionally uses an LLM to decompose the theorem into queries targeting definitions, high-level proof strategies, and individual proof steps before the first proof attempt. Retrieved evidence is aggregated across subqueries and truncated to K declarations. Readers can refer to Gao et al.
