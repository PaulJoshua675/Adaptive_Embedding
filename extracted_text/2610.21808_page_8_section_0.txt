Placement determines how much this budget recovers (Table 3). At 1.99 bpw on post- trained Qwen3-8B, knowledge-Fisher selection beats random placement by 6.2 MMLU points and Hessian×weight by 9.0; the latter falls below random. On post-trained 1.7B, the full selector set separates by score family (Appendix Table 12): loss-sensitivity scores yield 9.7–10.3 PTQ perplex- ity, versus 13.7–17.9 for error-magnitude scores, with random placement in the latter range. The 1.7B budget sweep supports this distinction: random placement’s perplexity relative to Fisher falls from 1.96× at +0.15 bits to 1.12× at +0.9 (Appendix Figure 3). At matched bits, the comparison with four-level grids depends on size and basis (Table 2; Appendix Table 11). Under r1, standard per-OC INT2-g128 has 1.48× and 1.29× the planes run’s perplexity at 0.6B and 1.7B. In the recipe basis, the corresponding ratios are 1.039× at Qwen-8B and 1.27× at Llama-8B PTQ. Planes also outperform the stronger role-placed grid at 0.6B, at Qwen-8B PTQ and on Llama at both stages. At 14B, however, four-level grids lead under r1 (10.76 and 10.90 versus 12.19), so neither format dominates across sizes. On task accuracy, Llama’s PTQ gains over per-OC exceed the seed spread (+2.9 zero-shot, +10.7 MMLU). Qwen’s PTQ task differences have split signs and lie within one seed range; after distillation, the MMLU gain is +2.4 points but remains below three ranges. Ternary planes thus offer competitive quality at equal bits while retaining a multiplication-free matmul with no codebook or group metadata. 5
