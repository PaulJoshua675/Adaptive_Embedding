Parallel Methods for Spiking Neurons There are several parallel methods for spiking neurons. Some studies primarily focus on parallelizing subthreshold evo- lution by exploiting refractory periods or chunks between spikes (Taylor, King, and Harper 2023; Morrill, Pehle, and Zador 2026). To parallelize the full sequence, another line of work redesigns or approximates spiking dynamics to avoid reset-related temporal dependencies. SPSN (Yarga and Wood 2023) introduces stochastic spiking, separating the nonlinear spiking function and thus enabling parallel computing. PSN (Fang et al. 2023) removes the reset of the spiking neuron and leverages a temporal matrix to capture temporal dependency. Its variants, Masked-PSN and sliding PSN, only maintain a window of a limited number of steps and thus capture only recent steps. PSU (Li et al. 2024) points out the importance of the reset mechanism and estimates the reset via a paral- lelizable reset estimation component. Building upon PSN, MF-PSN (Xue et al. 2026) also removes the reset and further designs channel-wise temporal convolutions, using bit shift- ing to replace multiplication operations. The Dynamic Decay Spiking Neuron (Huang et al. 2026) analyzes the function of reset and, based on this, redesigns the neuronal dynamics to achieve parallelization. PMSN (Chen et al. 2026) uses state space models (SSMs) to model multi-compartment spiking neurons and achieves parallelization via temporal-decoupled reset. SpikingSSMs (Shen et al. 2025) uses a surrogate dy- namic network to estimate the LIF dynamics for acceleration. Although these methods are effective, most of them achieve parallelization by modifying or approximating the neuronal dynamics to eliminate temporal dependencies, which may compromise fidelity to standard LIF dynamics. Fixed-Point Iteration and Associative Scan Fixed-point iteration has been widely used in deep learning. These methods solve for the equilibrium states (fixed points) of the network and use implicit differentiation for training (Bai, Kolter, and Koltun 2019; El Ghaoui et al. 2021).
