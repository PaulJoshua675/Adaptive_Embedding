Dashed and dotted lines indicate the median and mean, respectively; zero dispersion denotes evidence contained in a single contiguous block. 4.4 DATASET PROPERTIES Figure 3 shows context length and the three properties over all instances. Contexts have a median of 103k tokens and a mean of 110k. Within them, the supporting evidence is sparse, with a median of just 0.66% of the context and spanning more than three orders of magnitude. Only 11% of instances have all evidence in a single contiguous block, and the median dispersion among the rest is 0.39. Reasoning depth is mostly multi-step: 65% of instances require two or more dependent steps. The benchmark therefore couples long inputs with sparse, widely dispersed evidence and non- trivial compositional reasoning. For individual tasks, the ones with similar median context lengths exhibit different evidence profiles: HotpotQA has narrow, localised evidence, whereas SciTrek and CorpusQA have broader, highly dispersed evidence (Appendix F). 5 EXPERIMENTS 5.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETTINGS We evaluate two proprietary LLMs, GPT-5.6 Luna and Gemini 3.8 Flash, and two open-weight LLMs, Qwen3.8-27B and Qwen3.5-4B, using the full context for each question and the prompts and scoring procedures described in Section 4.1. For each question, we sample three responses from each LLM and use their mean score as the outcome for that question–LLM pair. We analyse all 6,550 questions from the 17 tasks retained in LURCH. We apply log2 to context length, evidence scope, evidence dispersion, and reasoning depth (as defined in Section 3). We replace any zero values with half the smallest positive value of the corresponding predictor before taking logs. We then standardise each logged predictor to zero mean and unit variance across the analysed questions. Before standardisation, a one-unit increase on the log scale corresponds to doubling a positive pre- dictor value; after standardisation, a one-unit increase corresponds to one standard deviation of the logged predictor. For the regression analyses, we compute standard errors clustered by question to account for dependence between the four LLMs’ scores on the same question. Unless stated oth- erwise, brackets and bands give individual, unadjusted 95% question-clustered confidence intervals (CIs). For RQ1 and RQ2, we report Holm-adjusted padj values (Holm, 1979), with a separate family of tests for each research question as specified below. RQ3 is exploratory and the per-LLM break- downs are descriptive; neither includes formal hypothesis tests. The analyses estimate conditional associations within LURCH, rather than causal effects. 6
