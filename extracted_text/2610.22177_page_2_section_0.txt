Methods such as VGGT[1] demonstrate that strong geometric priors can emerge from large-scale train- ing, enabling accurate reconstruction without computationally expensive Bundle Adjustment [2] involved in Structure-from-Motion [3] and dense matching with cost volumes in the Multi-view stereo methods [4]. This paradigm has significantly improved efficiency and scalability, making feed- forward reconstruction an increasingly attractive solution for real-world per- ception tasks. Despite these advances, most existing feedforward models rely primarily on appearance-driven attention mechanisms and treat each input view as a collection of static visual tokens. In practice, however, real-world scenes are rarely static: independently moving objects, camera ego-motion, and temporal inconsistencies introduce challenges that cannot always be resolved from appearance alone. Without explicit motion reasoning, the model must implicitly infer whether observed changes arise from viewpoint variation or true scene dynamics, which can lead to unstable correspondences and degraded reconstruction quality in dynamic or ambiguous scenarios. In addition, feedforward reconstruction methods typically operate with- out global physical orientation cues. As a result, the predicted geometry may suffer from scale ambiguity, drifting orientation, or inconsistent spatial layouts across views, especially when visual evidence is weak or repetitive. Incorporating simple physical priors, such as gravity direction, offers a nat- ural way to stabilize spatial reasoning and enforce a consistent reference frame, complementing purely appearance-based token interactions. Motivated by the importance of motion and orientation cues in robust visual understanding, we propose to augment 3D vision foundation mod- els with explicit motion- and gravity-aware signals. Specifically, we inject optical flow as an additional motion representation that highlights local pixel displacement patterns, enabling the model to better separate static background structure from dynamic foreground regions. Furthermore, we incorporate gravity direction as a lightweight global orientation prior, pro- viding a stable reference axis that improves spatial consistency across views. These cues introduce structured inductive biases that are absent in standard feedforward architectures, while preserving the simplicity and efficiency of transformer-based reconstruction. To maintain architectural integrity and isolate the impact of our pri- ors, we adopt the Learning Using Privileged Information (LUPI) paradigm during fine-tuning and treat the new modalities as privileged data.
