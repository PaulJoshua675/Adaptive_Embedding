We attribute this to the fact that such edits generate new content rather than pre- serving existing details. In contrast, edits that require con- tent to persist across many turns are more susceptible to ac- cumulated errors. Our work therefore focuses on sequences in which unaffected content must remain consistent. Our approach substantially reduces accumulated drift, but some challenges remain. Particularly difficult edit- ing instructions may still affect previously edited content (Fig. 6), suggesting that stability and instruction follow- ing are related but distinct objectives. Furthermore, al- though our method improves stability beyond the sequence lengths seen during training, extending this robustness to much longer editing sequences remains an interesting di- rection for future work. Beyond image editing, our approach suggests a more general training principle for recursive generative pro- cesses. Self-Forcing exposes the model to the distribution it will encounter during inference, while asymmetric teacher supervision provides reliable guidance from a clean refer- ence. Score distillation connects these two paths without requiring paired target outputs, which is particularly use- ful when many valid results are possible. We believe this combination may be useful in other generative tasks where models repeatedly operate on their own outputs. Acknowledgments We thank Ron Mokady for his valuable feedback.
