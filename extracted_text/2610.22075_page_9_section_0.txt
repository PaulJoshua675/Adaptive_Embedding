Since every pass through the recurrent block keeps its own keys and values, our model holds 2.55× the KV cache of the base model, in line with the 56 against 22 traversed layers per token, while it stores 18.1% fewer weights and outperforms it (Table 1). We see that the cost grows as the budget n shrinks. With n=3 the cache shrinks to 1.45× that of the base model at a cost of 0.75 pp, and with n=1 it shrinks to 0.727×, below the base model itself, at a cost of 5.43 pp. This can be attributed to the fact that the keys and values of a recurrence step are computed from the state of that step, so a recurrence step that reads the cache of another step attends to representations that do not match its own input. Training the model with the sharing active does not close this gap either, with a cost of 0.73 pp for sharing the keys and 2.94 pp for sharing the keys and values (Appendix A.3). We thus keep a separate cache per recurrence in our best-performing setting, and note that the cache size can be traded against accuracy at inference without retraining.
