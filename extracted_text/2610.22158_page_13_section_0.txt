B.4 Human Verification We conduct human verification on 2,160 outputs from one open-weight model (DeepSeek V3.2) and one proprietary model (GPT-5.4). The sample is evenly stratified: each model and each source benchmark contributes 1,080 outputs, each prompt- ing setting contributes 720, and each of the nine fine-grained error types contributes 240, yielding 80 outputs per prompting-setting–error-type com- bination. Human annotators follow the same eval- uation protocol as the automatic evaluators. Two graduate-level annotators familiar with SQL and database systems independently evaluate the same 2,160 sampled outputs using the rubrics applied by the automatic evaluators. Agreement between the two human annotators is high, with Cohen’s κ of 0.9912, 0.9632, and 0.8759 for SQL Behavior, Detection, and Type Identification, respectively. B.5 Robustness to Judge–Target Overlap GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.6 serve as both eval- uated models and automatic evaluators, creating potential judge–target overlap. Although averag- ing their judgments reduces the influence of either evaluator, it does not by itself eliminate possible self-evaluation or model-family bias. We there- fore conduct a leave-self-out robustness check. For GPT-5.4 outputs, we retain only the judgments of Claude Opus 4.6; for Claude Opus 4.6 outputs, we retain only those of GPT-5.4. Table 7 shows no systematic self-favoring pat- tern. Self-evaluation slightly raises some Base scores but lowers others, including GPT-5.4’s Ex- plicit TYPE score. After removing self-evaluation, the aggregate SQL, DET, and TYPE scores change by less than 0.001 in every prompting setting. The leading models also remain unchanged: Claude Opus 5.5 under Base prompting, DeepSeek V3.2 under Implicit prompting, and GPT-5.4 for Explicit DET and TYPE. We further apply a stricter family-disjoint evalua- tion in which all OpenAI models are scored only by Claude Opus 4.6 and all Claude models are scored only by GPT-5.4; models from other families retain the two-judge average. Across the nine setting– metric combinations, aggregate scores change by at most 0.0016, the top-ranked model remains un- changed, and the original and family-disjoint model rankings have Spearman correlations ranging from 0.944 to 1.000. These results indicate that judge– target family overlap does not materially affect the aggregate results, leading models, or conclusions about prompting behavior. GEN and VAL are un- affected because they are derived directly from the models’ structured outputs rather than from auto- matic evaluator judgments. B.6 Paired Evaluation Design The paired inputs expose a progression from under- critique to instruction-driven rejection. Each pair contrasts an original query–context combination with its perturbed counterpart. Reliable critique should change the response to the perturbation while maintaining correct validity judgments and SQL generation for originals. Base prompting usu- ally answers both; stronger prompts increase ab- stention, but Explicit prompting often suppresses originals as well. The separation among DET, TYPE, and SQL on flawed queries and VAL and GEN on originals therefore distinguishes evidence- based critique from broad rejection. The five measures provide complementary ev- idence about premise critique behavior. DET records whether any flaw is noticed, TYPE whether the intended defect is explained, and SQL whether that assessment changes generation. On originals, VAL records the explicit judgment and GEN the action. Their divergence is informative: under Ex- plicit prompting, LLaMA 4 Maverick retains 0.974 GEN but only 0.363 VAL, so it often generates despite incorrectly judging an original query as flawed; Qwen3.5-397B is low on both and instead follows a broad rejection policy. Evaluation on originals prevents either pattern from being mis- taken for robust critique.
