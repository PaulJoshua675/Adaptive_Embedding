In this section, we present WeGR, a unified generative retrieval framework for e-commerce search. WeGR consists of two comple- mentary components. First, CoCo-VAE (§ 3.1) incorporates query- to-item and item-to-item collaborative signals into SID construction while mitigating codebook collapse through complementary reg- ularization. Building on these SIDs, DHPO (§ 3.2.1) progressively expands reward evaluation and policy optimization horizons, en- abling coarse-to-fine preference learning under sparse outcome rewards without relying on auxiliary reward models. The overall framework is illustrated in Figure 2. 3.1 Collaborative and Collapse-free Tokenizer To preserve collaborative information while maintaining effective codebook utilization, we propose CoCo-VAE, a collaborative-aware residual quantization framework. Specifically, CoCo-VAE incor- porates query-to-item (Q2I) and item-to-item (I2I) collaborative signals to guide representation learning, while combining Code- book Refresh with Fidelity Anti-collapse Regularization (FAR) to mitigate codebook collapse during hierarchical quantization. 3.1.1 Q2I And I2I Collaborative Regularization. We introduce Q2I and I2I collaborative signals to address the lack of co-occurrence in- formation in multimodal embeddings [40]. Specifically, an auxiliary contrastive loss is employed to capture collaborative relationships and guide SID construction. Unlike existing methods that impose constraints directly within the discrete codebook space, which can cause codebook collapse by forcing semantically dissimilar embed- dings to share the same SID, we apply such constraints exclusively to the continuous representations of the encoder with a detached projection head. This design decouples collaborative representation learning from codebook optimization, allowing behavioral signals to enrich item representations without disrupting codebook assign- ments, thereby preserving representation diversity, quantization fidelity, and codebook stability. 3.1.2 Codebook Refresh. In residual quantization, some codewords may become “dead” due to insufficient usage during training, limit- ing effective codebook capacity. Incorporating Q2I and I2I collabo- rative signals may further concentrate item assignments on a few popular codewords, exacerbating codebook imbalance and collapse. We restrict Codebook Refresh to the first quantization layer 𝐿1, as frequent reinitialization in deeper layers may disrupt residual quantization. 3.1.3 Fidelity Anti-collapse Regularization. Although Codebook Refresh mitigates codebook collapse in the first quantization layer 𝐿1, codewords in deeper layers may remain underutilized. To ad- dress this issue, we introduce Fidelity Anti-collapse Regularization (FAR) from two complementary perspectives. At the sample level, we enable each sample to update multiple semantically relevant codewords instead of only its nearest one, expanding gradient cov- erage. At the codeword level, we regularize codeword assignments across samples by penalizing excessive collisions, preventing a large number of samples from concentrating on a few codewords.
