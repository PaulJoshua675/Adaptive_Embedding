Patching rules. BLT [Pagnoni et al., 2025] uses a global entropy threshold (and an approxi- mate monotonic variant); entropy spikes as boundaries go back to dynamic pooling [Nawrot et al., 2023]. SpaceByte [Slagle, 2024] patches at word boundaries; H-Net [Hwang et al., 2026], AU-Net [Videau et al., 2025], MrT5 [Kallini et al., 2024] and ByteSpan [Goriely et al., 2025] learn or derive boundaries in other ways. Scratchpad Patching [Zheng et al., 2026] adds entropy-triggered global steps inside fixed patches, Fast BLT [Kallini et al., 2026] speeds up decoding, and EntropyMoE [Liu et al., 2026] routes patches to experts by entropy. Haltiuk [Haltiuk, 2026] argues that the boundary distribution can be changed nearly independently of language modeling. Boundaries and surprisal. SOMBRERO [Neitemeier et al., 2026] defines boundary enrichment, the mean surprisal at chunk starts relative to all bytes, and trains a hierarchical byte model to raise it; it reports bits per byte (including math and code) and log-likelihood downstream tasks at 1B scale, but not accuracy on computed values. ByteFlow [Deng et al., 2026] selects boundaries by the marginal coding-rate gain of the encoder’s latent states, a learned, latent notion of new information, and reports that it beats an entropy criterion inside its architecture on bits per byte and commonsense tasks. Our results are consistent with ByteFlow’s direction and quantify it on answer accuracy. Compression and segmentation.
