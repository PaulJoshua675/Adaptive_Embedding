App. B gives the sweep budgets, the choice of λ, how LPCN differs from L at finite γ, and the contrastive phase, which on the PCN needs no nudge at all. 3.2.1 THE LINEARISED NUDGE GradEP’s cost is the loss as a function of the visible state, C(x) = 1 2∥v(x) −u∥2, and being quadratic in x, its nudge acts as a second spring (of strength β) against the tether (of strength λ). Balancing the two forces gives a nudged equilibrium x∗β −x∗= −βγg/λ(λ + β), with g = v∗−u (App. C), which is nonlinear in β. The ±β displacements are therefore asymmetric about the free equilibrium, and the centred difference equation 11 acquires an O((β/λ)2) bias. This is standard EP’s weak-nudge requirement, with the tether’s λ in place of an ordinary network’s O(1) curvature. At λ = 1 it caps β near one, which is restrictive since the displacement the parameter update is read from scales with βγ and must stay resolvable in finite-precision arithmetic, while γ must stay small for the correspondence. We therefore instead nudge with the loss’s linearisation at the free equilibrium, as in Dual Prop- agation (Høier et al., 2023): freezing g after the free phase and taking Clin(x) = g⊤v(x), which targets the same parameter gradient but adds no curvature in x. Its gradient ∇x(β′Clin) = βγg/λ is a constant force, where the quadratic cost’s depends on x and so makes the equilibrium nonlinear in β. The displacement x∗β −x∗= −βγg/λ2 is therefore linear in β at any nudge strength and the ±β equilibria stay symmetric about the free one. What remains is only that ∂θEPCN varies nonlinearly along the straight path between them, so the estimate at finite β departs from the β →0 limit it targets by a relative error of order |x∗β −x∗|2 (App. C), and the constraint becomes β γ |g| ≪λ2 in place of β ≪λ, (12) so smaller γ permits larger β: at the γ = 10−4, λ = 1 and |g| ≈0.39 of our phase-1 training runs, the ceiling moves from β ≪1 to β ≪2.6 × 104.
