We add trap probes about content that should not have entered long-term memory, such as an assistant’s generic encouragement. Their reference answer is that the bank contains no such record. If the writer stores the irrelevant statement, retrieval exposes it and the answer becomes incorrect. Traps and the bank-growth penalty are complementary. A size penalty discourages indiscriminate volume, while traps directly test semantic selectivity. A model cannot avoid the trap merely by compressing irrelevant text into a shorter entry. 5.6 Rubrics and gold-fact attribution Each probe and outcome question contains weighted credit points with semantic equivalents; explicit full-, partial-, and zero-credit bands; named distractors, including stale or superseded values; evidence chunk ids; and, for outcome questions, the source facts introduced by each evidence chunk. The answer judge performs semantic matching; code only aggregates returned credit. Gold facts are visible only to the outcome judge. They are not inserted into the bank, retrieval query, or answer-model prompt. Facts from earlier windows may provide context but are marked history-only and cannot generate credit for a current chunk. 5.7 Data verification Reward data must be tested as an instrument, not accepted because it is well-formed JSON. Four layers are enforced. Schema validity removes records with missing fields, unnormalized weights, illegal evidence ids, or illegal grading bands. Causal locality requires per-chunk probes to refer to the current chunk and cross- chunk questions to contain multi-chunk evidence when required. Rubric independence checks that credit points do not entail one another and that distractors represent real failure modes. Discriminability checks that the judge scores five synthetic answers per item in the expected order: reference and paraphrase receive full credit, a partial answer receives partial credit, and a distractor or refusal receives zero credit. In the pilot, faithful paraphrases retain an average score of 0.90. The structural pass keeps approximately 410k of 478k generated probes and 61k of 72k generated cross- chunk questions. To control judge cost, at most three positive probes and one trap are retained per chunk. The final artifact contains 164,576 positive probes, 8,599 traps, approximately 60.8k outcome questions, and 222,395 chunk-level gold-fact references. Future work includes a random human audit with error categories and inter-annotator agreement; a train/evaluation near-duplicate and sequence-level leakage audit; per-scenario and per-failure-type calibration on strong and weak writers; and judge sensitivity to prompt, model, and repeated sampling. 5.8 Harness-level artifact statistics Each complete interaction sequence is assigned to one harness configuration (Table 5).
