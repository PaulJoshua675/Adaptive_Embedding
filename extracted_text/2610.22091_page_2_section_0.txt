Overview of DomSet-MIL: SORR routes patch evidence in severity order, and DCL predicts the dominant label. Severity-Ordered Residual Routing (SORR) applies the rule in descending severity: a malignant gate claims matching patches, an atypical gate processes residual evidence, and unclaimed mass forms the benign remainder. Top-q pooling connects these severity-specific patch assignments to DCL’s censored bag scores. The two compo- nents therefore impose the same severity-ordered factorization at complementary resolutions. Our contributions are threefold: (1) We identify lower-severity lesions as structurally censored under dominant-label supervision, explaining their suppression as negative classes. (2) We introduce DomSet-MIL, coupling DCL with SORR to align latent evidence at bag and patch levels. (3) We evaluate dominant-label prediction and latent-lesion recovery on BRACS [12], with test-time ROI annota- tions and on controlled EBRAINS/TCGA mixed-bag benchmarks. 2. METHOD 2.1. Dominant-label formulation Let D = {(Bi, yi)}I i=1 denote the training set of I WSIs. Slide i is represented by a bag Bi = {xin}Ni n=1 of Ni patch embeddings extracted by a fixed encoder. We consider the ordered diagnosis set C = {B, A, M}, where B ≺A ≺M denote benign, atypical, and malignant findings, respectively. Each slide contains an unobserved, nonempty lesion set Si ⊆C, whereas training provides only its most severe element: yi = max ≺ Si. (1) Consequently, yi = M confirms malignant tissue but leaves atypical presence unknown. In contrast, yi = A confirms atypical tissue and rules out malignancy, while yi = B rules out both higher-severity findings. DomSet-MIL encodes these three supervision states in- stead of treating yi as a complete one-hot description of the slide. The complete framework is illustrated in Fig. 2. 2.2. Severity-ordered residual routing Model specialization has been studied through geometry-aware dense-to-expert reconstruction of vision–language models [15]; the AsyMoE preprint discussed modality-specific experts but has been withdrawn [16]. SORR assigns severity-specific patch mass using ordered gates. We first map each fixed embedding to a trainable representation, hin = ReLU(Wxin + b), gin,c = σ(w⊤ c hin + ac), c ∈{M, A}, (2) where W and b form a shared projection, (wc, ac) parameterize a class gate, and σ is the logistic sigmoid. SORR converts the two gate values into severity-specific routing masses by applying the malig- nant gate first and restricting the atypical contribution to its residual: πin,M = gin,M, πin,A = (1 −gin,M)gin,A, (3) πin,B = (1 −gin,M)(1 −gin,A). Thus, P c∈C πin,c = 1 for every patch, and benign evidence is the parameter-free remainder rather than a third competing gate. The lo- cal residual limits atypical support to the mass left by the malignant gate; it does not suppress atypical evidence elsewhere. To obtain bag scores without diluting sparse lesions or saturat- ing a noisy-OR operator, we retain a fixed fraction of the strongest responses. Let q ∈(0, 1], Ki = max{1, ⌈qNi⌉}, and Ti,c be the indices of the Ki largest values in {πin,c}Ni n=1. We define a class- specific evidence score for every route as pi,c = 1 Ki X n∈Ti,c πin,c, c ∈C. (4) Because Ki scales with Ni, the same q has a consistent coverage meaning across slides with different tissue areas.
