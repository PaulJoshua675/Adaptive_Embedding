7.2 Limitations The current system remains a research prototype and therefore presents several limitations. First, it relies heav- ily on lexical methods. Weighted keywords, gazetteers, and trigger lists are interpretable, but they provide limited recall for paraphrases, spelling variations, and emerging terminology. Additionally, the temporal component sup- ports only a bounded set of explicit date patterns and does not resolve complex relative expressions or event-specific temporal scopes. Another limitation concerns sentiment analysis. Lexicon-based counts do not adequately capture nega- tion, sarcasm, opinion targets, or aspect-specific senti- ment. The system also relies on transitive clustering, which may cause connected components to group chains of documents even when the first and last documents are only weakly related. Moreover, confidence assigned to singleton clusters should not be interpreted as evidence of corroboration. 7.3 Evaluation agenda Future work should evaluate each stage separately and end to end. Relevance filtering can be measured with precision, recall, and F1 on a stratified energy/non-energy corpus. Entity, temporal, and event extraction require span- and type-level annotations. Interlinking should be evaluated using document-pair and cluster metrics, with attention to time-separated events sharing the same entities. Retrieval can be measured with recall@k, mean reciprocal rank, or normalized discounted cumulative gain. Grounded answer quality should include factual consistency, citation correctness, evidence sufficiency, and abstention when the corpus is incomplete. Scalability experiments should report ingestion through- put, per-document processing time, database growth, pair- wise clustering cost, and the effect of source failures. Be- cause the current interlinker compares all document pairs, approximate candidate generation or blocking by time, entity, and category will become necessary as the corpus grows. Finally, multilingual expansion should move beyond isolated English and French lexicons. Language identi- fication, multilingual entity normalization, cross-lingual clustering, and evaluation on comparable reports are nec- essary to fulfill the original multilingual ambition of En- eMonIE. 8
