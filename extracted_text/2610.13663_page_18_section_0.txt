PINN results: Kuramoto–Sivashinsky In the traveling-wave problem with a Gaussian perturbation, the Claude Opus 4.6 agent generated the 𝐹𝐼, 𝐹𝑂 transformations while minimizing the boundary-value-problem residual Resbvp. The mass drift Δ(𝑢) 𝑚was recorded alongside it but did not drive the search. The best values are summarized in Table 7, and the two metrics are computed by different hypotheses. On the primary metric (Resbvp), the best architecture is MultiplicativeIC_DualAdditive_DualZoom, which reduces Resbvp from the baseline value of 1.41 ⋅10−3 to 1.511 ⋅10−4, a factor of 9.3. On the physical diagnostic, the best architecture is BoundedMultAdd_SechZoom_DualPhase, which reduces Δ(𝑢) 𝑚from 9.31 ⋅10−2 to 6.56 ⋅10−3, an approximately 14-fold reduction, while also improving Resbvp by a factor of 8.3. Both winning hypotheses share a multiplicative–additive decomposition̂ 𝑢 = 𝑢(𝑥, 0) 𝑒−𝛼𝑡(1 + 𝑡𝛾𝑚𝑁1 ) + 𝑡𝑊(𝑥)(𝛾1𝑁2 + 𝛾2𝑁3 ), in which 𝑁1,2,3 are the three MLP outputs, the initial profile is embedded exactly at 𝑡= 0, the window 𝑊(𝑥) = 𝑠𝐿𝑠𝑅built from the two normalized distances to the boundaries vanishes at 𝑥= −30 and 𝑥= 40, and the input block supplies features in the co-moving coordinate 𝜉= 𝑥−𝑡— a rescaled tanh(𝜉∕𝜆), the profile sech2(𝜉∕2) of the unperturbed pulse, and a phase marker sin(𝜋𝑡∕6) of the collision. The hypothesis that wins on mass drift differs in one detail: the multiplicative channel is bounded, 𝑁1 ↦tanh(𝑁1), so the correction cannot rescale the embedded initial profile without limit. This bound costs little in terms of the residual but keeps the integral quantity 𝑀= ∫𝑢𝑑𝑥 an order of magnitude closer to its initial value, which illustrates that the two criteria are not interchangeable.
