AI Consilium: a Benchmark of LLM Clinical Multidisciplinary Second Opinions A PREPRINT A separate challenge is how quality should be evaluated. Contemporary studies of medical LLMs increasingly rely on physician-developed rubrics rather than diagnostic agreement alone [Cabral et al., 2024, Goh et al., 2024]. Scalable automated evaluation methods are also emerging: LLMEval-Med combines expert-designed checklists with LLM- as-a-judge evaluation [Zhang et al., 2025], MedThink-Bench evaluates expert-curated clinical reasoning trajectories using reference-informed LLM judging [Zhou et al., 2026], and studies of clinical summarization have reported high agreement between some reasoning models and physician evaluators [Croxford et al., 2025]. These findings make automated evaluation a promising measurement tool, but they do not eliminate the need for physician reference ratings, criterion-specific validation, or analysis of judge-model bias and stability. In this study, we develop a benchmark of LLM-generated clinical second opinions using 300 published NEJM cases from the NEJM-MedQA diagnostic-reasoning dataset [Savage et al., 2024]. Response format, inference method, role composition, and model choice are treated as separate comparison dimensions. The benchmark has two evaluation checkpoints. At the first checkpoint, every case-specific clinical role produces an independent, standalone second opinion. At the second, all role-specific opinions are provided to an interactive multidisciplinary consilium that synthesizes a case-level final opinion through iterative review and revision. The interactive consilium protocol provides a fixed synthesis stage across these comparisons. This design supports controlled comparisons without requiring benchmark users to reproduce one fixed generation architecture. A fixed set of 110 cases is used for physician annotation and judge calibration, the remaining 190 cases are reserved for held-out evaluation. The main contributions of this work are: 1. a benchmark that evaluates role-specific opinions and final consilium reports as distinct clinical products on the same cases 2. a multidimensional clinical-quality framework that separates factual grounding, problem prioritization, data use, reasoning coherence, differential diagnosis, final-conclusion quality, management, and safety 3. controlled comparison of model identity, response structure, and synthesis stage together with deliberation dynamics and inference requirements 4. a physician-developed rubric and criterion-wise calibration of an LLM Judge for scalable evaluation of open-ended clinical second opinions 2
