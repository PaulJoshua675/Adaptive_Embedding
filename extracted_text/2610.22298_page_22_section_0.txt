Not-evaluable outcomes were excluded from the means. Case-level distributions reinforced this separation. Problem prioritization, reasoning coherence, and differential diagnosis each had a median of 2.812, with narrow interquartile ranges near the top of the scale. Clinical-conclusion quality had a markedly lower median of 1.215 (IQR, 0.667–2.000) and the widest between-case spread. Factual grounding (median, 2.375) and safety (median, 2.188) occupied intermediate positions. These profiles identify conclusion formation, rather than generation of a plausible differential, as the main target for further improvement. 4.6 Judge Scores and Consilium Completion Rounds Review duration varied strongly by model–format configuration and showed no monotonic relation with Judge-rated case complexity. Qwen3.5-27B produced no one-round reports in either format, and rounds 4–5 were concentrated in GPT-OSS-120B and Qwen3.5-27B (Table 3). Completed rounds therefore captured persistent role-conditioned disagreement more clearly than case difficulty. GPT-5.6-Sol combined the highest Judge scores with the shortest review trajectories. It averaged 1.22 review rounds for free-form reports and 1.23 for structured reports, with first-round unanimous acceptance in 81.6% and 79.5% of cases and eventual report consensus in 100.0% and 98.9%, respectively. This combination identifies GPT-5.6-Sol as both the quality leader and the most rapidly converging pipeline in the evaluated cohort. 22
