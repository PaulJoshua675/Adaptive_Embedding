Prior work on scale control has ad- dressed individual aspects of scale for specific pur- poses. For example, units-equivariant learning handles the physical units of input and output data (Villar et al., 2023), maximal-update parameterization (µP) prescribes parameterization and update scales for hy- perparameter transfer (Yang and Hu, 2021; Yang et al., 2021), unit scaling chooses scales for low-precision train- ing (Blake et al., 2023), and the normalized Transformer (nGPT) constrains activations and weights to the unit hypersphere (Loshchilov et al., 2025). In contrast, our NDA framework offers a unified view of scale control over the whole computation graph, covering activations, objectives, gradients, and optimizers, and additionally provides diagnostics and local corrections. App. C.6 develops these connections. Contributions. We make three contributions: 1. A graph-level unit calculus covering activations, objectives, gradients, and updates, together with a theoretical analysis. This calculus links forward computation and backpropagation through a single set of unit rules (Prop. 1). To guide unit-consistent network design and training, we prove theorems on loss weighting, optimizer updates, canonical factorization, and quantization error (App. E). 2. Diagnostics and designs derived from the calcu- lus. To make the unit rules testable in trained networks, we introduce dimensionless UVI and a weight-space audit with per-block rescaling predic- tions (Prop. 6). GCT computes every block on dimensionless coordinates and uses an orthogonal projection to control scale growth during residual updates. The calculus also provides local scale corrections for existing architectures, along with unit-consistent training objectives and parameter updates. 3. Language-model experiments from 51M to 1B pa- rameters under matched recipes. GCT training remains stable across a 16× learning-rate range, and the GCT hyperparameters transfer unchanged to other GCT models up to 6× as deep, with clean validation loss comparable to strong base- lines (Figure 2; Tables 1 and 4). The orthogonal projection also improves nGPT in the depth com- parison, reducing nGPT’s loss by 0.275 nats at L = 48.
