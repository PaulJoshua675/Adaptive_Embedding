2.1 ENERGY MATCHING Energy Matching (Balcerak et al., 2025) generates images by relaxation on a learned scalar potential. A feedforward network with a scalar output defines Vθ(x) = α Fθ(x), (1) where Fθ(x) is the network output and α a fixed output scale set by the architecture, and samples are produced by integrating ˙x = −∇xVθ(x) from noise. Unlike diffusion or flow matching, the velocity field is the gradient of a time-independent potential: the same landscape is descended at every step, and the model’s minima are where the data lies. Training has two phases. The first is flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) on exact optimal-transport couplings (Tong et al., 2024): for a noise sample x0 paired with a data sample x1, an interpolant xt = (1 −t)x0 + tx1 with t ∼U[0, 1), and a target velocity u = x1 −x0, the loss is L(θ) = 1 2 −∇xVθ(xt) −u 2.
