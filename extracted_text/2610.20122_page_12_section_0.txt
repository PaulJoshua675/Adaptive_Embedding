LambdaMART is a matched-feature control rather than an ex- haustive hyperparameter study. Does learned fusion improve over fixed fusion? The most direct fixed- fusion comparison is RRF over the same original, hypothesis, and step-back views. XViewRank improves over this baseline by 0.0207 nDCG@10 and 0.0212 MAP on DL19. The MAP gain reaches significance under a paired sign-flip test (p = 0.0457), while the nDCG@10 sign-flip p-value remains above 0.05 despite a positive bootstrap interval. On DL20, the gains are 0.0098 nDCG@10 and 0.0081 MAP and are not significant. Recall@1000 remains nearly unchanged on both years. Therefore, the advantage of XViewRank comes mainly from reordering the shared candidate pool more effectively, rather than from retrieving many additional tail documents. Given the small number of topics and the several reported metrics, we treat the DL19 MAP result as limited evidence rather than a broad claim of statistical superiority. The scorer comparison in Table 7 provides another matched-view check. To- gether with the CombSUM and weighted CombSUM results in Table 1, it sug- gests that the gain is not merely due to pooling the three views, but to learning a nonlinear combination of score evidence under view-overlap context.
