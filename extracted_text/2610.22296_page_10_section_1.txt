We presented the first systematic study of backward compatibility in zero-bit deep-learning audio watermarking. We formalized the backward-compatibility objective via the legacy-chain abstraction, identified the embedder-collapse failure mode that breaks version boundaries when a new detector is trained against frozen legacy embedders, and proposed a training framework that combines performance-weighted sampling and push loss together with ADL balanced by gradient normalization. Across eight versions and two architecture families, the method restores out-of-chain TNR without degrading within-chain detection or perceptual quality, and the restored version boundary holds under distortion. The same mechanism enables revocation: a leaked embedder dropped from the chain is reliably rejected by detectors trained without it. We see the legacy chain as a useful abstraction for future watermarking systems that must evolve under deployment constraints, and we leave robustness under adversarial attack and multibit extensions to future work. 10
