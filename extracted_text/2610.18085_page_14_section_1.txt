Parameterization and initialization. Variance-preserving initialization (Glorot and Bengio, 2010; He et al., 2015) fixes a single scale at the start of training. The Maximal Update Parametrization (µP) of Yang et al. (2021) provides a principled framework for hyperparameter transfer across widths. T-Fixup (Huang et al., 2020) instead rescales residual branches so that deep Transformers can be trained more stably at a fixed width. Our work is complementary: µP governs how to scale across widths, and T-Fixup chooses a particular residual scale, while reparameterization symmetry reveals a continuous family of forward-equivalent configurations at fixed width and depth. Weight normalization and reparameterization. Weight normalization (WeightNorm) (Salimans and Kingma, 2016) decouples the direction and magnitude of weight vectors, implicitly addressing some scale ambiguities. Dinh et al. (2017) explicitly derive the inverse gradient-coordinate law under function-preserving reciprocal rescaling. We use that law as background for the full-state optimizer analysis. Neyshabur et al. (2015) introduced path-regularization to account for rescaling invariances in ReLU networks. Transformer-NFN (Tran et al., 2025) characterizes more general matrix symmetries of multi-head attention; the scalar Q–K and V–O rescalings used here are special cases. Our focus is the response of first-order optimizer states and resumed trajectories to these edits. SAGGS instead selects the Frobenius-balanced representative of an exact scalar pair orbit. Standard WeightNorm has a trainable magnitude and does not impose this pairwise canonicalization. Depth-dependent techniques. Pre-LN (Xiong et al., 2020) places the normalization before the attention and MLP sub-layers, improving gradient flow relative to post-layer normalization (Post-LN). Admin (Liu et al., 2020) diagnoses unbalanced residual-branch magnitudes in Transformers and rescales those magnitudes at initialization. σReparam (Zhai et al., 2023) constrains Q–K spectral scales to prevent attention entropy collapse, a failure mode adjacent to unconstrained motion along the Q–K orbit. Query-Key Normalization (Henry et al., 2020) removes that free Q–K scale by unit-normalizing the two factors. These techniques act on initialization, the residual stream, or spectral constraints, whereas our analysis concerns reciprocal rescalings inside attention and MLP blocks. The existence of an internal symmetry alone does not determine a training recipe’s stability or final PPL. Weight balancing and equivariance. Du et al. (2018) proved conservation of differences of squared layer norms under gradient flow in homogeneous networks and established discrete-time balancing results for matrix factorization. Conservation alone does not drive an arbitrary initial imbalance to zero. Equi-normalization (Stock et al., 2019) uses the same ReLU scale freedom to iteratively rebalance hidden units toward a minimum-norm representative; the procedure does not canonicalize first-order optimizer state. Symmetry Teleportation (Zhao et al., 2022) moves along a function-preserving orbit to change the local descent geometry. SAGGS uses a fixed orbit-selection criterion: the Frobenius-balanced representative of an exact scalar pair.
