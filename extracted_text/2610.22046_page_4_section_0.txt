V ∗ tPIT−1 depends on later sampled states, so a probe that reads only the steps generated so far need not determine it (Section 4). Definition 1 (Best reachable value and the PIT step). For an incorrect trace, the best reachable value at step t is V ∗ t = maxt′≥t V π t′ , which is non-increasing in t, and the envelope of the trace is the sequence V ∗ 0 , . . . , V ∗ T . Step 0 denotes the problem before any reasoning, with s0 = (x). Let tPIT be the first step at which the envelope is at or below the threshold θ: tPIT = min{ t ∈{0, 1, . . . , T} : V ∗ t ≤θ }, θ = 0.1. (2) An incorrect trace is a capability gap if tPIT = 0, a PIT if 1 ≤tPIT ≤T, in which case tPIT is its PIT step, and a near miss if the envelope stays above θ at every step, written tPIT = ∞. Failure types are assigned to incorrect traces only. The running maximum V ∗is defined along one sampled trace and differs from the optimal value of a policy. In the collections, the envelope is computed from the rollout rates ˆpt at the evaluated prefixes, with the maximum taken over the evaluated prefixes at or after step t, so the PIT step is always the last step of an evaluated prefix. V ∗ t gives the highest continuation value available at the current or any later step in the sampled trace. For example, a trace can establish a valid approach, introduce a sign error, and then develop the resulting wrong branch. Its envelope is high before the sign error and near zero afterward, so its PIT step is the step that introduces the sign error. The threshold θ = 0.1 exceeds 1/16 = 0.0625, the rollout rate of a single success among 16 rollouts, the largest K used to assign labels. At a prefix where the envelope is at or below θ, the rollout rate there and at every later evaluated prefix therefore reflects at most one success in 16 rollouts and no success in 8 or fewer. Self-Explore treats a prefix as unrecoverable only if no sampled continuation succeeds [Hwang et al., 2024]; the threshold relaxes this test so that a single success in 16 rollouts does not make a prefix recoverable. Setlur et al. [2024] select the first step with the lowest estimated value in an incorrect trace, arg mint ˆVt. With few rollouts per prefix, zero estimates are common and a zero estimate can be followed by a rate above θ, so the arg min step and the PIT step rarely coincide at either scale (Table 10); all PIT steps in this paper follow Definition 1. A capability gap has no recoverable prefix; it is related to problems that remain unsolved across repeated attempts [Yue et al., 2025] and to difficulty regions in which accuracy is at most 10 percent and does not rise with more sampled reasoning paths [Chen et al., 2024]. A near miss remains recoverable at every evaluated prefix although the trace ends with a wrong answer; it is related to problems that are solved on another attempt [Wen et al., 2026]. A PIT is recoverable before its PIT step and unrecoverable from it onward (Figure 1). Near misses and PITs are the incorrect traces whose prefix at step 0 is recoverable; we call them recoverable traces. Capability gaps are the most frequent failure type at both scales, followed by PITs and near misses, and fewer than half of the incorrect traces are recoverable (Figure 2).
