In diffusion models [1–3], the forward process gradually corrupts a clean image x0 by adding Gaussian noise over t ∈[0, T] timesteps: xt = αtx0 + σtϵ, ϵ ∼N(0, I), (1) where αt and σt denote the signal and noise scales at timestep t, respectively, satisfying α2 t + σ2 t = 1. The reverse process aims to recover x0 from xT by iteratively denoising, guided by the score function ∇xt log pt(xt), which represents the gradient of the log-density of the noisy distribution pt(xt) with respect to xt [19]. Intuitively, the score function defines a vector field that points toward regions of higher data likelihood, guiding the noisy sample toward the clean data manifold throughout the denoising process. In practice, the score function is estimated via a neural network ϵθ trained to predict the noise ϵ added at each timestep. The score is then obtained from the predicted noise as: s(t) = ∇xt log pt(xt) ≈−1 σt ϵθ(xt, t, c), (2) where c denotes the conditioning signal (e.g., text prompt). Tweedie’s Estimation. Tweedie’s formula [21] provides a closed-form expression for the posterior mean of the clean sample x0 given the noisy observation xt: ˆx0(t) = E[x0 | xt] = xt + σ2 t ∇xt log pt(xt) αt . (3) Substituting the learned score estimator from Eq. 2, this becomes: ˆx0(t) ≈xt −σt ϵθ(xt, t, c) αt , (4) which gives a denoised estimate of x0 at timestep t, termed the Tweedie denoised estimate. Unlike the noisy sample xt, which is directly modulated by the noise level σt, ˆx0(t) provides an estimate in the predicted clean image space [40], offering a noise-normalized view of the current generation state. 3.2 Spatiotemporal Tweedie Importance Signal Theoretical Comparison of Temporal Signals.
