When allowed to adjust priorities, it weakened the avoidance reflex, protected the network from obstacle inhibition, and reduced the drive to explore, always in the direction of yielding control to what has been learned. This supports the idea of reducing designer intervention in basal ganglia-inspired controllers [23] and qualifies the idea of layered architectures with fixed priorities [13], [21]. The persistence provided by the basal ganglia was confirmed in the isolated test, but it was not enough to compensate for the interference. One possibility we did not explore is that the basal ganglia select among several learned proposals rather than among fixed reflexes, which would be closer to the vertebrate organization, in which the cortex proposes and the basal ganglia select [9]. 5.4 Limitations Each main condition has three runs. This is sufficient to show large and consistent differences, but not to accurately estimate the frequency of each communication strategy. Conclusions about behavior are based on the champion of each run, not the entire population. All results are from simulations, although with a model validated against Webots. Simulation physics are not identical across computers, so all runs were performed on the same machine. Finally, we used a single task, a single network form, and a single evolutionary algorithm, and the EASA variant explores only one way of combining innate reflexes with a learned network. 6. Conclusions We built an open, validated system of two e-puck robots to study the emergence of a one-bit signal. With it, we showed three things. First, communication disappears when the information it provides is available through another channel. When it is not, communication appears in most runs, though not in all of them. Second, a more difficult task does not make communication more frequent, but it does distinguish the referential signal, which retains its value, from less informative strategies. Third, in a social task, the innate reflexes of a basal ganglia architecture constrain the learned network, and evolution consistently responds by shifting control to the learned network. A natural next step is to test an architecture in which the basal ganglia select among learned proposals, and to verify these results with physical robots.
