Existing benchmarks each measure one stage of a delivery. Table 4 arranges them by the capability they isolate. To our knowledge, none joins these stages into a single causal chain in which a misreading of the problem renders good code worthless and the loss appears on a business metric. The most closely related work was published within weeks of ours. τ τ-bench [9] makes agent construction the task: a developer agent receives a business’s records, a client who holds the requirements, an existing codebase, and limits on serving cost, and the customer-service agent it delivers is then deployed against held-out simulated users. Its strongest configuration passes 23.9% of simulations, compared with 82.2% for an expert-authored reference; we likewise find the best model well short of a reference written by experts. FDE-Gym differs in what it varies and in what it measures. The task instruction varies from a full statement of the rules to a request that may be misdirected, stakeholder contact incurs a charge, the result is measured as uplift on the customer’s business metric relative to a do-nothing baseline of zero, which a delivery can fall below, and the scenarios span 18 sectors and deliverables beyond customer-service agents. Trains but Doesn’t Learn [10] places an agent in the role of a forward-deployed engineer for model post-training and identifies the failure mode in which every check continues to pass while the delivered model is no better than the base model; our literal-compliance anchor is designed to expose the same kind of delivery. Two lines of work bear directly on our task instruction levels. XYEval [11] turns existing benchmarks, τ 2-bench [34] among them, into XY problems, in which the user asks about an attempted solution rather than the real problem, and reports relative drops of up to 46.7%. Our leading models lose little when the sponsor’s request is misdirected, perhaps because, within a full engagement, the documents, data, and stakeholders around the sponsor are all avail- able to contradict it. UnderSpecBench [35] holds the environment and the correct action fixed while degrading the instruction along three axes, an isolation strategy that our task instruction levels also use, and finds that underspeci- fication leads agents to guess rather than fail; Implicit Intelligence [23] reports a best pass rate of 48.3% on simple requests whose constraints must be discovered. Both are consistent with where our levels locate the largest drop: at the constraints that nobody stated. Three of the benchmarks in Table 4 shaped FDE-Gym directly.
