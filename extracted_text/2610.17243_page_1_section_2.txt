Energy systems generate a continuous stream of heteroge- neous information: news articles, research papers, public datasets, policy announcements, organizational reports, and social-media posts. The value of these materials lies not only in their individual content but also in their relationships. A power-grid investment reported by a util- ity, discussed by the public, and evaluated in a research preprint may represent a single underlying development. Manually monitoring such evidence is costly, slow, and difficult to reproduce. The conceptual EneMonIE architecture was originally proposed by Dilek Küçük [Küçük, 2022]. The present work implements, restructures, and extends that proposal as an application-independent backend. The original conceptual design combines text categorization, named-entity recog- nition (NER), temporal-expression and event extraction, sentiment analysis, social-network construction, media interlinking, information fusion, summarization, a central database, and Web-based retrieval and visualization. It also emphasizes modularity, bilingual processing, and the reuse of domain ontologies and lexical resources. The original proposal is deliberately broad. It speci- fies the semantic functions that the system should pro- vide, but it does not prescribe an executable control flow, concrete storage schema, connector contract, question- answering strategy, or graceful degradation mechanism. These implementation decisions become essential when the architecture is turned into software. They determine how duplicate documents are handled, how relevance is en- forced before expensive analysis, how semantic objects are represented, and how evidence is exposed to downstream consumers. This article presents EneMonIE-Backend, a backend architecture derived from a working Python implemen- tation of the EneMonIEKüçük [2022] concept. The central design decision is to isolate the semantic and data-management core from any particular application. FastAPI endpoints, a desktop client, an HTML dashboard, and Web visualization are therefore outside the boundary of the architecture described here. The backend can in- stead be invoked by a command-line process, a scheduler, a test harness, or a future service layer.
