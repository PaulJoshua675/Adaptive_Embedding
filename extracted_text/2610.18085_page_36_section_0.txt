We train three ReLU models at λ = 0 for 30K steps with two seeds: a small model with 22M parameters and L = 12, a medium model with 101M parameters and L = 24, and a deep model with 51M parameters and L = 48. We track the maximum absolute log-ratio across layers and measure its growth from initialization to the last logged step. Every reported seed shows positive growth. Q–K pairs have the largest mean increases, ranging from 0.14 to 0.20 (Table 16). Appendix G.4 defines the measurement and its numerical stabilizer. These results establish naturally occurring imbalance; its effect on PPL is assessed by the paired training comparisons in Sec. 5.2. G.1.3 Additional Stress and Scale Checks Supplementary experiments examine deeper models, SwiGLU, 354M models, and a single-seed 1.2B training run. These experiments generally show larger PPL effects under imposed rescaling than during unperturbed training, with exceptions documented in the experiments below. These comparisons complement the primary seven-seed results. G.2 Supporting Ablations and Replications Three-seed replications give Vanilla-minus-Soft PPL differences of +18.278 on OpenWebText, +21.156 at 123.8M parameters, and +85.813±4.730 at 296.7M under λ = 0.2. Appendices G.14, G.15, and G.16 give the results. Their smaller seed counts support comparisons of direction and magnitude. Synthetic contraction and optimizer-state ablations provide separate diagnostics of the rebalancing operation, as reported in Appendices G.13 and F.5. G.3 FP32 Gradient-Law Verification We verify the gradient transformation law of Eq. 2 by computing gradients at the original parameterization and the reparameterized configuration for the same batch. Table 15 uses FP32; the 1.2B check uses BF16 mixed precision. The verification compares gradient norms. Writing gA := ∥∇AL∥F and g′ A := ∥∇A′L∥F, its relative residual is εA := |g′ A −c−1gA| max{c−1gA, ϵnum}, εB := |g′ B −cgB| max{cgB, ϵnum}, ϵnum := 10−12. (26) The reported εrel is the maximum over tested blocks and layers. This residual verifies the norm consequence of the law; small norm residuals alone do not verify gradient directions.
