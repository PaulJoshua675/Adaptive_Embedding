Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is central to align- ing pretrained LLMs with human instructions (Wei et al., 2021; Ouyang et al., 2022; Taori et al., 2023), with data quality, diversity, and scale being critical for downstream behavior (Wang et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023a). Yet, most existing SFT datasets re- main English-centric and are affected by a less vis- ible but equally serious problem: licensing. Many corpora carry permissive labels (MIT, Apache 2.0, Creative Commons), while their content was gen- erated by proprietary models such as GPT-4 or Gemini, whose terms of service explicitly prohibit use for training competing models. We introduce Multi-SFT, a collection of 502,818 instruction-response pairs spanning five European languages (English, Italian, French, Spanish, and German), aggregating 13 heterogeneous datasets built exclusively from permissively licensed data and models (Table 1). Both pipelines undergo sys- tematic human evaluation by native or near-native annotators, yielding inter-annotator agreement of at least 0.87 across all datasets and languages. We validate Multi-SFT by fine-tuning nine models from three families (EuroLLM, OLMo 2, Qwen3; 1B–32B parameters) on 13 bench- marks. Our experiments show that the multi- lingual, permissively-licensed collection achieves stronger performance on non-English languages than the original English sources, which contain non-permissive data from closed LLMs, while surprisingly maintaining performance on English benchmarks as well. Furthermore, in multilingual settings, Multi-SFT outperforms official instruct models in most cases and stays competitive on En- glish.
