Separating these stages allows the expensive behavioral backbone to be learned once and subsequently held fixed while each capability adapts only the reader and output components required by its prediction geometry. We do not assume that sequential specialization is universally superior to joint training. Rather, joint learning, partial backbone adaptation, and cross-task distillation are alternative operating points whose relative value depends on task compatibility and available training and serving budgets.
