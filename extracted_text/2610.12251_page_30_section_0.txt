Von Neumann’s universal constructor uses a description once as instruc- tions for construction and once as data to be copied [3, 47]. Moore and Myhill gave abstract formulations [49, 50], Lee and Thatcher constructed Turing machines that print their own descrip- tion [21, 22], cellular constructions were given by Arbib, Codd, Langton, Pesavento, and Perrier, Sipper and Zahnd, the last with an attached program and a growing data tape [4, 5, 6, 7, 8], and Ib´a˜nez et al. reproduce by self-inspection [51]. Self-replicating field-programmable arrays copy configurations between cells [52], and Cell Matrix cells read and write the truth tables of their neighbours [53]. Program replicators include Tierra [54] and those that emerge in [55]. Cotler, Hongler and Hudcov´a give an informal condition for self-replication in cellular automata and place the automata that meet it strictly between the globally and the locally universal ones, so that Tur- ing universality does not imply self-replication; they require the encoder and the decoder between symbolic computation and the dynamics to be computationally simpler than the computation at- tributed to the system [56]. In those terms the host running P ∗(Section 6.3) is a replicator relative to an instantiation map. The organism and the stored host need no such map: each is a configu- ration of a fixed threshold network, the only map between the computation and the dynamics is the encoding of a state of the ring machine in the bits of that network (Theorem 3.2), and what is reproduced includes the weights of the network that computes. Networks that produce their own parameters. Neural networks have been trained to output their own weights [57, 58], to reproduce over generations [59, 60] and to emit the program text that trains them [61]; hypernetworks and self-referential weight matrices generate or modify weights [62, 63, 64]. In each case the relation between a network and its parameters is learned and approximate. Machine-checked universal machines. A universal Turing machine has been programmed and verified in Coq, with polynomial time and constant-factor space overhead [65], and the Game of Life has been simulated in the Game of Life by circuits verified in HOL4 [66].
