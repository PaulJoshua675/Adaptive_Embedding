This gap leads to accumulating artifacts, loss of fine details, and unintended changes in appearance (see Figure 1). Effective image edit- ing therefore requires not only high-quality individual edits, but also stability across successive editing turns. Several approaches have been proposed to mitigate degradation in multi-turn image editing. Inference-time methods target specific sources of degradation [27, 38, 47, 62], but may incur computational overhead and are lim- ited by the complexity of distribution shifts in multi-turn editing. Other approaches train models on multi-turn edit- ing sequences [16, 39, 52, 61]. Training on ground-truth sequences leaves a gap between training and inference, while training on self-generated sequences poses supervi- sion challenges, leading to inconsistent targets, restrictions on training sequences, or objectives that do not explicitly address distribution shifts. We present a training scheme for multi-turn image edit- ing that maintains stability under accumulating distribution shifts. Our scheme is inspired by autoregressive video gen- eration, where models similarly operate on their own out- puts and face accumulating distribution shifts. Following the principle of Self-Forcing [17], we train the editor on its own outputs from previous editing turns, allowing it to learn from the distribution shifts encountered during multi-turn inference. However, self-generated editing trajectories in- evitably deviate from ground-truth trajectories, making di- rect supervision with ground-truth images unreliable. To address this challenge, we employ score distillation in a teacher-student framework to train the editor on its self- generated trajectories without requiring ground-truth tar- gets for its intermediate outputs [37, 48, 57]. We intro- duce an asymmetric conditioning scheme for the student and teacher. The student’s input may already deviate sig- nificantly from the teacher’s training distribution, making it unsuitable for reliable teacher guidance. We therefore con- dition the teacher on the original image and a compound instruction combining all edits up to the current turn, while the student performs edits sequentially on its own outputs. This provides a training signal that reflects the intended ed- its without inheriting the errors accumulated along the stu- dent’s trajectory. We evaluate our method against existing multi-turn im- age editing approaches and demonstrate significant quali- tative and quantitative improvements in preserving editing capabilities while mitigating accumulated distribution drift. Our training is efficient and introduces no inference-time overhead, while making no assumptions about the nature of distribution shifts or restrictions on editing sequences. Through ablation studies, we further demonstrate the im- portance of our asymmetric conditioning scheme for stable multi-turn editing.
