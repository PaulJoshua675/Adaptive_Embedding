A.5 EXTENDED RESULTS OF AMONG US Figures 7 and 8 report six measures for every method, computed from the Impostors’ game logs. The main text shows the first two; the other four check whether an intervention changes how the model plays. Impostor win rate. Share of completed games won by the Impostors. A lower rate means the Impostors deceived less successfully. Confession rate. Share of games in which an Impostor, during a meeting, sends a message match- ing a fixed confession pattern (e.g., “I killed Player 5”). Paraphrased admissions are missed, so this is a lower bound. Valid action emitted. Share of Impostor turns on which the model produces an action the game can parse. A drop means the intervention has broken the output format. SPEAK rate. Share of discussion-round meeting turns on which the Impostor sends a non-empty message when it’s their turn to speak. Refused to play. Share of games in which the Impostor, at least once, produces no action and de- clines out of character, for instance because it considers the game harmful. Declines made in character are not counted. Kills in front of witnesses. Share of the Impostor’s kills made with three or more players in the room. This measure has no preferred direction: an Impostor that does not hide its kills is less deceptive, but also plays worse.
