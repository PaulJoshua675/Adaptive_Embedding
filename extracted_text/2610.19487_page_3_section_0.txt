How Long Is the Coast of a Neuron? 3 B. What Coarse-Graining Guarantees Suppose that a coarser observable is obtained from the variables at resolution ε through Z = T(Xε). The data- processing inequality gives I(Y ; Z) ≤I(Y ; Xε). (12) Thus coarse-graining cannot increase the total predictive information available about Y . In the notation of the previous section, Iavail can only decrease or remain un- changed as information is discarded by the projection. It is important, however, to separate this absolute in- formation loss from changes in the structure of the surviv- ing dependence. Equation (12) does not, by itself, order either the higher-order residual Ihigher or the normalized direct fraction F. The reason is that the optimal direct model is defined relative to the observable coordinates themselves, and that admissible family changes when Xε is replaced by Z. A blanket claim that higher-order infor- mation must decrease under every coarse-graining would therefore be false. A stronger statement becomes possible under an addi- tional closure condition. Form the joint direct surrogate Q⋆ ε(y, x) = Pε(x)Q⋆ ε(y | x), (13) which preserves the true distribution of the observed in- puts while replacing the conditional response by its op- timal direct approximation. Project both the true joint distribution Pε and the surrogate in Eq. (13) through the same map T. If the conditional distribution induced by the projected surrogate remains within the direct fam- ily defined at the coarse scale, then the family is closed under that projection. Under this condition, two inequalities apply in se- quence. First, because the direct model is refitted after coarse-graining, the optimal coarse direct model can per- form at least as well as the projected fine-scale surrogate. Second, KL divergence contracts when both distributions are passed through the same stochastic map. Together these facts give Ihigher(Z) ≤DKL(PY,Z∥Q⋆ Y,Z) ≤DKL(PY,X∥Q⋆ Y,X) = Ihigher(X). (14) The first inequality follows from optimization within the coarse direct family; the second is KL contraction. Equation (14) therefore provides a sufficient condition under which the absolute non-additive residual cannot increase with coarsening. The closure condition is essen- tial: without it, projection can change the form of the admissible direct representation, and no universal order- ing of Ihigher follows. Even when Eq. (14) holds, however, the normalized direct fraction need not increase. From Eq. (11), we have F = 1 −Ihigher Iavail , (15) so coarse-graining acts on both the residual in the numer- ator and the total available information in the denomi- nator. F moves toward unity only when Ihigher is lost proportionally faster than Iavail. If the two are lost at the same rate, or the available information is lost faster, F is unchanged or falls. Because the denominator moves with the numerator, F carries no information about how much predictive information survives. We call the de- nominator trap the error of reading a high F as evidence of a simple mechanism without checking Iavail: F can approach unity precisely because almost everything, in- teractions included, has been discarded. The data-processing inequality therefore guarantees loss, or at most preservation, of total predictive infor- mation; closure of the direct family additionally guar- antees contraction of the absolute higher-order residual, Ihigher(Z) ≤Ihigher(X). Neither result mandates that a coarse description will appear increasingly direct. That final behavior depends on how the particular projection redistributes the information that survives between Idir and Ihigher. Whether coarse-graining simplifies the ap- parent computation is consequently a property of the measurement map and the underlying dependency struc- ture, and must be evaluated scale by scale. C. Interaction Order Changes Under Projection Coarse-graining can change not only how much infor- mation is retained, but also the order at which the sur- viving dependence appears. A dependency that is irre- ducibly higher-order in one set of observables can become an ordinary pairwise dependency after projection. This distinction is important for mechanistic inference: ob- serving pairwise structure at a coarse scale does not es- tablish that the process generating it is pairwise at finer scales. A three-spin construction makes this ambiguity exact. Let si ∈{−1, +1}, then the joint probability distribution is PK(s) = exp(Ks1s2s3) 8 cosh K . (16) The distribution in Eq. (16) contains only a three-body coupling, with K setting the coupling strength. Because the interaction depends on the triad product s1s2s3, marginalizing out any single spin cancels the coupling term and leaves the remaining pair completely uncor- related. Therefore, every one- and two-spin marginal is uniform, and the pairwise maximum-entropy distribution eP (2) is uniform over the eight possible states. Its entropy is exactly 3 bits.
