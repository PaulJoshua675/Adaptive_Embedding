The effect is purely per-call work: disabling early exit leaves the dual-tree distance count unchanged on every ablation dataset (159.6M calls on corel either way), so the traversal is identical and only the cost of each rejected candidate differs. In fact none of the five flags changes the count by more than 0.1%; their time effects are constant-factor effects. The confidence intervals make the noise floor explicit, which is essential to reading the table cor- rectly. On corel, covtype, and mnist they are tight, so the effects there are measurable. On pima and pendigits they are not: their per-allocation timings are bimodal—on pendigits, allocations of the same binary cluster near 170 ms or near 260 ms, and the same split appears in an independent earlier sweep—so a ratio of two medians reflects which mode each binary’s allocations happened to land in rather than the optimization. The sub-1.0 entries in those columns (e.g., pendigits SmallVec 0.65×) are of this kind. Conclusions about micro-optimizations on small datasets are therefore not supportable, and we draw none. Among the other optimizations, none has a consistent effect: halfsort, SmallVec, the k=1 fast path, and the triangle pre-filter are within 4% of 1.0× on covtype and mnist, and on corel only half- sort (1.10×) exceeds that. Differences of this size also arise between builds that should behave identically—for example, disabling the k=1 fast path makes corel’s k≥5 queries 6–9% faster (Ta- ble 8), although that path is never taken at k>1—so we treat effects of up to about 10%, corel’s halfsort included, as code-layout noise rather than as properties of the optimization. 5 Experiments 5.1 Experimental Setup We describe the hardware and software setup for our experiments in Appendix B. Datasets. Table 2 lists the 12 benchmark datasets. Eleven originate from the UCI Machine Learn- ing Repository [40]; MNIST is from the original LeCun distribution [41]. Sizes span three orders of magnitude (n = 150 to 581,012) and dimensionalities range from 4 to 784, providing broad coverage of the regimes where cover trees are applicable. The smallest sets are included for two reasons: coverage of the (n, d) plane, and direct comparability with the seminal cover tree evalua- tions [7, 8], which use exactly these sets—re-evaluating that literature under controlled conditions is part of this paper’s purpose. They are not the high-performance target, and as Section 4.2 quanti- fies, their millisecond-scale timings are noise-dominated; the performance conclusions rest on corel, covtype, and mnist. Appendix J adds non-tiny synthetic data (n=20,000) whose intrinsic dimension is known by construction, which real datasets cannot provide. All experiments use the Euclidean (L2) distance.
