Embedder Collapse and the Need for a Diversity Loss. Performance-weighted sampling together with push loss is sufficient to obtain high TPR within the legacy chain, but it leaves the new embedder 𝐸𝑣entirely free to position its residual watermark anywhere in the signal space. The detector 𝐷𝑣is itself trained from scratch, what is fixed are the legacy embedders 𝐸1, . . . , 𝐸𝑣−1, whose residuals are frozen throughout training while 𝐸𝑣’s residual is the only one free to move. The easiest path to a low detection loss is therefore for 𝐸𝑣to drift toward the frozen legacy embedders’ residual distributions, so that 𝐷𝑣can satisfy every in-chain pair with a single decision boundary. We refer to this failure mode as embedder collapse: the new embedder’s residual ends up implicitly close to those of the frozen legacy embedders, destroying the version boundary. Angular Diversity Loss. We treat the mel spectrogram of the audio watermarked by each embedder as a “class” on the unit sphere and separate these classes with an additive angular margin, inspired by the margin formulation from ArcFace [16]. The setting, however, is not the fixed-identity classification ArcFace was designed for: an embedder has no identity vector to learn, and only 𝐸𝑣 is trainable while the legacy embedders are frozen. The ADL adapts the angular margin to this setting with two changes. First, we remove ArcFace’s learnable per-class prototypes. The proposed loss is essential for preserving the separation between model versions. Without it, the embedding spaces of the legacy and new embedders collapses, eliminating the intended version boundary. The mel spectrogram is a robust representation in which perceptually similar signals produce very similar mel features, so a strong margin or sharp scale would destabilize training.
