Exact sampling and scoring hold for the induced distribution regardless of whether the DLM matches the data conditionals. Recovery of the data distribution requires exact conditional predictions; learned DLMs need not satisfy this assumption, and their induced distributions may depend on the decoding policy. While our construction excludes parallel token revelation, it permits generating and scoring only the designated targets T while other masked positions remain unresolved. 3.3 Reward Guidance Even with demonstrations visible, a DLM may favor completions that do not follow their demonstrated patterns, as illustrated in Figure 3. The tractable likelihoods above allow us to measure how strongly the demonstrations support each candidate and use this signal for additional reward guidance. Let E ⊆[N]\T denote selected demonstration-output positions, and write z \ E for the canvas with these positions additionally masked. We compare the likelihood of the same candidate with and without these demonstration outputs visible: P1(u) := P(u | z), P0(u) := P(u | z \ E). Both distributions use the same decoding policy over the original target positions T, leaving the additional masks in E unresolved. Their log-likelihood ratio defines the reward R(u) := log P1(u) P0(u). (3) A higher reward indicates greater relative support from the demonstrations, rather than necessarily greater correctness. We incorporate this reward into generation by defining the guided distribution Qβ(u) := P1(u) exp(βR(u)) Zβ , (4) where β ≥0 controls guidance strength and Zβ is the normalizing constant 1. At β = 0, we recover unguided sampling from P1, which already conditions on the demonstrations. Increasing β favors candidates with higher rewards, potentially counteracting a pretrained model’s preference for familiar but demonstration-inconsistent completions. 1This form of reward guidance is also known as exponential tilting (Korbak et al., 2022) 6
