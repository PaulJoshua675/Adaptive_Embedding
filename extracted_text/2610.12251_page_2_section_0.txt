These objects exist by classical results. By Kleene’s recursion theorem a universal computer has programs that print their own text [10]; universal circuits and fixed-weight networks run any circuit or network whose code they are given [11, 12]; and von Neumann’s design separates construction from copying [3]. This paper builds them as ternary threshold networks and proves what they do. It gives exact sizes and depths for every parameter, shows where ternary weights and threshold units cost a level of depth, reproduces a stored network bit for bit, weights and biases included, and bounds the effect of noise. 1.1 Contributions (1) A network that runs stored networks. The ring machine holds K banks of S records and K tapes of L cells, with heads at absolute positions, and a fixed ternary netlist of depth 19 computes its step from every state, for every window, ring, bank and tape (Theorem 3.2). One pass over a bank evaluates the netlist stored in it, and every ternary unit compiles into records (Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4). (2) A self-reproducing constructor. An organism of 143 records with a description code of its own builds every network whose description fits a tape into the next bank, copies the description onto the next tape and hands control to what it built. Started on its own description it reproduces: the copies accumulate until every bank holds one, and from then on the state repeats every K generations (Theorems 4.5 and 4.6). (3) A universal computer. A family HA of ternary networks executes SUBLEQ, a computer with a single instruction [13], on 2A words of A bits. Its step has depth 13 at every word width, and the member with eight-bit words has 17,598 units (Proposition 5.9); a read and the branch each need two levels of ternary units (Lemma 5.7, Remark 5.5). A program of 21 instructions builds every byte string from a run-length description of it, and one of 27, which separates construction from copying as von Neumann did, makes the host emit the serialization of itself, its memory and its tape (Theorems 6.9 and 6.12). This is the quine scheme on a universal machine: a map outside the network turns the emitted bytes back into a running network. (4) A universal computer that reproduces by the dynamics. Stored in the ring machine, the host of every word width from 8 on runs the second program, builds every network that fits a bank and reproduces in every ring as the organism does, with no map outside the network; with eight-bit words it is 36,080 records (Theorems 7.4 to 7.6). (5) Reproduction under noise. Every orbit survives perturbations below 1/2 of each pre-activation. Replicating every unit r times, Pierce’s interwoven redundancy [14] with threshold units, mul- tiplies that margin by r. This suffices against Gaussian noise of any size on the pre-activations. Against von Neumann’s output flips with probability ε it suffices only for ε < 1/(2ek∗), k∗ the largest fan-in, and above 1/(2k∗) a disjunction of fan-in k∗fails with a probability that tends to one (Theorem 8.8, Proposition 8.10). A bound computed from the orbit is the exact probability that some unit of a step fails, as measured, and exceeds the rate at which the host leaves its orbit by the 5 to 6% of failures that the state does not show; a second-order bound certifies the complete run of the threefold host where the first-order bound exceeds one (Propositions 8.5 and 8.14). Every result is proved in Rocq for every word width, ring and size it names, except Lemma 6.3, which concerns the serialization format.
