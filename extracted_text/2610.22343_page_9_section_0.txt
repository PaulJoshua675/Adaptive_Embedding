Moreover, our predicted states con- sistently outperform camera-matched Copy-Last under both ground-truth and predicted cameras, showing that the learned scene dynamics also generalize beyond the training domain. 4.5 ABLATION STUDIES Geometric Reconstruction Quality. We first examine whether compressing GFM features into GeoState preserves their geometric information. Table 3 compares direct VGGT-Ωdepth predic- tions with depth reconstructed through our Scene Tokenizer and Geometry Decoder using ground- truth query cameras. Both use target-time RGB observations and are evaluated against ground-truth LiDAR depth. Their comparable accuracy shows that the fixed-length GeoState preserves the ge- ometric information required for accurate reconstruction, providing a compact and geometrically faithful representation for subsequent world modeling. Effect of the View-Decoupled Scene State. We compare GeoState with a feature-compression baseline that directly compresses VGGT-Ωfeatures into a 512 × 512 latent state. For a fair com- parison with the feature-compression baseline, only the target FRONT GFM output block is passed to the Scene Tokenizer. Both representations use the same world model, training data, forecast- ing objectives, optimization, and evaluation protocol; implementation details are provided in Ap- pendix A.5.4. As shown in Table 4, the two representations achieve comparable reconstruction quality, but GeoState is substantially easier to predict, improving AbsRel and δ1 at every horizon. Because the two representations achieve comparable reconstruction quality, the forecasting gap is unlikely to arise from reconstruction accuracy. Instead, the results suggest that GeoState provides a more predictable state space for temporal modeling. By separating scene content from viewpoint- dependent observation, GeoState reduces camera-induced variation in the state dynamics, allowing the World Model to focus more directly on the underlying evolution of scene geometry. 9
