The lazy neuron study (Li et al., 2023) documents the emergence of sparse ReLU activations in trained transformers. Exploiting the sparsity in ReLU networks for inference has long been explored in CNNs Parashar et al. (2017). More recently, ReLU Strikes Back (Mirzadeh et al., 2024) investigated ReLU-based LLMs, exploiting their activation patterns to reduce inference computation. These methods avoid loading weights corresponding to zero activations. Unfortunately, they do not generalize to training, different tokens activate different channels. 6 Experiments 6.1 Sparsity in LLMs We first examine activation sparsity in the feed-forward layers of two LLMs using ReLU2: a 162M-parameter Modded- NanoGPT transformer (Jordan et al., 2024a) and the 8B-parameter Nemotron-H-8B-Base-8K model. These models differ in both size and architecture: Modded-NanoGPT uses transformer blocks, whereas Nemotron-H combines attention, Mamba, and MLP blocks. Full details are given in Appendix E.1. The Modded-NanoGPT model is trained following the Track 3 baseline procedure. Activation sparsity is measured on the validation set every 300 steps for every layer. Figure 5 shows an initial sparsity of approximately ρ = 0.5, rapidly increasing to ρ = 0.82. This phenomenon has been observed in other works Haziza et al. (2025). Next, we evaluate the pretrained Nemotron-H model. The mean activation sparsity is 0.9273, with minimum batch-wise mean sparsity over 256 evaluation batches of 0.9176, corresponding to 92.73% and 91.76% sparsity, respectively. The sparsity is extremely consistent, with the minimum batch-wise mean sparsity only 0.97 percentage points below the mean, meaning activation compression can be used effectively. 6.2 Benchmarking and Ablations BisSparse is benchmarked in Table 2, which measures encoding and decoding time for a 1 GiB tensor with ρ = 0.5 and ρ = 0.9, with and without sign-bit packing. Decoding reaches 97.4% of the attainable bandwidth at ρ = 0.5 without sign-bit packing, while encoding is slower at 60.9%. BitSparse-sb is slower, since an additional kernel is needed. Higher sparsity reduces encoding time, although effective bandwidth utilization is lower since less data is moved. To assess the benefit of our custom kernels, we compare our Triton implementation with a pure PyTorch implementation of BitSparse on a 65,536 × 8,192 BF16 tensor. Both versions produce identical outputs. The Triton implementation en- codes 7.59× faster and decodes 7.91× faster, while reducing peak allocated memory by 37.5% and 21.9%, respectively (Table 12, Appendix E.4). The fused backward gradient kernel is compared with the standard PyTorch version on a 16,000 × 21,504 BF16 tensor with 50% sparsity. BitSparse reduces peak allocated memory by 46.8% for ReLU and 60.9% for ReLU2, while reducing runtime by 17.2% and 39.2%, respectively.
