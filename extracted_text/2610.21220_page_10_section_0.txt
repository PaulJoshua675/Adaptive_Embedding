Motivated by this observation, we propose RecSoup, which mitigates parameter interference through pruning and enables con- trollable trade-offs via a lightweight gating network. Empirical results further indicate that model-merging approaches offer a more efficient and effective paradigm than parameter-generation approaches for controllable multi-objective recommendation. For MMR evaluation, the recommenda- tion model first generates a candidate pool containing the top-100 items for each user, and MMR reranking is applied to produce the final top-100 recommendation list. The relevance term is aligned with the accuracy objective, while the diversity term is computed using Jaccard dissimilarity over category-level one-hot item en- codings. We evaluate 11 controllable preference vectors satisfying 𝜇𝑎𝑐𝑐+ 𝜇𝑑𝑖𝑣= 1, where 𝜇𝑎𝑐𝑐∈{0.0, 0.1, . . . , 1.0}. Coefficient Learner Architecture. The coefficient learner is im- plemented as a lightweight routing module conditioned on the preference vector. In all experiments, we use a single-layer linear router. The routing weights are initialized using a Gaussian distri- bution with standard deviation 0.01. During training, the backbone model and expert task vectors are frozen, and only the routing parameters are updated. The coefficient learner is optimized using Adam with learning rate 0.001, batch size 512. DARE [45] randomly drops task-vector parameters followed by rescaling. CABS [44] performs sequential sparse pruning to reduce overlap among task vectors, and LwPTV [47] prunes layer-wise salient parameters with low inter- task deviation. RecSoup achieves the highest average hypervolume, controllability, and Pareto-front coverage, while also obtaining the best average ranking among all methods. In particular, RecSoup consistently outperforms DARE in all settings and demonstrates stronger overall Pareto efficiency than CABS and LwPTV, indicat- ing that incorporating cross-objective relevance into the pruning process leads to more robust controllable model merging.
