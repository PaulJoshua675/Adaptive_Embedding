The relative-error magnitude, by contrast, does not separate the two groups as cleanly: in the in-graph setting, interaction and linear are essentially identical (0.286 vs. 0.288), since overall surrogate fit quality there is dominated by factors common to both mechanism types (finite training, the downstream model’s own regularization) rather than by the interaction term specifically; in the omitted-variable setting, both groups show substantial error because removing a parent’s edge deletes its entire linear contribution regardless of mechanism type, which dominates the error budget for both. We therefore treat the correlation test as the primary evidence that the misallocation is systematic and tied to the interaction structure, and report relative error only to establish that indirect-effect errors of practically meaningful size do occur — not as an independent confirmation that interaction specifically causes larger errors than an equivalent linear mechanism. Despite these errors, the learned surrogate still achieves close to oracle-level Spearman correlation with the true CATE on the main benchmark (Section 4.1). 41
