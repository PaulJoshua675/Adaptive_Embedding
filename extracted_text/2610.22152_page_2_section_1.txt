2.1 Specification Gaming and Reward Hacking Specification gaming – an agent exploiting a literal reward specification in a way that diverges from the designer’s intent – has been documented extensively across both simulated and deployed systems [Krakovna et al., 2020]. These cases are largely catalogued observationally: a system is found, after deployment or training, to have discovered an unintended shortcut. Skalse et al. [2022] take a step toward formal rigor, characterizing when a reward function admits a hacking policy relative to an intended true objective, distinguishing reward hacking from ordinary reward misspecification more broadly. We adopt this proxy/true framing directly, but construct an environment where the gap is specified by design rather than discovered after the fact, which lets us pair the demonstration with a mitigation whose effect on that specific, known gap can be measured exactly. 2.2 Reward Shaping and Policy Invariance Dense, informative reward signals are often necessary for an RL agent to discover a sparse or delayed objective within a practical training budget. Ng et al. [1999] show that reward shaping of the form F(s, a, s′) = γϕ(s′) −ϕ(s), for any potential function ϕ, leaves the optimal policy of the underlying MDP unchanged – shaping accelerates discovery of an optimum without altering what that optimum is. This guarantee is what allows us to introduce dense positional shaping in Section 3.3 without that shaping itself becoming a second, uncontrolled source of proxy/true divergence. 2.3 Constrained Reinforcement Learning Constrained Markov decision processes [Altman, 1999] formalize policy optimization subject to a secondary cost objective remaining below a threshold, typically enforced via a Lagrangian relaxation. Achiam et al. [2017] extend this to deep RL with a trust-region method with per-update constraint satisfaction guarantees. The present work uses the simpler, fixed-multiplier end of this family (Section 3.4): rather than tuning the Lagrange multiplier via dual ascent, we fix it and instead derive a problem-specific identity (Section 4) that lets a hard cap on the cost signal translate directly into a deterministic bound on true-objective performance. We treat an implementation of the dual-ascent or hard-constrained variant, in the style of Achiam et al. [2017], as natural future work rather than a contribution of the present paper. 2.4 Positioning Our contribution sits at the intersection of these threads: we construct a specification-gaming environment in the spirit of Krakovna et al.
