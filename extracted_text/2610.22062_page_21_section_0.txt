1 Supplementary Material for Documenting Trofinetide Treatment Phenotypes in Rett Syndrome with a Dual-LLM Consensus Pipeline SUPPLEMENTARY NOTES Supplementary Note S1. Inter-LLM Agreement and the Consensus Gate Because only diarrhea has an expert ground truth, we also measured the agreement between the two independently prompted agents, which requires no labels. Cohen’s κ between LLM 1 (clinical NLP expert) and LLM 2 (GI/neurology specialist) was computed over the 60,530 note–phenotype instances that both models classified (Figure S1A). Overall agreement was high (κ = 0.930, raw agreement 96.6%), and all 17 phenotypes reached κ ≥0.73, with per-phenotype values in Table S3. Seven of the 60,537 matches did not return a valid YES or NO from both models and were scored negative. Because the two prompts were written independently, their agreement is a proxy for how well specified a phenotype is. It does not track the per-phenotype expert agreement from the spot-check, however. Attention/alertness has the lowest κ (0.732) yet reached 6/6 on review, while dysautonomia (κ = 0.917) and refusing medication (κ = 0.890) reached only 4/6. With six cases per phenotype the spot-check cannot resolve differences of that size, so the two signals should be read as independent rather than concordant. Diarrhea is scored by the diarrhea-specific pipeline rather than by the presence prompt, and reaches κ = 0.878 at raw agreement 98.0% over the 2,260 notes both agents classified. It is therefore shown separately from the 17, as a hatched bar in Figure S1A and below the rule in Table S3. Prompt wording drives agreement. The two diarrhea prompts isolate what wording alone contributes, because both were applied to the identical 2,260 notes with the same two models. The deployed prompt V0 reaches raw agreement 95.1% and κ = 0.805, and the reported prompt V2 reaches 98.0% and κ = 0.878 (Table S9). Specifying the target more tightly leaves the two agents less room to differ, and the same effect appears at the cell level across the prompt ladder (Table S10). Among the 17 phenotypes the two lowest values are attention/alertness (κ = 0.732) and the open-ended other-side-effects category (0.756), which are also the two whose target is least concretely specifiable. Inter-LLM disagreement may therefore be a useful label-free signal for which phenotypes most need prompt refinement or human review, although the present spot-check is too small to validate it. What the consensus gate costs and buys. Across the full corpus the two agents disagreed on 3.4% of instances (Figure S1B, with raw counts in Table S9). The gate resolves these disagreements conservatively, counting only “both YES” instances (38.4%) as positive and treating one-sided calls as negative. This is how it suppresses one-sided false positives, at some cost in recall. On the diarrhea ground truth the mechanism is measurable but small. Under the deployed prompt, the only configuration for which both agents were also scored alone, the gate changes two of 183 cells against the stronger of them (Table S10, panel A), so it neither costs nor buys measurable accuracy there. The case for two agents rests on making their disagreement observable across the 17 phenotypes that have no ground truth.
