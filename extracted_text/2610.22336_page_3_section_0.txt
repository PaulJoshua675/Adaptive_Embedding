Finally, we formulate robustness to shifts in the patient dynamics as a Markov game between the controller and the context over an ambiguity set of transition kernels [29], and find that the resulting kernel-robust policy nearly Pareto-dominates the nominally trained policy and AT50 across all the conditions we consider. Specifically, the kernel-robust policy (1) performs nearly as well as the nominally trained policy in the nominal model, (2) substantially outperforms every other type of policy under shifts in the dynamics, and (3) substantially improves on the nominally trained policy and AT50 even under errors in the observation. Our findings suggest that robustness to shifts in the patient dynamics can be attained at almost no price in the nominal model, and that the resulting policy remains robust against a broad spectrum of errors in the observation and shifts in the patient dynamics. In view of this, we believe that robust RL holds great promise as a principled methodology for learning adaptive therapy policies. The rest of this manuscript is organized as follows. In Section 2.3, we further introduce the robust counterparts of the contextual MDP against errors in the observation and shifts in the patient dynamics. 2.1 Contextual Markov Decision Processes We consider a finite-horizon contextual MDP [19,44] with state space X, action space A, observation space Y, horizon T, and context space C. Each context c ∈C specifies an initial distribution µc over X, a transition kernel Pc, an observation map Oc : X →Y, and a reward function rc : X × A →R, where Pc(· | x, a) denotes the distribution of the next state when action a is taken in state x. At the beginning of an episode, a context c is drawn from a population distribution D and remains fixed thereafter, after which the initial state is drawn as x0 ∼µc, and the state at every period t evolves as xt+1 ∼Pc(· | xt, at). The decision maker observes neither c nor the state xt, and receives at period t the observation yt = Oc(xt). Consequently, the action at period t can only be based on the history ht = (y0, a0, . . . , at−1, yt) of past observations and actions, i.e., a policy π = (πt)t≥0 selects at ∼πt(· | ht), and Π denotes a given class of such history-dependent policies. For each context c, let J(π; c) := Eπ c hPT −1 t=0 rc(xt, at) i denote the expected cumulative reward of π, where Eπ c denotes the expectation over the trajectory generated by π under µc and Pc. We consider max π∈Π J(π; D) := Ec∼D [J(π; c)] .
