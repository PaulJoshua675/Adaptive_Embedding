Online resource savings. Beyond the improvements in business metrics, WeGR substantially reduces online serving costs. The key reason is that WeGR replaces, rather than simply supplements, a large fraction of candidates produced by conventional retrieval channels. In V1.2, cutting off 80% of the candidates from the other retrievers reduces the number of items entering pre-ranking to 61.90% of the baseline, i.e., 38.10% fewer items need to be scored. Since pre-ranking performs item-wise feature processing and model inference over the merged candidate set, this reduction directly decreases its CPU and GPU workloads. As shown in Figure 3, the normalized CPU and GPU usages decrease from 100% to 74.5% and 73.9%, corresponding to resource savings of 25.5% and 26.1%, respectively. The resource reduction is smaller than the reduction in scored items because the online pipeline still incurs fixed overhead from retrieval, request processing, and other serving components. These results demonstrate that WeGR improves online effectiveness while alleviating computation cost of the downstream cascade. Online latency reduction. WeGR also improves serving efficiency across two production search scenarios. As reported in Table 5, it reduces the average online latency by 21.7 ms in WeChat Chan- nels Search and by 15.4 ms in WeChat Search. These consistent latency reductions are primarily attributable to the reduced number of candidates passed to the pre-ranking stage, thereby lowering the computational overhead of per-item feature processing and model inference. Together with the online A/B testing gains, the results demonstrate that WeGR improves business performance while reducing serving latency in industrial search systems. Industrial search systems operate over a continuously changing item corpus and user interests. It is therefore important to examine whether the retrieval quality of WeGR remains stable as the system evolves. Figure 4 reports the daily Click and Order HR@200 from June 19 to August 19, 2026. Despite short-term fluctuations, both metrics exhibit a clear upward trend. Specifically, the average Click HR@200 increases from 69.98% in the first seven days to 76.99% in the last seven days, while the corresponding Order HR@200 rises from 80.24% to 87.29%. The Order HR@200 remains consistently higher than the Click HR@200 throughout the observation period, indicating that WeGR effectively retrieves high-value items asso- ciated with downstream conversions. These results demonstrate that WeGR maintains robust retrieval quality over time and bene- fits from continuously accumulated interaction signals rather than degrading as the item corpus and user interests evolve. 2) Which user groups contribute most to the online gains of WeGR? We divide users into six groups according to their lifecycle status: active existing users (UG1), reactivated existing users (UG2), lapsed existing users (UG3), active new users (UG4), reactivated new users (UG5), and lapsed new users (UG6). As shown in Table 6, WeGR yields positive Order, CTR, and CVR gains across all six groups, demonstrating that its online improvements are not driven by a single user cohort. The gains are particularly pronounced among reactivated and lapsed new users. UG5 achieves the largest improvements in Order and CTR, reaching 9.855% and 3.448%, re- spectively, while UG6 obtains the highest CVR gain of 7.382%.
