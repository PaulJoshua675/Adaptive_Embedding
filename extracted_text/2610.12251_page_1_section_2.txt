McCulloch and Pitts introduced networks of binary threshold units as a logical calculus of neural activity [1], and von Neumann used their notation for the logical design of the stored-program computer [2]. He later asked how a machine can build a copy of itself, and answered with a universal constructor: given the description of a machine, it builds that machine, and, supplied with its own description and a mechanism that copies descriptions, it builds a copy of itself together with a copy of the description [3]. The self-reproducing automata that followed [4, 5, 6, 7, 8], surveyed in [9], reproduce configurations of a cellular space under a fixed transition rule. This paper asks the same question of threshold networks: whether the weights of a threshold network can be reproduced exactly by the dynamics of a threshold network. The networks have weights in {−1, 0, 1} and integer thresholds and are iterated as a fixed composition of layers, so that each is a map F : {0, 1}n →{0, 1}n whose behaviour is the orbit of an initial state. Exactness comes from the integers. A trained network that outputs an approximation of its weights loses information in every generation, while an orbit of integer pre-activations carries a weight set bit for bit and survives every perturbation of a pre-activation below one half. We answer it with a fixed ternary network that holds other networks as data. Its state is a ring of banks of records with a tape for each bank. A record is a two-input unit of a stored network, and each step evaluates one record of the active bank. The fixed network plays the part of von Neumann’s transition rule and the stored networks the part of his configurations.
