Figure 5a varies the memory window of REKI at finetuning and at evaluation, on LIBERO-Plus after finetuning on LIBERO, on RoboTwin Clean and on RoboCasa365. Finetuned and evaluated with three frames (3 →3), REKI reaches 90.4 on LIBERO-Plus, 73.4 on RoboTwin and 38.4 on RoboCasa365, against 88.9, 67.9 and 33.0 without memory (1 →1). Most of the gain already comes with the first past frame (3 →2: 90.1, 73.6 and 39.3). As every frame of a training window is supervised (Eq. (6)), the model finetuned with memory also learns to act without it. Evaluated without memory (3 →1), it performs close to the model finetuned at K=1 (88.9, 67.2 and 34.9), so the gain comes mainly from reading the past frames at inference, not from training on windows. Oscillation. For each arm, we compare how it moves during one chunk with how it moved during the previous one, ignoring small movements. Figure 5b counts the episodes in which an arm moves in roughly the opposite direction at three or more consecutive chunks, that is, it goes back and forth at least twice (Fig. 7). We set this criterion on 16 of the 50 tasks and confirmed the effect of memory on the other 34. Fewer than 5% of these episodes succeed. With the same weights, removing the past frames at evaluation raises their share from 4.1% to 14.8%, and one past frame already brings it down to 4.6%. Trained without memory, REKI oscillates in 18.6% of the episodes, and in half of those that fail. The difference is largest in two-arm tasks (Fig. 5c). Why memory helps. A policy without memory maps the current observation to the next chunk. If two poses A and B each look like demonstration states that continue towards the other, the chunk predicted at A leads to B, and the chunk predicted at B leads back to A.
