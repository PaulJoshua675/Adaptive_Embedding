It is a property of the nudge form itself, not of the solver or the arithmetic: reducing the relaxation step size at fixed nudge time makes it worse — at β = 3 the norm grows by a factor of nearly 400 between dt = 1 and 0.1 — and in float64 the collapse occurs at the same β, the two arithmetics lying on top of each other from β = 1 upward. The linear nudge is unaffected by the step size, and shows no collapse in either arithmetic. Solver and relaxation budgets. The implicit solve’s parameter update is insensitive to the number of conjugate-gradient steps, giving cos(∆θIFT, ∆θBP) = 1.0000 at every nCG from 1 to 50 and at every γ tested, which is expected when the relaxation Hessian is a perturbation of the identity (App. E). The truncation therefore contributes nothing, leaving the O(γ) correspondence bias as the solve’s only error. The parameter update also remains exact at the small γ where Section 4.1 finds the inference velocity arithmetic-limited, since a floor-level perturbation to vPCN barely rotates the direction of the parameter gradient it produces. The two remaining EP budget axes, Tnudge and Kh, are likewise flat from a single sweep (Table 4), for the deadbeat reason of App. E, so Tfree is the only budget with a threshold, and the cost of an EP update is essentially the free-phase equilibration alone. 25
