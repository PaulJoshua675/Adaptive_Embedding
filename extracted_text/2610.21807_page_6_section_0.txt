Word counts are document-level, so a single post’s repetition cannot dominate the statistic; words with fewer than 15 combined occurrences were discarded. The best-answer comparison restricts to threads with ≥ 2 competing answers and an explicit best answer (15,104 threads, 35,980 non-best answers), comparing the chosen best answers against the passed-over answers. Because this pooled comparison ignores the matched thread structure and weights questions by their number of answers, the headline nouns are additionally validated with a within-thread permutation test: the best/other labels are shuffled 500 times within each thread, and each reported noun’s observed |z| is compared against its permutation distribution. The engagement compar­ ison splits questions with ≥ 1 answer-tier post into high (≥ 75th percentile: ≥ 4 responses; n = 6,747) and low (≤ 25th percentile: ≤ 1 response; n = 9,602) groups by total answers plus replies, comparing question-text word usage; the percentile split is used only for this descriptive lexical contrast, and a tertile split yields closely overlapping top-noun lists. 3.5 Semantic noun clustering Word-level keyness cannot show whether related nouns form a coherent group. A con­ tinuous bag-of-words Word2Vec model (gensim 4.4.0, single-threaded for bit-reproduc­ ibility) was trained on the noun-tokenized corpus, and the embeddings of the 800 most frequent nouns were L2-normalized, PCA-reduced, and clustered with HDBSCAN [18]. Parameters were fixed by a pre-stated rule over a grid of PCA dimensionality, minimum cluster size, minimum samples, and cluster-selection epsilon (Table 3): among settings yielding 6 to 15 clusters whose largest holds at most 50% of classified nouns, take the one maximizing frequency-weighted coverage. Unassigned words (“noise”) stay unclassified. The chosen solution (12 clusters, 50.3% of noun-token frequency) is validated in Table 8 for coherence, parameter stability, and sensitivity to dropping reply and thanks posts, with each cluster’s similarity to the “logistics” vector as an interpretive aid. For visualization only (Fig. 4), the same vectors are projected with UMAP. 3.6 Cluster-level regressions To separate a cluster’s association with an outcome from simply writing more, each post was reduced to cluster-presence indicators plus log(1 + character length); a robust­ ness check replaces indicators with log(1 + cluster token count). Best-answer selection is modeled with a conditional logistic regression stratified by question, comparing each selected answer against its own competitors and thereby absorbing all question-level characteristics. Controls are log length, chronological order within the thread, and question-answer noun overlap (Jaccard similarity). The originally submitted pooled logistic model with question-clustered standard errors is retained as a robustness check. Question engagement is modeled in two parts so that the 16,104 questions (39%) with­ out responses are not excluded: a logistic regression for receiving any response, and a negative binomial for the number of responses among answered questions (Poisson is overdispersed, Pearson χ²/df ≈ 1.45). Both use the 40,836 questions with a usable time­ stamp and non-empty text, of which 24,756 are answered, and control for log length and centered posting year. Robustness checks separate answers from replies and refit on the 2004–2014 and 2015–2026 subperiods.
