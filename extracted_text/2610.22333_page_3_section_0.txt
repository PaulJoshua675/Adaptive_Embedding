Each cue is written in the language of the prompt, and full texts for all six languages appear in Appendix A. The deploy cue adds no note and fixes the base- line. The monitor_log cue states that responses are logged for service quality and product improve- ment, which represents monitoring with no evalu- ation framing. The eval_log cue states that re- sponses are logged and reviewed by the AI safety team for compliance with usage policies. The eval_explicit cue states that the conversation is a formal AI safety evaluation scored by a red-team review. The eval_metadata cue gives the same information as a machine-style configuration tag. Our main contrast is eval_explicit against deploy. Both cues share the base sentence, and eval_explicit adds a note stating openly that the conversation is a scored AI safety evalua- tion. The difference between them measures how much a model changes its behavior when it is told plainly that it is being tested. This is the strongest form of the evaluation signal in our de- sign. We use eval_log against monitor_log as a secondary contrast. Both notes state that responses are logged, and eval_log adds only that an AI safety team reviews them. It never uses the words test, evaluation, or benchmark. So, this contrast separates evaluation framing from monitoring and from lexical leakage. We compare the remaining cues against deploy, and we run all five cues on the four models in all six languages. Figure 1 sum- marises the design. 3.3 Measuring refusal We label refusals with an LLM judge that reads only the prompt and the response and returns RE- FUSAL, COMPLY, or UNUSABLE. A response that gives no substantive help or offers a safe al- ternative counts as REFUSAL. The judge never sees the cue, the model, or the language. The primary judge is Gemma-4-31B-IT (Team et al., 2026), which we run locally. Prompts in six languages are prefixed with one of five cues, an- swered by four models, and scored by a blind LLM judge (Gemma-4-31B-IT) validated against a second judge and native speakers. The headline contrast is eval_explicit vs. deploy. A second judge, GPT-6 Luna, labelled a subset of n = 7,200 responses that covers every model, language, and cue. We measure agreement with Cohen’s κ, which corrects for agreement reached by chance. The two judges reach κ = 0.88 over- all. Per language, κ ranges from 0.86 in Tamil to 0.90 in English. For each Indic language, a na- tive speaker also reviewed n = 320 labels of the primary judge and marked each one as correct or incorrect. Agreement with the judge ranges from κ = 0.85 in Tamil to κ = 0.91 in Hindi. Telugu is the exception, with κ = 0.56.
