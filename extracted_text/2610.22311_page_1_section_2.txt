Video world models are emerging as a practical interface for interactive simulation. A user specifies an action trajectory or camera motion, and the model rolls the world forward by generating future video frames. This direction builds on rapid progress in video diffusion transformers and large video foundation models (Ho et al., 2020; Rombach et al., 2022; Peebles & Xie, 2023; Blattmann et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024b; Yang et al., 2024b; Kong et al., 2024; Wan et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2025). Recent works increasingly target controllable long video worlds rather than isolated short clips (Alonso et al., 2024; Valevski et al., 2024; Bruce et al., 2024; Parker-Holder et al., 2024; Sand.ai, 2025; Gao et al., 2026; HY-World et al., 2026). For these models, context management is a central methodological challenge. Past frames carry the appearance, geometry, and object state that future frames must respect. Without access to the right history, long rollouts drift, forget revisited regions, or re-synthesize the same scene inconsistently. The most direct way to preserve this history is to retain an ever-growing visual context. Chunked or causal video diffusion models let each new chunk attend to historical frame tokens through a KV cache. Dense FlashAttention makes this practical at moderate lengths (Dao et al., 2022; Dao, 2023), and causal diffusion or self-forcing training makes long rollouts natural (Chen et al., 2024a; Huang et al., 2025b). The cost, however, grows with the number of generated chunks. Later chunks inherit larger context even when the current camera overlaps only a small part of the past scene. As a result, chunk-wise generation becomes progressively slower exactly when long-horizon consistency matters most. This motivates context selection rather than naive context accumulation. Frame- and view-memory methods select a smaller set of relevant past observations. Context-as-Memory retrieves historical views by camera field-of-view overlap, and VMem stores surfel-indexed views for consistent re- visits (Yu et al., 2025a; Li et al., 2025).
