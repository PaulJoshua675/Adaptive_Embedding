To identify where agents fail, we present each scenario at four task instruction levels with progressively less in- formation (from a fully specified problem (L0) down to the sponsor’s original request, which may not address the underlying problem (L3)). The environment, stakeholders, and grader remain unchanged across all four levels. Be- cause only the task instruction varies, differences in performance isolate the effect of the information withheld. We evaluate 13 model configurations from seven providers using this setup. Five main findings emerge. • Even the best model configuration falls well short of the reference solution. At L3, Claude Opus 5.5 achieves the highest mean score of 0.56 and resolves 14 of 95 tasks, while five of the thirteen configurations resolve none. Selecting the best-performing configuration for each task still yields a score of only 0.70, with 30 tasks resolved. • Discovering unstated constraints is the primary bottleneck for leading models. When the relevant rules and constraints are provided, the three leading models score 0.73–0.82. Requiring these models to discover the constraints reduces scores sharply to 0.39–0.62. On the original tasks, L3 scores are at most 0.08 points lower than L1 scores. • Obtaining the necessary information does not ensure effective delivery. At L3, leading models obtain 72–85% of the key facts available from stakeholders, yet the delivered systems achieve only 0.60–0.70 of the reference improvement. Stakeholder contact cost reduces the final scores by a further 0.14–0.22. • Weak models can leave customers worse off than if no changes had been made. Claude Haiku 4.5 scores below the do-nothing baseline on 79 of 95 tasks, while systems delivered by Mistral Small 4 fall below this baseline on 51 tasks even before deducting stakeholder contact cost. • No model performs well across all scenarios. In five of the 20 scenarios, no model achieves a mean score of at least 0.5. Across tasks within a scenario, the median score range for a leading model is 0.34–0.44. We make three contributions. First, we introduce FDE-Gym, a benchmark that evaluates whether agents can identify the underlying customer problem and deliver a working solution that measurably improves business outcomes. Second, we develop a methodology for constructing scenarios that combines task variants, scripted stakeholders, and predefined scoring anchors to support reproducible evaluation and limit rewards for literal compliance and shortcut solutions. Third, we evaluate 13 model configurations and provide an online platform where humans and agents can practice delivery under the same grading criteria. 2 Anatomy of a Task Each task represents a single customer engagement involving one customer (the organization that hires the agent, whose people are the stakeholders the agent can question), one sponsor (the customer-side person who assigns the task), and one production system to improve.
