Full results for (a) to (d) are in Appendix Tables 4 to 6. Pretraining gains. Trained from scratch on each benchmark, REKI reaches 52.0 on Clean2Rand, on par with Qwen-RobotManip (52.3) and ahead of StarVLA (36.6), which both have larger VLMs (4.5B and 4.4B). REKI is ahead of both under the Hard condition (26.3 against 22.6 and 10.6). On LIBERO- Plus it leads with 86.1, ahead of Qwen-RobotManip (78.3) and StarVLA (75.0). Pretraining on public data benefits REKI most out of distribution, and in particular under the RoboTwin randomisations (Appendix Tables 4 and 5). In distribution, it adds 2.9 and 0.8 points on RoboTwin and 1.0 on LIBERO. Out of distribution, it adds 13.8 points on Clean2Rand and 4.3 on LIBERO-Plus, and the gain grows where REKI without pretraining fails most, with 30.3 points under clutter and 22.4 under the Hard condition. On LIBERO-Plus, the gain is also largest under perturbed robot states (11.7). Additional data or supervision. REKI is outperformed by several larger and more recent models that add data or supervision to the vanilla recipe, with large in-house data collections (Xiaomi Robotics Team, 2026) or video generation models (Song et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026). Similarly, InternVLA- A1.5 uses the same VLM as REKI, with an additional video generation model, a larger action expert (24 layers vs 10 for REKI) and a multi-stage pretraining, and is ahead in distribution on RoboTwin (93.3 vs 85.9). Out of distribution, it is behind on LIBERO-Plus (84.8 vs 90.4). Qwen-RobotManip, pretrained with additional synthetic data, gains more from pretraining than REKI (16.2 vs 13.8 points on Clean2Rand).
