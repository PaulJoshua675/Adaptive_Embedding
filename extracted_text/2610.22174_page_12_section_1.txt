In this paper, we first identify a mismatch between the single-session assumption underlying most ex- isting agentic RL methods and advanced agentic inference techniques such as context compaction and subagent forking. This motivates our formulation of Multi-Session Agentic Reinforcement Learning, which supports an arbitrary count of sessions within a trajectory. To address the coarse supervision issue existed in the currently widely used broadcasting approach, we further propose Mosaic, a multi-session agentic RL method with critic-free hierarchical optimization. By combining session- aware trajectory-level advantage estimation with session-level advantage shaping, Mosaic trains a Qwen3.5-4B agent that outperforms both single-session and multi-session baselines on BrowseComp. Ablation studies confirm the contribution of each component, while further analyses suggest that effective multi-session optimization might depend on improving session utility and allocation, a pattern observed in both broadcasting baseline and our Mosaic. We hope these findings will inform future research on multi-session agentic RL. 12
