GPD goodness of fit (Tab. 2). We audit the per-query fit directly on a 5% sample. The fit never falls back to counting for lack of tail data (failure rate ≤0.0%). The bounded constraint is not doing all the work: the unconstrained maximum-likelihood shape is already ξ ≤0 for most queries, and the cap ξ ≤0 binds in only 10%–30% of them (more often for the heavier-tailed backbones), where it prevents a physically impossible power-law tail. The median fitted shape is negative and the median fitted upper endpoint is ≈0.84—safely below the cosine ceiling 1, and query-specific rather than pinned. The one residual concern is the ∼20–30% of fits whose endpoint falls below the positive’s own score s⋆(Tab. 2): there the tail model treats the positive as effectively immune, which is right for well-separated positives but a coarse approximation for marginal ones—an error absorbed, like the top-k truncation term, into the end-to-end 100M validation rather than bounded in closed form. Why the tail estimate suffices for mAP. Empirically the truth sits at the tail edge of the count/tail bracket (Sec. 4), and the metric’s response to hardness error explains why. The top-rank survival (1 −xq)N is exponential and amplifies error in ˆxq; mAP’s per-positive precision j/(j + N ˆxq,j) is a gently varying rational response that damps the tail estimate’s over-shoot, so for tail-dominated reporting scales (mAP@1000 at 100M) the tail estimate alone is nearly exact (Tab. 1), while on shallower pools the count edge over-predicts from above.
