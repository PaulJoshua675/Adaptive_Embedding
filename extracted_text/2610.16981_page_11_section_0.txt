Frequent token-sequence patterns among weighted shortest paths. Rectangle size represents the number of borrow–repay paths. The percentages are calculated relative to the total number of paths. Because direct paths and patterns that occur fewer than 500 times are omitted, the percentages in the figure do not sum to 100%. For each shortest path, we strip transaction hashes and account addresses, retaining only the ordered sequence of tokens. Direct repayment without token changes is the most common path (46.88%), but there is little information on the use of borrowed assets. Thus, we exclude it and focus on paths involving more than one token in the sequence. Fig. 4 presents an icicle plot of patterns occurring in at least 500 borrow–repay pairs, covering 41.35% of all paths. The most frequent interpretable patterns are relatively simple round-trip swaps or Aave supply–withdraw cycles. Round-trip swaps, where borrowers swap the borrowed asset for another token and back before repayment, involve USDT → USDC →USDT (3.4%) and USDC →USDT →USDC (2.6%). Supply–withdraw cycles, in which depositing the borrowed asset to Aave and withdrawing it for repay- ment, include USDC →aEthUSDC →USDC (3.1%) and USDT →aEthUSDT →USDT (1.2%). Longer sequences combine both patterns, indicating the borrowed asset is swapped, supplied to Aave for an aToken, withdrawn, and exchanged back before repayment, e.g., USDC →USDT →aEthUSDT →USDT →USDC (1.7%) and the same pattern for borrowed USDT swapped with USDC (1.2%). However, the frequent patterns also reveal shortcuts that should not be read as economic strategies. Many shortest paths route through debt-position tokens as an intermediate—most prominently WETH →variableDebtEthWETH →WETH (8.8%), and similarly for USDC (3.2%) and USDT (2.8%) because no valid shortest path exists otherwise, so the weighted search falls back on this shortcut. Other patterns skip expected intermediate steps: USDC →WETH →aEthWETH → USDC (3.1%) omits an explicit return from aEthWETH to WETH, while USDC → aEthWETH →USDC (3.1%) bypasses both WETH conversion steps entirely. Such patterns may arise from fallback teleport edges or unresolved multi-contract interactions; whether they have a systematic cause remains an open question and a priority for future work.
