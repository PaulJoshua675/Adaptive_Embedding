We introduce Idea2Prior, an extension of PaperArena and its implementation [1, 2]. The contribution is an engineering and evaluation framework for inspectable idea-to-paper retrieval: it preserves search queries, candidate identities, assessments, filtering reasons, PDF availability, and the final ranking. This makes it possible to distinguish a discovery miss from a candidate that was retrieved but lost during filtering, or from a task that failed operationally. A high-level result alone cannot make those distinctions. The central design separates inexpensive assessment of a broad candidate pool from a bounded examination of document evidence. After abstract assessment and citation expansion, an Expert model jointly considers up to 60 candidates. Five papers are selected for PDF acquisition; unavailable PDFs are not replaced by additional papers in retrieval-only mode. Complete available abstracts remain in the Expert input, along with evidence availability and up to six locally selected PDF passages per acquired paper. A missing PDF does not make a paper irrelevant and does not prevent it from ranking first. The optional full-report mode retains downstream synthesis for users who need a written literature analysis. The revised evaluation covers 30 query cases: ten from each of three prompt tiers, with shared targets across tiers. The reported aggregate Hit@5 is 16/30 for Idea2Prior, 13/30 for ChatGPT web Deep Research, and 11/30 for Gemini web Deep Research. The ChatGPT total includes one convention-based credit, disclosed in Section 5.1. Idea2Prior leads both baselines by 20 percentage points on EASY and MEDIUM, but trails ChatGPT by 10 points on HARD. Mean selected-run latency is 8.89 minutes, and a separately reported nine-query cost sample averages $0.0945. Thus the present evidence shows a recovery advantage in two tiers and a low observed API cost, with longer latency than the supplied browser averages. Our contributions are: 1. An auditable retrieval-and-ranking workflow with identity-aware candidate records, semantic citation filtering, and a joint Top 5 decision over a bounded shortlist. 2. A retrieval-only execution mode that preserves abstract and PDF evidence assessment while omitting report-specific processing, exposed through local web, Python, CLI, and Notebook interfaces. 3. A three-system historical evaluation with explicit target-recovery, agreement, latency, token, and cost accounting, together with preserved prompts and outputs. 4. Optional implementation-analysis, citation-evolution, planning, benchmark- landscape, and citation-gap extensions, with their earlier exploratory evaluations distinguished from the retrieval benchmark. 2
