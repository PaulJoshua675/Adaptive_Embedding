We introduced Realtime-Bench, a suite of diverse dynamic game and web tasks for evaluating real- time interactive capability in multimodal agents. Our evaluation of 12 frontier models shows that real-time interaction remains challenging: under Wall Clock, the best success rate reaches only 44.0%, and the best Passˆ3 is only 36%. To disentangle the effects of serving latency and models’ real-time streaming capability, we compared three clock protocols, introducing Token Clock to pre- serve the temporal cost of deliberation independently of physical serving speed. Substantial gaps relative to Paused Clock persist under Token Clock, highlighting the difficulty of translating task competence into timely action. Across the six models analyzed under different reasoning efforts, higher effort improves success under Paused Clock but has mixed effects under temporal pressure. Relaxing the temporal cost of generation generally improves performance but does not ensure suc- cess. These findings motivate adaptive reasoning with temporal awareness and continuous percep- tion and action, allowing agents to coordinate deliberation and execution with the time available. Realtime-Bench provides a controlled setting for studying these capabilities and measuring progress toward effective real-time interaction. 10
