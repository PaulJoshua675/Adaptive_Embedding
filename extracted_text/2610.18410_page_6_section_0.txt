Graph-free Retrieval with Graph-Modified Embeddings Preprint Original embeddings encode chunk text or, for document and entity nodes, their names. We embed nodes and queries with OpenAI’s text-embedding-3-small, using 1,536 dimensions. Each transformation produces one modified vector per node. We denote the original and modified embeddings of node i by xi and zi, respectively. Appendix B lists all evaluated transformations and hyperparameter configurations. One-hop neighbour blend (hop-1). For each node i, we consider its one-hop neighbours. We define Si as the 20 % of these nodes with the lowest cosine similarity to the original embedding xi, selecting at least two whenever available. The modified embedding zi combines the original vector with the mean of the original embeddings of the selected neighbours: zi = αxi + (1 −α) 1 |Si| X j∈Si xj, (1) where α = 0.2. Selecting the least semantically similar one-hop neighbours is intended to introduce graph-related information that is not already encoded in the source representation. Two-hop neighbour blend (hop-2). This method uses the same selection and blending procedure, applied to nodes at graph distance exactly 2. Convex neighbour pull. Let Z collect the modified node embeddings and E denote the graph’s edge set. We minimise the classical retrofitting objective [3] with uniform anchor and edge weights: J(Z) = X i ∥zi −xi∥2 2 + X (i,j)∈E ∥zi −zj∥2 2. (2) The first term anchors each node to its original embedding, while the second pulls adjacent nodes closer together in the modified space. The anchoring term makes the objective strictly convex, ensuring a unique global minimiser to which the iterative optimisation procedure converges. Random-walk endpoint blend (walk). This transformation launches 100 random walks of exactly two steps from each node. The modified embedding is computed as the frequency-weighted mean of the endpoints’ original embeddings. Louvain cluster blend. This transformation partitions the unweighted, undirected graph using Louvain community detection at resolution 0.5; we refer to Blondel et al. [11] for details of the community-construction algorithm. Each node i is then moved towards the centroid µc(i) of its community c(i): zi = αxi + (1 −α)µc(i). (3) Each centroid is the mean of its community’s original embeddings and remains fixed. The selected configuration uses α = 0.5, assigning equal weight to the node’s original embedding and its community centroid. 3.4 Retrieval Strategies We compare retrieval strategies that use the original and graph-modified embeddings separately or together. These range from direct searches in either space to combinations of independent rankings and multi-stage searches guided by earlier results.
