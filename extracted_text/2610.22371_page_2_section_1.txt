Feed-forward GFMs directly regress pixel-aligned geometry, from pairwise point maps (Wang et al., 2024) to sequence models that use spatial memory (Wang & Agapito, 2025), recurrent scene states (Wang et al., 2025b), or joint multi-view attention with multiple prediction heads (Wang et al., 2025a). Later models remove the reference view (Wang et al., 2026c), accept optional geometric inputs (Keetha et al., 2026), or learn unified depth–ray targets (Lin et al., 2025). Point Tracking in 2D and 3D. 2D point tracking has been extensively studied in both sparse and dense settings (Doersch et al., 2022; Karaev et al., 2024; 2025; Lai et al., 2026). 3D point tracking methods lift pixels with monocular depth and iterative refinement (Xiao et al., 2024), improve dense long-range estimation through optimized surrogate queries (Ngo et al., 2025b;a), or jointly estimate geometry, camera, and object motion (Xiao et al., 2025). GFM-based 4D perceivers represent dynamic 3D scenes through per-frame point maps (Zhang et al., 2025a), dynamic point maps (Sucar et al., 2026), conditional frame-wise decoding (Luo et al., 2026), or motion bases (Lin et al., 2026). Other approaches include common-time point-map pairs (Feng et al., 2025), parametric trajectories (Liu et al., 2026), and scene flow (Zhu et al., 2026; Karhade et al., 2026; Qian et al., 2026). Most rely on frame-aligned grid decoding, coupling output sampling and cost to spatial resolution, whereas D4RT (Zhang et al., 2026a) decouples prediction from predefined grids through on-demand queries. Generative Priors for Geometry and Motion. Large pretrained generators encode geometry and motion priors transferable to perception tasks (Zhan et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2026). Image adaptations estimate depth through iterative denoising (Ke et al., 2024) or single-step regression (He et al., 2025).
