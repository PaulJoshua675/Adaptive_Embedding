We introduced weak to strong escalation as a general inference paradigm for adaptive agent systems, in which inexpensive weak models handle routine execution and stronger models are invoked selectively when additional capability is needed. Under this paradigm, we proposed LEASH, a training-free, uncertainty-aware framework that supports initial routing, runtime escalation, and continued post- handoff oversight. Our results suggest that a shared latent uncertainty signal can support both selective escalation and post-handoff oversight, providing a practical mechanism for dynamically allocating model capability while retaining continued control over strong-agent execution. Limitations Our current evaluation is limited to computer-use agents on OSWorld, so the generality of weak-to- strong escalation beyond this domain remains to be validated. In addition, our uncertainty score estimates weak-agent failure risk rather than the expected benefit of escalation; Proposition 1 requires a condition that the escalation gain be nondecreasing in the score, which may fail when both weak and strong agents struggle on the same task. Finally, post-handoff Shadow monitoring operates under a distribution shift from weak-agent trajectories, and its intervention effect is not causally isolated in our current evaluation. 12
