The first pass of a generation loads the same state of the host and the same registers into every window with booted = 0, and the heads and the tapes they read are the same in every generation; every cell a record after the startup reads is written earlier in the pass, apart from the cells under the heads and the sink. So, pass by pass, the windows of any two generations agree on every cell the records read or write, and after a generation the window is the same in every generation. The records of SA,w grow linearly in w and its window logarithmically, so B grows at most quadratically in w while 2w grows exponentially, and wA exists. Appendix B gives GA, wA and the bounds in closed form. 8 Reproduction under noise The reproductions of Sections 4 to 7 are exact because every pre-activation is an integer: a run rebuilds the weights bit for bit while no perturbation of a pre-activation reaches one half. This section asks how much noise a reproducing run tolerates. It proves this margin of one half, bounds the probability of leaving the orbit under random perturbations from the pre-activations the orbit visits, and compares the bounds with the host under noise (Section 8.1). It shows that replicating every unit multiplies the margin, which keeps a run exact under three models of noise, below a threshold in one of them (Section 8.2), and it gives the noise under which a generation of the stored host, which rebuilds the host’s weights in the next bank, runs exactly (Section 8.3). 22
