At the third level, the inner loop splits the outer training set into five inner folds. It uses them only to choose the layer. For each layer, it fits the probe on four inner folds and predicts the fifth, five times over. The layer whose predictions have the highest class-balanced accuracy wins; ties go to the lower layer. We then fit the probe at the chosen layer on the whole outer training set and predict the outer test fold. Every fit computes µ, vk and σk from its own training data. After all ten outer folds are processed, each subject of T has one prediction, made by a probe that never saw it. Pre-linearity is the class-balanced accuracy of these predictions, computed as for candidate accuracy. We subtract a baseline, the class-balanced candidate accuracy of always predicting the most frequent answer of the outer training set. This baseline is close to chance, one over the number of answers |E|. Controls. A relation might generalize well for reasons simpler than Pre-linearity. We measure four such properties of each relation and ask whether Pre-linearity still predicts Generalization once they are taken into account. The four are (i) Recall; (ii) the log number of answers, ln|E|; (iii) the log number of fine-tuning subjects, ln|T|; and (iv) subject frequency, how often the relation’s subjects occur in pretraining text, as the mean of log10(1 + count) over its subjects. For each model, we compute the partial correlations between Pre-linearity and Generalization across its relations, given each control alone and given all jointly. 4 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP This section describes the pretrained relations, models and label sets we use, and how we repeat runs to measure the effect of randomness. Pretrained relations. A pretrained relation enters our experiments only if, on every model, (i) it has at least five answers, (ii) each answer has at least five subjects, four to fine-tune on and one to hold out, and (iii) it has at least 25 fine-tuning subjects, the fewest for which every probe fit in the nested cross-validation of Pre-linearity contains every answer and more subjects than the probe’s q = 16 principal directions. These requirements depend only on subject counts and tokenization. We screen relations from three sources against these requirements and keep 70: • Hernandez: 13 of the 47 relations of Hernandez et al. (2024) qualify. We drop the two about spelling (a word’s first or last letter), whose answer can be read from the subject’s own letters, and keep eleven. An example is “British Railways Board is headquartered in the city of London” for Company →headquarters. • Town: six relations of our own, all of which qualify.
