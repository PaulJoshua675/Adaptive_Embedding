B.3 LLM-as-a-Judge Results on In-Domain Benchmarks To examine whether the in-domain results depend on the token F1 metric, we follow ASearcher (Gao et al., 2025) to supplement LLM-as-a-Judge (LasJ) accuracy for all methods in Table 1. The judge model and prompt template are the same as in the out-of-domain evaluation (Appendix A.2). As shown in Table 5, LasJ accuracy exhibits the same trend as Table 1. The RL improved steering agent achieves the best results consistently on all seven benchmarks, and RL raises the gain of steering from 1.60 to 6.77 points. LasJ accuracy is higher than token F1 overall, as the judge accepts answers that match the reference in meaning but differ in surface form. C Prompt Templates In this section, we list the full prompt templates used in the experiments. Curly-braced slots are filled at run time from the current observation. In the single-agent setting, the slots carry the question, the executor’s rendered action history, the action count with the remaining turn budget, and a line naming the executor’s currently legal actions. A committed note is injected into the executor’s prompt under the header “A steering supervisor has reviewed your progress and left a note for your next action (follow the note):”.
