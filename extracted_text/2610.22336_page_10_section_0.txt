First, under perturbations with ε = 0.2, we can no longer ensure through the forced-treatment rule that treatment is restarted before progression. Indeed, since ε > log 1.2, an error that lowers the measured PSA can postpone the forced restart until after progression. Since AT50 resumes treatment only through the condition yt ≥0, such an error can postpone its restart in every treatment break. Second, we pay a price in the nominal model for accounting for errors in PSA, with 45.94 months for random-perturbation RL compared with 50.35 months for nominal RL. In summary, policies that do not account for errors in PSA lose a substantial part of their benefit under errors of realistic magnitude, and by training under random perturbations we retain the benefit of adaptive therapy under such errors. 3.4 Robustness to Shifts in the Patient Dynamics at Almost No Price We next turn to shifts in the patient dynamics, which arise when the competition and growth parameters deviate from their estimated values. We model such shifts by the ambiguity set Pc of Section 2.3.2 with K = 32 generating kernels drawn from Qnom, and refer to the policy learned from (2.8) with training radius ρtr ∈{0.1, 0.25, 0.5} as kernel-robust RL. We evaluate all policies at the evaluation radii ρ ∈{0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1}. At ρ = 0, the kernel is drawn uniformly from the generating kernels in each month, and for ρ > 0, each policy is evaluated against a best-response adversary, i.e., an adversary trained by PPO to minimize the reward of the policy while the policy is held fixed. In addition to the 32 generating kernels used in training, referred to as the training kernels, we evaluate the policies on 32 held-out kernels drawn independently from Qnom. Figure 3 shows the mean progression-free time and the harm rate in the nominal model and as functions of the evaluation radius, with the complete values in Table 2. Kernel-robust RL improves on nominal RL and AT50 at every evaluation radius on both sets of kernels. In the nominal model, it forgoes between 1.58 and 2.22 months compared with nominal RL, which is small compared with its improvement of between 7.33 and 7.71 months over nominal RL at ρ = 0 on the training kernels. On the training kernels, kernel-robust RL evaluated at its training radius ρ = ρtr improves on nominal RL by between 7.00 and 8.74 months, and in particular attains 46.57 months at ρ = 0.25, compared with 39.12 months for nominal RL. At ρ = 1, which exceeds every training radius, kernel-robust RL with ρtr = 0.5 attains 25.08 months, compared with 17.46, 11.65, and 12.72 months for nominal RL, AT50, and continuous treatment. In addition, the harm rate of kernel-robust RL is at most 1.7% at every radius on both sets of kernels, while on the training kernels, the harm rate of nominal RL grows from 8.9% at ρ = 0 to 29.1% at ρ = 1, and that of AT50 from 44.4% to 65.1%. A few remarks are in order. First, nominal RL loses much of its advantage once the parameters vary, even in the absence of an adversary.
