Private Retrieval Augmented Generation via Random Projection A PREPRINT For simplicity, we denote our random projection as RP, DP-guaranteed random projection with Gaussian noise as DP-RP- G [17], and the work of Koga et al. [6] as DPSparseVoteRAG. We also report results for vanilla RAG and the parametric model without a datastore. For differential privacy, we use a wide range of privacy budgets with ϵ ∈{1, 5, 10, 20}. For DP-RP-G, the original DP-RP method by Blocki et al. [17] assumes binary vector embeddings. To adapt this framework to RAG and document retrieval, we first project both queries and datastore embeddings into a lower-dimensional space, clip the projected embeddings, and then add Gaussian noise to obtain differential privacy guarantees. The complete procedure is in Section D. We sweep the clipping bound from {0.1, 0.5, 1, 5, 10, 15, 25, 50, 100} under each setting and report the results with optimal clipping bound. Remaining settings are in Section F. For DPSparseVoteRAG, we sweep the subset number m over {10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60} and choose the best value 50 (Section F.2). In our methods, for KNN-LM, we set λ as 0.1 and K as 1024. For RP, the only tunable parameter is k (the projection dimension). Larger k values better preserve utility while maintaining privacy protection. We choose k = 100 since it achieves comparable utility to larger values (e.g., k = 1600) while providing better computational efficiency. On the Enron dataset, we evaluate protection against structured data including email addresses, phone numbers, and URLs. As shown in Table 3, random projection effectively protects all three types, with leakage rates below 3% across both KNN-LM and Standard RAG architectures. We observe that URLs are the easiest to leak. For unstructured text, we evaluate on the ECHR dataset, which contains legal case narratives with sensitive personal information embedded in natural language. Our method maintains strong privacy protection (2.19– 2.27% leakage) while preserving utility, demonstrating that random projection works across both structured and unstructured sensitive information. This suggests that the privacy-preserving mechanism operates at the embedding level, making it agnostic to the format or structure of the sensitive content. A further extension of the repeated-query attack is the membership inference attack (MIA). MIA is important in RAG privacy, but it targets a different adversarial objective from the data extraction attacks studied in this paper: determining whether a known record is contained in the datastore, so we do not focus on evaluating various MIA methods. We evaluate this threat over the full private dataset and measure attack accuracy following E-MIA [32], which follows the standard MIA protocol for RAG systems under our threat model.
