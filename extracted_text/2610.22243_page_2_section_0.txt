Label Predictability: Can a deliberately capability-poor rule predict released labels? 2. Observational Guard Association: Do guard decisions correlate with the suspected cue in released records? 3. Paired Decision Sensitivity: Does the guard change when the cue is changed while the candidate action’s authorization and effect are held fixed? Paired edits test whether changing the input changes the guard’s decision. To interpret that change as spurious shortcut reliance, the edits must preserve the relevant safety information. For a deterministic guard, evaluating each original–edited pair reveals whether the strict decision changes. Stochastic guards require additional controls because identical inputs can produce different outputs and sequen- tial calls may drift over time. We therefore interleave repeated original and edited calls, include a formatting placebo, and report identical-input disagreement separately. Our contributions are: • Three-level shortcut-audit framework. We introduce a inference ladder separating label pre- dictability, observational guard association, and paired decision sensitivity with explicit construct- preservation caveats, plus repeat and placebo controls for stochastic guards. • Audit of released agent-safety evaluations. We apply shortcut-auditing to AGENTDOJO-TRAJ, a LINUXARENA public sample, and to AGENTHARM-TRAJ. Across LINUXARENA and two TS-BENCH-derived datasets, we expose label separability, exact-tool domain-transfer failure, and evaluation-unit sensitivity: command length reaches AUC .910, exact- tool lookup falls from F1 = .901 to .495 under domain holdout, and TS-GUARD F1 falls from .902 to .719 under equal-interaction weighting. • Guard-level shortcut validation. We move beyond showing that benchmark labels are predictable by testing candidate cues directly against guard decisions, using a purpose-built TS-GUARD reproduction and hosted models configured as pre-execution guards. Repeated paired AGENTDOJO- TRAJ interventions with a placebo control reveal material Haiku sensitivity, while Luna and Q8 TS-GUARD checks show that the effect is configuration-specific. Haiku blocking changes by −8.33 percentage points (pp) for the marker rewrite and +11.46 pp for aliases. With the same cohort, conditions, schedule, repetitions, and estimand, GPT-5.6 Luna gives 0.00 pp and +1.04 pp, while the deterministic TS-GUARD reproduction changes its decision for 3 of 64 marker-edit pairs. The result is deliberately not “these benchmarks are broken.” Some proxies are intentional and semantically relevant: an untrusted-content marker communicates provenance, and a malicious request is relevant to a harm policy. Our claim is that such features create shortcut opportunities. Evaluation authors should measure whether conclusions survive group holdouts, equal-unit weighting, paired perturbations with construct-preservation caveats, and repeat-original controls.
