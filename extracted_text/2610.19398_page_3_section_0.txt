It jointly selects complementary passages and derives cross-evidence inferences, re- constructing the reasoning context for composi- tional answer derivation. 3 Method We propose RCER, an iterative RAG method that reconstructs cross-evidence relations over accumulated passages to guide subsequent re- trieval and answer derivation. Figure 2 illustrates its three components. 3.1 Inter-Round Relational Evidence Acquisition Inter-Round Relational Evidence Acquisition de- termines subsequent information needs by recon- structing relations across accumulated passages. For a question q, initial retrieval from corpus Dq produces A0. At iteration t, the controller input xt contains q together with the accumulated passages At and query history Ht. The latest batch ∆t is identified within At, whose passages retain acqui- sition order. An LLM-based retrieval controller jointly analyzes the passages to determine how new evidence connects with earlier facts and what requires further investigation. It generates (ft, It, Mt, qt+1) = Cθ(xt). (1) Here ft specifies the relation under investigation, with It identifying the passages considered jointly. The relational analysis Mt expresses their current interpretation through mt entries Mt = [(rtj, utj, Btj)]j∈[mt]. (2) Each entry pairs a relation rtj with its current interpretation utj, including unresolved aspects. The passage identifiers Btj connect this interpre- tation to the retrieved text. Newly established entity correspondences can make an earlier fact a premise for the current investigation. Dynamic problem decomposition translates these judgments into the next subquestion, specifying the entity and relation that require further evidence.
