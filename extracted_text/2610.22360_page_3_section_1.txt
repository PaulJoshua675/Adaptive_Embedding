Recent 3D-language models connect native 3D representations to language and evaluate object, part, spatial, and embodied understanding (Xu et al., 2025; Deng et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025a; Zhang et al., 2026a). In parallel, 3D generation benchmarks and evaluators measure semantic alignment, human preference, geometric quality, and generation defects (Wu et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025a;b; Jia et al., 2026; Duggal et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2026). Together, these works substantially improve our ability to describe and assess 3D content. However, predicting a property or scoring an asset does not directly measure whether a model can verify a specific requirement, reject a plausible but incorrect candidate, and identify heterogeneous 3D evidence that makes it wrong. We focus on this verifier-side evaluation against human judgments, with scope comparisons to prior work in Figure 2. 3 3DVerifierBench To benchmark AI against human verification, 3DVerifierBench unifies 10 tasks under a common interface, evaluating reliability across decisions, explanations, and consistency. Together, these dimensions assess whether a verifier can provide correct, informative, and consistent feedback for reflection in 3D generation. Unified Verification Interface and Task Taxonomy. Given task instructions I, a claim C, candidate evidence Ecand, and optional reference evidence Eref, a verifier returns a binary answer ˆy ∈{True, False} and an explanation ˆe: x = (I, C, Ecand, Eref), fθ(x) = (ˆy, ˆe). We denote σ = (C, Eref) as the requirement, and the candidate may be an asset, part, grasp, or animation. Verification checks whether 3D evidence fulfills requirements, rather than general plausibility or visual quality.
