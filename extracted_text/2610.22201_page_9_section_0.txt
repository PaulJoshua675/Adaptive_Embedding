Asymmetric Data Markets (News Market): We construct the News Market (Appendix F) to simulate a pay-as-you-go research pipeline. Competition is driven by both asymmetric information and query strategy. The market contains four publisher categories (wikipedia, medical journal, social media, news) operating over strictly disjoint document corpora, alongside a sales agent that synthesizes the final answer. To establish imperfect competition within each category, we deploy two competing publisher agents per category. These intra-category competitors share the same data access but are differentiated by distinct search prompts: a precision strategy generating narrow, entity-specific queries versus a coverage strategy casting a broader, gap-filling net. The market successfully learns to route queries to relevant publishers based on expected down- stream valuation, achieving a 73.4±1.7% success rate over the final 100 episodes (fig. 2). Importantly, active market participants achieve positive profit margins as well, overcoming the participation barrier raised in Section 3.2. In comparison, a centralized ReAct baseline (Yao et al., 2023) limited to the same 8-step horizon achieves 48.4 ± 2.8% with Gemini-3.5-flash-lite at 0.035 ± 0.0004 ¢/task and 78.8 ± 1.3% with Gemini-3.1-pro-preview at 1.060 ± 0.368 ¢/task (1¢=$0.01). Operating exclusively with Gemini-3.5-flash-lite agents, the market recovers 82% of Gemini-3.1-pro-preview’s accuracy lift over Gemini-3.5-flash-lite ReAct at an agent inference cost of just 0.054 ± 0.003 ¢/task (0.102¢ includ- ing the market assessor)—a 19.7× cost reduction relative to Gemini-3.1-pro-preview (10.4× total). Although the market takes slightly fewer steps per task than Gemini-3.5-flash-lite ReAct (3.12 vs. 3.23), its higher retrieval success accumulates richer multi-document contexts for the sales agent to synthesize, trading a minor 0.067 ¢/task increase for a +25.0% accuracy gain. Bidding Strategy Performance: We found Mirror Descent (MD) to outperform PPO empirically, particularly in the later stages of training. MD decouples the learning of a) valuations and b) shading, providing a clear signal to each: a) regressing valuations toward realized payoffs upon winning, and b) adjusting the shade factor up or down strictly from the binary loss/win outcome. In contrast, while PPO tracks MD early in training (when shades are initialized near 0.5 and winning agents accumulate a positive profit baseline ¯𝑈𝑖,ema > 0), its performance degrades later as competing bids rise.
