Our contributions are summarized as follows. • We quantify the optimization by the gradients of empirical risks, and the generalization by their uniform convergence to the population gradients. We employ polynomially decaying step sizes and tune the iteration number to balance optimization and generalization, achieving a convergence rate of O(d log n/n) for the population gradients, where d is the model dimension and n is the sample size. This result is comparable to the generalization guarantee of SGD for general nonconvex problems [35], while requiring only standard smoothness assumptions [47, 56]. • We derive bounds on the excess population risks for gradient-dominated problems, which nearly match the optimistic rates in convex and low-noise settings [37, 56, 74]. A key to the proof is the use of more rapidly decaying step sizes than those in the general nonconvex setting, which enables us to refine the estimate on the norm of iterates and thereby control both the empirical and population gradients. • The uniform convergence approach often leads to an explicit dependency on the dimensionality d, which can result in vacuous generalization bounds for high-dimensional problems. To address this issue, we impose a structure on loss functions and assume fast-decaying eigenvalues for the associated kernel to establish dimension-independent bounds for both general nonconvex and gradient-dominated problems. The paper is structured as follows. We review the related work in Section 2 and formulate the problem in Section 3. We present our main results for general nonconvex problems in Section 4.1 and for gradient-dominated problems in Section 4.2, and provide dimension-independent bounds in Section 4.3. We conclude the paper in Section 5. 2
