As shown in Tab. S7, any deviation from the standard s = 1 causes severe image degradation across all quality metrics. At s = 0.5, Recall collapses to 0.0002 and ImageReward drops to −2.28, indicating that the denoiser operates far outside its training distribution and produces degenerate outputs. Increasing the scale to s ≥1.2 yields similarly catastrophic failures: Re- call falls below 0.04, CLIPScore drops by more than 8 points, and ImageReward becomes strongly negative across all configurations. This behavior is expected: diffusion model denoisers are trained exclusively with s = 1, and any pertur- bation to the noise scale pushes the initial latent out of the distribution seen during training, causing the reverse process to produce corrupted images re- gardless of the direction of the scale change. These results confirm that noise scale manipulation is not a viable mechanism for diversity enhancement, and that the diversity gains of DetNO arise from structured optimization within the valid noise distribution rather than from distributional shift.
