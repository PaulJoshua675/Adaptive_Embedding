We return to this decoupling in Sec. 6.5. 6.3 Loss study: which losses drive which descriptor Figure 4 summarizes all 16 configurations, together with the two pretrained baselines, as the percent change in each mean distance relative to the standard fine-tuning control; full means, ranks, and win counts are in Appendices A–E. We report every configuration so that the final objective can be judged against the alternatives, including those that are better on individual distances. Figure 5 plots each PD distance against MT distance. To separate the contributions of individual losses we use the fac- torial structure of the study (Sec. 4.4). The main effect of a loss is the change in a mean distance when that loss is added, averaged over all combinations of the other losses in the design; an interac- tion measures how much the effect of one loss depends on whether another is present. All effects are listed in Appendix C. Gradient supervision improves PD agreement but not MT. Across the eight combinations of speed, gradient, and level-set losses, adding the gradient loss lowers mean dB, W2,∞, and W2,2 by 0.61, 5.32, and 7.99 on average, while the speed and level- set effects and all interactions are roughly an order of magnitude smaller. Gradient supervision alone lowers dB, W2,∞, and W2,2 by 22.6%, 28.1%, and 32.0% relative to the control. Its average ef- fect on MT, however, is unfavorable (it raises mean MT distance by 0.19): the gradient-only configuration is slightly worse than the control in mean MT (by 0.7%), and every combination of gradi- ent with speed or level-set losses is worse still (by 2.5% to 4.6%). This behavior is consistent with gradient supervision moving the persistence-pair distribution closer to the ground truth without, on its own, preserving how components merge. Critical-location supervision is associated with stronger MT improvement. The losses that constrain predicted values at se- lected critical locations give the largest MT improvements. Adding the local-maximum loss to the control improves MT by 5.4% and dB by 14.3%, but the Wasserstein distances by less than 5%. Fixed- pair supervision is stronger on the PD distances: added to the con- trol, it lowers dB, W2,∞, and W2,2 by 32.0%, 28.0%, and 30.6% and gives the best mean MT of all 18 methods (5.594, 7.0% below the control).
