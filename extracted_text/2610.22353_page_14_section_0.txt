MirrorMoE: Rethinking Routing Exploration in Modern MoEs Appendix A ADDITIONAL RELATED WORK Expressiveness does not necessarily translate directly into performance. Beyond those we have discussed earlier in Sec.1.1, additional works similarly suggest that the expressive capacity of neural networks may not be fully utilized in practice. For example, (Csordás et al., 2025) shows that many deeper layers in Llama 3.1 70B behave close to identity mappings. Since identity mappings are available “for free”, this motivates redesign of residual connections for scaling deeper (Zhang et al., 2026). MoE systems. A complementary line of work focuses on efficient MoE systems, including faster and more principled expert parallelism, fine-grained all-to-all communication, and topology-aware communication strategies; see e.g., (Hwang et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025). These methods aim to improve hardware utilization, communication efficiency, and end-to-end throughput. MirrorMoE is largely orthogonal to these system-level optimizations and can be integrated with them. Routing in MoEs. A broad line of work studies alternative routing mechanisms for MoEs. Hash Layers (Roller et al., 2021) use hash-based assignments, while BASE Layers (Lewis et al., 2021) and expert-choice routing (Zhou et al., 2022) promote balanced expert utilization through carefully designed token assignments, at the cost of not being strictly autoregressive. Clark et al. (2022) study scaling laws for routed models. Other approaches replace hard top-𝐾selection with differentiable routing mechanisms (Muqeeth et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025; Vahidi et al., 2026). Several works post- train or fine-tune routers to enable token-adaptive computation (Yue et al., 2024; Zeng et al., 2024), while Kang (2025) uses reinforcement learning to improve routing decisions. During pretraining, noisy routing has also been explored to encourage broader expert exploration: Shazeer et al. (2017) introduce Gaussian noise, whereas Nie et al. (2021) use Gumbel noise. Routing has also been explicitly studied for multimodal models (Wei et al., 2025b). In contrast to approaches that encourage broader exploration, MirrorMoE adaptively suppresses experts to shrink the exploration space. The work of Liang et al. (2026) studies shared experts from an inference perspective, showing that they can hinder expert offloading, partly because shared experts reduce the importance of routed experts. However, their focus is on routing behavior at inference time, whereas we study how shared experts affect routing throughout pretraining. B EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS B.1 Training details The model configurations for the shared-expert MoE baseline and MirrorMoE are summarized in Tab.4. Following Kang et al. (2025), we adopt a Llama-style MoE architecture. We also follow DeepSeekMoE (Dai et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024) by using a dense FFN in the first Transformer layer, so that the first sparse layer receives sufficiently developed token representations for routing. MoE-100M-A38M is trained on two H100 GPUs, while all other models are trained on four. DCLM dataset (Li et al., 2024) is adopted. We use AdamW for optimization. The learning rate is selected from {1, 3, 5} × 10−4 based on performance after 500 training steps on a held-out validation set. We adopt a warmup-stable-decay (WSD) learning-rate schedule. The global batch size is 1024, with gradient accumulation used to reduce memory consumption. The model analyzed in Sec.2.2 is MoE-498M-A121M. For MoEs without shared experts, we convert the shared experts into routed experts to preserve the same total parameter count. Due to implementation constraints in Megatron-LM, this requires a dif- ferent expert layout across GPUs, i.e., a different expert-parallelism configuration, and consequently a different number of GPUs. Specifically, Megatron-LM requires the number of routed experts to be divisible by the expert-parallelism size. Therefore, configurations with 66 or 130 routed experts use three or five GPUs, respectively. The global batch size consequently differs across configurations, while the total number of training tokens is kept approximately the same to ensure a fair comparison. 14
