Human annotation. Each benchmark sample is independently evaluated by three annotators fol- lowing the 39-label protocol in Appendix B.3. The benchmark annotators do not overlap with those used for the training set. Rather than directly assigning scores, annotators first inspect the three di- mensions independently and select all applicable labels before providing the corresponding scores. This label-first protocol requires explicit checks of the relevant event existence, timing, attributes, and relation types, making the annotation process more traceable and reducing unconstrained score judgments. Annotators then assign a score from 0 to 1, with a step size of 0.1, for global narrative harmony, event-level temporal synchronization, and fine-grained attribute alignment. We use agreement among the three annotators’ label records within each sample as an additional quality-control signal before constructing the final benchmark reference. We further quantify inter- annotator agreement on the retained annotations, obtaining ICC(2,k) of 0.83, Krippendorff’s α of 0.60, inter-annotator PLCC of 0.78, inter-annotator SRCC of 0.50, pairwise accuracy of 0.76, and ∆-Pairwise@0.2 of 0.92. The high ICC and margin-based pairwise agreement indicate stable ag- gregated human judgments, especially for samples with clear perceptual differences. Human reference score. Let s(r) i,d denote the score assigned by annotator r to sample i on dimen- sion d ∈{mac, temp, attr}. Since different annotators may use different scoring ranges across dimensions, we first normalize each annotator independently. Specifically, for each annotator r, we compute dimension-wise z- scores over the benchmark: z(r) i,d = s(r) i,d −µ(r) d σ(r) d , where µ(r) d and σ(r) d denote the mean and standard deviation of annotator r on dimension d computed over all benchmark samples. To further align the scales among the three dimensions for each annotator, we apply min-max nor- malization over the complete three-dimensional z-score matrix of each annotator: ˜z(r) i,d = z(r) i,d −min(z(r)) max(z(r)) −min(z(r)), where min(z(r)) and max(z(r)) are computed over all samples and all three dimensions for annota- tor r.
