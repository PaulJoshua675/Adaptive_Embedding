JigShape (Li et al., 2026c) evaluates geometric reconstruction from puzzle pieces, and OrigamiBench (Agar- wal et al., 2026) studies physically grounded fold operations. SnorkelSpatial (Vishwakarma, 2025) studies transformations of textual boards, querying properties after translations and rotations. Grid-Bench evaluates the complete configuration produced by a prescribed event history, allowing errors to be located throughout the resulting world. Predicting the result of an event history also connects spatial reasoning to instruction execution and state tracking. bAbI (Weston et al., 2015) includes questions about locations and possessions after narrated events; SCONE (Long et al., 2016) grounds instruction sequences in executable worlds; and ProPara (Dalvi et al., 2018) follows entity existence and location through procedural text. Entity-tracking evaluations more directly test whether models update affected entities while retaining unaffected information across operations (Kim and Schuster, 2023; Tang et al., 2026). Beyond state tracking, GT Bench (Xu et al., 2026e) examines algorithmic reasoning over graphs, while AlgoWorlds (Xu et al., 2026d) evaluates tool-guided decisions against verifiable optima. Closest to our complete-state setting, Alien Grid (Chapra et al., 2026) studies sequential depth through rotations, corner swaps, and shifts of a 3 × 3 grid. Chess-World-Model (Walker and Lyons, 2026) trains sequence models to recover complete chess states at successive move prefixes. Grid-Bench evaluates LLMs given explicit textual rules across geometric, neighborhood, connectivity, and movement operations, using complete predictions to compare recovery across spatial conditions. Appendix A develops these connections and compares the corresponding evaluation settings. 3 Grid-Bench 3.1 Spatial states and event histories Grid-Bench asks for the complete spatial state after all events in a supplied sequence. A discrete world occupies a bounded d-dimensional grid with side lengths L1, . . . , Ld and domain X = Qd j=1{0, . . . , Lj −1}. Cell values are integers from a finite alphabet A containing 0. A state W ∈AX assigns one value to every coordinate. Boundaries do not wrap, and 0 denotes an empty cell. The model receives the grid specification, initial state W0, explicit rules, and the complete event sequence hT = (e1, . . . , eT ) as text. It is asked to predict the state after all T events in a single response. Each event specifies an operation and its parameters. We use one core transition family f per history to measure recovery over repeated applications of its operations. The sequence may also contain single-cell assignments SET(p, v), which write v at p. Let Wt denote the reference state after the first t events. The simulator applies the family’s deterministic transition operator Ff: Wt = Ff(Wt−1, et), t = 1, . . . , T.
