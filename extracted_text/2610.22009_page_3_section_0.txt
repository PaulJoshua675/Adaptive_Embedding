False accept is computed over 1,800 invalid images per run, pooling near-neighbor, same-surah-far, and cross-surah lexical foils; Appendix Table 3 breaks this down by foil type. Closed models show a large capability gradient: GPT-4o and GPT-4.1 have substantial false acceptance, GPT-5.6-Terra improves sharply, and GPT-5.5 is nearly saturated. Open mod- els show a different failure mode: several produce clean labels while saying VALID on 90–99% of images, yielding near-zero discrimination despite near-perfect displayed-reference reading. Visual cue effects. Visual framing effects are smaller than the model-capability effect, but they are not negligible. For GPT-4o under the natural prompt, false acceptance is 13.00% on plain invalid images and rises to 17.33% for decorative neutral-authority images. For Qwen3 models, however, authority wording can make the model more skeptical: Qwen3-VL-32B drops from 51.33% on plain images to 34.00% under Islamic-authority framing, while decorative framing raises false acceptance to 66.33%. For InternVL3.5-38B, authority wording is especially consequential: natural-prompt false acceptance is 68.67% on plain invalid images but 89.33% under both Islamic-authority and neutral-authority headers. For MiniCPM-V 4.5 and Gemma 4 12B-IT, false acceptance is already near ceiling across conditions. Appendix Figure 3 and Table 4 show the full condition-wise break- down. Prompt effects. The de-biased instruction reduces false acceptance for several models under paired McNemar tests [McNemar, 1947] on invalid items, but Table 1 shows why this is not the same as solving citation verification. For example, Qwen3-VL-32B’s de-biased false-accept rate improves from 52.17% to 43.17%, while discrimination falls from 12.16 to 10.83 because false re- jection increases. Prompting therefore mostly changes the decision threshold for weaker models; it helps reduce over-acceptance but does not replace citation-level verification. 5 Discussion and Limitations Visual Sanad supports a concrete claim: multimodal citation verification in Islamic contexts remains nontrivial for several widely used VLMs. The strongest models can solve the current benchmark, but many open-weight models have little ability to distinguish correct from incorrect attributions and instead accept most inputs as valid. Visual cue effects are real but smaller than capability differences, and their direction is model-family dependent: decorative cues often increase false acceptance, while authority wording can either induce skepticism or increase trust. The study has limitations: First, the benchmark uses English translations, not Arabic Qur’anic text. Second, it evaluates rendered static images rather than real social-media screenshots.
