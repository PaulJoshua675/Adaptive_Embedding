H EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS H.1 Language-model training and controlled comparisons Under matched controls, we test how unit consistency affects training robustness, functional rescaling invariance, deployment quantization, and unit-system-invariant multi-loss optimization. All runs use a single NVIDIA GH200-96GB GPU. Within each controlled comparison, architectures share data order, budget, and recipe. Only the declared intervention changes, which in the main comparisons is the block structure. Main language models. The main language models are decoder-only Transformers with d = 768, L = 12, 12 heads, and context length 1024. These models have approximately 124M parameters, with all variants matched to within 0.3%. We train the models from scratch on FineWeb-Edu (Penedo et al., 2024) with the Generative Pre-trained Transformer 2 (GPT-2) byte-pair encoding (BPE) tokenizer (Radford et al., 2019) for 2.75B tokens. This budget is approximately Chinchilla-optimal for the model size (Hoffmann et al., 2022). A disjoint shard supplies a held-out 43M-token validation split. Training recipe. The training recipe follows standard practice at this scale (Karpathy, 2022; Touvron et al., 2023). We use Adam with decoupled weight decay (AdamW), setting β = (0.9, 0.95) and weight decay 0.1 on matrices only (Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019). The peak learning rate is 6 × 10−4, the warmup occupies 2.7% of training and lasts 151 steps at 124M, and cosine decay ends at 10% of the peak rate. The global batch contains 491,520 tokens, the gradient clip is 1.0, and training uses bf16 autocast and weight tying. Every variant uses rotary positional encoding (RoPE) (Su et al., 2024), which strengthens the Pre-LN baseline relative to learned positions. All eight architectures are replicated with three seeds at 124M. Unless a table explicitly reports a multi-seed aggregate, perturbation and quantization analyses evaluate one model per architecture trained with the primary seed. The remaining seeds quantify training variability. App. H.4 states the seed protocol for every scale. Architectures compared. The comparison includes Pre-LN with LayerNorm and GELU in the GPT-2 style (Radford et al., 2019; Xiong et al., 2020). LLaMA-style blocks use RMSNorm and SwiGLU (Touvron et al., 2023). QK-Norm adds per-head RMS normalization of Q and K (Henry et al., 2020; Dehghani et al., 2023). We also include NormFormer (Shleifer et al., 2021) and LayerScale initialized at 0.1 (Touvron et al., 2021b). Three further variants control scale through normalized or explicitly corrected representations. nGPT is the closest normalized-representation prior to GCT and uses a sphere-normalized state, per-dimension eigen-learning- rate linear-interpolation updates, unit-norm weights, and QK-Norm (Loshchilov et al., 2025). UCT uses the local-correction modules of App. H.4, and GCT is the canonical design derived in Sec. 4.
