For the CMA-ES component, the covariance learning rate LR(t) cov controls the adaptation speed of the search distribution, while the step-size adjustment factor ∆σ(t) enables adaptive expansion or contraction of the search radius. Their bounded ranges prevent abrupt distribution distortion, excessive exploration, or premature convergence caused by inappropriate step-size evolution. The remaining action variables regulate population-level and hybridization behaviors. The population scaling factor λ(t) permits moderate variation in the effective population size, allowing the algorithm to balance exploration and exploitation without incurring excessive computational cost or risking population impoverishment at small sizes. The restart probability p(t) res introduces occasional stochastic reinitialization to facilitate escape from local optima, while the elite preservation ratio r(t) eli balances inheritance of high-quality solutions against population renewal, avoiding both excessive elitism and search instability. Finally, the orchestration ratio β(t), primarily configured within the bound [0.10, 0.90], governs offspring allocation between the CMA-ES and DE components. By excluding the extreme values of 0 and 1, this bound preserves continuous cooperative interaction between covariance-guided exploitation and difference- vector-driven exploration, allowing the DRL agent to dynamically prioritize one mechanism without fully excluding the other. For strongly ill-conditioned, nonconvex problems, β(t) may additionally be constrained to a narrower, more stable range of [0.30, 0.70], ensuring a persistently balanced exploration–exploitation tradeoff and more robust search behavior throughout the optimization process. In both phases, the DRL-DCO algorithm follows the same nested episode-generation structure, in which the outer episode loop governs long-term progress while the inner generation loop executes population evolution. Within each episode, a maximum of 1000 generations is employed, during which the algorithm sequentially performs dynamic DE-CMA-ES orchestration and objective evaluation; only in the agent-training phase are reward calculation, replay buffer storage, and actor-critic parameter updating additionally performed, through which the policy progressively improves its capability to regulate search behavior according to the evolving optimization state. To enhance training stability, the agent-training phase begins with a warm-up stage prior to formal policy- guided optimization. Since actor-critic learning relies on diverse experiences stored in the replay buffer, insufficient initial samples may result in unstable gradient estimation and biased policy updates. During the first five episodes, populations are therefore independently and randomly initialized at the start of each episode to generate diverse optimization trajectories without inheriting previous search states. After this warm-up stage, the best-performing optimization state obtained during initial exploration is selected as the starting point for subsequent policy-guided evolution, and training enters an inheritance-based mode in which population information and the evolving policy progressively cooperate to refine the solution.
