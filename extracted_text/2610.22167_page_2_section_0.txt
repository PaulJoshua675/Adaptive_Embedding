We first train the model on reasoning traces that connect conversational evidence to task predictions, using two added special tokens to mark quoted source spans. We then use GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) to optimize the initialized model with task correctness and our proposed reward, Grounded Information Utilization (IUG). This reward credits new, task-relevant social inferences supported by quoted evidence while accounting for reasoning effort. These inferences must add information that the preceding reasoning has not already established. At inference time, the trained model reasons over the supplied interaction in a self-contained manner. To assess the effectiveness of CLUE, we train Qwen3.5-9B (Qwen Team, 2026) on a small set of conversational examples from the Computational Cultural Understanding (CCU) program (Corvey, 2021). We evaluate in-domain performance and also test generalization to new languages and conversational settings, covering emotion detec- tion, dialog act recognition, norm violation detec- tion, and power relation prediction. Compared with base Qwen, CLUE achieves relative gains of 17.3% in-domain and 9.3% out-of-domain in av- erage weighted F1, with higher F1 on four of five out-of-domain evaluations. We also find that these gains are accompanied by over 80% fewer reason- ing tokens on average in both groups, as detailed in Figure 4. Analysis of Wang et al. (2024a)’s emotion-cause dataset further shows that the model trained with CLUE identifies causes more often in its reasoning, which may help explain the improvement in emo- tion recognition. Human evaluation of a sample spanning all eight tasks also finds that CLUE more often explains how specific conversational content supports its predictions, while its quoted evidence aligns more closely with the lines selected by hu- man readers. To summarize our main contributions: • CLUE, a two-stage training pipeline com- bining supervised evidence reasoning with GRPO guided by IUG, which rewards new, evidence-supported social inferences while accounting for effort. • Trained on limited conversational data from one language, CLUE improves average weighted F1 over base Qwen both in and out-of-domain while using over 80% fewer reasoning tokens on average. • Cause analysis and human evaluation show that, compared with base Qwen, CLUE’s rea- soning more often identifies relevant earlier exchanges and explains how conversational evidence supports its judgments. 2
