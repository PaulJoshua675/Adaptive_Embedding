Language models have reopened the question from both sides. Budding argues that LLMs can acquire tacit knowledge in Martin Davies’s sense—internal structure that admits semantic description, syntactic structure and causal systematicity—although Davies himself denied this of neural networks (Budding 2025). Collins and Thorne reply from the research front. The judgements by which scientists decide which papers to take seriously rest on collective tacit knowledge formed in the spoken discourse of small, closed expert groups, which LLMs cannot currently reach; much of the apparent progress of LLMs, they argue, reflects human discourse being written down and formalized, so that the intelligence lies with the humans (Collins & Thorne 2026). Our question differs from both. They ask whether a given kind of machine has tacit knowl- edge, and the machine in question is a learned network whose internals are hard to read. We ask where, in a substrate whose every operation can be inspected, the tacit–explicit bound- ary falls, and what moves it. The answer developed in §4 agrees with Collins and Thorne that collective tacit knowledge lives in communities (§5.3).
