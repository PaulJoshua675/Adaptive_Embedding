The frozen REMBGO implementation retains an absolute centroid-eligibility test fi < 107. This constant is inher- ited from an earlier application-oriented implementation; it does not delimit the admissible region of the engineering objective used here, whose nominal hard-penalty levels are of order 104 and 105 and whose observed campaign values lie well below 107. On raw CEC2022 functions the objective is the benchmark value itself and its magnitude is function dependent, so the same constant can become operationally active. A side-effect-free audit at the primary budget repro- duced all 720 archived terminal values exactly and found activation confined to F1, F6 and F8. A matched sensitivity run that removed only the finite cutoff left every originally inactive run bit-identical and produced no Holm-significant terminal-performance difference in any affected function–dimension cell. The threshold is therefore operationally ac- tive and objective-scale dependent on a subset of CEC2022 functions, but this analysis provides no evidence of a material terminal-performance change from removing the finite cutoff at the tested 1040-evaluation condition, so the reported benchmark conclusions do not rest on it. Supplementary Section S6 gives the activation counts, the sensitivity design and the per-cell results. Recent analysis of the CEC2022 winner shows that added mechanisms and hidden parameters should not auto- matically be read as useful complexity, and that a simplified variant of that algorithm can outperform the original [4]. Component value should therefore be established empirically rather than asserted, which is why REMBGO is assessed here through a matched parent comparison, a component-removal study and a budget-dependent characterization rather than through terminal-rank claims. The component-removal result, in which no single removal survives multiplicity correction, is reported in that spirit: it indicates that the behaviour arises from interacting components and cannot be attributed confidently to any one mechanism. In the transfer test the selected-vector main effect accounts for only 3.6% (DQ-PI) and 5.8% (Lyapunov) of the descriptive crossed validation variation, against 35.0% and 32.8% for arc realization and approximately 61.4% for interaction plus residual, so robustness across stochastic trajectories matters more than the small differences among nominal tuning winners; this does not conflict with the benchmark parent result, as the two experiments answer different questions. 7 Limitations The benchmark study covers the CEC2022 suite only, at D=10 and D=20 with thirty runs per condition, and Holm correction is applied within each budget without cross-budget multiplicity control, so the win/tie/loss counts describe breadth and direction rather than magnitude. The application study uses one EAF–STATCOM plant family, two con- trollers, a single tuning trajectory and twenty unseen stochastic arcs, evaluated by simulation replay rather than on hard- ware. Finally, two implementation details—range-normalization of the neighbourhood radius and the midpoint-based restart subgroup—have limited identifiability on the symmetric isotropic CEC2022 box, though matched diagnostics found no detectable change in the reported conclusions. Benchmark breadth is a further limitation. CEC2022 contains twelve functions per dimension, which is small by the standards of recent benchmarking methodology [2]: although the present design compensates partly through thirty repetitions, two dimensions, several evaluation budgets, a matched parent comparison, a component-removal study and an independent engineering transfer test, cross-suite validation remains necessary before broader optimizer-ranking claims are made.
