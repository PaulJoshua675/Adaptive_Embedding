AI USE STATEMENT In the preparation of this manuscript, we utilized LLMs to generate synthetic datasets, to refine the writing (including grammar and spelling corrections), to draft sections of the paper, and to assist with the preliminary derivation of mathematical formulas in Appendix E. The authors reviewed and take responsibility for the research design, experiments, analysis, claims, and final presentation. ETHICS STATEMENT This work does not involve human-subject studies or the collection of private data from real users. We study training methods for general-purpose computer-using agents, and conduct all trajectory collection, reinforcement learning, and evaluation in controlled, isolated Ubuntu virtual machines. The VMs use fixed application versions and a curated local file library, and are separated from per- sonal host files and credentials. This setup reduces the risk that exploratory actions during research inadvertently modify host state or access unauthorized resources. Although the experiments themselves present no substantial direct ethical risk, deploying agents with combined GUI interaction and code execution on real devices raises important safety and pri- vacy concerns. Such agents can continuously observe screen content, modify local files, and execute Python or Bash, and may therefore encounter personally identifiable information, confidential docu- ments, or authentication credentials. They may also be exposed to prompt injection through interface content or external files. Code actions further increase the potential impact of erroneous behavior or malicious workflows. The isolated experimental setting used in this work does not resolve these deployment-time risks. Real-world deployment should follow least-privilege and safety-by-design principles. These include granular permissions over applications, directories, networks, and code execution; explicit human confirmation before irreversible, credential-bearing, or otherwise high-impact operations; runtime policy checks, sandboxed execution, audit logs, and post-action verification; and data minimization and sanitization for screenshots, trajectories, and tool outputs. Users should retain the ability to define and revoke the agent’s authority, with sensitive applications such as banking, private commu- nication, and credential management inaccessible by default. Because general-purpose automation is inherently dual use, future CUA research should advance capabilities together with systematic red teaming, interpretable action auditing, and reliable human takeover mechanisms.
