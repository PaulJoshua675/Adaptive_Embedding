Additional alternating- training curves are given in Appendix B.2. 4.4 RQ3: Can Selective Retrieval Improve Strong Black-Box Translators? We next examine whether selective retrieval can improve a stronger black-box translator whose pa- rameters are inaccessible. Specifically, we freeze Claude Sonnet 4.6 and train a Qwen-based SRAT policy to decide when and what to retrieve, us- ing Claude’s final translation quality as reward. We compare against direct Claude translation and Claude-Ret, which prompts Claude itself to make retrieval decisions without policy training. Table 4 shows that directly prompting a strong LLM to retrieve is not consistently beneficial. On IdiomKB Zh→En, Claude-Ret reduces COMET from 0.826 to 0.821 and MQM from −0.92 to −0.99; on RAGtrans En→De, it similarly de- grades BLEU, COMET, and MQM. In contrast, SRAT-Claude provides more reliable gains. On IdiomKB, it improves most metrics over direct Claude with only 1.9–10.2% retrieval, although the gains remain modest given Claude’s strong para- metric knowledge on the relatively simple idiom- translation tasks. The benefit becomes substantially larger on the more challenging RAGtrans benchmark. SRAT- Claude improves MQM over direct Claude by 12.4% on En→Zh and 16.8% on En→De, together with consistent gains in BLEU, COMET, and GRF. We further observe the same overall trend with GLM 5 in Appendix A.5: prompted retrieval de- grades translation, whereas a trained SRAT policy can recover the loss and improve over direct GLM translation. These results show that retrieval pol- icy learning can be decoupled from the translator and generalized across frozen black-box LLMs, improving their translation outcomes without mod- ifying their parameters. 5
