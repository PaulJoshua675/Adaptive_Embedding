The probability the positive holds rank one is (1 −x)N, where the single per-query hardness x is the fraction of the distractor similarity distribution above the positive. This x is size-independent. It is a property of the model and the distractor source, and it does not depend on how many distractors one draws. From it, the metric at any scale follows in closed form. The corollary that matters in practice: a small random sample suffices to estimate x, and the large-scale number can be predicted rather than measured. Why has retrieval not been doing this? The sampled-rank machinery is mature, but it has lived elsewhere: classical IR (Madigan et al., 2003; Hawking & Robertson, 2003), recommender sys- tems (Krichene & Rendle, 2020; Jin et al., 2021), and biometrics (NIST, 2022; Scheirer et al., 2011; Grother & Phillips, 2004; Slavutsky & Benjamini, 2021; Rocher et al., 2025). That machin- ery has been used with a single assumed score distribution and no sample-based estimation (e.g. Madigan et al. (2003) analyze one shared relevant/irrelevant score pair); what has been missing for visual retrieval is estimating, per query and from a small sample, these extremely rare exceedance probabilities and validating the resulting metric against a real system at web scale. We turn that machinery into a practical, validated evaluation framework for web-scale instance retrieval: per-query hardness estimated from a small sample with a tail-aware estimator, validated at genuine 100M scale, and turned into a low-cost protocol.
