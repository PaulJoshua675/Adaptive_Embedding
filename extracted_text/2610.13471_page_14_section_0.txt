Supplementary Material This supplement collects the secondary material referenced from the main paper. Section S1 gives the complete evaluation-budget ladder, Section S2 the sensitivity study at the exact 1040-evaluation budget, Section S3 additional component diagnostics, Section S4 the full fixed-gain EAF–STATCOM generalization results, and Section S5 gives the reproducibility and statistical conventions together with a compact application-objective summary. Every table and figure regenerates from the archived CEC2022 and application result files without rerunning any optimizer. S1 Complete five-optimizer evaluation-budget ladder Table S1 lists the full twenty-eight-rung ladder underlying Figure 1 and Table 2 of the main paper: the Friedman mean rank of each optimizer (lower is better), the optimizer with the lowest observed mean rank, the per-rung omnibus Fried- man p-value, and the REMBGO-versus-EMBGO function-level record (REMBGO better / no significant difference / EMBGO better) after Holm correction across the twelve CEC2022 functions at that rung. The 1040-evaluation rung is the exact application-aligned budget. sweep around the prespecified defaults was run at the exact 1040-evaluation budget for both dimensions, varying the Cauchy probability, the triple-leader probability, the elite count and the restart fraction while holding all other settings at Table 1 values. The sweep uses six representative CEC2022 functions (F1, F4, F6, F7, F9, F12) with twenty matched runs each. This subset is chosen to span the four CEC2022 landscape classes at a fraction of the full-suite cost—F1 (unimodal), F4 (basic multimodal), F6 and F7 (hybrid), and F9 and F12 (composition)—so that the sensitivity sweep probes each landscape type while keeping the four-factor grid tractable; it is deliberately smaller than the thirty-run, twelve-function protocol used for the confirmatory benchmark and component-removal studies, and is reported as an exploratory robustness check rather than a tuning procedure.
