Lakshmi Rajendram Bashyam and Ralf Krestel This is the observation we build on: a subject’s relevance to a document is evidenced by whether its neighborhood matches that document too, and the same descriptor should be read differently for different documents. The neighborhood of a subject in the thesaurus is defined by its broader parent, narrower children, and related terms. We cast automated subject indexing as a two-stage retrieve-then- rerank problem and present TAGHN1, a relational reranker that scores each candidate descriptor over a per-document subgraph of retrieved candidates augmented with non-assignable BT/NT/RT context nodes from the thesaurus. To our knowledge, this is the first reranker to condition message passing on the document, so that the same descriptor is read differently depending on the text being indexed • We introduce a document-conditioned message-passing layer with two multiplicative channels: a per-relation edge- type gate and a three-way attention over target, neighbor, and document. • We fold a document–descriptor cross-encoder into the same attention through a gate initialized at zero, so the model begins training without the cross-encoder signal and learns how much of it to use. • We design a decomposed scoring head that combines in- terpretable graph-derived features (semantic, NT-, and RT- match) with retrieval signals, and a specificity loss that encodes the indexing convention of preferring a narrower descriptor over its broader parent. • We evaluate TAGHN on a bilingual thesaurus of 6,198 de- scriptors, with a per-frequency-bin analysis across the head and tail and a qualitative study of how the graph resolves errors that per-label scoring cannot. 2
