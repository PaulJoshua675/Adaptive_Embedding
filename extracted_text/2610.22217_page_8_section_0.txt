These results indicate that explicit skills extracted from expert experience provide more targeted distillation signals, facilitating specialized capability distillation. 5.3 GRADIENT INTERFERENCE IN MULTI-CAPABILITY TRAINING As illustrated in Figure 2, we investigate the cross-capability gradient interference phenomenon (Yu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021) during shared-policy optimization from three complementary perspec- tives: training reward, expert-guided update alignment, and pairwise gradient conflict. We first evaluate the training dynamics in Figure 2a. While both independent capability RL and Mix-RL steadily improve their average rewards, independent optimization maintains a consistent advantage throughout most of the training process. Although the performance gap narrows toward conver- gence, the overall trend clearly indicates that mixed-capability optimization structurally limits the gains achievable through capability-specific learning, preventing the model from fully benefiting from independently optimized capability objectives. To uncover the mechanistic cause of this lim- itation, we analyze how the realized Mix-RL updates align with the behaviors acquired by inde- pendently trained capability experts. We characterize this parameter-space update alignment using cosine similarity (Xu et al., 2026b). Specifically, for each capability, we derive a local expert-guided direction from the negative gradient of the KL divergence between the current Mix-RL policy and the corresponding fixed capability expert under matched response prefixes. We then compute its cosine similarity against the actual parameter displacement between adjacent Mix-RL checkpoints. In this formulation, positive values denote movement toward the expert, whereas near-zero or negative val- ues indicate orthogonal or contradictory updates. As shown in Figure 2b, the shared updates show weak alignment with the expert-guided directions, with negative values observed across multiple capabilities and training stages. This confirms a severe see-saw effect: shared-policy optimization inevitably forces trade-offs, improving certain capabilities at the direct expense of others rather than consistently approximating all capability-specific experts simultaneously. This interference is further supported by analyzing the capability-specific optimization directions themselves. At each Mix-RL checkpoint, we estimate capability-specific gradients using the corre- sponding capability data and compute pairwise cosine similarities among the four capabilities (Yu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021). Positive cosine similarity indicates aligned optimization directions, whereas negative values indicate that two capabilities favor opposing local updates. Figure 2c reveals recurrent negative cosine similarities across multiple capability pairs at different training stages, pro- viding direct empirical evidence that heterogeneous capabilities can favor competing local optimiza- tion directions. Together, these results demonstrate that shared-policy optimization is constrained by cross-capability interference. These results further support the motivation of SCOPED to decouple capability acquisition into independently optimized experts prior to subsequent integration. 8
