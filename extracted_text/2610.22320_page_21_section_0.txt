We further analyze vari- ations of EOS-based semantic control in Tab. 6. Beyond the main variant pre- sented in the primary experiments (Tab. 3), we systematically examine different EOS construction strategies by varying the base EOS components, injected di- rections, and mixing weights. Specifically, ∆app (Tab. 6C) corresponds to directly replacing the EOS embedding with the appearance residual, following [9]. The configuration eosorg sc +eosorg app +eosorg sty naively concatenates EOS embeddings ob- tained from each phrase-level component in the benchmark. The relatively lower performance of ∆app can be attributed to the absence of scene information in the resulting embedding. While another variant eosid + ∆sc is mathematically similar to our additive formulation, it exhibits slightly reduced alignment. We hypothesize that this variant introduces multiple semantic directions originating from overlapping bases, whereas our formulation injects a consistent appear- ance residual ∆app into multiple scene-grounded ⟨EOS⟩anchors {eosn sc}N n=1. By shifting several scene embeddings along a consistent semantic direction, our ap- proach maintains a structured integration of narrative context and appearance cues. This parallel infusion results in more stable semantic control and ulti- mately improves text–image alignment compared to subject-anchored alterna- tives. Overall, these results indicate that simply replacing or naively combining EOS components does not sufficiently preserve semantic grounding. In contrast, controlled injection of the appearance direction into a scene-aware EOS consis- tently improves text alignment, highlighting the importance of structured and context-preserving semantic modulation.
