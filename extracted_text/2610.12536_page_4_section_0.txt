Candidate fitness is the arithmetic mean over the ten fixed search seeds S = {0, . . . , 9}: F(π) = 1 10 X s∈S R(π, s). (3) EliteTable ranks candidates by higher fitness scores. Tasks formulated as minimization problems would require an appropriate score transformation. All candidate slots must finish evaluation or be dis- carded before the elite table is updated. The top K programs are then selected from the new evaluated can- didates and existing elites, using the tie-breaking order described above. After search finishes, the fixed historical winner from every generation is evaluated on 100 held-out episode seeds, H = {1000, . . . , 1099}, which are disjoint from both the search panel and the previously inspected pilot panel 100–199. Each distinct selected policy is evaluated once per held- out seed within a search. Repeated generation winners within that search reuse cached measurements. Held-out outcomes do not influence generation, repair, promotion, stopping, or winner selection. For environment e, search i, and generation g, held-out performance is Ye,i,g = 1 100 X s∈H R(π∗ e,i,g, s), (4) where π∗ e,i,g is the winner selected by search fitness. A valid held-out mean requires finite returns on all 100 specified seeds and no recorded evaluation failures. Partial means are excluded. Missing evaluations remain missing; they are not replaced with zero or rerun. The paired held-out change is ∆e,i = Ye,i,10 −Ye,i,1. I report the median of these ten within-search changes for each task, rather than the difference between the first- and final-generation medians. Descriptive 95% percentile intervals use 10,000 boot- strap resamples of the ten whole-search paired records, with replacement. Each resample preserves the within- search pairing and recomputes the median change. These intervals describe variation conditional on the fixed episode panels and tested configuration; they do not establish robustness across new panels or models. As a retrospective exploratory analysis, I applied two- sided exact paired sign tests [11] to the ten endpoint changes per task. The null is Pr(∆> 0 | ∆̸ = 0) = 1/2, not equality of mean returns. Exact zero changes are excluded from sign counts. Holm adjustment [12] covers all six tasks, retaining an all-tied task as p = 1 for correction only. These tests assume independent searches and condition on the fixed episode panels and tested configuration. They do not establish comparative superiority or causal operator effects. 3.5 Execution and reproducibility Sixty searches were completed: ten initialization seeds across six tasks. Each began at generation zero with no existing solutions. Evaluation ran on an AMD64 host using eight worker processes, with up to 25 concurrent model operations for generation and repair. The config- ured deadlines were 45 seconds per episode, ten seconds per policy action, and 120 seconds per model-operation attempt. The episode and action deadlines exclude Docker startup, queueing, and final cleanup. Generated poli- cies ran in Docker containers with networking disabled, a read-only root filesystem, and resource limits. Core reproduction code and recorded evidence are avail- able at https://github.com/StuartFarmer/elitetable. The accompanying reproduction guide identifies the pinned revision and offline reconstruction commands. The retrospective sign-test extension and additional in- puts and scripts for supplemental analyses accompany this manuscript locally; they are not included in that pinned public release. Reconstructing recorded results and running new stochastic searches are distinct proce- dures.
