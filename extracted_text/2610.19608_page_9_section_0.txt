Since forecasts and outcomes use the same rule, the verdict concerns the lexical target, and the check found no sign that this target departs from a human reading of whether a brand is named. 6 Discussion For these questions and this model, the rate at which a question names a brand held for 20 days. Most cells are all-or-nothing and stay that way: across twelve answers spread over three weeks, 182 of 216 cells were named every time or never. The registered ceiling allowed the Brier score to more than double; the observed rise used 7% of that allowance, and the frozen forecasts show no trend with the horizon. The verdict sits far from the boundary of the rule. The latest wave alone was nearly as accurate as the pooled history. Three answers per question from one session predicted the answers of 20 days later almost as well as nine answers from three sessions. For a measurement design this means that a small sample per question from one session carries most of the information, and that more sessions within a few hours add little. The three history waves lie within 10.2 hours of one another, so this study cannot say whether waves spread over weeks would add what waves spread over hours do not. No D1 answer searched the web, and neither did the v0.4 answers. The model answered from what it stores, under a version string that did not change. D1 shows that sampling at temperature 1.0 and day-to-day serving conditions did not move the rates over three weeks; it gives no evidence about a model update, answers that do search, or other providers. A practical reading: for a fixed model version, a monthly measurement of brand mentions is still current when it is used, and a change in the reported model version is the event that calls for a new one. The plain count beat the shrinkage estimator wherever both were scored: shrinking a cell towards its brand’s rate costs accuracy when most cells sit at 0 or 1 and stay there. 7 Limits The study covers one model (gemini-3.1-pro-preview) from one provider, accessed through its programming interface. It covers 12 known brands and 54 questions in three software categories, in three languages and two requested markets, all asking for up to five products. One later wave measures one interval of 20.3 days, so the study estimates no rate of decay. The outcome is lexical mention, negative mentions included; the study says nothing about recommendation, ranking within an answer, sentiment, factual correctness, citations, new brands, new questions or other providers. The labels come from a whole-token alias rule that misses inflected forms; the human check in Section 5 covers 96 labels read by one rater, the author, and the cross-vendor label reading named in the plan is still pending.
