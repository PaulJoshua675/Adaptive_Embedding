Decentralized agent swarms should scale at inference time. We investigate whether learned self-orchestration enables such scaling through larger agent populations. We vary the population size across 3, 6, 9, and 12 agents by using one to four copies of each trained agent. We conduct experiments on the Iron Pickaxe crafting task over 32 seeds, with three runs per seed, and evaluate pass@3 on five subtasks: Wooden Pickaxe, Stone Pickaxe, Furnace, Iron Ingot, and Iron Pickaxe. Figure 9 shows that larger populations improve performance on all subtasks. Increasing the population from 3 to 12 agents raises pass@3 from approximately 78% to 97% on Wooden Pickaxe, from 69% to 94% on Stone Pickaxe, and from 66% to 91% on Furnace. Pass@3 across five crafting milestones as the agent population increases from 3 to 12. Another advantage of decentralized agent swarms is that they do not rely on a central agent. This can limit the impact of occasional agent unavailability, which becomes increas- ingly likely as the population grows. To evaluate this, we introduce agent dropout at inference time and measure the reward under dropout. We conduct experiments on the Iron Pickaxe task over 32 seeds, averaging three runs per seed. Figure 10 shows that larger populations can preserve more capability even under severe dropout. At a dropout proba- bility of p = 0.9, the 12-agent population maintains a mean episode reward of approximately 33, compared with 19 for the 3-agent population.
