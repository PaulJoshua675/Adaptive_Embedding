This allows us to compare not only a single tuned configuration, but the full trade-off frontier induced by each approach. Figure 2 illustrates the NDCG@10 versus Novelty@10 trade-off. The standard cross- entropy loss serves as the main reference point for comparison. PR-entmax shifts the Pareto front upward, improving both met- rics simultaneously on all datasets. On the Zvuk dataset, all other debiasing approaches exhibit relevance degradation as novelty in- creases. On other datasets, some baselines admit regimes where Novelty improves without sacrificing NDCG. In particular, such configurations are observed for Pop Sampling and Logit Adjust- ment on Tmall, for Pop Sampling, Logit Adjustment, and IPW on Yambda-50M, and for Pop Sampling on Yoochoose. Nevertheless, PR-entmax achieves a more favorable relevance-debiasing trade-off, simultaneously reaching higher Novelty and NDCG levels. These results suggest that the proposed sparse prior-regularized objective improves not only individual operating points, but the overall Pareto frontier of relevance and popularity debiasing. 5.5 Exposure Analysis To further analyze the debiasing behavior of different objectives, we perform an exposure analysis based on Lorenz-style exposure curves. For each method, the x-axis represents the fraction of the most popular items in the catalog, while the y-axis shows the cu- mulative fraction of total recommendation exposure allocated to these items. Such curves provide a more detailed view of popularity bias than aggregate metrics such as Novelty or Coverage, since they characterize how recommendations are distributed across the popularity spectrum.
