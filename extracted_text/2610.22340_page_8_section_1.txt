Dexterity from Supervised Play turns limited goal-free human play into autonomous contact-rich data, an action- conditioned world model, and goal-directed control through play-guided MPC. In simulation, the learned model supports arbitrary-goal reorientation with far fewer interactions than PPO-Match in counted training frames, and a controlled model-stack comparison shows improved goal acquisition under a ﬁxed planner. On real-robot data, held-out predic- tion improves monotonically as autonomous play grows. A provisional hardware summary is consistent with a beneﬁt from world-model search, but requires a frozen protocol and uncertainty before supporting a ﬁnal task-success claim. The ﬁnal evaluation includes both Push-T and RGB reorientation; their frozen quantitative results, together with curiosity and matched play-policy and world-model architecture compar- isons, remain to be inserted.
