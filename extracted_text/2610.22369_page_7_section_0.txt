FLUX.1-dev (Black Forest Labs, 2024) generates one 1024 × 1024 image for each of the 200 DrawBench prompts (Saharia et al., 2022). HunyuanVideo (Kong et al., 2024) generates one 65-frame 640 × 480 video for each of the 946 VBench prompts (Huang et al., 2024), and Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B (Wan et al., 2025) one 81-frame 832×480 video for each of 100 VBench prompts, stratified by dimension (Sec. A.2). Every backbone samples 50 Euler steps. On FLUX we compare against the dy- namic caches TeaCache (Liu et al., 2025a) and SeaCache (Chung et al., 2026), against the re- cent DPCache (Cui et al., 2026), which fixes its refresh schedule before sampling from a cali- brated cost, and against TaylorSeer (Liu et al., 2025b), which forecasts features rather than reusing them. Under our compute budget, on video we compare against SeaCache and DP- Cache, which both report gains over TeaCache and TaylorSeer (Chung et al., 2026; Cui et al., 2026) and are the two strongest baselines on FLUX. On each backbone all methods share the prompts, seeds, generation settings and hardware (Sec. A.1). Methods are compared at matched wall-clock speedup. For each method we sweep the parameter that sets its speed, the threshold for the dynamic caches and the number of fresh steps or the refresh interval for DPCache and TaylorSeer, and take the run nearest each speed band (Tab. A.1.1). PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018) and DreamSim (Fu et al., 2023) measure fidelity to the uncached sample of the same prompt and seed, since a cache approximates the uncached sampler. We ad- ditionally report ImageReward (Xu et al., 2023) for images and VBench for video, which score a sample on its own and separate the methods far less than the fidelity metrics do (Secs. B.3 and 5.3). Tabs. D.2.1 and D.2.2 give the full prompts used in every qualitative figure. 5.2 IMAGE GENERATION Tab. 1 reports four fidelity metrics at three matched speed bands on FLUX, Sec. B.1 adds latency, TFLOPs and peak memory, and Sec. B.2 resolves each band per prompt. SoftCache is ahead of every baseline on all four metrics in all three bands, and at the fastest band it leads SeaCache by 1.12 dB PSNR and 0.082 LPIPS. Because each method’s speed is set by its own parameter, a lead in quality could come from running slower. None of the leads above does. SoftCache is faster than SeaCache and DPCache in every band, and the only two runs faster than it, TeaCache at the slowest band by 0.23× and TaylorSeer at the middle band by 0.15×, trail it by 7.2 and 8.6 dB. Fig. 4 shows generation for three DrawBench prompts at the ≈5.2× band, and illustrates what the gaps of Tab. 1 look like in the image. In these examples the baselines lose the structure of objects, not only their detail. In the first row two of them break the zebra itself, leaving it without a head (TeaCache) or with a second one (DPCache, red ellipses), and even SeaCache, the closest to SoftCache, loses the stripe pattern in the enlarged region, where black bleeds into the white stripes. The canopy of the second row grows curved struts it does not have, and the scarecrow of the third row grows wings it does not have (red ellipses). SoftCache keeps every object the prompt names, with its shape intact, in all three rows. Sec.
