Relevant information in a legal document may be concentrated in a single provision or section, while the remainder of the document may address different topics. Treating the whole document as a single retrieval unit can therefore result in an overly broad representation in which relevant information is diluted by unrelated content [4]. The structure of legal documents provides an opportunity to address this problem. Structural components can be indexed separately, allowing retrieval models to match queries against more focused portions of the document. At the same time, smaller units may lose information needed to interpret their content. The choice of retrieval unit therefore involves a trade-off between repre- senting localized information and preserving the context in which that information occurs. Legal documents are suitable for studying this trade-off because their structural organization is explicitly defined [5]. Brazilian legal documents provide a useful setting for this investigation. Legislative propos- als, laws, and other normative acts follow formal drafting rules that organize their content into explicit structural units [6, 7]. These units include articles and their subdivisions, as well as higher- level components such as sections and chapters. They provide natural boundaries for document segmentation, enabling retrieval representations based on the structure of legal acts. The Brazilian legal retrieval context also presents challenges for current retrieval approaches. A recent benchmark of five Brazilian legal datasets showed that lexical retrieval models remained competitive with and, in some cases, outperformed dense retrieval models [8]. The largest gaps occurred on NormasTCU and Ulysses-RFCorpus, two collections of highly structured legal documents [9, 10]. In this paper, we investigate structural segmentation of Brazilian legal acts for document retrieval. We represent each document as a set of structural chunks and evaluate different repre- sentation strategies. During retrieval, the model matches queries with individual chunks, and the resulting scores are mapped back to the original documents for document-level ranking. We evalu- ate these strategies on NormasTCU and Ulysses-RFCorpus using both lexical and dense retrieval models. 1.1 Research Questions We address two research questions: RQ1. Does structural segmentation improve Brazilian legal document retrieval compared with whole-document indexing? RQ2. Which representation components contribute most to retrieval effectiveness? 1.2 Contributions This work investigates the representation and retrieval of Brazilian legal acts. Its main contribu- tions are as follows: • We investigate structural segmentation as a document representation strategy, comparing it with whole-document indexing using both lexical and dense retrieval models; • We evaluate different structural representations by varying the structural chunks that are indexed and the contextual information associated with them; • We show that context-enriched structural representations improve dense retrieval over whole- document indexing, while a simplified representation based on core chunks and document metadata can achieve comparable or better results than the full hierarchical representation.
