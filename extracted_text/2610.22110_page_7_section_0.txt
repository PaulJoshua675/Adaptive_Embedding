We report chrF++ for its tolerance of inflectional variation [22], but add linguistic diagnostics because overlap cannot judge regional appropriateness, possession, or honorific licensing. 4 Finite-State Morphosyntactic Engineering While neural sequence-to-sequence models excel at cross-lingual transfer, subword tokenizers frequently fracture polysynthetic words into arbitrary pieces that obscure grammatical relations. In Masewalkopa, a single verbal form can encode subject person, object person, reflexive marking, tense, and direction. With only 2,665 curated training pairs, a purely data-driven neural encoder struggles to generalize across sparse morphological paradigms. To provide the neural translation model with explicit structural grounding, we developed a rule-based finite-state transducer using the Helsinki Finite-State Transducer (HFST) toolkit [16]. Rather than replacing the neural model, this analyzer acts as a linguistic feature extractor. It parses surface tokens into interlinear glossed text (IGT) and functional syntax labels, which are then passed directly to the NMT encoder as plain-text prefixes during training and inference (Subsection 5.4). The analyzer combines final-state morphotactic continuation lexicons with two-level phonological alternation rules [2] to capture the core grammatical inventory of Santa Ana Tlacotenco speech. 4.1 Stem Classes and Morphotactics An undifferentiated lexicon overgenerates because stems select different suffixal paths.
