3.3.2 Interaction Stabilizes Network Dynamics Next we compared the dynamics in the read-out layer over the time course of signal processing with self- and pooled-feedback to investigate how interaction alters the decision process of networks in the dyadic model. Figure 3a shows time series for each of the nine decisions averaged over signals. Time series were compiled such that the first trajectory (plotted with o’s) corresponds to the correct response and the following trajectories (plotted with x’s) indicate competing decisions ordered by rank. With self-feedback, networks failed to retain the correct classification decisions until the end of a signal, demonstrated by trajectories that increasingly diverge from their target activation as signals persist. However, with pooled-feedback this divergence was mitigated. Networks maintained more stable decision trajectories over time, shown by a delay in how quickly, and to what extent, each trajectory diverges in the coupled networks. The first-order temporal difference (Equation 3) averaged over test signals was used to measure the dispersion of the trajectories over time in each condition (Figure 3b). A linear mixed-effects regression model with condition as a predictor of dispersion and random effects of node, signal, and time step confirmed that trajectories with pooled-feedback dispersed significantly less over time compared to self-feedback (𝛽= −0.13, p < 0.0001). Interaction may introduce a negative feedback dynamic that stabilizes individual behavior and enables dyads to outperform their best individuals. 3.3.3 Correlated versus Independent Input Noise To simulate the experimental conditions of Bahrami et al. (2010), Gaussian noise was added to the inputs for each ESN that was sampled independently of noise added to other ESN inputs. Therefore, pooling may have helped by averaging out these independent noise sources, or it may have stabilized intrinsic reservoir dynamics that differ across ESNs due to weight randomization and teacher forcing. We tested these two possibilities by comparing performance in self- and pooled-feedback conditions with independent input noise (per the original simulation) versus identical input noise for each ESN in a dyad (noise was always sampled independently across dyads). Input noise was set to 𝜎𝑖𝑛𝑝𝑢𝑡= 2 to match the "high noise" condition from the first simulation, and the same parameter settings were used to compare unstable and stable network architectures. Figure 3c compares dyadic performance with independent versus matched input noise for dyadic networks in both feedback conditions. Unstable reservoirs showed a benefit of pooled- feedback, consistent with prior results, and the benefit persisted in networks with matched input noise. This result indicates that pooling was beneficial at least in part because it stabilized reservoir dynamics.
