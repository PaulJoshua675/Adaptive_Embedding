Table 3 reports the composition of the two parallel collections. Two regimes, separated by the language of the intruders. The models fall into two groups. In the first, on Belebele, entrants are almost entirely in the query’s own language: 88–92% same-language for mE5-base, mE5-large, and e5-mistral, with English essentially absent (at most 0.5%). These models bury a query under its own language. In the second group, the same-language material is a minority, and the displaced slots are filled from other lan- guages. For BGE-M3, Qwen, and Gemma, the same-language share falls to 9–38% while other- language documents dominate. XQuAD shows the same two regimes. The two regimes seen in the aggregate rankings thus correspond to two distinct sources of interference at the level of individual documents. English is the dominant intruder in the second regime. Within the second regime, English enters far more often than any single other language, ris- ing from near zero in the self-crowding models to 7–19% of entrants on Belebele. The other-language column appears larger, but it aggregates every re- maining language, eight on Belebele and four on XQuAD, whereas English is a single language. Di- vided by the number of languages it spans, the other-language share falls to a few percent per lan- guage, below the English share for every model except bge-m3. The second regime is therefore not a uniform spread across foreign languages but a specific pull toward English. The cause is not hubness, and the drop is late. Hub documents account for under 9% of entrants in every model, so the demotion is not explained by a few globally attractive vectors dominating nearest- neighbor search. The drop is also a late-layer event: across models, the average layer at which a gold leaves the top list is the last or second-to-last, in- dicating that the burying is produced by the final task-specializing layers rather than accumulated through the network. 5.3 Interventions The diagnosis shows that a language’s queries are misplaced in the shared space, not that the model fails to represent it. We therefore test correc- tions that act only on the final representation, com- paring the disagreement-guided steering against mean removal, subspace removal, and rotation to- ward a cross-lingual consensus.
