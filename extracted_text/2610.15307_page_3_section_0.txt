Based on this representation, a lightweight meta-learner only needs a very small number of labeled architectures to capture the task-dependent synergy among these priors. Extensive experiments show that FENAS achieves strong ranking quality with very limited labeled architectures while avoiding ex- plicit topology representation learning. In end-to-end search, FENAS remains competitive under constrained budgets, completing the closed-domain search on NAS-Bench-201 in only ∼1138 seconds and remaining in the most efficient tier on DARTS with 0.3 GPU days. The main contributions of this paper are as follows: – We propose a lightweight topology-agnostic NAS prediction frame- work with strong efficiency advantages. FENAS bypasses expensive graph construction and message passing, and instead performs ranking with multi-dimensional priors extracted at initialization. Under extremely lim- ited supervision, it demonstrates strong sample efficiency and competitive search performance, while keeping the overall pipeline structurally simple and computationally lightweight. – We design CAST to synergize heterogeneous physical priors. FE- NAS systematically integrates four complementary dimensions of architec- ture dynamics: expressivity, trainability, connectivity, and capacity. Through distribution alignment and synergy transformation, CAST mitigates scale inconsistency and improves the usability of raw ZCP signals for few-shot prediction. – We show that minimal supervision can turn unstable zero-cost signals into robust predictors. Extensive experiments demonstrate that FENAS consistently improves over individual ZCPs and achieves particu- larly strong results in extreme few-shot settings. The interpretability analy- sis further shows that FENAS adaptively adjusts the importance of different physical dimensions across tasks, providing a direct explanation for its im- proved robustness and cross-dataset generalization. 2
