The practical implication is that no single filtering mechanism is likely to cover the attack surface: the strategies that succeed against Claude differ from those that succeed against GPT and Gemini, so a comprehensive safety evaluation should exercise the full strategy space rather than a narrow set of known patterns. REPRO’s advantage is consistency, not peak performance. Concurrent single-turn methods (AutoDAN- Turbo, KDA, DAGR) achieve high ASR on individual targets, reaching up to 99–100% on specific models (Table 10). What distinguishes REPRO within the single-turn black-box comparison is uniform 100% across all evaluated targets: none of the concurrent single-turn methods reports this consistency across a comparably broad model set. We offer the combination of Bayesian strategy selection and semantic crossover as the likeliest explanation, since these are the two components the ablation isolates, but the comparison itself does not establish it: the concurrent methods were evaluated by their own authors under their own judges, budgets, and model versions, and none of them was re-run here. The claim we can support is that REPRO covers a broader set of targets at ceiling under one protocol, not that it is more robust than a specific competing method. Separately, the EvoSynth method reported in OpenRT [38] achieves 100% evaluated models, but against a different and generally newer model set than ours, making direct comparison infeasible; extend- ing REPRO to multi-turn interaction and evaluating on the same frontier targets is an important direction for future work. 5.2 Limitations Computational cost. While more efficient than exhaustive search, REPRO requires substantial LLM queries for both target evaluation and reflective operations. The worst-case per-behavior cost (5,589 queries) is nontrivial for large-scale deployment, and the reflective model must run continuously throughout optimization, adding inference cost beyond the target queries reported in Table 12. Single-turn focus. The current implementation optimizes single-turn attacks; multi-turn strategies such as Crescendo [33] achieve high ASR through extended interaction, a complementary attack surface that REPRO does not explore. Extending to multi-turn optimization is the most natural next step for the framework. Defended deployments. REPRO is evaluated against target models through their standard API end- points, which carry whatever provider-side safety mechanisms are active. Production deployments often add further layers, including external input classifiers and prompt-injection detectors such as JailGuard [43]. The reported ASR should be read as an upper bound relative to such multi-layered systems, and measuring the gap is an open question we identify in Section 6.1. The judge is inside the optimization loop. All ASR figures are computed using the Qwen3-8B judge, and the same judge scores candidates during the search. Because optimization terminates when the judge accepts a candidate, any response the judge misclassifies as compliant is recorded as a solved behavior and is never revisited. Judge error therefore does not average out across a run in the way it would for a fixed-budget method that reports whatever it obtained: it accumulates into the reported ASR. This is a known hazard of optimizing against an evaluator, and it applies with particular force at a reported ceiling of 100%, where the claim admits no slack for false positives.
