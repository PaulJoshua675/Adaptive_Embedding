Correctness probes. Probes on hidden states predict the truth of a statement or an answer [Azaria and Mitchell, 2023, Marks and Tegmark, 2024, Kadavath et al., 2022, Orgad et al., 2025], and on reasoning models the correctness of an intermediate answer [Zhang et al., 2025a]. Orgad et al. [2025] also define error types by resampling the answers to a question and show that probes predict them; their types are defined per question from sampled final answers, and ours per trace from rollouts at each step. A related study trains correctness probes on instruction-tuned models and one distilled reasoning model [Yuan et al., 2026]; the signal is decodable in all of these models. Steering along the probe direction in two instruction-tuned models does not raise accuracy consistently, which the authors read as a diagnostic signal. The internal signal is useful because verbal reports can differ from the model’s computation [Turpin et al., 2023, Zhao et al., 2026a]. This paper measures the failure types that a correctness probe distinguishes. Failure taxonomies. Uncertainty work commonly separates epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty [Ahdritz et al., 2024, Abbasi Yadkori et al., 2024] or data from model uncertainty [Ren et al., 2026], and a recent account separates input ambiguity, knowledge gaps and decoding randomness [Taparia et al., 2026]; decoding randomness is closest in definition to our near misses. Behavioral work separates failures that resampling fixes from failures it does not [Islah et al., 2026], and a similar division is hypothesized for self-correction [Li, 2026]. Under our taxonomy, the failures that resampling fixes combine near misses with PITs. Concurrent work predicts, from the output log-probabilities of failed rollouts, which intervention rescues a problem [Islah et al., 2026]. Pit-step targets. Process benchmarks label the first locally invalid step [Zheng et al., 2025] or every erroneous section of a long trace [He et al., 2025], which differs from the PIT step, and annotators can disagree about which step is wrong [Jacovi et al., 2024]. State geometry can identify a first local error [Alvarez and Baheri, 2026], and attribution graphs can recover local validity [Zhao et al., 2026b]. Both targets differ from the PIT step, which depends on the model’s continuations from each prefix, so an oracle for local validity need not locate it. Cui et al. [2026] and Yuan et al. [2025] infer step credit from final outcomes, which is a different target from the best reachable value (Proposition 1). Merrill and Srivastava [2026] locate commitment junctures by counterfactual resampling in strategic- deception settings. Among the definitions reviewed here, that counterfactual procedure is closest to the definition used in this study. Appendix A compares each claim with the nearest prior result. 7
