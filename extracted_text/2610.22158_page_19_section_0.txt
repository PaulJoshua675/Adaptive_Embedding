Distinguishing Neighboring Categories Sev- eral neighboring categories are distinguished by the source of indeterminacy. E1.1 applies when a schema-level condition admits multiple supported mappings or aggregations, whereas E1.2 leaves the overall SQL operation unspecified; E3.1 in- stead omits a task-defining constraint while pre- serving that operation. Thus, “sales” in the E1.1 example admits several computations over exist- ing columns, whereas “impactful reviews” in E3.3 lacks a unique rule for mapping the concept to the available indicators. E2.2 and E3.2 may both leave required schema support unavailable to the model, but they arise from different perturbations: E2.2 modifies the query against an unchanged schema, whereas E3.2 preserves the original valid query while withholding part of its required schema con- text. E3.3 differs from both because related fields remain available but an abstract concept lacks a unique semantic mapping. Finally, E4.1 concerns operations such as forecasting that cannot be per- formed by standard SQL over the stored data, while E4.2 concerns external facts that are absent from the database.
