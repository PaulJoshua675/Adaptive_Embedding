In what follows we consider mostly elementary excitations constructed this way. Notice, however, that we can start with arbitrary linear map ρ : h →B and impose a weaker condition that the map σ : x →L(x)ω commutes with translations. Then σ can be regarded as elementary excitation. (Here again L transforms x ∈h into Qρ(x).) Let us consider as an example a Jordan algebra deﬁned as a set of of self-adjoint elements of Weyl algebra. We deﬁne Weyl algebra corresponding to real pre Hilbert space h as a unital ∗- algebra generated by elements a(f), a+(g) depending linearly of f, g ∈h and obeying canonical commutation relations [a(f), a(g)] = [a+(f), a+(g)] = 0, [a(f), a+(g)] = ⟨f, g⟩. We deﬁne a map ρ of h into this Jordan algebra as a map sending f ∈h to a(f) + a+(f). Let us assume that h is an elementary space. Then the translations in h induce translations in Weyl algebra and in the corresponding Jordan algebra. The map ρ commutes with translations. Taking as ω the state corresponding to the Fock vacuum in positive cone or a dual cone we obtain an example of elementary excitation of this state. The same construction works for Cliﬀord algebra speciﬁed by canonical anticom- mutation relations. Notice that the Jordan algebra corresponding to Cliﬀord algebra can be regarded as JB-algebra, but starting with Weyl algebra we obtain a topological Jordan alge- bra, more precisely a Fr´echet Jordan algebra. (Fr´echet vector space is a complete topological vector space where the topology is speciﬁed by a countable family of seminorms. In what follows we are talking about JB-algebras, but our results can be generalized to Fr´echet analogs of these algebras.) Let us discuss the relation of the above constructions to the construction of el- ementary excitations in the approach based on consideration of associative algebra A with involution (∗-algebra). The set of self-adjoint elements of such an algebra can be regarded as Jordan algebra B over R; the complexiﬁcation CB of this Jordan algebra can be identiﬁed with A considered as complex Jordan algebra with involu- tion. The elements of dual cones of these Jordan algebras can be identiﬁed with not necessarily normalized states of A. Let us assume that spatial and time translations act on A as automorphisms; this action generates an action of translations on the cones of Jordan algebras. Let us ﬁx a translation invariant stationary state ω of algebra A; corresponding elements of dual cones of Jordan algebras are denoted by the same symbol.
