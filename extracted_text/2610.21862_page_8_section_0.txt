The number of category types in training data and their corresponding token counts during testing for each language are also shown 6 Conclusions We investigated the impact of dynamic ordering for tagging tasks by introducing TagInsert, a variant of the original encoder-decoder Transformer model of Vaswani et al. (2017) that allows for arbitrary ordering in tag or supertag generation. Our results showed that a BERT encoder model outperformed a L2R Vanilla Transformer and Tag- Insert in both tagging scenarios (POS and CCG), but not in the case of rare long-tail supertags. In comparison to the L2R Vanilla transformer, Tag- Insert significantly outperformed for all data sizes, as well as all the languages examined. Our error analyses of cases where TagInsert correctly tags a word, but models mistagged it, strongly showed that TagInsert defers these difficult decisions to later stages of building the tag sequence. Supertagging for under-resourced languages, as well as for the long tail of CCG categories even for well-resourced languages, is an important un- solved problem in CCG. Combining the TagInsert approach with ‘constructive’ supertagging (Kogka- lidis et al., 2019; Bhargava and Penn, 2020; Prange et al., 2021), where supertags are not treated as opaque labels, but are constructed on the fly, is a particularly interesting direction to combine the strengths of both approaches — the constructive approach has particular benefits on the long tail of rare categories, and TagInsert as well. This might be especially beneficial for languages with very lit- tle CCG training data. In the case of POS tagging as well, although largely a solved problem for En- glish, an interesting avenue for future work would be to experiment with dynamic tagging orders in free word-order languages. In this work, we used an easy-first decoding or- der, and random-sampling for trajectory building during training. Interesting directions for future work are to investigate the benefit of other, complex strategies during inference — of particular interest to us are to base decisions on the shape/complexity of CCG supertags — as well as other sampling methods for building trajectories during training. Finally, decoder-only approaches, which have seen considerable success recently (e.g. Duki´c and Sna- jder (2024) proposed removing the causal attention mask in a decoder-only model in sequence labeling for Information Extraction tasks), or prompt-based zero-shot learning are potential future directions. 8
