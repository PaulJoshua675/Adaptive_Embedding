B.3 Multi-Target Experiments Section 4.2 shows that the effects of example guidance vary with the number of jointly infilled targets. We investigate this behavior with two additional analyses: a reference-likelihood diagnostic that follows successive targets, and a separate cross-benchmark target-count study. The latter uses D = 4 and K = 64 samples per canvas, rather than the K = 16 pools of the main List Functions–Easy D × J grid. Reference-likelihood diagnostic. Previously revealed targets may supply information that would otherwise come from the demonstrations. To examine this possibility, we evaluate the reference tokens in reveal order with D = 4 and J = 8, making each preceding reference target visible under both P1 and P0 while keeping demonstration outputs masked under P0. In Figure 9, the gap between subsequent reference-token negative log likelihoods generally narrows as more targets are revealed. Cross-benchmark target-count study. The main D × J study uses only List Functions– Easy, so we also examine whether its guidance trends hold across other tasks. In this separate experiment, three DLMs are evaluated on four benchmarks at D = 4, J ∈{1, 2, 4, 8}, and K = 64 (Figure 10). At J = 8, guidance at β = 0.5 improves Aall on SST-2, decreases it on Movie Director, is nearly neutral on List Functions–Easy, and varies by model on SALT. Thus, the reduced guidance benefits observed on List Functions–Easy do not hold uniformly across tasks. B.4 Length Allocation Predictor architecture. To evaluate infilling without oracle target lengths, we train length predictors using frozen DLM representations, following Xu et al. (2026a). Each compact prompt ez contains one placeholder per target, whose hidden state before the final transformer block is passed through a trainable copy of that block and its final normalization.
