This metric helps in down voting LLM-generated hallucinations. • Helpfulness: Whether the response effectively addresses the student’s question. An answer can be factually correct, but not necessarily situated to the student’s question. • Solution Presents in Response: Whether the response directly provides solutions, particularly code snippets, for projects. Our system provides students with hints and directions, not solutions, which we try to achieve by tuning our prompts. • Translation Quality: Whether the translation of the generated output from English to Mandarin is free of errors that could impede understanding. Incomplete translations and missing translations are also considered as errors. • Generates Technical Term Glossary: Whether the generated output includes a glossary of the technical terms related to the question and the response. The prompt sent to the LLM includes instructions for generating such a glossary. Table 3 presents the evaluation scores for the generated responses, where each metric is binary, scored as 0 or 1. The last column of the table presents the inter-rater reliability for the five generation metrics. These reliability scores measured the initial round of evaluations conducted by two authors and excluding the final adjustments made by the third author in cases of conflict. We utilized Gwet’s AC1 to compute the reliability because it is more stable than Cohen’s Kappa, particularly when a specific binary rating predominates [21]. The Gwet’s AC1 scores are 94%, 85%, 83%, 67%, and 87%, reflecting the consistent evaluation. In terms of correctness, our pipeline demonstrated a high level of accuracy, achieving perfect scores for both conceptual and environ- ment setup questions (100% each), and a high score for programming questions (97.83%). However, the correctness score for logistics questions was lower at 80.95%. This reduction often occurred in situations where students inquired about specific requirements, such as the necessary formatting for a plot or the use of a particular function. In the absence of clear guidance from the RAG results, the model made assumptions rather than acknowledging the lack of available information. The helpfulness of the responses, as shown in the second row of Table 3, varied slightly across categories but generally remained ro- bust. Responses adequately addressed student questions in 61.90% of logistics posts, 93.33% of conceptual posts, 88.89% of environment setup posts, and 89.13% of programming posts. The responses generally avoided disclosing direct answers. As indicated in the third row, none of the logistics, conceptual, and environment setup questions included direct solutions. However, programming questions experienced a modestly higher rate of direct solution revelation at 13.04%, primarily because these responses sometimes included parts of code snippets. Translation quality is crucial for the comprehension of non-native English speaker students. The pipeline provided Mandarin responses without significant errors in most instances, achieving success rates of 71.43%, 86.67%, 77.78% , and 76.09% for logistics, conceptual, environment setup, and programming questions, respectively. Fail- ures typically occurred when responses were not fully translated or lacked Mandarin entirely. When translations were complete, they maintained high quality consistently. The inclusion rate of a technical term glossary is reflected in the final row of Table 3. The results demonstrate that 95.24%, 86.67%, 77.78%, and 86.96% of the queries in the four categories contain a technical term glossary. When the glossary is provided, the terms generated are essential to the questions, uncommonly encountered in everyday English, and their explanations are accurate. 5 Discussion and Future Work 5.1 Our Contributions Our multi-source RAG-based AI tutor system, which utilizes diverse course materials and a question extraction mechanism, distinguishes itself from single-sourced models prevalent in prior work. It can generate precise, contextually informed responses, achieving an average helpfulness score of 83.52% across the evaluated Piazza questions. Additionally, 82.89% of these responses benefit from the use of question extraction or RAG in generating helpful content. Over 76.92% of the responses provide quality responses in Man- darin, and 87.91% of them contain an effective technical term glossary at the end. Non-native English speaker (NNES) students may hesitate to approach instructors/TAs due to language challenges and confidence issues. Our system has the potential to mitigate such challenges by offering a non-judgmental, accessible interface, fostering engagement and inclusivity in computing education. 5.2 Limitations and Future Work For future work, we aim to deploy our AI tutor system to NNES students in a real classroom setting after making the following enhancements. We will work on improving accuracy of question extraction, particularly for translated posts, to ensure all target questions are retrieved. Additionally, we will implement safeguards to prevent the system from inadvertently revealing answers, especially in programming questions where unintended code disclosure can occur. To create a more engaging learning experience, the AI tutor will be designed to interact in a conversational manner rather than providing just a single response to posts. After deployment, we plan to assess the questioning behavior and needs of NNES students and tailor our system based on the feedback received.
