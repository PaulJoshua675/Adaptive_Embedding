E STATISTICAL METHODOLOGY AND SUMMARY OF RESULTS The primary comparisons and checkpoint-resume experiments use seven paired seeds; OpenWebText and the larger modern architectures use three. We report the mean paired PPL difference ¯∆, its sample SD σ∆, paired Cohen’s d, and exact sign-flip p-values. Unless stated otherwise, a difference is Vanilla minus the method, so a positive value favors the method, and ± denotes a sample SD. With seven seeds, the one-sided sign-flip p-value p+ sf takes its smallest possible value, 0.0078, exactly when all seven differences are positive; with three seeds, its smallest value is 0.125. E.1 Paired Comparisons For every paired comparison of Vanilla with a method at fixed λ and fixed model size, we use the same optimization seed s for both arms, covering data ordering, weight initialization, and dropout mask, so the paired difference ∆(s) := M Vanilla(s) −M Method(s) controls these stochastic factors. Checkpoint-branch diagnostics share the base checkpoint and use the continuation protocol specified in the corresponding experiment. The within-seed design reduces variation due to these shared stochastic factors. The primary 101M WikiText-103, 31M architecture replication, and Lion comparisons use seven paired seeds (Appendix D.2). E.2 Statistics and Sign-Flip Tests Reported statistics. The paired summaries use the mean ¯∆, the paired SD σ∆:= [(1/(n−1)) P s(∆(s)−¯∆)2]1/2, and paired Cohen’s d := ¯∆/σ∆. Tables report the subset of these statistics relevant to each comparison. Unless explicitly labeled otherwise, primary ± values are sample SDs, not standard errors. Earlier small-n tables use a different dispersion convention: Those summaries use population SDs, while the later paired analyses use the n −1 denominator. We do not pool these summaries. Differences are computed before rounding; subtracting two rounded means can differ by one last rounded digit from the reported paired difference. Later table captions state only experiment-specific details. Unless a caption specifies otherwise, a positive paired ∆is the left-minus-right or Vanilla-minus-method contrast on validation PPL, and p+ sf is the one-sided sign-flip p-value for a positive mean. Sign-flip tests. For finite-sample significance, we use exact paired sign-flip randomization tests as the primary p-value whenever the claim is a paired n = 7 contrast. Their finite-sample validity requires invariance of the joint null distribution under independent sign flips of the paired differences. For example, the condition holds for independent differences symmetric about zero. Pairing seeds or assuming only a zero mean does not establish this invariance. At n = 7, the smallest one-sided sign-flip p is 1/27 = 0.0078; at n = 3 the p-value floor is only 0.125, so three-seed comparisons can establish effect direction and scale but not a p < 0.05 sign-flip claim. We also report the paired statistic t := √n ¯∆/σ∆as a supplementary descriptive summary; significance claims use the sign-flip test.
