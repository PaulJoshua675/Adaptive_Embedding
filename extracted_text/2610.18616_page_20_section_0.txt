We report its H@1 and MRR from Tables 2 and 10 of the paper. mFAR (Li et al., 2025a) is a trained retriever that represents each node by one field per property or relation. On MAG and Prime, these fields also include entities up to two hops away. mFAR scores every field with BM25 and a Contriever encoder and combines the scores with query-conditioned softmax weights. The encoder and the weights are trained on the STaRK training queries. The All+2 configuration scores both a single-document and a multi-field representation. We report it from Table 1 of the paper. MoR (Lei et al., 2025) mixes structural and tex- tual retrieval. A Llama 3.2-3B planner, fine-tuned on 1,000 sampled training queries with LLM- generated planning graphs, outlines the entities that a query involves and the relations between them. A layer-wise traversal follows this plan and at each layer merges the neighbors of the planned node type with textual matches, which come from BM25 on Amazon and MAG and from a fine-tuned Con- triever on Prime. A trained reranker then scores each candidate from features of the trajectory that reached it (the node types along the path, whether each step was structural or textual and the text sim- ilarity of each node) and from the candidate’s ada- 002 similarity to the query. We report the MoR row from Table 1 of the paper. GraphRAFT (Clemedtson and Shi, 2025) stores the KG in a Neo4j graph database and retrieves answers in three stages. GPT-4o-mini identifies the entities in the query, and a name without an exact match is resolved to its most similar nodes through a vector index over ada-002 node embed- dings. A text-to-Cypher model then generates Cypher queries whose decoding is restricted to the schema-valid multi-hop queries around the iden- tified entities, and the most probable queries are executed until the retrieved subgraph reaches a size limit. A Llama 3.1-8B-Instruct model then reads the textualized subgraph and selects and orders the answers. The text-to-Cypher model starts from Neo4j’s Gemma 2-9B text2cypher checkpoint, and both models are fine-tuned with LoRA on MAG and Prime. We report the full system from Table 1 of the paper, which covers these two datasets. GRASP (Tao et al., 2026) is a three-stage retrieval pipeline. A prompted Qwen3-27B-Instruct plan- ner writes a JSON plan of anchors, hops and a target, which is compiled into one Cypher query on Neo4j. The resulting candidates are rescored with Qwen3-Embedding-4B on Amazon and MAG and with SapBERT on Prime. Their ranking is then fused with that of a trained mFAR retriever under a weight that can depend on the plan. Finally, a Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B, fine-tuned with LoRA on the STaRK training queries, reranks the top 100 candidates of the fused ranking, and its output is fused with that ranking once more. The fusion weights are tuned on the validation queries, and the Prime planner prompt contains 19 STaRK training queries with hand-curated plans. We report the full system, listed as supervised GRASP in Table 1 of the paper.
