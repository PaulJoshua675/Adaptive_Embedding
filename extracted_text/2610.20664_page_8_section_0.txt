As trajectories involve more search turns, vanilla OPD accu- mulates severe supervision misalignment (Figure 2), making our calibration increasingly crucial. Calibrating supervision helps where a turn curriculum and outcome rewards do not. TCOD’s turn scheduling offers marginal gains at 2B (46.2 vs. 46.0) and underperforms OPD at 4B (52.2 vs. 53.3), suggesting that delaying supervision alone is insufficient without calibrating credit magnitude or direction. Outcome-based GRPO also trails OPD at both scales (45.0/51.9), consistent with the difficulty of assigning credit through sparse trajectory-level rewards. Crucially, IGPO (incorporating IG into policy optimization) reaches only 46.0 at 2B and 53.0 at 4B, trailing IG-OPD by 3.4 and 2.0 points. Although its turn-level feedback improves over outcome-based GRPO, it only matches OPD at 2B and falls slightly below it at 4B. These results suggest that the benefit of information gain depends on how it enters the learning signal: using it to calibrate dense teacher supervision preserves token-level guidance while adjusting supervision according to retrieved evidence. In our setting, this combination of ITC and IQC is more effective than using IG as a turn-level policy reward. 5.3 WHICH CALIBRATION MATTERS? Both priors are harmful, and their corrections are complementary. Table 2 isolates and pro- gressively combines the components of IG-OPD. Replacing the length-induced budget nt/N with a uniform per-turn budget improves OPD by 2.6 points (58.3), surpassing TCOD (57.7); the token- volume prior is harmful even before information gain is introduced. Modulating this budget by the realized information magnitude adds 1.7 points (60.0), with absolute gain outperforming signed gain (59.3). This supports using magnitude to determine how much a turn is supervised, while IQC handles direction. IQC alone yields a 1.0-point gain (56.7), whereas adding it after ITC contributes 2.0 points (62.0). The two calibrations thus address distinct but coupled failure modes: ITC reallo- cates supervision toward informative turns but cannot correct a teacher-disfavored query, while IQC corrects its direction but is less effective when the original budget under-credits search turns. IG-OPD is robust to λ and benefits from a moderate evidence coefficient. Figure 3(a) shows that any λ ∈[0.1, 0.3] outperforms the uniform budget, with the best result at λ = 0.1 and a gentle decline thereafter (59.7, 59.0); since tanh(·) ≤1, even λ = 0.3 bounds the ratio between the most and least credited turns by 1.3, so ITC reshapes rather than overturns the teacher’s supervision. Fig- ure 3(b) shows that accuracy increases monotonically with αmax up to 10 (60.0→62.0), at which point the IQC correction amounts to roughly half of the OPD signal, and drops to 59.7 at αmax = 15 as excessively large corrections disrupt the underlying dense teacher prior, confirming that poste- rior calibration acts best as a complementary guidance rather than a complete replacement. These observations suggest that the posterior evidence should remain a moderate correction. 5.4 DOES IG-OPD RESOLVE EVIDENCE-BLIND SUPERVISION?
