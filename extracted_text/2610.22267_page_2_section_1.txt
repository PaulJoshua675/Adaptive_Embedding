Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) [12] has become the standard mechanism for steering text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models [11, 24, 27] toward high-fidelity, prompt-aligned images. Strong CFG, however, concentrates sampling toward the high-density peak of the conditional distribution, collapsing its effective sup- port onto a narrow region of the image manifold (Fig. 1). Under deterministic samplers such as DDIM [26], the initial noise fully determines the final image, yet independent noise draws collapse onto the same narrow visual mode. Multiple images generated from the same prompt converge to nearly identical visual pro- totypes. The collapse limits utility in applications that depend on distributional breadth, most critically synthetic data augmentation, where near-duplicate out- puts provide diminishing returns for downstream model training [9]. Recovering this diversity requires treating a batch as a whole, since diversity is a collective property of the set rather than a sum over its pairs. A batch can preserve every pairwise distance yet still collapse onto a low-dimensional sub- space, a higher-order failure mode to which independent pair contributions re- spond only weakly. Existing approaches meet this criterion only partially. Meth- ods that intervene during denoising repel intermediate latents [3, 14] or manip- ulate text embeddings [31], paying a per-step cost that scales with trajectory length. Contrastive Noise Optimization (CNO) [13] instead optimizes the initial noise with an InfoNCE loss in the Tweedie prediction space, intervening before denoising begins, where the noise largely determines semantic structure [2, 8].
