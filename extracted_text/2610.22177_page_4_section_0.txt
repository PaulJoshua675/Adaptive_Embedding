Despite their success, existing feed-forward methods primarily rely on appearance-driven features and implicit geometric regularities learned from data. They generally lack explicit physical or motion priors—such as struc- tured optical-flow cues to disentangle dynamic foregrounds or gravity direc- tion (e.g., from IMU or geo-calibration) to serve as a global orientation ref- erence. Consequently, these models must infer correspondences and spatial consistency purely from visual tokens, which can be brittle in dynamic scenes or under ambiguous textures [19]. Our work complements this paradigm by injecting lightweight motion and gravity signals into feed-forward trans- formers, improving geometric robustness while preserving the efficiency of the inference process. 2.2 Learning Using Privileged Information (LUPI) Classical supervised learning minimizes a loss function L(h(xi, θ), yi) over training pairs (xi, yi), where the input space X remains identical during both training and inference. In contrast, Vapnik [20] introduced the LUPI paradigm to mimic human learning, where a ”teacher” provides supplemen- tal guidance—such as explanations or comparisons—available only during training. This transforms the training set into triplets (xi, x∗ i , yi), where x∗ i ∈X∗represents privileged information that is withheld during inference. While early LUPI research focused on Support Vector Machines (SVMs) to predict slack variables and accelerate convergence[21][22], the framework has since evolved into a generalized mechanism for knowledge transfer[23]. LUPI has been successfully applied to diverse domains including image matting[24], person re-identification[25], and healthcare analytics[26]. In this work, we integrate the LUPI paradigm with transformer-based 3D foun- dation models, utilizing motion and orientation priors as privileged informa- tion to guide the model toward a more robust, geometrically-aware inter- pretation of visual inputs. 4
