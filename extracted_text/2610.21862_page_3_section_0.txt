We use CCG supertagging to evaluate our approach, since depen- dencies between supertags in a sentence are much more complex, indicate a preferred order of pre- dicting supertags, and thus the effects of a dynamic tagging order may be more pronounced on CCG supertagging. 3 TagInsert 3.1 Model Architecture TagInsert is a modified version of the origi- nal Encoder-Decoder Transformer that allows for order-agnostic training and inference, as an alterna- tive to the default L2R order. That means that given a sequence of N words, the model will perform N insertion operations of tags on an expanding target sequence with ordering defined by an independent strategy. To achieve this, we make two core modifi- cations: (i) We remove the attention mask on the de- coder side of the Transformer. This mask normally ensures that the model is only exposed to tags gen- erated in previous timesteps by assuming a left-to- right generation order. Since we aim to lift this assumption, the mask can no longer be used. An- other mechanism is needed to prevent exposure to future positions, which leads to the second modifi- cation. (ii) We modify the target sequence so that a special <U> token hides future tags. The <U> tokens serve as placeholders for unassigned po- sitions, effectively implementing a dynamic mask treated as opaque labels but are ‘constructed’. that allows the decoder to attend only to already inserted targets. In practice, the target sequence on the decoder-side starts with <U> tokens in all N positions. During training, the <U> tokens are gradually replaced at each timestep with their cor- responding gold targets. 3.2 Training At any given timestep, the output of the decoder is used to calculate the negative log-likelihood of inserting the correct tag at each position that corre- sponds to a <U> token. The losses are averaged to obtain the sequence loss for the current timestep. Formally, let k be the number of unknown tokens at a given time, U = [u1, ..., uk] is the list of in- dices corresponding to the unknown tokens, and let y be the target sequence. Let N be the length of the full target sequence, and PN = [p1(T), ..., pN(T)] are the probability distributions over the set of tags T generated by the decoder.
