Building on this dynamics-aware importance, we propose Dynamics-aware Token Merging (DaTMe), a training-free token merging framework for diffusion transformers [2, 5, 22]. At each denoising step, DaTMe assigns token roles at the intersection of the temporal and structural signals. High-scoring tokens are assigned as constraint tokens and excluded from merging as dynamically or structurally important regions, while low-scoring tokens are assigned as source tokens from relatively stable and homogeneous regions that can be safely merged. To meet the target compression budget, DaTMe supplements the source set with grid-based spatial sampling, preventing local collapse. The resulting source tokens are then merged only within nearby regions via locality-constrained matching, built upon [9]. We primarily evaluate DaTMe on PixArt-α and FLUX, where it outperforms prior methods while maintaining visual fidelity across the entire image. Our contributions are as follows: • We introduce a dynamics-aware importance map derived from the Tweedie estimate, jointly capturing the temporal evolution and structural saliency of each token. • We propose DaTMe, a training-free token merging framework that leverages the proposed temporal and spatial importance signals to identify safely mergeable tokens while preserving structurally critical regions. • We validate DaTMe on DiT (PixArt-α) and MM-DiT (FLUX), showing strong preservation of image-wide details and state-of-the-art performance across most metrics. 2 Related Works Token Reduction in Large Models. Token reduction is a widely used strategy for improving Transformer efficiency, mainly through token pruning or token merging [10, 23–26]. Representative methods such as ToMe [27] use training-free bipartite matching, while later approaches refine token selection using attention or frequency-domain cues [25, 26]. However, most of these methods target single-forward-pass architectures with relatively static token importance. Diffusion models, by contrast, update token representations over many steps, motivating diffusion-specific approaches such as ToMeSD [10]. Token Merging in Diffusion Models. A wide range of methods have been proposed to accelerate diffusion models, including improved samplers [28–32] and weight quantization [33, 34]. However, such approaches often require additional training or optimization, introducing non-trivial overhead. Training-free methods instead reduce the number of tokens processed during inference through prun- ing [18, 35], caching [36–38], or merging [9–13]. Among them, ToMeSD [10] extends token merging in ViTs [27] to diffusion models by grouping tokens based on feature similarity. Several subsequent methods [12, 13] leverage cross-attention maps to identify important tokens from semantically or structurally salient regions, aiming not merely to reduce computation but also to preserve informative representations during token reduction [39]. More recently, [9] uses classifier-free guidance (CFG) as a saliency signal without additional attention computation, but because CFG primarily reflects alignment with the conditioning signal, it fails to capture the spatially heterogeneous and temporally evolving dynamics of the full image. In contrast, our method leverages the Tweedie denoised estimate to construct a dynamics-aware importance map that captures both temporal evolution and spatial structure across the image. 3 Method Our goal is to design a token merging strategy for efficient image generation by accounting for diffusion dynamics. To this end, we first define a dynamics-aware importance signal that character- izes token importance from two complementary perspectives: temporal dynamics and structural saliency. Based on this importance map, we assign tokens into functional roles for merging, dis- tinguishing safe merge sources and structurally important constraint tokens. We then perform locality-constrained token merging, together with grid-based source completion to satisfy the target compression ratio.
