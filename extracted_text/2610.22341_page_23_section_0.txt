The 9,996 upstream Articraft records (Zhou et al., 2026) partition into 6,882 mesh-based CAD assemblies and 3,114 primitive-based articulated mechanisms. For the mesh-based assemblies, we perform a clean-room reimplementation of the Articraft geometry helpers coupled with a tree- shaking AST inliner to produce standalone CadQuery programs (median ≈900 lines), verifying every output against the upstream geometry via Chamfer distance (< 0.02) and bounding-box aspect ratio (∈[0.9, 1.1]); 6,878 of 6,882 (99.9%) pass verification and are rendered from eight canonical views. The primitive-based mechanisms are packaged as executable URDF specifications across geometry-only and textured variants (6,146 records in total, including 3,032 textured mechanisms) with multi-pose kinematic renders. Procedural, community, and agent-synthesized collections. The remaining sources (Table 5) are all execution-verified: (1) Bioinspired3D (Luu & Buehler, 2026a), 6,343 Blender Python pro- grams (4,557 geometry programs and 1,786 material-textured variants) that all execute, export, and render from five views; (2) Thingiverse OpenSCAD, 1,193 parametric projects curated from 7,378 community designs, with their original source files, five renders, and item-level license meta- data; (3) 3DCodeBench factories, 486 procedural Blender generators (243 factories derived from Infinigen (Raistrick et al., 2023), each in geometry and textured variants, averaging ≈880 lines) and 3,904 curated seeded instances (1,953 geometry and 1,951 textured), all held out from benchmark training because the 212 3DCodeBench tasks come from the same generator family (Appendix C.1); (4) agent-authored programs, 949 Blender Python programs and 1,134 Three.js scenes synthesized by coding agents under execution and render verification (from 1,040 and 1,389 attempts), plus 25 multi-file Three.js repositories and 1,051 web-animation programs across 21 categories; and (5) harness-generated bootstrap programs, 15,704 execution-verified programs carrying quality tiers, checklist verdicts, and gate telemetry, which substantially improve target-dialect executability in our fine-tuning mixtures (§C.4). C EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS AND ADDITIONAL RESULTS C.1 TRAINING DETAILS Training configuration. All fine-tuning runs are conducted with LLaMA-Factory (Zheng et al., 2024) on 4 × NVIDIA H100 (80 GB) GPUs in bfloat16 precision using gradient checkpointing and sequence packing. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) applies LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) of rank 64 (α = 128, dropout 0.05) across all linear projections with a learning rate of 1×10−4 (or 1×10−5 for full-parameter fine-tuning under ZeRO-3 (Rajbhandari et al., 2020)), a cosine learning-rate schedule, a 5% linear warm-up ratio, and a per-device batch size of 1 with gradient accumulation of 8 (4 for 27B). Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Rafailov et al. 2023) initializes from the merged SFT checkpoint and trains a rank-16 LoRA (α = 32) for 2 epochs under sigmoid preference loss (β = 0.1, learning rate 5 × 10−6). The sequence context length is set to 8,192 tokens for 9B runs and 4,096 tokens for 27B runs to fit single-device memory bounds under data-parallel training. Evaluation 23
