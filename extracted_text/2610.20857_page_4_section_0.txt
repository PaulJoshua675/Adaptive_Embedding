The protected relevance policy uses a BM25-like score over title and complete available abstract text, complemented by recorded retriever ranks. It reserves limited capacity for corroborated seeds, strong retrieval ranks, and missing-abstract candidates, and handles publication-year eligibility and duplicate entities explicitly. This is still a budgeted selection algorithm: a relevant candidate can be dropped below a capacity boundary. It is not a guarantee of recall, and it is distinct from the semantic citation-neighbor gate. 3.3 Abstract assessment and semantic citation expansion The merged abstract prompt produces an ordinal overlap label, covered facet IDs, task and method similarities, a short reason, and the paper’s high-level facets. The labels are 3 for high overlap, 2 for partial overlap, 1 for adjacent work, and 0 for no overlap. Missing evidence and invalid model outputs retain explicit unassessed or failed states; they are not converted into valid zero scores. In the staged profile, the older lexical-zero and Boolean LLM prefilters are bypassed before abstract scoring. Local budget selection still applies. After initial assessment and metadata enrichment, up to five papers seed citation expansion through Semantic Scholar. Unique neighbors with abstracts are assessed in batches of up to 20. The Light-role model receives the original research query, complete idea, generated search queries, organized keywords, facets, and each neighbor’s title and abstract. Its rubric recognizes synonyms and mechanism-level matches, and explicitly forbids assigning zero merely because a keyword is absent. The complete prompt and hypothetical calibration examples appear in Appendix B. Valid zero-score neighbors are excluded; positive neighbors are ordered 3 > 2 > 1, with up to 15 retained per seed under the default setting. Missing abstracts or scoring failures remain unassessed and bypass that positive-only cap, while still being subject to the global candidate budget. The default global budget admits at most 50 new citation candidates. Identity checks, deduplication, and abstract assessment then update the pool. Neither semantic filtering nor a citation relationship grants automatic access to the final shortlist. 3.4 Shortlisting and bounded PDF evidence The current profile selects up to 50 assessed works plus a buffer of up to ten boundary or uncertain candidates. Thus at most 60 distinct eligible works reach the Expert, and the actual count can be smaller. Assessment ordering considers overlap grade, task and method similarity, covered facets, and related structural signals; the staged policy does not use citation count as a relevance tiebreaker. Conflicting identities and known out-of-range publication years are excluded and logged. Exactly five candidate slots, or fewer when fewer eligible candidates exist, are selected for PDF acquisition. These are fixed paper-level attempts, not a promise of five successful PDFs or a cap of five HTTP requests. If a PDF is unavailable, the workflow does not substitute another paper. The attempted paper remains available through its abstract or metadata, and the acquisition outcome is included in the Expert input. For each available PDF, the default local extractor scans native text from all pages using windows of at most 1,800 characters, with a half-window stride. It orders windows by distinct idea/facet terms, capped term frequency, section cues, and deterministic location tiebreakers. It first takes a high-scoring window, then seeks nonoverlapping query-relevant coverage of method, experiment, and conclusion cues before filling the remaining slots. At most six windows are sent per paper, in reading order. This selector is a lexical window heuristic with section cues: it is not a guaranteed Introduction/Related Work/Method/Conclusion parser, and it makes no extra model call. Full extracted text stays in local artifacts. Sent windows record page locations, offsets, and hashes, and are explicitly marked partial. 4
