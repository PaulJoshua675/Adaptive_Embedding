The state vector is defined as: s(t) = h f (t) best, f (t) mean, f (t) std, f (t) div, f (t) ng , f (t) imp, σ(t) 1 , . . . , σ(t) d , ..., σ(t) Nd i where f (t) best denotes the normalized best fitness achieved so far, representing the global search performance; f (t) mean and f (t) std are the normalized mean and standard deviation of the population fitness, describing overall population quality and dispersion; f (t) div measures population diversity, defined as the average distance of individuals to the population centroid; f (t) ng is a normalized generation index, scaled by the maximum number of generations per episode, providing temporal information on search progress; f (t) imp captures recent fitness improvement, defined as f (t) imp = max{0, f (t−1) best −f (t) best}, serving as a convergence indicator; σ(t) d is the normalized standard deviation of the population along each parameter dimension (d = 1, . . . , Nd), capturing anisotropic spread of the search. This state construction provides an informative description of the optimization landscape, capturing both convergence status and population geometry. In particular, the diversity and improvement indicators allow the agent to adaptively balance exploration and exploitation through its action outputs, while the dimension-wise spread supports directionally sensitive (anisotropic) search regulation. 2.3.2 Action Vector and Control Variables The action a(t) at generation t is a continuous control vector that regulates the evolutionary operator, defined as: a(t) = [F (t), CR(t), λ(t), LR(t) cov, ∆σ(t), p(t) res, r(t) eli , β(t)] where each component governs a specific aspect of the evolutionary search: F (t)and CR(t) control the mutation scaling factor and crossover probability of the DE operator; λ(t) scales the effective population size relative to a base population; LR(t) cov is the learning rate of the CMA-ES covariance matrix update, governing how rapidly the search distribution adapts; ∆σ(t)introduces a bias term to the CMA-ES step-size adaptation mechanism, directly regulating the global search radius; p(t) res specifies the restart probability applied to newly generated individuals, supporting escape from local minima; r(t) eli determines the proportion of elite individuals preserved across generations, strengthening exploitation; and β(t) ∈[0, 1] governs the allocation ratio between DE-based and CMA-ES-based offspring generation. This action formulation enables continuous, coordinated orchestration of multiple evolutionary mechanisms within each generation, rather than discrete switching between algorithms, allowing the search dynamics to transition smoothly among exploration-dominant, exploitation-dominant, and hybrid regimes as optimization progresses across the FEMU or general-optimization landscape. 2.3.3 Reward Function The reward r(t) is designed to quantify how effectively the applied action a(t−1) advances the optimization process at generation t, combining improvement-driven reinforcement, diversity encouragement, and stagnation penalization: r(t) = λ1 · max(0, f (t−1) best −f (t) best) + λ2 · P (t) std −λ3 · C(t) sta + I(f (t) best < ε) where f (t) best denotes the best fitness value in the current population; P (t) std measures population diversity, computed as the per-dimension standard deviation of the population, averaged across all Nd dimensions at generation t; C(t) sta is a stall counter recording consecutive generations without improvement; and I(·) is an indicator function providing a terminal reward once the solution accuracy reaches a predefined threshold ε. The non-negative coefficients λ1, λ2, and λ3 balance the relative contributions of the improvement reward, exploration incentive, and stagnation penalty.
