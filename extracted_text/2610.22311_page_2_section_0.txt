These methods show that not all past frames are equally useful, and figure 1 summarizes this progression from dense history to view-level retrieval. Yet frame-level selection remains coarse for transformer attention. It provides the same selected context to all regions in the active chunk, although dynamic scenes often need local temporal continuity and different regions in the same noisy chunk can depend on different parts of the past. Methods that inject warped point renderings into the model input can also require architectural adaptation or fine-tuning, which limits direct reuse of a pretrained video generator. Velox routes each current latent region to compact, geometry- relevant history blocks using frustum slab correspondences. Inspired by inverse rendering works (Kerbl et al., 2023; Mildenhall et al., 2021), we observe that regions whose geometry has already been observed should primarily attend to their corresponding past regions. Newly revealed or uncertain regions, in contrast, may require model-derived semantic context and local temporal neighbors. A road block, a facade block, and newly revealed sky in the same noisy chunk should therefore not necessarily attend to the same past views. The question, then, is no longer only which frames to retrieve, but whether long-context video generation can assign each region its own relevant history while preserving local motion continuity and leaving the pretrained generator unchanged. We consider this question at the token-block level in the block-wise execution model of FlashAt- tention (Dao, 2023). Our key insight is that camera frustum slabs with depth intervals can provide region-specific routing signals at the same granularity used by tiled attention. Instead of adding a new conditioning branch or fine-tuning the video generator, Velox summarizes generated history as per-block frustum slabs and uses their overlap to guide which historical KV token blocks each current region should see. For each target chunk, Velox obtains query-side depth through recency-priority multiwarp and scores query and historical blocks by world-space OBB frustum slab overlap. In well-covered regions, this score softly reweights each layer’s model-derived block importance before selection. Uncovered regions retain the model-derived route without geometric bias. Previous- chunk retention and global-aligned Gilbert neighborhoods preserve local motion continuity without a separate cross-chunk seam table. Across 48-chunk timing traces and the 158-case WBench navigation split (Ying et al., 2026), Velox re- duces the growing long-context attention cost while preserving aggregate quality near dense attention. Its advantage grows with the number of generated chunks. At chunk 48, attention latency decreases from approximately 34.2 ms with dense FlashAttention to 7.8 ms, a 4.36× speedup. Denoising time decreases from approximately 7.19 s to 2.96 s under the same single-H200 asynchronous setting. Built on LingBot-Fast-Cam (Gao et al., 2026), Velox remains within 0.15 points of dense attention on the WBench API-free average and improves the 19-metric overall average from 75.52 to 75.60. These results support the scoped claim that soft frustum slab routing can alleviate the late-chunk attention bottleneck without materially degrading benchmark quality. 2
