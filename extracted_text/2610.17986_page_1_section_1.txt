A common practice in instance retrieval is to grow the gallery size to improve a benchmark’s discrimination capability. One prominent example is ILIAS, which adds up to 100 million distractor images. The prevailing assumption is that faith- ful evaluation demands web scale, and that indexing all 100M images through the backbone is the unavoidable price of a comparable number. That price is steep: hundreds of GPU-hours and ∼200 GB of descriptors per model, paid again for every method or ablation. We show the web-scale indexing price is largely avoid- able: evaluation itself obeys a scaling law. The number of distractors that outrank a positive is Binomial in the gallery size. We define the per-query retrieval hard- ness, a gallery-scale agnostic quantity that yields a retrieval metric at any scale, and demonstrate it can be derived from a small random sample. The underlying sampled-rank machinery is long established in recommender-system and biomet- ric evaluation, but to our knowledge has never been adapted to visual retrieval. We make the law per-query and estimable, and validate it at true 100M scale on ILIAS and INSTRE, and on the full 759k-distractor Google Landmarks v2 pool. Concretely, within a 5% sample (a flat 20× less indexing), every backbone’s tail- aware (EVT) estimate has converged onto the large-scale mAP, to within ∼0.01 (mean absolute error 0.005), across five backbones, three benchmarks, and two distractor pools (mAP@1000 at 100M on ILIAS/INSTRE; the official mAP@100 at the full 759k pool on GLDv2); the recommender-systems count baseline over- predicts everywhere (+0.037 to +0.068). This is an order of magnitude better than naively reporting the metric on the same subset (+0.08 to +0.13). We re- lease code and the per-query hardness to facilitate faster, cheaper, and greener retrieval evaluation. 1
