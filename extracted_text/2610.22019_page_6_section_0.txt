After 500B tokens, midtraining improves both pass@1 and pass@k on every benchmark, so it raises what the model can solve rather than only its reliability. benefits show up beyond SWE-bench pass@1, in file-state tracking and cross-file modeling (Tables 4 and 5) and robustness across agent harnesses (§4.5), and a blend of mostly traces would amount to a much longer SFT, which can hurt later RL trainability (Liu et al., 2026b; Kang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026). 4.3 SCALING N-3.5 LIGHTNING TO 500B TOKENS Our main N-3.5 Lightning experiment uses the long-context checkpoint N-3.5 Lightning-LC as the base model (§3.1). The blend has about 380B unique tokens (Table 1), enough to midtrain for 500B tokens while seeing each natural source about once. We apply identical full SFT to the base model and to midtrained checkpoints from 100B to 500B tokens (Table 3). Midtraining improves every benchmark over the base model. After 500B tokens, pass@1 rises by 3.8 points on SWE-bench Verified, 7.0 on SWE-bench Multilingual, 3.5 on SWE-bench Pro, and 1.0 on Terminal-Bench, and pass@k rises on all four, by 2.3 to 4.7 points. Most of the SWE- bench Verified and Pro gain arrives by 100B tokens, SWE-bench Multilingual keeps improving up to 500B, and Terminal-Bench is noisier and peaks at 300B. Unlike Table 2, these results start from N-3.5 Lightning-LC. After identical full SFT, its scores differ slightly from those of N-3.5 Lightning-8K, but stay within run-to-run variation. Midtraining also changes how the agent uses its context. Without midtraining, 4.1% of SWE-bench Pro rollouts fail by filling the 256K context window, compared with 0.6% after 500B tokens (Appendix E.1). The base-model post-trainability metrics show the same trend (Table 4). All four improve over the base model and keep improving from 100B to 500B tokens, reflecting the capabilities that the natural SWE data and traces add. On LoCoDiff, for example, the base model rebuilds no files exactly, while the midtrained model rebuilds 21.5% after 100B tokens and 26.8% after 500B. Most of this gain comes from the diff data, which raises exact match from about 1% to 30% in a controlled 10B-token comparison in Table 5. 4.4 SCALING TO N-3.5 SUPER We further apply the recipe to the larger N-3.5 Super to test how it scales with model size.
