Do score and overlap signals matter? Table 4 validates the two signals used by XViewRank. The score-only variant uses only the three normalized BM25 score features. The document-features-only variant uses document-level view indicators such as rank, view presence, and view count, but removes the query-level view-overlap features. The extended-features variant combines those document-level indicators with overlap. The results show that score features alone are not sufficient, and removing overlap causes a large drop, especially on DL19. Adding broader document-level indicators also does not consistently improve over the score-overlap representation. This supports the central design choice: the model should know not only how strongly each view retrieves a candidate, but also how redundant or complementary the views are for the query. The particularly low DL20 result of the document-features-only variant de- serves clarification. Rank, presence, and view count describe where a document occurs, but they discard the magnitude of its normalized retrieval support. Their distributions are also more sensitive to changes in candidate-pool composition across test years. The result therefore does not imply that provenance indi- cators are intrinsically harmful; it shows that they are a weak substitute for view-specific score evidence and transfer less reliably on their own. Does the model use the overlap values? To test whether the association between a query and its overlap context matters beyond the marginal overlap distribution, we perturb only the three overlap features at inference time while keeping the view-specific BM25 scores and trained checkpoint fixed. We inde- pendently permute complete overlap vectors among queries 100 times, preserv- ing both the marginal distribution and the dependence among the three overlap dimensions while breaking their association with the current query.
